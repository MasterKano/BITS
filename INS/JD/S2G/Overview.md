## 1) Takedowns & Standing Skills

**Vol. 1 — Fundamentals of Stance, Motion & Hand Fighting**  
Builds the standing foundation: posture, stance, head position, movement, hand fighting, defensive soundness, and the first principles of safe engagement. It establishes the core Jiu-Jitsu idea that standing success starts with posture and controlled contact, not reckless shot attempts.

**Vol. 2 — Grips, Arm Drags & Entries**  
Focuses on the setup phase before takedowns: grips, indirect gripping, arm drags, snap-down logic, and entry methods that reduce risk and increase clean access to the legs or back. This is the volume where neutral standing starts turning into meaningful attack structure.

**Vol. 3 — Single & Double Leg Mechanics**  
Covers the mechanics of the main lower-body attacks, especially singles and doubles, with attention to posture, penetration, weight management, and transitions when the first attempt meets resistance. It is the core lower-body takedown mechanics volume.

**Vol. 4 — Finishing Shots Under Jiu-Jitsu Rules**  
Takes shot entries through to completion under Jiu-Jitsu conditions, where the finish must account for submissions, scrambles, and post-takedown control. The emphasis is not just “finish the shot,” but finish in a way that actually scores and holds up in grappling.

**Vol. 5 — Defending Shots & Counter-Attacks**  
Addresses anti-shot defense and how to turn the opponent’s takedown attempts into your own offensive opportunities. It expands the standing game from attacking well to also winning the exchange when the opponent initiates first.

**Vol. 6 — Combining Shots With Submissions**  
Integrates takedown attempts with submission threats and counters, reflecting the specifically Jiu-Jitsu reality that shots cannot be separated from submission danger. This volume links wrestling-style entries to grappling-style finishing logic.

**Vol. 7 — Guard Pulling as a Complementary Tactic**  
Explains guard pulling as part of a complete standing strategy rather than a separate or inferior choice. It shows how pulling complements takedowns and gives the athlete a broader tactical decision tree in neutral standing.

**Vol. 8 — Building a Complete Standing System**  
Synthesizes the first series into one coherent standing game: stance, hand fighting, entries, singles, doubles, finishes, defense, submission integration, and guard-pull decision making. This is the “put it all together” volume for neutral standing.

## 2) Positional Dominance & Scrimmage Wrestling

**Vol. 1 — Standing Rear Body Lock & Short Offense**  
Centers on rear body lock and close-range mat returns as dominant standing positions that make takedowns safer and easier than neutral shots. It shifts the focus from entry mechanics to positional control before the finish.

**Vol. 2 — Breaking Stance & Re-Shot Mechanics**  
Covers how to exploit compromised posture and recovery movement, especially through re-shots. The volume teaches that attacking during the opponent’s reset or failed attack is often easier than initiating cleanly from neutral.

**Vol. 3 — Scoring Beyond the Takedown (Back Takes & Aftermath)**  
Focuses on what happens after initial contact or the takedown itself: back takes, control, and the scoring window that follows the descent to the mat. It frames the aftermath as an essential part of the standing system, not an afterthought.

**Vol. 4 — Defending Leg Tackles & Immediate Aftermaths**  
Deals with defending leg attacks and then winning the scramble immediately afterward. The emphasis is on using defense not merely to survive, but to reverse momentum and arrive in offensive control.

**Vol. 5 — The Four-Point Stand-Up & Standing Escapes**  
Covers structured methods of regaining the feet and escaping standing or grounded control. This volume broadens the system by making re-standing a trained skill rather than an improvised reaction.

**Vol. 6 — Integrating Submissions Into Standing Exchanges**  
Shows how submission threats can be woven into standing scrimmage situations as counters, traps, and finishing extensions. It deepens the Jiu-Jitsu-specific nature of the system by linking standing control directly to submissions.

**Vol. 7 — Scrimmaging for Points vs. Wrestling Criteria**  
Distinguishes Jiu-Jitsu scoring logic from wrestling logic and teaches how to scrimmage for the outcomes that actually matter under grappling rules. It clarifies that a technically successful takedown exchange is not always the same as a scoring success.

**Vol. 8 — Building a Jiu-Jitsu-Specific Standing Game**  
Brings the positional dominance series together into a coherent standing style built on rear control, re-shots, aftermath management, escapes, submissions, and rule-aware scoring. It is the synthesis volume for the scrimmage wrestling layer.

## 3) Upper Body Takedowns

**Vol. 1 — Philosophy of Upper Body Takedowns & Their Advantages**  
Introduces the strategic logic of upper-body takedowns and explains why they fit well within submission grappling. It frames upper-body attacks as a control-heavy alternative or complement to lower-body wrestling.

**Vol. 2 — Underhook-Based Throws**  
Develops the underhook as a central throwing hub and builds the main attacks that flow from it. This is the foundational offensive clinch volume for the upper-body series.

**Vol. 3 — The Cow Catcher & Head-Chancery Series**  
Focuses on head-and-arm control as a takedown system, using cow catcher and related head-chancery attacks. It gives the series a front head-and-arm branch distinct from standard leg-attack entries.

**Vol. 4 — Overhook-Based Throws**  
Builds a parallel throw system from overhook control, showing how upper-body dominance can be created from a different tie structure. This volume expands the clinch game beyond underhooks.

**Vol. 5 — Double Overhooks: Inside Trips & Step-Arounds**  
Centers on body-to-body clinch attacks from double overhooks, especially compact trips and step-around finishes. It emphasizes close control and efficient upper-body finishing mechanics.

**Vol. 6 — Adaptations for Older or Less Athletic Athletes**  
Adapts the upper-body game for athletes who need safer, lower-risk, less explosiveness-dependent options. The focus is on leverage, control, and practical selection rather than raw athletic output.

**Vol. 7 — Sacrifice Throws**  
Introduces sacrifice-throw mechanics as part of the upper-body takedown system. The volume broadens the throwing layer while still fitting it into a controlled grappling framework.

**Vol. 8 — Sumigeshi & Counters to Leg Attacks**  
Closes the series by linking upper-body throwing mechanics to anti-shot responses and counter-offense. It makes the upper-body system part of the answer to lower-body wrestling threats rather than a separate game.

---

## One-Line Summary of the Three Series

- **Takedowns & Standing Skills** builds the neutral standing game.
- **Positional Dominance & Scrimmage Wrestling** builds the control-and-aftermath layer.
- **Upper Body Takedowns** builds the clinch-and-throw layer that complements and counters leg-attack wrestling.
