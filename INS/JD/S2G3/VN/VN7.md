# VIEWING NOTES — Standing to Ground 3: Upper Body Takedowns (John Danaher)
## VN07 — Volume 7

### 7.1 Throwing From an Underhook — Uki Goshi (Half Hip Throw) (0:00–6:45)
- Objective
  - Convert a single underhook into a reliable half-hip throw that lands directly into a chest-to-chest pin.

- Gates
  - You have a true underhook (not shallow/loose).
  - You can control the far-side arm enough to rotate shoulders (prefer elbow control over wrist control).
  - You can pivot your feet before inserting the leg (no “leg-in first” entry).

- Controls
  - Underhook side: maintain tight chest connection.
  - Far-side arm: shift from wrist to elbow control to prevent peel/yank-out late-match.
  - Shoulder line: rotate opponent’s shoulders before entry.

- Primary line
  - Underhook established.
  - Inside wrist position (your wrist inside/on top) → capture elbow → pull elbow across belly line to rotate shoulders.
  - Pivot feet (heels light) to align throw direction.
  - Stab-step between legs + level drop → spiral down into pin.

- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent yanks arm free from wrist grip → prefer elbow grip early; keep wrist inside to access elbow.
  - Opponent tries roll-through after landing → keep hips low, head high, body perpendicular; if needed, post hand briefly and resettle into pin.

- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Failure: opponent peels far arm / you lose control.
    - Cause: wrist-only grip; wrist outside/on bottom; late transition to elbow.
  - Failure: you can’t pivot/rotate to throw.
    - Cause: you stabbed the leg in before pivoting feet.
  - Failure: you get rolled through.
    - Cause: hips high + head low + alignment parallel instead of perpendicular.

- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Hand-fight to inside wrist position → snap to elbow capture (reps).
  - Pivot-first entry drill (pivot feet before any leg insertion).
  - Finish drill: throw → immediate chest-to-chest pin with low hips/high head.

- System tags
  - [UH:UkiGoshi] [Controls:Elbow] [Rule:PivotBeforeEntry] [Landing:ChestToChestPin] [Risk:RollThroughMgmt]


### 7.2 Ogoshi (Full Hip Throw) (6:45–11:26)
- Objective
  - Use a full hip insertion variant from underhook situations when conditions allow, finishing with control (not a “demo lift”).

- Gates
  - You can bring hips fully across and get hip-to-hip contact (not half-hip).
  - You can maintain enough control to commit to the ground and land in a pin (training-safe variation encouraged).

- Controls
  - Underhook + waist/hip connection: draw opponent onto your hip line.
  - Far-side arm: commonly wrist control is shown here, but acknowledge peel risk; control must be firm enough to finish.

- Primary line
  - Underhook obtained → draw opponent’s hips in.
  - Step across and bring hips fully through (full hip insertion) → lift/turn enough to complete.
  - Commit to controlled landing (knee-down safety in training) → arrive in pin.

- Branches
  - Opponent is too skilled/active in front-on hip throw posture → avoid staying in the classic “standing in front” posture; use the more realistic entry that doesn’t linger in the vulnerable position.

- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Failure: you can’t load hips fully.
    - Cause: insufficient hip-to-hip contact / feet too far apart / you didn’t draw hips in first.
  - Failure: exposure to counters while standing in front.
    - Cause: lingering in a front-on, high-commit posture.

- Drills
  - Underhook-to-hip insertion entries with controlled knee-down landing.
  - Finish-to-pin repetitions emphasizing safe impact management.

- System tags
  - [UH:Ogoshi] [Entry:FullHipInsertion] [Landing:CommitToPin] [Safety:KneeDown]


### 7.3 Tai Otoshi (11:26–17:14)
- Objective
  - Execute a high-percentage throw from underhook using a strong two-feet structure, finishing into a pin.

- Gates
  - Opponent is squared enough (or you can square them) to rotate over the blocking/tripping line.
  - You can secure wrist control and actively pull it in (not “grab and hope”).

- Controls
  - Underhook: used to square and control shoulder line.
  - Far wrist: control + pull to reduce pull-away.
  - Head position: manage roll-through attempts post-landing (head high, hips down).

- Primary line
  - Underhook in place → draw opponent into squared stance.
  - Entry/back step/trip step sequence → throw toward posted foot.
  - Land hip-to-hip into pin; if opponent attempts roll-through, sprawl hips down + post if needed + head up → resettle.

- Branches
  - Can’t get full wrist grip (hand high/pushing) → cup the wrist/hand and pull it into your chest line.
  - Opponent tries immediate roll-through → hips down, head up, post and settle.

- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Failure: opponent pulls arm free.
    - Cause: you grabbed but didn’t pull wrist to your hip/chest line.
  - Failure: rotation feels awkward / throw stalls.
    - Cause: you attempted to throw before squaring opponent.
  - Failure: you get rolled through.
    - Cause: hips high/head low during landing.

- Drills
  - “Grab-and-pull” wrist control drill (include cup-grip variant).
  - Square-up then tai-otoshi reps (no throw until square).
  - Finish drill: tai-otoshi → anti-roll-through post/sprawl → pin settle.

- System tags
  - [UH:TaiOtoshi] [Control:WristPull] [Rule:SquareBeforeThrow] [Landing:Pin] [Risk:RollThroughMgmt]


### 7.4 Uchi Mata (17:14–22:59)
- Objective
  - Use underhook-based uchi mata as a primary forward throw with minimal entry distance, including realistic “ken-ken” and one-handed follow-ups.

- Gates
  - You can step inside and align feet to throw away from opponent’s base of support.
  - You can bring opponent’s head down (posture break) to prevent uchi mata becoming “a nightmare.”

- Controls
  - Underhook + head control: head-down effect is a central control lever.
  - Far-side control: when present, improves finish; when absent, accept a “hustling” finish model.

- Primary line
  - Underhook established → step inside (1) + second step to align direction (2).
  - Bring head down → begin hiking/lifting action to initiate hopping response.
  - Finish via decisive lift (no hop) OR ken-ken hop with opponent until they can’t maintain base → put them down and follow to control.

- Branches
  - Opponent begins hopping to stay upright → hop with them (ken-ken) until base collapses.
  - Lose far-side arm control (one-handed situation) → use posting hand to floor for balance while completing a “hustling” uchi mata finish.

- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Failure: uchi mata feels impossible/unstable.
    - Cause: opponent’s head stays up; you didn’t force head-down posture break.
  - Failure: you get countered when stepping behind legs with only one-sided control.
    - Cause: wrong footwork rule applied (stepping behind without controlling both sides).

- Drills
  - Head-down uchi mata entries (priority: posture break).
  - Ken-ken chase drill (hop with opponent).
  - One-handed balance drill: underhook uchi mata with brief hand post to floor (competition realism; apply safe training constraints).

- System tags
  - [UH:UchiMata] [Control:HeadDown] [Finish:KenKen] [Variant:OneHandHustle] [Rule:StepBetweenLegs]


### 7.5 Kouchi Trip (Small Inside Trip) (22:59–30:26)
- Objective
  - Provide a safe backward takedown option from a single underhook when opponent’s weight shifts onto heels.

- Gates
  - You have single underhook without reliable far-side control.
  - Opponent’s weight/read is backward (heels) or they are reacting defensively to forward-throw threat.

- Controls
  - Maintain underhook angle while stepping between legs (avoid stepping behind).
  - Use timing off opponent’s defensive reactions (often after uchi mata threat).

- Primary line
  - From underhook: step between legs → step across to trip inside line → put opponent straight down.

- Branches
  - Opponent defends forward throw by sinking weight onto heels → kouchi becomes primary.
  - Opponent steps out of kouchi → transition back to uchi mata “hustle” model rather than stepping behind for kosoto (single underhook risk).

- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Failure: you get countered stepping behind with single underhook.
    - Cause: violating the “one-side control → don’t step behind” rule.
  - Failure: trip doesn’t bite.
    - Cause: timing off; opponent weight not actually on heels / you didn’t step between to create safe line.

- Drills
  - Weight-read drill: forward threat → opponent heels → kouchi.
  - Combination reps: uchi mata look → kouchi finish → if step-out → hustling uchi mata continuation.

- System tags
  - [UH:Kouchi] [Direction:Backward] [Rule:NoStepBehindOnOneSideControl] [Chain:UM→Kouchi→HustleUM]


### 7.6 Placido Asks a Question (30:26–44:36)
- Objective
  - Build a realistic combination model: reading weight + chaining forward/backward attempts with multiple iterations and “hustling” finishes.

- Gates
  - You can feel opponent’s weight (toes vs heels) through push-pull.
  - You accept that higher-level opponents require multi-iteration chains (up to ~3–4 actions).

- Controls
  - Push-pull to elicit weight shift.
  - Maintain safe underhook mechanics when far-side arm is lost.
  - Use defensive hand post only in low-amplitude “hustle” contexts (competition realism, training caution).

- Primary line
  - Read weight:
    - Toes → forward throw attempt (e.g., uchi mata).
    - Heels → backward option (kouchi).
  - If opponent steps out of kouchi and you lack far arm → finish with one-handed “hustling” uchi mata using hand post for balance.

- Branches
  - Opponent posts hand to avoid being scored during low-amplitude uchi mata → continue to “walk them over the post” by persistent hip chase/hustle.
  - Underhook slippage risk during hustling uchi mata → switch underhook style to far-trap (over/around neck line) to maintain high elbow + head control and reduce limp-out/back exposure.

- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Failure: chain stops after first attempt.
    - Cause: you don’t iterate; you expect one clean throw vs skilled defense.
  - Failure: you lose underhook and give back exposure during hustle.
    - Cause: shallow underhook / no far-trap adaptation.
  - Failure: unsafe posting in training.
    - Cause: posting on high-amplitude throws; posting is only discussed as common in low-amplitude competition contexts.

- Drills
  - Push-pull reading rounds (feel toes vs heels).
  - 3-step chain reps: uchi mata → kouchi → one-handed hustling uchi mata.
  - Far-trap grip maintenance drill during hop/chase.

- System tags
  - [Rule:WeightRead] [Chain:ForwardBackwardIterations] [Finish:Hustle] [Grip:FarTrapAntiSlip]


### 7.7 Pinch Headlock — Uki Goshi (44:36–53:49)
- Objective
  - Convert failed waist/body lock situations into a high-control head-and-shoulder lock that enables a reliable uki goshi-style spiral-down, ending in strong top control (often half guard passing alignment).

- Gates
  - Opponent’s hips are back and breaking your waist/body lock.
  - You can climb the lock from waist to shoulders (head/shoulder capture).

- Controls
  - Pinch headlock controls both sides (one under, one over) with head/shoulder dominance.
  - Grip options:
    - Five-finger grip
    - Palm-to-palm
  - Preference: as hips move back, control shifts higher (waist lock weakens; shoulder/head lock strengthens).

- Primary line
  - From failed body lock (hips back): climb hands up → lock pinch headlock.
  - Step/turn inside the space in front of opponent’s hips.
  - Spiral them down (uki goshi mechanic) → land into strong top control (commonly half guard with underhook + crossface-like control).

- Branches
  - Opponent bases with hand during low-amplitude spiral → continue to churn/step until their base collapses; finish into control.

- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Failure: grip breaks as opponent hips sag back.
    - Cause: staying locked at waist when hips retreat (wrong control height).
  - Failure: you can’t turn/enter.
    - Cause: you didn’t step inside hip space; you’re trying to rotate outside their hips.

- Drills
  - Body lock → opponent hips back → pinch headlock conversion reps.
  - Pinch headlock spiral-down with controlled landing into half guard passing start.

- System tags
  - [PinchHL:UkiGoshi] [Conversion:WaistToShoulders] [Rule:LockHighWhenHipsBack] [Landing:HalfGuardPassStart]


### 7.8 Kosoto and Kouchi From Pinch Headlock (53:49–57:03)
- Objective
  - Add backward takedown options off pinch headlock when opponent’s weight shifts onto heels, using head control to reduce counter risk.

- Gates
  - Pinch headlock is locked and head control is secure.
  - You read weight backward (heels) or opponent is retreating from forward spiral.

- Controls
  - Head control makes stepping behind safer than the single-underhook case.
  - Maintain locked hands to prevent disengagement during trips.

- Primary line
  - From pinch headlock:
    - If heels: step and trip backward (kouchi) to put opponent down.
    - Alternative: kosoto becomes viable because head control reduces uchi mata counter line.

- Branches
  - Opponent retreats hard (heels) → prioritize backward trip line rather than forcing forward spiral.
  - Opponent posts hand in low-amplitude trip context → continue turning/churning to collapse base and settle into top control.

- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Failure: opponent escapes headlock as you step.
    - Cause: loose lock / cosmetic grip without pull-in pressure.
  - Failure: you choose backward stepping without head control (wrong platform).
    - Cause: confusing pinch headlock rules with single-underhook rules.

- Drills
  - Pinch headlock: forward spiral look → heels read → kouchi.
  - Pinch headlock: heels read → kosoto entry with controlled finish.

- System tags
  - [PinchHL:Trips] [PinchHL:Kosoto] [PinchHL:Kouchi] [Rule:HeadControlEnablesStepBehind]


### 7.9 Reverse Lat Grip — Osoto Gari (57:03–1:08:05)
- Objective
  - Counter opponents who get deep on leg attacks or duck behind by using reverse lat grip + elbow control to throw with osoto gari and land hip-to-hip into pin.

- Gates
  - Opponent’s head/ear comes behind your shoulder line (double leg coming up / duck under / back exposure scenarios).
  - You can turn side-on (avoid being square/front-on).

- Controls
  - Near-side: control opponent’s elbow/tricep.
  - Far-side: reach across and grip far lat/scap area (reverse lat grip).
  - Body orientation: side-on stance so you can move your leg outward and prevent being pulled straight back.

- Primary line
  - As opponent comes behind: secure tricep + reverse lat grip.
  - Turn side-on.
  - Hook outside leg for osoto line; hop to clear position if needed.
  - Reap while simultaneously pulling with lat grip (opposing forces) → put opponent down.
  - Knee-slide recovery to settle into pin (hip-to-hip + elbow control).

- Branches
  - Application variants shown:
    - Countering a double leg where opponent comes up behind.
    - Countering a duck under.
    - From bottom/behind situations: capture elbow, post leg, reach palm-up to lat as you stand with opponent → then hook/reap.
  - Safety branch:
    - When reaching back: palm-up straight arm to avoid accidental elbow strikes/cuts.

- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Failure: you get taken backward while square.
    - Cause: you didn’t turn side-on; you stayed front-on with opponent behind.
  - Failure: reap lacks effect.
    - Cause: you didn’t pull with the lat grip while reaping (no opposing force).
  - Failure: training partner injury/cuts.
    - Cause: reaching back palm-down with bent elbow (unsafe reach).

- Drills
  - Double leg comes up behind → reverse lat + tricep → side-on → osoto finish.
  - Duck under → reverse lat + tricep → osoto finish.
  - Stand-up drill from behind: palm-up lat reach safety reps.

- System tags
  - [RLat:Osoto] [Control:Lat+Tricep] [Rule:SideOnNotSquare] [Safety:PalmUpReach] [Landing:HipToHipPin]


### 7.10 Harai Goshi (1:08:05–1:12:26)
- Objective
  - Use harai goshi off the same reverse lat grip platform, especially when opponent drives forward (directional complement to osoto).

- Gates
  - Reverse lat grip + tricep control established.
  - Opponent pressure is driving into you (forward energy available).

- Controls
  - Same grips as 7.9.
  - Same side-on requirement.
  - Hook/sweep leg reaps outside line but the throw direction is opposite of osoto.

- Primary line
  - Opponent drives into double leg → secure reverse lat + tricep.
  - Turn side-on.
  - Make outside hook/sweep and hop in the direction that throws opponent forward (complementing their drive).
  - Finish with knee-slide recovery and pin.

- Branches
  - If one-legged hopping balance is difficult → a lower, knee-drop style adaptation is referenced (tai-otoshi-like/knee-down completion).

- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Failure: you clash force-on-force and stall.
    - Cause: attempting to throw backward while opponent drives forward (wrong direction choice).
  - Failure: you get flattened while square.
    - Cause: you didn’t turn side-on before throwing.

- Drills
  - Double leg drive-in → reverse lat platform → harai goshi finish.
  - Direction-choice drill: osoto for backward line vs harai for forward-drive line (based on opponent pressure).

- System tags
  - [RLat:HaraiGoshi] [Rule:UseOpponentDrive] [Platform:SameAsOsoto] [Landing:PinRecovery]


### 7.11 Special Topic: Georges St. Pierre Hybrid Throw (1:12:26+)
- Objective
  - Provide a high-reliability hybrid throw for athletes who struggle with extended one-legged balance/hopping throws, using underhook + wrist-hug control and a knee-drop finish to guarantee pinning outcomes.

- Gates
  - Underhook established.
  - Opponent’s far hand is available to be “hugged” to chest (stronger than hand-only grip in slippery conditions).

- Controls
  - Far-side hand control: “hug to chest” (not thumb-grip); increases retention.
  - Underhook for post-throw pinning dominance.
  - Finish height: knee-drop lowers your center to prevent roll-through and improve pin.

- Primary line
  - Underhook + wrist/hand hugged tight to chest.
  - Initiate a harai-type hook action to start turn.
  - Transition into a tai-otoshi-like line and immediately knee-drop to stabilize.
  - Drive off the “drive leg” to complete throw at an angle (not perfectly straight forward).
  - Land into immediate chest-to-chest pin.

- Branches
  - MMA gloves increase friction and make the hand-hug control even stronger (not required, but noted as an advantage).
  - If opponent attempts roll-through → knee-down finish + head up/hips down makes roll-through difficult.

- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Failure: opponent yanks hand free.
    - Cause: you held with fingers/thumb only instead of hugging to chest.
  - Failure: instability during completion.
    - Cause: lingering too long on one leg instead of transitioning quickly to knee-drop stability.
  - Failure: poor power generation.
    - Cause: neglecting the drive leg push-off during the knee-drop completion.

- Drills
  - Underhook + hand-hug retention rounds (partner tries to yank free).
  - Hybrid sequence reps: hook → transition → knee-drop → pin.
  - Drive-leg awareness drill: isolate the push-off that powers the finish.

- System tags
  - [Hybrid:GSP] [Control:HandHug] [Finish:KneeDropStability] [Underhook:PinBias] [Risk:RollThroughMgmt]
