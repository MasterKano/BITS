Viewing Notes – S2G – Volume 8: Upper Body Takedowns for Older/Less Athletic Athletes

8.1 Older/Less Athletic Concepts – Why Upper Body Takedowns Feel Hard (0:00–4:20)
- Goal
  - Select and prioritise throws that remain high-percentage when mobility, reaction speed, balance, and tolerance for impact are reduced.

- Key concepts
  - Two hardest demands for most people (especially older / less athletic):
    - Turning ~180° in front of a resisting opponent.
    - Balancing on one leg while carrying both bodies.  [oai_citation:0‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
  - Adaptation rule-set:
    - Prefer throws that keep you on two feet (wide base, easier balance).
    - Prefer grips/positions that let you turn *without fighting their arms/shoulders*.  [oai_citation:1‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Coaching cues
  - “Two feet beats one foot.”
  - “Don’t fight his arms—change the grip so you can turn.”  [oai_citation:2‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Common errors
  - Choosing throws that require extended one-legged hopping.
  - Trying to force full rotational turns from “clamped” clinches instead of changing grips.  [oai_citation:3‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)


8.2 Tai Otoshi from Arm Drag & Lat Grip (4:20–8:46)
- Goal
  - Use a low-demand entry (2-on-1 → arm drag → lat grip) to hit Tai Otoshi while staying on two feet and avoiding “arm-fighting,” then convert to front headlock/back takes.

- Key concepts
  - If they clamp your arm in a conventional clinch, turning feels impossible; arm drag + lat grip removes their ability to block your turn with their arms.  [oai_citation:4‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
  - “Modest goal” approach:
    - Don’t insist on big amplitude/back exposure.
    - Put them to hands-and-knees → immediate front headlock → drag down/back exposure (jiu-jitsu scoring).  [oai_citation:5‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Positional landmarks
  - Start: classic two-on-one.
  - Convert: two-on-one → arm drag grip → reach over and secure lat muscle.
  - Alignment: you are *outside* their arms; turning becomes mechanically easy.  [oai_citation:6‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Mechanics & details
  - Establish 2-on-1 → convert to arm drag → secure lat.
  - Pull and square them while you step across for Tai Otoshi (two feet maintained).
  - “Whip” them down and immediately connect to a quick front headlock.
  - From front headlock: drag down / circle behind to familiar scoring positions.  [oai_citation:7‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Coaching cues
  - “Outside his arms = you can always turn.”
  - “Two feet Tai Otoshi.”
  - “Down to knees → front headlock → jiu-jitsu time.”  [oai_citation:8‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Common errors
  - Staying in the arm-clamped clinch and trying to force rotation.
  - Trying to throw flat to the back without controlling their far arm (overcommitting).
  - Not connecting immediately to front headlock after the off-balance.  [oai_citation:9‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)


8.3 The Uki Goshi Hustle (8:46–11:41)
- Goal
  - Use “hustling” mechanics—repeated small steps and continuous forward pressure—to finish without explosive rotation or prolonged one-leg balance.

- Key concepts
  - “Hustle” = first action destabilises, then you keep stepping/churning until they fall (not a single clean, high-amplitude throw).
  - For older/less athletic athletes: emphasis is on persistence, posture, and incremental breaking rather than speed/explosion.

- Positional landmarks
  - Typically accessed after the same 2-on-1 → arm drag / lat-grip control used earlier in the curriculum.  [oai_citation:10‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Mechanics & details (high-level, as signposted in Volume 8)
  - Enter to Uki Goshi to break posture/balance.
  - Continue stepping (“hustle”) to keep their base compromised until you can complete to the mat.

- Coaching cues
  - “Don’t try to win in one step—keep hustling.”
  - “Small steps, continuous pressure.”

- Common errors
  - Expecting one clean rotation to finish.
  - Pausing after first off-balance instead of continuing the hustle.


8.4 Arm Drag & Hip Kouchi Trip (11:41–13:55)
- Goal
  - Convert arm drag/lat control into a low-risk trip/ankle finish that stays mostly two-footed and avoids major turning.

- Key concepts
  - Sequence: arm drag + lat control → initial Uki Goshi to off-balance → second step + “hustle” → foot behind their foot → reach to ankle → drive with chest to knock straight down.  [oai_citation:11‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
  - Design constraints:
    - Minimal one-leg balance requirement.
    - Minimal need to turn fully in front of opponent.  [oai_citation:12‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Positional landmarks
  - Start: classic 2-on-1 → arm drag grip → lat grip.
  - Entry: modest Uki Goshi to make them light/unstable.
  - Finish: your foot planted behind their ankle/foot line; your hand reaches to ankle; chest pressure drives them down.  [oai_citation:13‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Mechanics & details
  - Off-balance them with a simple Uki Goshi entry (not necessarily finishing the throw).
  - “Step in a second time and hustle.”
  - Plant foot behind their foot.
  - Reach to ankle and push forward with chest to knock them down.  [oai_citation:14‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Coaching cues
  - “Off-balance first, then hustle.”
  - “Foot behind, hand to ankle, chest drives.”
  - “Two feet most of the time.”  [oai_citation:15‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Common errors
  - Trying to finish the Uki Goshi cleanly when the safer ankle/knockdown is available.
  - Reaching to the ankle without first breaking posture/balance.
  - Failing to keep forward chest pressure on the finish.  [oai_citation:16‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)


8.5 The Lateral Drop – Uki Waza (13:55–20:11)
- Goal
  - Use a sacrifice-style lateral drop that requires minimal turning, flexibility, balance, or athletic explosion—taught via slow-motion progression for safety and confidence.

- Key concepts (why it’s selected for older athletes)
  - No requirement to turn shoulders in front of opponent (no big rotation).
  - No balance requirement; you sacrifice under their weight.
  - No spinal flexibility/back-arch demand in the way many big throws do.
  - “Start position is the throw position” → minimal transition. 
  - Danaher’s teaching progression for older/less athletic:
    - Start slow; partner supports your weight; build speed as confidence increases.  [oai_citation:17‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Positional landmarks
  - Entry detail: thumb inside; ear to back of opponent’s head; punch elbow deep into overhook structure.  [oai_citation:18‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
  - Partner provides strong base/hips back to support initial reps.  [oai_citation:19‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Mechanics & details (drilling progression)
  - Slow-motion reps:
    - Step in as far as possible.
    - “Sit back in the chair” while looking where you want them to land.
    - Wheel them over and finish beside them in a pin.  [oai_citation:20‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
  - Speed progression:
    - Lock → step → look → roll-through.
    - Increase penetration step depth over time; increase speed only after control is consistent.  [oai_citation:21‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Coaching cues
  - “Start slow; build speed like weight training.”  [oai_citation:22‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
  - “Big step inside.”
  - “Look where you want them to land.”  [oai_citation:23‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Common errors
  - Rushing to full-speed reps before confidence/control.
  - Shallow penetration step (insufficient entry depth).
  - Losing head/ear positioning and failing to keep elbow deep.  [oai_citation:24‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)


8.6 Uki Goshi (Half Hip Throw) – Older Athlete Application (20:11–23:45)
- Goal
  - Use the “half-hip coverage” version as a low-impact, two-footed, shoulder-friendly option that spirals opponent down rather than lifting and dumping.

- Key concepts (why it fits older athletes)
  - Only cover half of opponent’s hips → far less turn than major forward throws.
  - Two feet maintained → reduced balance demand.
  - Works from both underhooks and overhooks → adaptable to resistance.
  - “Low impact” spiral-down finish (control > amplitude).  [oai_citation:25‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Mechanics & details
  - Prefer elbow control rather than extended wrist control:
    - Capture elbow from inside position; draw elbow forward/across.
    - Turn toes; extend/stab leg inside their legs; drive forward to spiral down.  [oai_citation:26‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
  - Works from:
    - Underhook: control elbow; turn toes; leg inside; drive down.  [oai_citation:27‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
    - Overhook: hand inside wrist to access elbow; draw across; same entry.  [oai_citation:28‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
    - Over/under: recommended to get angle first (turn side-on), then same toe-turn + leg-stab action.  [oai_citation:29‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
  - Gripping note for older athletes:
    - If using underhook, grip higher (not low at waist) for easier head control and pinning finish.  [oai_citation:30‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Coaching cues
  - “Control the elbow; pull it tight to your body.”
  - “Turn toes; stab inside; spiral him down.”  [oai_citation:31‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
  - “Angle first (side-on) before the throw” (especially over/under).  [oai_citation:32‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Common errors
  - Holding the wrist at distance (hard to control if they pull away).
  - Trying to lift/dump rather than spiral-down control.
  - Gripping too low on the underhook (losing head control).  [oai_citation:33‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)


8.7 Kouchi Trip – Conservative “Best Adapted” Option (23:45+)
- Goal
  - Use a highly conservative, low-balance-demand takedown built around a stabbing step + foot block + heel/ankle pick—minimising counters and athletic requirements.

- Key concepts
  - Framed as “probably the most conservative upper body throw/takedown” for less athletic/older athletes.  [oai_citation:34‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
  - Structure:
    - Upset base with stabbing step (not Uchi Mata).
    - Place foot as blocker behind their foot.
    - Pick/collect heel/ankle and walk them down.
  - Benefits:
    - On two feet for most of the move → minimal balance requirement.
    - No need to bring hips across in front of resisting opponent; step *inside legs*, not across hips.  [oai_citation:35‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
    - “Relatively risk-free” and limits major counters; stepping between legs reduces classic counter lanes.  [oai_citation:36‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Positional landmarks
  - Can be done from underhook or overhook (and discussed as broadly applicable).  [oai_citation:37‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
  - Your lead foot points in same direction as their lead foot (alignment for the stabbing step).  [oai_citation:38‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Mechanics & details
  - Draw them in from underhook/overhook.
  - Stab step inside their leg line; straighten leg to load their weight onto target leg.
  - Walk across and place your foot behind their foot.
  - Pick the heel/ankle and put them down to the mat; take top position.  [oai_citation:39‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
  - Applicability note:
    - Works equally from underhook or overhook—same concept and finish.  [oai_citation:40‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Coaching cues
  - “Stab inside, load the weight, then block and pick.”
  - “Stay on two feet; keep it conservative.”  [oai_citation:41‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)

- Common errors
  - Trying to step across hips (unnecessary; increases difficulty).
  - Failing to load their weight onto the targeted leg before the heel pick.
  - Letting posture get tall/loose so they can regain base before the block/pick.  [oai_citation:42‡John Danaher Standing2Ground Upper Body Takedowns 8.txt](file-service://file-P9JvFgxrycTSFp2xhQG8qj)
