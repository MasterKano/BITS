# VIEWING NOTES — Standing to Ground 3: Upper Body Takedowns (John Danaher)
## VN08 — Volume 8: Upper Body Takedowns for Older / Less Athletic Athletes

### 8.1 Older / Less Athletic Concepts — Why Upper Body Takedowns Feel Hard (0:00–4:20)
- Goal
  - Select and prioritise throws that remain high-percentage when mobility, reaction speed, balance, and impact tolerance are reduced.

- Key concepts
  - Two hardest demands for most people (especially older / less athletic):
    - Turning ~180° in front of a resisting opponent.
    - Balancing on one leg while carrying both bodies.
  - Adaptation rules:
    - Prefer throws that keep you on two feet.
    - Prefer grips that allow turning without fighting the opponent’s arms.

- Coaching cues
  - “Two feet beats one foot.”
  - “Change the grip so you can turn.”

- Common errors
  - Choosing throws that require prolonged one-leg balance.
  - Forcing rotation from arm-clamped clinches instead of changing grips.


### 8.2 Tai Otoshi from Arm Drag & Lat Grip (4:20–8:46)
- Goal
  - Use low-demand entries to hit Tai Otoshi on two feet and transition immediately to front headlock or back exposure.

- Key concepts
  - Arm drag + lat grip removes the opponent’s ability to block your turn with their arms.
  - Modest goal: hands-and-knees → front headlock → jiu-jitsu scores.

- Positional landmarks
  - Start from two-on-one.
  - Convert to arm drag.
  - Secure lat grip so you are outside the opponent’s arms.

- Mechanics & details
  - Two-on-one → arm drag → lat grip.
  - Square opponent.
  - Step across for Tai Otoshi while staying on two feet.
  - Whip down and immediately lock a front headlock.

- Coaching cues
  - “Outside the arms = easy turn.”
  - “Two feet Tai Otoshi.”
  - “Down first, then dominate.”

- Common errors
  - Staying in a clamped clinch.
  - Overcommitting to a flat-back throw.
  - Failing to connect immediately to front headlock.


### 8.3 The Uki Goshi Hustle (8:46–11:41)
- Goal
  - Finish takedowns through repeated small steps and continuous pressure rather than explosive rotation.

- Key concepts
  - Hustle = incremental off-balancing, not one big throw.
  - Persistence replaces speed and athleticism.

- Positional landmarks
  - Typically accessed from arm drag and lat-grip control.

- Mechanics & details
  - Enter Uki Goshi to destabilise.
  - Continue stepping and churning until the opponent collapses to the mat.

- Coaching cues
  - “Don’t stop after the first off-balance.”
  - “Small steps, constant pressure.”

- Common errors
  - Expecting a single clean rotation.
  - Pausing after the first entry.


### 8.4 Arm Drag & Hip Kouchi Trip (11:41–13:55)
- Goal
  - Use a conservative trip/ankle finish that minimises turning and balance demands.

- Key concepts
  - Arm drag + lat grip → modest Uki Goshi to off-balance → second step → ankle knockdown.
  - Designed to keep both feet on the floor most of the time.

- Positional landmarks
  - Two-on-one → arm drag → lat grip.
  - Foot planted behind opponent’s foot.
  - Hand reaches ankle as chest pressure drives forward.

- Mechanics & details
  - Off-balance with Uki Goshi.
  - Step again and hustle.
  - Block foot, grab ankle, drive chest to knock down.

- Coaching cues
  - “Off-balance first.”
  - “Foot behind, hand to ankle.”
  - “Chest does the work.”

- Common errors
  - Reaching to the ankle without breaking posture.
  - Trying to finish the hip throw when the knockdown is safer.


### 8.5 Lateral Drop — Uki Waza (13:55–20:11)
- Goal
  - Apply a sacrifice throw requiring minimal rotation, balance, or flexibility.

- Key concepts
  - No need to turn shoulders in front of the opponent.
  - No one-leg balance requirement.
  - Start position equals throw position.

- Positional landmarks
  - Thumb inside grip.
  - Ear to back of opponent’s head.
  - Elbow punched deep into overhook structure.

- Mechanics & details
  - Drill progression:
    - Step deep.
    - Sit back.
    - Look where you want them to land.
    - Wheel and finish beside them.
  - Gradually increase speed and penetration depth.

- Coaching cues
  - “Start slow, build speed.”
  - “Big step inside.”
  - “Look where they land.”

- Common errors
  - Rushing to full speed.
  - Shallow penetration.
  - Losing head or elbow position.


### 8.6 Uki Goshi — Older Athlete Application (20:11–23:45)
- Goal
  - Use half-hip throws to spiral opponents down with low impact and strong control.

- Key concepts
  - Only half the hips are covered.
  - Two feet stay on the mat.
  - Control > amplitude.

- Mechanics & details
  - Prefer elbow control over wrist control.
  - Turn toes, stab leg inside, spiral down.
  - Works from underhook, overhook, and over/under (angle first).

- Coaching cues
  - “Control the elbow.”
  - “Turn toes, stab inside.”
  - “Spiral, don’t lift.”

- Common errors
  - Holding the wrist at distance.
  - Trying to dump instead of spiral.
  - Gripping too low on the underhook.


### 8.7 Kouchi Trip — Conservative Primary Option (23:45+)
- Goal
  - Apply a highly conservative takedown with minimal balance and rotational demands.

- Key concepts
  - Stab step to load weight.
  - Foot block behind opponent’s foot.
  - Heel/ankle pick to walk them down.

- Positional landmarks
  - Underhook or overhook.
  - Step inside leg line, not across hips.
  - Foot placed behind opponent’s foot.

- Mechanics & details
  - Draw opponent in.
  - Stab step inside and load weight.
  - Block foot, pick heel, walk down.

- Coaching cues
  - “Stab, load, block, pick.”
  - “Two feet, stay safe.”

- Common errors
  - Stepping across the hips.
  - Failing to load weight before the pick.
  - Standing tall and losing pressure.
