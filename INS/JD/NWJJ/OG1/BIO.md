# 2.0 Executive Summary

## 2.1 System definition
Open guard is presented as a **closed-loop control system**: deny the passer’s required build sequence, restore alignment at the hip line, break base to create an entry window, then convert to a leg-dominant hub that prevents reset. The system’s strategic purpose is to **change opponent behavior** (hesitation, reduced passing volume) by enforcing constant counter-offense.

## 2.2 Operating loop
**Read → Connect → Retain → Break Base → Enter → Stabilize → Finish → Recycle**

- **Read:** Dynamic Energy (push/pull, forward/back) + context laws (kneeling: shoulders/hips; standing: heels/toes; head: under/over).
- **Connect:** build **handles** and **redundant attachment** (hands + feet); never allow “he’s connected, I’m not.”
- **Retain:** Knowledge/Denial—identify which requirement is being built and break it.
- **Break Base:** force posting/base collapse; entries are low yield until posture is compromised.
- **Enter:** select entry family by range/posture (distance, kneeling, standing bridges).
- **Stabilize:** choose the hub that matches escape pressure (forward drive vs turn-away vs backside).
- **Finish:** trap exits (backstep/flee), defeat heavy/high leg, maintain backside push advantage.
- **Recycle:** if the exchange resets, re-enter the loop immediately (no passive reset).

## 2.3 Governing gates (the system’s control points)
1) **Hip Line Gate (retention escalation)**
   - **In front of hip line:** movement solutions dominate; re-square; feet lead.
   - **At/past hip line:** defensive cycle; frames become structural; rebuild legs/inside position.
   - Cycle ends only when you are **re-centered** with legs/frames between bodies.

2) **Hands-Down Gate (entry validity)**
   - “HANDS DOWN” is treated as proof the entry window exists.
   - If base is intact/upright: continue off-balancing; do not spin/rotate under stable posture.

3) **Orientation rule (retention integrity)**
   - Never allow a state where **neither feet nor knees** point at the opponent.
   - Displacement of one demands immediate replacement by the other.

## 2.4 Pass denial model (what you’re actually defending)
Passing is modeled as a build chain:
**Distance → Grip → Angle → Level → Penetration → Pin**

Your job is not to “defend the pass,” but to:
- diagnose the **current link** being assembled,
- deny that link decisively,
- then re-center and reinsert barriers before the next link forms.

## 2.5 Counter-offense doctrine (why retention alone is insufficient)
Defense-only retention degrades over repeated cycles; therefore the system mandates:
- **Defensive cycle** at hip-line danger,
- **Immediate counter-offense** on cycle break.

Fast selector (Golden Rule):
- **Head high → attack legs**
- **Head low → attack arms/head + go-behind**

This is paired with **constant threat theory**: legs are the one target the opponent cannot hide by posture, so leg access is used to force caution and slow passing entries.

## 2.6 Control hubs (where entries are meant to land)
Four hubs act as stabilization/finishing platforms:
- **Inside Senkaku:** forward barrier value; strong when opponent drives in to hand-fight.
- **50–50:** turn-away containment; controls disengagement attempts.
- **Double 50:** tighter multi-leg control; reduces mobility exits.
- **Criss-cross (inside-outside ashi):** backside-capable stability and breaking alignment.

Backside governance is explicit: if your legs cannot out-push their hands into the hip/back line, you get flattened; if they backstep, you follow (no reset).

## 2.7 Volume roles in one line each
- **Vol 1:** connection primitives (grips/handles/tempo).
- **Vol 2:** decision laws + six-element pass model.
- **Vol 3:** demarcation + movement engine for retention.
- **Vol 4:** passer archetypes + retention-to-counter loop.
- **Vol 5:** constant threat strategy + distance leg-entry tools.
- **Vol 6:** kneeling/standing entry system (cross-catch → hip pin).
- **Vol 7:** X-guard/RDLR/HQ bridges with repeated “hands-down” gate.
- **Vol 8:** DLR + closed-guard governance (anti-flee/anti-backstep, outside→inside).

## 2.8 Intended training outcome
If the gates are obeyed and hubs are selected correctly, the predictable results are:
- earlier pass denial (break the chain before penetration/pin),
- faster guard restoration (hip line + orientation discipline),
- higher conversion rate from defense to offense (head-height selector),
- reduced opponent passing aggression (constant threat enforcement).

# 4.0 Core Concepts

## 4.1 Guard as an Attacking Platform
- Retention is the enabling skill: if you do not trust your retention, you will not commit to offense.
- The objective is not “survive,” but **survive into initiative**: every defensive exchange must end with counter-offense.

## 4.2 Connection, Four-Limb Gripping, and Tempo
- Connection is defined by **handles** plus **redundancy** (hands + feet working together).
- Four-limb gripping principle: you should rarely rely on a single point of attachment; the opponent must have to clear multiple layers.
- Tempo is not speed; it is **who is causing reactions**. You are winning when the opponent is responding to your grips/frames/hooks rather than freely stepping and building angles.

## 4.3 Handles and Knot-on-Rope Targeting
- A handle is a segment you can reliably control under movement (ankle/heel line, wrist/forearm, waist line, head/neck line).
- “Knot on rope” concept: control the distal end (foot/ankle) so the leg cannot slide free while you climb into deeper control.
- Handle quality test: if they can step and your control breaks, you did not have a true handle.

## 4.4 Inside vs Outside Position vs Control
- Inside position is not automatically control; it becomes control only when it restricts exit routes.
- Outside relationships can be useful as entry bridges, but the system repeatedly migrates to **inside domination** to prevent backsteps and fleeing.
- Decision driver: choose inside/outside relationship based on whether the opponent’s primary escape is **turn-away** (containment needed) or **drive-in** (barrier needed).

## 4.5 Demarcation Lines
- Demarcation is used to decide whether you are in an offensive or defensive cycle.
- Key lines:
  - Center line: alignment and re-centering reference.
  - Toe line: early angle warnings.
  - Hip line: primary danger gate (past hip line triggers defensive cycle).
  - Shoulder line: pinning stabilization risk (late-stage danger).

## 4.6 Knowledge/Denial Retention Model
- Retention is approached as requirement denial:
  - identify the passer’s current objective,
  - deny a single required element,
  - re-center and rebuild barriers.
- The six pass elements are treated as a chain; breaking any one link prevents completion.

## 4.7 Dynamic Energy Theory and Three Laws
- Dynamic Energy: read opponent commitment directionally (push/pull; forward/back).
- Context laws provide the selection rule:
  - Kneeling: shoulders vs hips.
  - Standing: heels vs toes.
  - Head posture: head under vs head over.

## 4.8 Counter-Offense Doctrine: Hip Line Gate and Head-Height Rule
- Hip line gate decides whether defense is mandatory.
- On cycle break, counter selection is governed by head height:
  - head high → leg attacks,
  - head low → arms/head and go-behind counters.
- Objective: convert retention into threat quickly enough that the opponent cannot restart passing with momentum.

## 4.9 Anti-Exit Governance
- The system assumes the opponent will attempt to reset rather than “lose slowly.”
- Primary exits and defenses:
  - backstep,
  - flee the mat,
  - heavy leg (unliftable),
  - high leg (leg retraction),
  - backside flattening (hands pushing you flat).
- Governance principle: select hubs and attachments that make exits **slow or mechanically unavailable** long enough to finish.


# 5.0 System Overview

## 5.1 Operating Loop
**Read → Connect → Retain → Break Base → Enter → Stabilize → Finish → Recycle**
- Read selects the correct response family (energy + context laws).
- Connect builds redundant handles (hands + feet) that survive steps.
- Retain denies the specific pass element being built.
- Break base creates the entry window (hands-down proof).
- Enter chooses the correct family based on range/posture.
- Stabilize selects the hub that best suppresses the opponent’s exit.
- Finish is governed by anti-exit rules; recycle if the exchange resets.

## 5.2 Master Gates
- **Hip line gate:** determines defensive cycle vs offense-capable zone.
- **Hands-down gate:** determines whether entries/spin-unders are valid.
- **Orientation rule:** feet or knees must face the opponent at all times.
- **Cycle break condition:** guard is restored only when you are re-centered with barriers reinserted.

## 5.3 Pass-Denial Chain
Passing is modeled as a chain that must be completed in order:
**Distance → Grip → Angle → Level → Penetration → Pin**
- Your job is to identify the current link and break it early.
- The chain framing prevents “late defense,” where you only react once pinning begins.

## 5.4 Entry Families (selection by range and posture)
- **Distance entries:** 2-on-1 ankle concepts, partial inversions, shin-to-shin connectors.
- **Kneeling entries:** single/double/reverse double entries; cross-catch and hip pin logic.
- **Standing bridges:** X-guard (classic and momentum), RDLR (Parts 1–4), headquarters split-squat entries.
- **Outside-to-inside bridges:** DLR entries and closed-guard-to-X-guard routes.

## 5.5 Hub Selection (control platform choice)
- **Inside senkaku:** barrier value vs forward drive and hand-fighting pressure.
- **50–50:** containment vs turn-away and disengagement attempts.
- **Double 50:** higher-control variant that reduces opponent mobility options.
- **Criss-cross (inside-outside):** backside-capable stability and strong breaking alignment.

## 5.6 Backside Governance
- Backside is treated as a pushing contest:
  - if your legs cannot out-push their hands into the hip/back line, you get flattened.
- Structural rule: select configurations that generate leg-driven push into the opponent’s hip line.
- Tactical rule: if the opponent backsteps to disengage, you follow (no reset).

## 5.7 Conversion Rules (retention to counter-offense)
- Retention is incomplete until it creates initiative.
- On cycle break, counter selection is governed by head height:
  - **head high → legs**
  - **head low → arms/head and go-behind**
- Constant threat is the behavioral weapon: make every pass attempt costly.


# 6.0 Technique Library

## 6.1 Grips and Handles
- Two-on-one hand control (wrist/forearm) as a primary connector.
- Ankle/heel line control as the “knot on rope” handle.
- Waist/hip line control (especially vs kneeling/low base).
- Head/neck line control used selectively when risk is low (notably vs kneeling).

## 6.2 Retention Movement Vocabulary
- Foot pummeling and leg re-insertion
- Crossovers and re-squaring
- High-leg recovery
- Inverted spin and shoulder-supported movement
- Scissor sit and scissor roll
- Pendulum
- Shrimping and reverse shrimping
- Scooting (hip travel)
- Framing layer: frame high / frame low activation
- Orientation discipline: feet or knees always face the opponent

## 6.3 Mobility Pass Defense
- First contact with feet; deny “hands push legs aside for free.”
- Asymmetrical feet and continuous re-centering to deny angle.
- Escalate to frames only when hip line danger appears.

## 6.4 Pressure Pass Defense
- Deny lock formation early (distance + grip).
- Stack: wedge + shoulder-walk; do not bench press compression.
- Body lock: fight connection early; rebuild inside barriers quickly.
- Knee cut: deny inside lane capture; re-square and reinsert legs.

## 6.5 Standing Leg Attacks
- 2-on-1 ankle attacks and directional off-balances (toes read).
- Partial inversions as distance breakers (connection priority).
- Shin-to-shin as a repeatable connector into leg engagement.

## 6.6 Kneeling Entries
- Single leg entry vs two-knees: separate hips from heels to create catch window.
- Double leg entry: half sumi-gaeshi used as entry trigger; hand path between feet; inside-senkaku access.
- Reverse double leg entry: consolidate both legs from reverse direction; route into stabilizing hubs.
- Cross-catch system (standing one-leg-forward): force backstep; remove heavy/high leg defenses.
- Hip pin extension: follow hip sit-down; knee-on-hip pin; branch to backstep or kani basami; rotate inside to criss-cross when leg straightens.

## 6.7 X-Guard Entries
- X-guard triple attack: redundant tie-up; hands-down validity; convert to hub.
- X-guard momentum entry: throwback to create wedge; protect feet; re-achieve hands-down quickly.

## 6.8 Reverse De La Riva Entries
- Part 1: RDLR as base-break platform; hands-down gate before rotation.
- Part 2: hybrid entanglement for forward barrier + turn-away containment.
- Part 3: near/far switching; preserve wedge/angle before spin-under.
- Part 4: leg-closure response; fill ankle space to keep wedge alive.

## 6.9 Headquarters Entries
- HQ Part 1: off-balance to hands, then thread/turn into hub.
- HQ Part 2: backside governance; maintain leg-driven push advantage; follow backsteps.
- HQ Part 3: integrate consolidation and exit-trapping to prevent resets.

## 6.10 De La Riva Entries
- DLR Part 1: no-gi redundancy; attach before turning; protect feet from counters.
- DLR Part 2: convert to exit-trapping control that slows backstep and fleeing.

## 6.11 Closed Guard Entries
- Part 1: two-leg control requirement; avoid single-leg chase/back exposure.
- Part 2: closed guard to X-guard bridge; hands-down gate; migrate outside→inside; trap exits before finishing.

# 8.0 Application Scenarios

## 8.1 Mobility Passer Cycle
- **Trigger:** fast step-around, low commitment, angle-first.
- **Primary objective:** keep passer in front; deny angle accumulation.
- **Tools:** feet-first connection, asymmetry, re-square, early toe-line read.
- **Gate:** if hip line threatened → escalate to frames, then reinsert legs.
- **Counter on break:** head high → legs; head low → go-behind/upper-body.

## 8.2 Pressure Passer Cycle (Stack / Body Lock / Knee Cut)
- **Trigger:** closes distance, compresses, stabilizes before finishing.
- **Primary objective:** deny lock formation early; if late, survive without being flattened.
- **Tools:** wedges + shoulder safety, frame discipline, shoulder-walk, rebuild legs.
- **Gate:** if pin is forming, you are late—prioritize de-pinning and recomposition.
- **Counter on break:** immediate, not after reset.

## 8.3 Standing at Range: “Out of Touch” Problem
- **Trigger:** opponent stays just outside your feet/hands.
- **Primary objective:** create attachment without chasing.
- **Tools:** 2-on-1 ankle concepts, partial inversion distance breakers, shin-to-shin connector.
- **Failure signature:** reaching forward → backstep/reset.

## 8.4 Standing One-Leg-Forward (Staggered Stance)
- **Trigger:** staggered posture, forward leg offered, backstep threat.
- **Primary objective:** force reactions that remove heavy/high leg options.
- **Tools:** ashi connector → cross catch; anti-foot-clear structure.
- **Branches:** heavy leg / high leg / backstep → follow and consolidate.

## 8.5 Kneeling Opponent (Two Knees / One Knee)
- **Trigger:** opponent drops to knees to “shorten legs.”
- **Primary objective:** separate hips from heels; create catch window.
- **Tools:** collar/neck steering (low risk here), half-sumi trigger, between-the-feet pathing.
- **Hub selection:** inside senkaku first; upgrade to criss-cross/double 50 if backside pressure appears.

## 8.6 Hip Sit-Down Defense to Cross Catch
- **Trigger:** opponent sits to hip + posts to avoid being forced into backstep.
- **Primary objective:** prevent reset by converting sit-down into control.
- **Tools:** knee-on-hip pin → branch to backstep-over or kani basami.
- **Counter to straight-leg:** inside-hand rotation + knee-up discipline → criss-cross.

## 8.7 X-Guard Bridge Scenario (Safe Entry vs Backstep)
- **Trigger:** you can tie legs but can’t safely reach far leg.
- **Primary objective:** hands-down gate, then convert.
- **Tools:** redundant tie-up → X-guard → off-balance until hands post.
- **Counters:** heavy/high leg; leg-closure → fill ankle space.

## 8.8 Momentum X-Guard Entry (Too Far to Attach)
- **Trigger:** distance denies clean wedge insertion.
- **Primary objective:** create wedge without walking in.
- **Tools:** throwback entry; protect your feet; re-achieve hands-down quickly.
- **Failure signature:** “thin” entry → opponent steps away immediately.

## 8.9 RDLR System Scenario (Base-Break Before Rotation)
- **Trigger:** RDLR connection available; opponent posture still stable.
- **Primary objective:** force hands down, then rotate/spin.
- **Tools:** RDLR → hands-down gate → exposure.
- **Extensions:** hybrid barrier+containment when turn-in/turn-out dilemma appears.

## 8.10 HQ / Split Squat Passing Stance
- **Trigger:** opponent in headquarters/split squat, beginning knee-cut style entries.
- **Primary objective:** off-balance to hands, then thread/turn.
- **Tools:** destabilize → hands-down → thread/turn into hub.
- **Backside rule:** legs must out-push hands; follow backsteps.

## 8.11 Closed Guard to Leg Entries (Standing Opponent)
- **Trigger:** opponent stands inside closed guard; classic entries risk back exposure.
- **Primary objective:** two-leg control + outside-to-inside migration.
- **Tools:** closed guard → X-guard bridge → hands-down → shoelace/ankle line → consolidate inside.
- **Exit governance:** anti-backstep / anti-flee before finishing.

## 8.12 Troubleshooting Scenario Index (fast diagnosis)
- **Backstep happening early:** you reached or rotated without redundancy → rebuild tie-up; trap backstep at hub.
- **Heavy leg stall:** you lost wedge/close contact → reattach at ankle/hip line; force posture break.
- **High leg extraction:** you allowed separation → occupy space; reinsert wedge; deny clean lift.
- **Flattened backside:** structure lacks leg-driven push → switch to criss-cross/shoelace-adjusted stabilization.
- **No entries “stick”:** hands-down gate not achieved → prioritize base collapse over rotation.

# 9.0 Drilling Progressions

## 9.1 The three-layer structure
Progressions are organized as:
1) **Mechanics** (clean reps with known reactions)  
2) **Gates** (win the required condition: hip line, hands-down, orientation)  
3) **Resistance** (live but constrained, then free)

Each drill below specifies:
- start condition,
- objective,
- success test,
- common failure.

## 9.2 Retention engine: demarcation → re-square
**Drill 9.2A — Hip line gate recognition**
- Start: partner begins to circle; you maintain open guard.
- Objective: call “in front” vs “hip line” as soon as it changes.
- Success: correct call + immediate behavior shift (mobility → frames) within 1 beat.
- Failure: late recognition; framing after penetration begins.

**Drill 9.2B — Orientation discipline**
- Start: partner pushes legs aside to create an angle.
- Objective: ensure **feet or knees** remain pointed at partner continuously.
- Success: no “double loss” moment (neither feet nor knees facing).
- Failure: both feet and knees displaced; partner reaches hip line.

## 9.3 Six-element denial: isolate and break the chain
Run 6-minute rounds where only one element is “live” at a time.

**Drill 9.3A — Distance denial round**
- Start: partner tries to enter chest-to-chest range.
- Objective: keep distance with feet barriers and re-centering.
- Success: partner cannot connect hips/shoulders for 60 seconds.
- Failure: you accept closing distance and react late.

**Drill 9.3B — Angle denial round**
- Start: partner allowed to circle; no grips.
- Objective: re-square using hips + feet; keep opponent in front.
- Success: opponent cannot get outside your knee line.
- Failure: you chase with hands; angle snowballs.

(Repeat for Grip, Level, Penetration, Pin using the same structure.)

## 9.4 Base-break ladder: earn hands-down
**Drill 9.4A — Hands-down gate drill (X-guard / RDLR)**
- Start: you have a valid X-guard or RDLR attachment; opponent stands.
- Objective: force posting/hands-down before any spin/rotation.
- Success: opponent posts hands within 5–10 seconds.
- Failure: you spin under upright base; partner steps away.

**Drill 9.4B — Off-balance maintenance**
- Start: you have hands-down; opponent tries to re-stand.
- Objective: keep them compromised long enough to enter.
- Success: you retain wedge + angle while transitioning.
- Failure: you lose wedge; they regain posture.

## 9.5 Entry family blocks (build to hubs)
**Drill 9.5A — Kneeling entry engine (double leg entry)**
- Start: opponent on two knees.
- Objective: trigger post/widen base and thread between feet to inside angle.
- Success: you lock initial entanglement with correct knee orientation.
- Failure: outside pathing; delayed corner turn.

**Drill 9.5B — Cross-catch to hip pin**
- Start: standing one-leg-forward; opponent clears your foot.
- Objective: cross-catch to force backstep; if hip sit-down occurs, knee-on-hip pin.
- Success: opponent cannot reset; you enter criss-cross on straight-leg defense.
- Failure: you stay at distance after hip sit-down; they stand and clear.

## 9.6 Hub consolidation: make exits slow
**Drill 9.6A — Anti-backstep round**
- Start: you are in your chosen hub; opponent’s only goal is to backstep clear.
- Objective: follow and trap the backstep.
- Success: opponent cannot reset within 30 seconds.
- Failure: you accept reset; lose contact.

**Drill 9.6B — Anti-flee round**
- Start: you have ankle/heel control from an entry.
- Objective: prevent “run away and reset.”
- Success: opponent’s retreat is slow enough that heel exposure arrives.
- Failure: your attachment is thin; they flee immediately.

## 9.7 Backside governance: win the pushing contest
**Drill 9.7A — Backside stability**
- Start: backside position; opponent attempts to push your shoulder flat.
- Objective: maintain configuration where legs out-push hands.
- Success: you remain on your side; breaking alignment is preserved.
- Failure: flattened; angle lost.

## 9.8 Integration rounds (progressive resistance)
- **Phase 1 (constraints):** opponent may only use mobility passing.
- **Phase 2 (constraints):** opponent may only use pressure passing.
- **Phase 3 (constraints):** opponent’s only goal is to exit (backstep/flee).
- **Phase 4 (free):** full open sparring with one scoring rule:
  - you score only after a successful cycle break plus immediate counter initiation.

## 9.9 Session template (30–45 minutes)
- 5 min: hip line + orientation drills
- 10 min: six-element denial (one element focus)
- 10 min: hands-down gate ladder (X/RDLR)
- 10 min: entry-to-hub consolidation (anti-backstep / anti-flee)
- 5–10 min: integration rounds

# 10.0 Four-Week Curriculum

## 10.1 Structure
- **Frequency:** 3 sessions/week (12 sessions total).
- **Format per session (60 minutes):**
  - 10 min: retention engine (hip line + orientation)
  - 15 min: targeted module mechanics
  - 15 min: gate drilling (hands-down / anti-exit)
  - 15 min: constrained rounds
  - 5 min: notes (failure signatures observed)

## 10.2 Week 1 — Retention Engine + Pass-Denial Model
**Objective:** stop late defense by learning to deny the pass chain early.

- **Session 1:** demarcation + hip line gate; feet/knees orientation discipline
- **Session 2:** six-element chain focus: distance + angle denial rounds
- **Session 3:** six-element chain focus: grip + level denial rounds

**Success criteria (end of week)**
- you consistently identify hip line danger early and reinsert barriers
- opponent struggles to build clean angle without being re-centered

## 10.3 Week 2 — Base-Break Gate + X-Guard Bridge
**Objective:** earn hands-down consistently before entries.

- **Session 4:** X-guard alignment + hands-down ladder (no rotations until posting)
- **Session 5:** X-guard momentum entry (too-far scenarios) + foot protection
- **Session 6:** leg-closure defense: fill ankle space + maintain wedge

**Success criteria**
- you can force posting from X-guard within 5–10 seconds
- you do not spin under upright base during live constraints

## 10.4 Week 3 — Entry System: Kneeling + Cross Catch to Hip Pin
**Objective:** install high-percentage entries that survive common resets.

- **Session 7:** kneeling entries: double-leg trigger (post/widen) → inside control
- **Session 8:** cross catch vs one-leg-forward: force backstep; remove heavy/high leg
- **Session 9:** hip sit-down defense: knee-on-hip pin → branch backstep-over / kani basami; straight-leg counter → inside-hand rotation to criss-cross

**Success criteria**
- opponent cannot “kneel to safety”
- hip sit-down no longer resets the exchange

## 10.5 Week 4 — Consolidation: Hubs + Exit Traps + Counter-Offense
**Objective:** make entries stick and finish or recycle without reset.

- **Session 10:** hub consolidation: inside senkaku vs 50–50 vs double 50 vs criss-cross selection rules
- **Session 11:** anti-exit rounds: backstep/flee only; follow disengagement; backside pushing contest
- **Session 12:** integration rounds: mobility + pressure mix; scoring only after cycle break + immediate counter initiation

**Success criteria**
- you prevent backstep and fleeing resets from your hubs
- you consistently counter immediately after guard restoration

## 10.6 Weekly evaluation checklist
- Hip line recognition is early (not after penetration).
- Hands-down is earned before rotations/spin-unders.
- Entries are built on redundant attachment (no reaching).
- Hub selection matches opponent escape pressure.
- Anti-exit governance prevents easy resets.
- Every defensive cycle ends in immediate counter-offense.

# 11.0 Failure Patterns

## 11.1 Late Defense (hip line ignored)
- **Signature:** you frame only after they are already past the hip line or beginning to pin.
- **Root cause:** demarcation not read; you treat every moment as “offense-capable.”
- **Fix:** hip line gate discipline; reinsert barriers before countering.

## 11.2 Dead Feet (no meaningful connection)
- **Signature:** opponent pushes legs aside and circles freely.
- **Root cause:** feet touching, not hooking; no redundancy with hands.
- **Fix:** first contact with feet; rebuild asymmetry; re-square with hips.

## 11.3 Hand-Chasing (arms lead, hips lag)
- **Signature:** you reach for shoulders/upper body while angle accumulates.
- **Root cause:** using hands to solve a hip/angle problem.
- **Fix:** hips re-square first; hands only reinforce structure.

## 11.4 Spinning Under a Stable Base (hands-down gate violated)
- **Signature:** you rotate/spin-under and opponent simply steps away or smashes you.
- **Root cause:** you entered before base collapse.
- **Fix:** treat hands-down as proof; off-balance longer before rotating.

## 11.5 Reaching for the Far Leg (backstep invitation)
- **Signature:** far-leg reach triggers immediate backstep reset.
- **Root cause:** thin attachment; no redundant tie-up.
- **Fix:** hands + foot redundancy; make the backstep slow or mechanically trapped.

## 11.6 Thin Entries (attachment fails under one step)
- **Signature:** entry seems “there” but disappears as opponent takes a single step.
- **Root cause:** lack of a true handle (“knot on rope” not secured).
- **Fix:** control ankle/foot line before climbing; maintain wedge + angle.

## 11.7 Losing Orientation (neither feet nor knees facing)
- **Signature:** both feet and knees are displaced; passer reaches hip line.
- **Root cause:** failure to replace one barrier with the other.
- **Fix:** orientation rule as a non-negotiable; feet ↔ knees substitution.

## 11.8 Failing to Trap Exits (backstep/flee resets)
- **Signature:** you enter but opponent backsteps or runs away and resets instantly.
- **Root cause:** hub selected without exit governance; poor containment.
- **Fix:** anti-backstep / anti-flee drilling; select double 50/criss-cross when needed.

## 11.9 Backside Flattening (pushing contest lost)
- **Signature:** opponent pushes your shoulder flat and you lose breaking alignment.
- **Root cause:** structure does not allow leg-driven push into hip/back line.
- **Fix:** switch to criss-cross or shoelace-adjusted stabilization; keep on your side.

## 11.10 Retention Pride (defend then pause)
- **Signature:** you stop the pass but do not counter; opponent restarts with momentum.
- **Root cause:** treating retention as the finish line.
- **Fix:** cycle-break mandate—counter immediately using head-height selector.

## 11.11 Wrong Module (mobility treated as pressure, or vice versa)
- **Signature:** framing too early vs mobility, or “moving” without wedges vs pressure.
- **Root cause:** passer archetype not identified.
- **Fix:** identify archetype first; run the appropriate defense ladder/loop.

## 11.12 One-Leg Focus from Closed Guard (back exposure)
- **Signature:** you chase a single leg line and get rotated into back-trap risk.
- **Root cause:** violating two-leg governance.
- **Fix:** two-leg control requirement; outside-to-inside migration via X-guard bridge.


# 12.0 Key Passages

## 12.1 Retention is the enabling skill
- Retention is treated as the prerequisite to committed offense: without retention confidence, you will hesitate and lose initiative.
- The system frames retention as a cycle, not a single act: defend, restore alignment, then attack.

## 12.2 Dynamic Energy and context-based selection
- Correct action selection depends on reading opponent commitment (push/pull; forward/back) and applying the correct context law (kneeling, standing, head posture).
- The model prioritizes simple, repeatable reads over complex move recognition.

## 12.3 Passing as a requirements chain
- Guard passing is modeled as a chain of requirements: Distance, Grip, Angle, Level, Penetration, Pin.
- Denying any one requirement early prevents the pass from completing, and is emphasized as higher leverage than late-stage survival.

## 12.4 Hip line gate as the master defensive trigger
- The hip line is treated as the primary threshold between offense-capable guard work and mandatory defensive cycling.
- When hip line danger appears, the system mandates structural framing and re-alignment before countering.

## 12.5 Hands-down gate as entry validity
- Entries are framed as valid only when opponent base is compromised—commonly expressed as “hands down.”
- Rotating/spinning under upright posture is treated as a predictable error condition.

## 12.6 Constant threat theory
- Opponent behavior is governed by belief: if they believe passing is low-risk, they will pass aggressively; if they believe it is costly, they will hesitate.
- Legs are treated as the one target that cannot be hidden by posture, making leg threats the primary behavior-shaping tool.

## 12.7 Counter-offense mandate and the head-height selector
- Retention without counter-offense is incomplete; every cycle break should convert into immediate threat.
- Head height provides a fast selector:
  - head high → legs
  - head low → arms/head and go-behind

## 12.8 Hub selection and exit governance
- Inside-senkaku, 50–50, double 50, and criss-cross are presented as control platforms chosen based on the opponent’s escape pressure.
- Anti-exit governance (backstep/flee, heavy/high leg, backside flattening) is treated as mandatory for reliable finishes.

# 13.0 Terminology

## 13.1 Core system terms
- **Dynamic Energy Theory:** directional read of opponent commitment (push/pull; forward/back) used to select action families.
- **Law (context law):** a simple selection rule tied to posture context (kneeling, standing, head posture).
- **Handle:** a controllable segment that remains reliable under movement (wrist/forearm, ankle/heel line, waist line, head/neck line).
- **Redundancy:** multiple simultaneous attachments (hands + feet) so one step does not clear your control.
- **Knot on rope:** controlling the distal end (foot/ankle) so the leg cannot slide free while you climb to deeper control.
- **Demarcation:** reference lines that determine risk and required response (center, toe, hip, shoulder).
- **Hip line gate:** threshold that triggers defensive cycling; in front = mobility solutions, past = frames + re-alignment.
- **Hands-down gate:** proof of base collapse; entries are considered valid only after posting/collapse.
- **Orientation rule:** continuous requirement that feet or knees must point at the opponent.

## 13.2 Retention and passing model terms
- **Knowledge/Denial:** retention method that denies pass requirements rather than reacting to moves.
- **Six elements of passing:** Distance, Grip, Angle, Level, Penetration, Pin.
- **Defensive cycle:** phase where hip line is threatened and frames/re-alignment are mandatory.
- **Cycle break:** moment guard is restored (re-centered with barriers reinserted) and counter-offense must begin.

## 13.3 Counter-offense terms
- **Golden rule (head-height selector):**
  - head high → legs
  - head low → arms/head and go-behind

## 13.4 Positional and entry terms (as used in these notes)
- **X-guard:** leg configuration used primarily as a base-break bridge (hands-down emphasis).
- **Reverse X-guard:** X-guard variant used to elevate/align into leg entries.
- **RDLR (Reverse De La Riva):** outside-leg relationship used as a base-break platform before rotation/spin-under.
- **DLR (De La Riva):** outside-leg hook relationship; in no-gi requires redundancy and careful foot protection.
- **HQ / Headquarters:** split-squat passing stance context used for leg entry when base is compromised.
- **Inside senkaku:** inside leg entanglement with strong forward barrier value.
- **50–50:** leg entanglement emphasizing containment against turn-away exits.
- **Double 50:** higher-control 50–50 variant emphasizing multi-leg control.
- **Criss-cross (inside-outside ashi):** entanglement favored for backside stability and strong breaking alignment.
- **Cross catch:** cross-body catch/control that forces opponent backstep toward you to remove heavy/high leg defenses.
- **Hip pin:** knee-on-hip pin used to prevent reset when opponent sits to the hip.
- **Backside:** orientation where opponent can push your shoulders toward the mat; governed as a pushing contest.
- **Heavy leg:** opponent weights leg to make lifting/turning difficult.
- **High leg:** opponent yanks leg up to deny close contact and clear the entanglement.
- **Backstep:** opponent steps away/around to clear entanglement and reset.
- **Flee the mat:** opponent retreats rapidly to disengage and reset distance.

## 13.5 Movement vocabulary (retention)
- **Foot pummel:** reinsert feet as barriers/hook lines.
- **Crossover:** hip/leg switching motion to re-square under circling.
- **High-leg recovery:** lifting leg high to recover inside position when angle is lost.
- **Inverted spin:** rotational recovery using shoulder support.
- **Scissor sit / scissor roll:** scissoring recoveries for reorientation.
- **Pendulum:** swinging leg/hip action to recover alignment.
- **Shrimp / reverse shrimp:** hip escapes for space and barrier rebuilding.
- **Scoot:** seated hip travel to re-center and reinsert.
- **Frame high / frame low:** framing variations used as the escalation layer when hip line is threatened.
