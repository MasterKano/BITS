# VN7 — Open Guard Part 1 — Volume 7 (Titles-Aligned; Timestamps Added)

## 7.0 Volume 7

### 7.1 X-Guard Triple Attack (0:00 - 17:21)
**Purpose**
- Use X-guard as a safe bridge into legs that creates a three-way dilemma: (a) off-balance to hands, (b) near-leg exposure, (c) far-leg exposure.

**Core problem it solves**
- Reaching for the far leg from neutral distance invites the backstep and collapses your angle.

**Primary decision rules**
- No reaching. Tie up first (redundant leg control: hands + foot).
- Hands-to-mat is the validity gate.
- Off-balance first, route second (X-guard base-break → leg entry conversion).

**Core build (conceptual)**
1) Redundant leg control → rumi-style connection → classic X-guard alignment.
2) Destabilize until hands touch the floor.
3) Convert to your chosen leg hub (inside-senkaku / 50–50 family / hybrid).

**Common counters + rules**
- Heavy leg / High leg: treat as predictable; maintain close contact + wedge.
- Leg triangle: fill the space between ankles (space is the escape engine).

**Failure signatures**
- Leg-entering while upright (no hands-down) → clear/backstep.
- Reaching for far leg → immediate reset.
- Leaving ankle/knee-line gaps → their closure becomes functional.

---

### 7.2 X-Guard Momentum Entry (17:21 - 2:58)
**Purpose**
- Solve “too-far-to-touch” by using momentum to bring hips into the leg line without walking in or reaching.

**Core mechanism**
- Throw your body backward to throw their hips forward and establish the X-guard wedge.

**Primary decision rules**
- Connection > landing aesthetics.
- Protect your feet during the transition (retract/cover; avoid foot exposure).

**Validation tests**
- If you can immediately re-achieve hands-down, the momentum entry is functional.
- If they flee/backstep before attachment, entry was “thin” (no redundancy).

**Failure signatures**
- Throwback without attachment → opponent steps away.
- Foot left hanging → opponent seizes/clears.

---

### 7.3 Leg Entries From Reverse De La Riva (2:58 - 15:06)
**Purpose**
- Use RDLR family control to force hands-down, then rotate into knee/heel exposure.

**Core sequence logic**
1) Build RDLR control.
2) Force hands-down (validity gate).
3) Pull knee across hip line → exposure appears.
4) Convert to a breaking hub (behind-knee / strong control emphasis).

**Primary decision rules**
- RDLR is a base-break platform, not a static hook.
- If they’re upright, keep driving hands-down before you rotate/spin.

**Failure signatures**
- Spinning under a stable base → scramble.
- Static holding → opponent clears and re-passes.

---

### 7.4 Leg Entries From Reverse De La Riva Part 2 (15:06 - 43:10:00)
**Purpose**
- Install a “best-of-both-worlds” entanglement that covers:
  - forward hand-fight pressure (needs a barrier),
  - turn-away escapes (needs containment).

**Hybrid entanglement concept**
- Inside-senkaku barrier + 50/50 containment compromise:
  - one foot behaves like inside (forward barrier),
  - one foot behaves like outside (containment),
  - reduces turn-out while maintaining breaking alignment.

**Primary decision rules**
- Inside-only without containment can allow turn-outs.
- 50/50-only without barrier allows forward hand-fighting to kill progress.

**Failure signatures**
- Over-committing inside without heel control → turn-out.
- Over-committing containment without barrier → opponent drives in and strips.

---

### 7.5 Leg Entries From Reverse De La Riva Part 3 (43:10:00 - 48:45:00)
**Purpose**
- Enable target switching (near ↔ far leg) without turning the exchange into a scramble.

**Core mechanism**
- Small arm-position changes switch the target; then spin-under to connect.

**Primary decision rules**
- Preserve wedge + angle before spin-under.
- Hands-down still governs: don’t spin under upright posture.

**Failure signatures**
- Switching with no wedge → opponent steps away.
- Spinning under stable base → you rotate under pressure and lose control.

---

### 7.6 Leg Entries From Reverse De La Riva Part 4 (48:45:00 - 56:54:00)
**Purpose**
- Consolidate finishing mechanics when the opponent uses leg-closing and alignment defenses.

**Key constraint**
- When they close/triangle legs: fill the space between ankles.

**Primary decision rules**
- Gaps are exits; occupy space immediately.
- Convert while they’re still recovering base (hands-down moment).

**Failure signatures**
- Allowing closed-leg defense with space → entry window disappears.
- Chasing after posture returns → low-yield grind.

---

### 7.7 Leg Entries vs Split Squat (Headquarters) (56:54:00 - 1:10:34)
**Purpose**
- Show leg omnipresence in split-squat/HQ passing stances—entries work if base is compromised first.

**Core sequence logic**
1) Establish control preventing immediate clearing.
2) Off-balance to hands (validity gate).
3) Thread/turn corner into a high-control breaking structure (criss-cross/backside-capable routes).

**Primary decision rules**
- If you can’t force hands down, threading/turning is low yield.
- Off-balance is mandatory.

**Failure signatures**
- Threading without off-balance → opponent stays tall and clears.
- Attacking leg line while base is intact → you lose the race.

---

### 7.8 Leg Entries vs Split Squat (Headquarters) part 2 (1:10:34 - 1:15:54)
**Purpose**
- Backside governance: prevent flattening and preserve breaking alignment when opponent pushes back.

**Backside rule**
- If your legs can’t out-push their hands into the hip/back line, they flatten you over time.

**Primary decision rules**
- Prefer backside structures with leg-driven push advantage (criss-cross / shoelace-adjusted options).
- If they backstep to disengage: follow (no reset).

**Failure signatures**
- Losing the pushing contest → flattened.
- Allowing backstep reset → lost advantage.

---

### 7.9 Leg Entries vs Split Squat (Headquarters) part 3 (1:15:54 +)
**Purpose**
- Integrate HQ scenarios: trap exits, keep alignment, and stay inside the hands-down → convert loop.

**Primary decision rules**
- Off-balance → consolidate → trap exit → finish.
- If they create an exit lane (backstep/flee), you must already have containment + angle to follow without scrambling.

**Failure signatures**
- Winning an entry moment but failing to trap exits → instant reset.
- Pursuing without containment → chase and structural loss.
