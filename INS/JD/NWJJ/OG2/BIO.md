## 2.0 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Scope.** Open Guard 2 (8 volumes) specifies a no-gi open-guard program designed to convert bottom position into (a) submission finishes and (b) reliable reversals to top/back scoring positions, by chaining leg threats, two-phase reversals, kneeling-opponent sweeps, and heist/go-behind scoring. 

**Primary operating thesis (no-gi constraint).** No-gi grips on extremities are comparatively weak and transitory; therefore, high-percentage offense relies on:
1) creating off-balances/knockdowns with “good-enough” grips, then  
2) upgrading to robust, high-control grips (especially locked hands/waist) to complete the score.  
This is formalized as the **two-phase sweep/takedown model**. 

**System architecture (attack cycle).**
- **Start:** retention baseline (assumed from prior work) → **leg entanglement threat** as the default opening gambit.
- **If opponent uses distance / pullback to avoid legs:** transition to **wrestling reversals from guard** (picks, knee pulls, double legs, foot sweeps) and finish via **two-phase** (knockdown → waist). 
- **If opponent drops to knees to hide legs:** switch to **upper-body submissions** (triangle/armbar/guillotine) and/or **sumigaeshi** (butterfly hook sweep) as principal kneeling-opponent sweep. 
- **If head posture collapses / front-headlock emerges:** use **hip heists** and **go-behinds** to convert bottom guard to top/back scoring positions, including knee-block/hand-block/arm-drag go-behinds. 
- **Overlay:** “dilemmas” (notably push–pull and its posture/weight rules) are used to force predictable reactions that feed entries to the above branches.  [oai_citation:0‡NW Open Guard V2 Ch8.docx](file-service://file-4u5k95tUZumdpZEkbNCtwj)

**Key performance goals (what “success” means in this system).**
- Achieve **initiative** by making the opponent reactive to a constant leg threat.
- Convert reactions into **either** (a) a high-percentage submission channel (triangle/armbar/guillotine) **or** (b) a **scoring reversal** completed by reaching the **waist** (not merely “knocking over” the opponent).

## 4.0 CORE CONCEPTS

### 4.1 No-Gi Grip Reality (Design Constraint)
- Problem: extremity grips (wrists/head/ankles) are comparatively weak and transient in no-gi.
- Implication: build sequences that use transient grips to create instability, then upgrade to durable control (locked hands, waist control).

### 4.2 Two-Phase Sweeps / Reversals (Primary Scoring Model)
- Phase 1: use a jiu-jitsu-style off-balance/knockdown to put hands or hips on the mat.
- Phase 2: immediately transition to a wrestling finish—priority is capturing the waist (or strong locked-hand control), not “chasing the second leg.”

### 4.3 Distance Dilemma (Trigger for Reversal vs Leg Game)
- Opponents who fear legs often try to increase distance; this opens a lane for seated shots / wrestling reversals.
- System behavior: leg threat → opponent retreats → reversals become high percentage.

### 4.4 Inside Position as Safety + Mobility (Especially for Leg Work)
- Inside foot position is treated as a safer default in a leg-lock competent room.
- Standing opponent + inside position often transitions through ashigurami entries → X-guard as a hub to knock down and heist to the waist.

### 4.5 Posture/Disposition Rule: Kneeling vs Standing
- Kneeling opponent (one/both knees down): default preference shifts to sumi gaeshi as a principal sweep channel.
- Standing opponent: sumi gaeshi degrades; system reorients to ashi/x-guard knockdowns and wrestling-style reversals.

### 4.6 Sumi Gaeshi Mechanics (Kneeling Sweep Fundamentals)
Operational mechanical priorities include:
- Forehead to mat (head lower than hips)
- Elbow inside (post prevention)
- Two legs = elevator + drive leg (hips off mat; chest turns down)
- Correct directionality (roll opponent over head-line, not into their base)
- Finish the scramble: end hips over opponent’s torso to secure the score

### 4.7 Upper-Body Submission Triad (Kneeling Leg-Denial Response)
- When opponents drop to knees to hide legs, the system prioritizes: triangle, armbar (juji gatame), guillotine as the main upper-body attacks from guard.

### 4.8 Triangle “Elbow Rule” (High-Percentage Entry Condition)
- Central requirement: keep the trapped-side elbow forward and inside the thigh line; failures largely correlate to the elbow retracting.
- Preferred entry framework highlighted: 2-on-1 elbow control to enforce elbow position.

### 4.9 Guillotine: Football Snap → High Wrist (Core Front-Headlock Path)
- Football snap: cross-centerline snap that “catches” the head tight to the torso.
- Centerline shift enables high-wrist coverage for stronger strangulation and reduced hand-fighting access.

### 4.10 Hip Heist as a Scoring Engine (Height Advantage → Go-Behind)
- Hip heisting is framed as a neglected but central skill for converting guard exchanges into front headlock / go-behind outcomes (top/back).

### 4.11 Dilemmas (Meta-Framework for Forcing Predictable Reactions)
- Dilemmas are preferred over “problems” because they force two bad outcomes.
- Example: push–pull dilemma governed by posture/weight heuristics (shoulders vs hips for kneeling; heels vs toes for standing) to time snaps vs pushes/entries.

---

### 4.12 Transitory-to-Firm Grip Sequencing (Connection Upgrade Rule)
- Start with transitory grips (often upper-body hand fighting) only long enough to create an opening.
- Upgrade to firm connections—first to legs (arms/legs), then climb to the waist as the primary control group for finishing reversals.

### 4.13 Waist Is the Primary Finish Target (Not the “Second Leg”)
- A knockdown is not the objective; it is the gateway.
- Against strong opponents, the decisive step is getting to the waist to prevent limp-leg escape and to consolidate scoring.

### 4.14 Distance Dilemma (Leg Locks vs Reversals as a Two-Way Lever)
- Opponent increases distance to avoid leg entanglements → wrestling reversals become easier.
- Opponent pressures forward to suppress reversals → leg entries/ashi garami become easier.
- The system alternates these to keep the opponent in a forced-choice loop.

### 4.15 Seated Pick Mechanics (No-Head / Post-Hand Rule)
- Picks from seated guard emphasize:
  - post hand to the floor (brace/leverage),
  - outside sweep direction (keep head safer),
  - create either a hip-drop or a recovery that enables leg climbing to score.

### 4.16 Foot-Sweep Micro-Dilemmas (Ashi Waza as Knockdown Tools)
- Foot sweeps are often used less to “finish” and more to stumble/knockdown into two-phase finishes.
- Example dilemma framing: outside step triggers one sweep; inside step triggers another, funneling into reversals or back to legs if late.

### 4.17 X-Guard Entry Safety Rule (Irimi Ashi → X Guard vs Direct X Guard)
- Direct X-guard on a standing opponent is treated as riskier due to alignment/vulnerability.
- Preferred: enter with Irimi Ashi Garami (keeps you square), then transition to X-guard as the opponent clears.

### 4.18 X-Guard as “Space Between Knees” Control (Off-Balance Engine)
- X-guard’s key contribution is controlling the space between the knees, enabling reliable off-balances to hands/hips and transitions into waist hunting.

### 4.19 Sumi Gaeshi: “Big Five” Mechanical Priorities (Expanded)
- Core mechanical stack:
  1) forehead to mat (head below hips),
  2) elbow inside / under jaw (deny posting),
  3) elevator leg + drive leg (two-leg engine),
  4) hips rise / chest turns down (power),
  5) directionality along opponent’s spine line (roll over head, not over foot).

### 4.20 Sumi Gaeshi Posture Recovery: Forward Shift (Supine → Seated Conversion)
- Opponents try to flatten you to kill sumi gaeshi.
- Forward shift is the solo body mechanic to re-establish seated attack posture (knees-to-chest slingshot → extend along the mat).

### 4.21 “Knees to Chest” as a General Lightening Mechanism
- Bringing knees toward chest reduces perceived opponent weight and improves sweep efficiency (notably in shoulder crunch / related sumi gaeshi variants).

### 4.22 Upper-Body Submission Focus as Resource Allocation
- Upper-body attacks from guard are intentionally narrowed to triangle / armbar / guillotine to maximize progress and finishing percentage against tough opponents.

### 4.23 Guillotine Entry Principle: Football Snap → High Wrist via Centerline Shift
- Football snap: cross-centerline “catch” to trap head tight.
- Centerline shift enables high-wrist coverage and reduces opponent hand-fighting access.

### 4.24 Hip Heist as the “Third Option” (Sweep / Submit / Stand)
- Guard offense has three options: sweep, submit, or heist/stand.
- Constant heist threat forces the opponent to hold you down as well as pass, expanding attack availability and improving scoring outcomes (go-behind often preferable to ending inside guard).

### 4.25 Go-Behind Scoring Constraint (Knee-Down Control Window)
- The go-behind reversal scores equivalently, but requires holding at least one knee down long enough to consolidate the score.

---

### 4.26 Under/Over Dilemma (Kneeling Opponent)
- When an opponent drops to two knees (often to reduce leg-attack danger), you create a dilemma:
  - Go underneath their center of gravity (enter underneath → legs/ashi entries), or
  - Go over the top of their back (front head / go-behind style outcomes).
- Selection is conditioned by posture: if shoulders are driving forward in front of hips, “underneath” tends to open.

### 4.27 Contraction vs Extension Dilemma (Positional vs Submission Targeting)
- If opponent contracts (limbs tight / protective): positional attacks (off-balances, sweeps, go-behinds) tend to rise in value.
- If opponent extends (bases wide / posts hands): submission access rises (extended limbs become attackable).
- System rule: structure sequences as positional threat → submission threat (or vice versa) to exploit the forced oscillation.

### 4.28 Lower-Body vs Position Dilemma (Leg Attacks as Reversal/Back-Take Engines)
- Leg entanglements are not only for submission; they are used to force:
  - reversal to top (come up and consolidate), or
  - back exposure when opponent turns away.
- Rule-of-thumb: if you are between the opponent’s knees, the higher-percentage outcome is often reversal/top; if they turn away, prioritize coming up to the back.

### 4.29 Upper-Body to Lower-Body Transition Asymmetry
- A key asymmetry is emphasized:
  - You generally can go directly from upper-body attacks (triangles/armbars) to leg entries because posture defenses “leave the legs behind.”
  - You generally cannot go directly from leg entanglements to upper-body submissions without disengagement.
- This drives a reflex: upper-body bite → opponent postures → immediate leg entry.

### 4.30 Ear-to-Back + Bottom-Side Tight Waist (Scramble Control Anchor)
- In two-phase finishes, the primary consolidation is:
  - head connection (“ear to back”) plus bottom-side tight waist, to win the race as the opponent tries to flee.
- This is treated as a repeatable “anchor” that makes subsequent breakdowns and scoring predictable.

### 4.31 X-Guard as a “Between-the-Knees” Control Platform
- X-guard is framed as a “between-the-knees” control platform enabling off-balances to hands/hips and transitions to waist hunting.

### 4.32 Shoelace X-Guard to Reduce Athleticism (Knee-Down Forcing)
- Use shoelace-on-shoelace control to remove foot contact and force a knee to the mat, lowering opponent height and mobility, improving your ability to heist and reach the waist.

### 4.33 Sumi Gaeshi Opportunity Rule + Primary Mechanical Cue
- Opportunity gating:
  - opponent standing → shift away from sumi gaeshi toward ashi/x-guard,
  - opponent shows one/both knees down → sumi gaeshi becomes a principal sweep channel.
- Primary mechanical cue: bring the opponent’s forehead toward the mat (head lower than hips) to make the sweep mechanically easy.

### 4.34 Forward Shift (Supine → Seated Restoration for Sumi Gaeshi)
- When opponents flatten you to supine to kill sumi gaeshi, forward shift is the solo body mechanic to re-attain seated posture (knees-to-chest slingshot → extend along the mat, not “lift”).

### 4.35 Transitory Grips → Firm Connections (Explicit Pattern Statement)
- The declared gripping pattern is:
  1) transitory upper-body grips (short-lived),
  2) to firm leg connections (arms/legs),
  3) to the waist as the primary finishing control group.

### 4.36 Shoulder-to-Mat as a Finishing Constraint (Anti-Wrestling Principle)
- When consolidating from a rising scramble, forcing the opponent’s shoulder to the mat sharply reduces their wrestling effectiveness and improves your conversion to control/score.

---

### 4.37 Control-Group Substitution (Gi → No-Gi)
- Gi’s collar/cuff/belt control groups are replaced (in practice) by legs / hips / waist as primary no-gi control groups.
- This substitution is treated as a prerequisite for making classical sweeps work reliably in no-gi.

### 4.38 “Pull Them In” vs “Push Yourself To Them” (No-Gi Movement Constraint)
- Because sustained pulling grips are weaker in no-gi, the system emphasizes bringing yourself to the opponent (coming up) more than trying to drag them into you.
- This is presented as a foundational movement adjustment when moving from gi to no-gi guard.

### 4.39 Seated Shots vs Standing Shots (Head-Use Constraint)
- Guard-based “shots” are not treated as classic wrestling shots: head commitment is minimized due to submission threats.
- The system emphasizes hand/angle mechanics and posting/brace behaviors rather than head-drive finishes.

### 4.40 Inside-Grip Preference Without Head Control
- When you cannot safely control the head from guard, inside grips on the leg (ankle/heel/knee tendon) are preferred because turn-out runs into the grip rather than away.
- Outside picks become higher-risk without head control.

### 4.41 Directionality Rule for Picks (Toes-Pointing Heuristic)
- When finishing an ankle pick-style reversal from guard, pull in the same direction the toes are pointing to reduce mat friction and increase finish reliability.

### 4.42 “Short Arms” Rule (Anti-Extension Safety)
- Extended arms during leg picks/reversals create vulnerability; the system repeatedly emphasizes short arms on contact and during completion.

### 4.43 Knee-Tendon Precision (Handle Selection)
- For knee pulls, the “handle” is the inside knee tendon (precision target), not generic hamstring grabs.
- This precision is framed as a major reason the control becomes sticky as the opponent turns/withdraws.

### 4.44 Double-Leg Grip Hierarchy (Knees vs Locked Double at Hip-Line)
- Two options are distinguished:
  - wrist-deep behind both knees (knee tendons), and
  - locked double gripping at the buttocks–hamstring line, with elbows controlling.
- Locked double is described as superior but harder to obtain.

### 4.45 X-Guard Entry Risk Management (Square First, Then X)
- Direct X-guard against a standing opponent is presented as riskier due to alignment and potential failure modes.
- Preferred sequencing: Irimi Ashi Garami entry (stay square) → transition to X-guard as the opponent clears/defends.

### 4.46 Knockdown Isn’t the Point (X-Guard Completion Rule)
- X-guard reversals are framed as easy to start (knockdown), but hard to finish at elite level unless you can get to the waist.
- Completion strategies are explicitly framed as climb or shoot to the waist.

### 4.47 Triangle: Trap Triangle → Figure-Four (Two-Stage Locking)
- Triangle finishing is framed as typically two-stage:
  1) trap triangle (ankles crossed) to secure initial control, then
  2) adjust to figure-four (knee/ankle close; shin across neck) for finishing pressure.
- A repeated mechanical constraint is knees pinched to prevent elbow extraction.

### 4.48 Triangle: “Elbow Control Is the Real Key”
- The decisive condition for triangle entries is controlling the trapped-side elbow so it stays forward and inside the thigh line during the lock phase.
- The “2-on-1 elbow” is presented as a preferred solution because it directly controls the elbow line.

### 4.49 Guillotine: High-Wrist as the Default Strangulation Preference
- High-wrist guillotine is framed as more efficient (better carotid coverage) and structurally harder to hand-fight.
- The enabling mechanic is the centerline shift to access high-wrist depth.

### 4.50 Guillotine: Overhooking the Arm to Limit Defense/Rollout
- After achieving high-wrist, the secondary arm is often routed over/around the opponent’s arm to reduce defensive frames and complicate rollout-style escapes.

### 4.51 Dilemmas as a Forcing Function (Not Just Philosophy)
- Dilemmas are used operationally to force predictable reactions (push–pull timing and weight-distribution reads) that trigger entries to reversals/legs/upper-body attacks.

### 4.52 CORE CONCEPTS — DECISION FRAMEWORK (IF/THEN GATES)

#### 4.52.1 Posture Gate (Opponent base determines primary lane)
- IF opponent is standing (wide base + high mobility),
  THEN prioritize leg entanglement entries → X-guard hub → knockdown → waist (two-phase completion).
- IF opponent is on two knees / at least one knee down,
  THEN prioritize sumi gaeshi as the principal sweep channel and/or upper-body submissions (triangle/armbar/guillotine).

#### 4.52.2 Distance Gate (Distance dilemma routing)
- IF opponent maintains distance / pullback to avoid leg entanglement,
  THEN transition to wrestling reversals from guard (picks, knee pulls, double legs) and finish via two-phase (knockdown → waist).
- IF opponent closes distance (often to suppress reversals),
  THEN leg entries become easier; reassert primary leg threat and force the next reaction.

#### 4.52.3 Grip/Connection Gate (transitory → firm control upgrade)
- IF you only have transitory extremity control (wrist/head/ankle) and the exchange is stable,
  THEN do not “hold and hope”; use it to create a knockdown or entry to a locked/waist connection.
- IF you achieve hands/hips touch mat (knockdown),
  THEN immediately upgrade to waist control (or locked hands) to complete the score.

#### 4.52.4 Knockdown Type Gate (Phase 1 completion criteria)
- IF you can put hands on the mat (forward knockdown),
  THEN heist/climb/shoot to waist while opponent is posted and de-athleticized.
- IF you can put hips/butt on the mat (backward knockdown),
  THEN win the race to waist before they spring up (do not default to chasing the second leg at elite level).

#### 4.52.5 Finish Strategy Gate (CLIMB vs SHOOT to the waist)
- IF opponent is limp-legging / dancing away as you rise,
  THEN choose one:
  - CLIMB (treat body like a rope) to reach the waist, OR
  - SHOOT with stance correction to win the race to hips.
  Priority remains waist, not the second leg.

#### 4.52.6 Leg-Pick Mechanics Gate (safe no-gi shot constraints)
- IF you are picking from guard without safe head control,
  THEN prefer inside grips (turn-out runs into your grip).
- IF ankle-picking from guard,
  THEN pull in the direction the toes point (reduce mat friction).
- IF knee-pulling,
  THEN grip inside knee tendon precisely (sticky handle).
- IF opponent squares up,
  THEN double legs become higher value (knee-tendon grips or locked double at hip-line).

#### 4.52.7 X-Guard Entry Gate (risk management)
- IF opponent is standing and directly in front,
  THEN avoid “hope-based” direct X-guard; prefer Irimi Ashi Garami entry → transition to X-guard as they clear.
- IF you secure X-guard,
  THEN your objective is controlling space between knees to produce knockdown and/or access the waist via climb/shoot.

#### 4.52.8 Kneeling-Defense Gate (legs hidden → upper-body channel)
- IF opponent drops to knees to deny legs,
  THEN prioritize the upper-body triad: triangle / armbar / guillotine.
  - Triangle gating rule: IF you cannot control the elbow forward inside the thigh line,
    THEN expect failure; prefer 2-on-1 elbow control entries.
  - Guillotine gating rule: IF you can snap head tight,
    THEN use football snap → centerline shift → high-wrist as the default structure.

#### 4.52.9 Hip-Heist Gate (stand-up scoring option)
- IF opponent’s head posture is compromised / front-headlock opportunity appears,
  THEN hip heist to gain height advantage and convert to go-behind scoring options.

#### 4.52.10 Dilemma Overlay Gate (timing engine)
- IF opponent is kneeling,
  THEN time pulls/snaps vs pushes using shoulder relationship (shoulders vs hips/knees) to force reactions that open entries.
- IF opponent is standing,
  THEN read weight distribution by feel (heels vs toes):
  - heels-heavy → knock backwards / come up,
  - toes-heavy → draw forward (often into legs).
  This is the operational layer that triggers the above branches.

### 4.53 FULL DECISION TREE (Expanded)

This decision tree is written as an operational “routing table” for live guard exchanges.

---

#### 4.53.1 Start-State Gate (Baseline)
- IF guard is at risk of being passed,
  THEN prioritize retention → re-establish a playable open guard connection.
- IF guard is stable enough to attack,
  THEN proceed to DILEMMA SELECTION (4.53.2).

---

#### 4.53.2 DILEMMA SELECTION (Five Dilemmas as Top-Level Switches)

##### D1 — Push/Pull Dilemma (Timing + Entry Trigger)
- IF opponent energy is forward (kneeling: shoulders forward; standing: weight on toes),
  THEN pull/snaps/arm-drags/front-head entries become higher value.
- IF opponent energy is backward/upright (kneeling: shoulders over hips; standing: weight on heels),
  THEN pushes/come-ups/takedown-style reversals become higher value.

##### D2 — Under/Over Dilemma (Route Under the Center vs Over the Back)
- IF opponent posture is compressible and you can get under hips/knees,
  THEN choose UNDER route (leg entanglements / X-guard / heist reversals).
- IF opponent head posture is available (chin/head can be snapped),
  THEN choose OVER route (front headlock → go-behind / guillotine).

##### D3 — Contraction/Extension Dilemma (Submission vs Positional)
- IF opponent is extended (posts hands, limbs reaching, elbow line exposed),
  THEN submissions increase (triangle/armbar/guillotine).
- IF opponent is contracted (elbows tight, base tight, “hard to submit”),
  THEN positional off-balances increase (sweeps/reversals/go-behinds).

##### D4 — Lower-Body vs Position Dilemma (Leg Attack vs Score)
- IF leg entanglement yields a clean bite,
  THEN threaten leg finishing.
- IF leg entanglement forces turn-away / base break,
  THEN prioritize coming up to score (waist/back) over “just staying in legs.”

##### D5 — Upper-Body vs Lower-Body Dilemma (Attack Both Ends)
- IF opponent hides legs by kneeling,
  THEN upper-body triad (triangle/armbar/guillotine).
- IF opponent postures hard to escape upper-body threats,
  THEN legs become newly available → re-enter leg entanglements.

After selecting the dilemma that best fits the moment, route via POSTURE (4.53.3).

---

#### 4.53.3 POSTURE GATE (Opponent Base)
- IF opponent is STANDING,
  THEN go to STANDING ROUTES (4.53.4).
- IF opponent is KNEELING (one/both knees down),
  THEN go to KNEELING ROUTES (4.53.5).
- IF opponent is in TRANSITION (one knee hovering / switching),
  THEN treat as “best available”:
  - if head posture is reachable → OVER route,
  - else → UNDER route.

---

#### 4.53.4 STANDING ROUTES (Primary Hub = Legs → X-Guard → Two-Phase Finish)

##### 4.53.4A Primary Standing Plan: Leg Threat → Entanglement Hub
- Goal: create a knockdown (hands/hips) and immediately reach WAIST.
- IF you can enter safely while square,
  THEN enter via Irimi Ashi (safer entry posture) → transition to X-guard.
- IF you already have a usable under-leg connection,
  THEN go directly to X-guard structure.

##### 4.53.4B X-Guard / Entanglement Knockdown Gate
- IF you can control “space between the knees,”
  THEN force:
  - hands-to-mat knockdown (forward), OR
  - hips-to-mat knockdown (backward).
- IF opponent is too athletic / escapes by bouncing away,
  THEN use “de-athleticizing” behaviors (force knee-down where possible) and re-attempt.

##### 4.53.4C Two-Phase Completion Gate (THE finish is the WAIST)
- Phase 1 achieved: hands or hips touch mat.
- Phase 2 requirement: WAIST capture.
  - IF opponent is escaping by limp-leg / stepping away,
    THEN choose:
    - CLIMB (treat body like a rope) → waist → breakdown → score, OR
    - SHOOT (stance/angle correction) → waist → breakdown → score.
- IF you cannot reach waist in time,
  THEN do NOT chase “second leg” in open space; instead:
  - re-enter legs (reset to entanglement), OR
  - switch to go-behind if head posture appears during scramble.

##### 4.53.4D Standing Wrestling-Reversal Insert (Distance Dilemma Response)
- IF opponent is staying far to deny legs (distance/pullback),
  THEN seated shots become viable:
  - ankle pick family,
  - knee pull family,
  - double leg if opponent squares stance.
- Completion rule remains identical: knockdown → WAIST.

##### 4.53.4E Standing Failure Modes → Resets
- Failure: knockdown created but no waist capture → opponent limps/escapes.
  - Reset: re-enter legs (entanglement hub) OR switch to front head/go-behind if head posture breaks.
- Failure: head/arms overcommitted during shot (submission risk).
  - Reset: return to safer inside grips / post-hand mechanics / re-approach via entanglement.

---

#### 4.53.5 KNEELING ROUTES (Primary Hubs = Sumi Gaeshi + Upper-Body Triad + Heist/Go-Behind)

##### 4.53.5A Primary Kneeling Plan: Sumi Gaeshi Hub
- IF opponent has one/both knees down and their posture can be tilted,
  THEN Sumi Gaeshi is the principal sweep channel.
- Mechanical completion rules (must be present for high percentage):
  - head/forehead driven toward mat,
  - elbow inside to deny posting,
  - two-leg engine (elevator + drive),
  - correct directionality (roll over head line),
  - win scramble into control/top.

##### 4.53.5B Sumi Gaeshi Variant Gate (Grip/Connection Determines Variant)
- IF you have collar-tie style head control + elbow line,
  THEN collar-and-elbow style Sumi Gaeshi.
- IF you have double triceps access,
  THEN double-triceps Sumi Gaeshi.
- IF you have double overhooks / over-under structures,
  THEN overhook-based Sumi Gaeshi variants.
- IF you have arm-trap / shoulder-crunch style compression,
  THEN shoulder-crunch / arm-trap Sumi Gaeshi variants.
- IF you can connect chest-to-chest body-lock from butterfly structure,
  THEN body-lock butterfly sweep option.

##### 4.53.5C Sumi Gaeshi Scramble Finish Gate (Two-Phase Logic Reappears)
- IF Sumi Gaeshi produces a knockdown but opponent scrambles up,
  THEN immediately hunt WAIST / go-behind rather than settling for loose top.
- IF opponent flattens you supine to kill Sumi Gaeshi,
  THEN forward shift to restore seated attack posture → re-enter Sumi Gaeshi or switch to legs.

##### 4.53.5D Upper-Body Triad Gate (Legs Hidden → Submit)
- IF opponent kneels to hide legs or gives you elbow exposure,
  THEN upper-body attacks take priority:
  - Triangle system
  - Armbar system (juji gatame)
  - Guillotine system

###### Triangle Decision Sub-Tree
- IF you can control the elbow line forward (best: 2-on-1 elbow control),
  THEN enter trap triangle first → adjust to figure-four triangle → finish.
- IF elbow retracts / cannot be held inside thigh line,
  THEN abandon prolonged triangle attempts → switch to armbar or return to legs.

###### Armbar Decision Sub-Tree
- IF triangle threat forces posture/arm extension,
  THEN route to juji gatame (including inverted entries when clamp structures exist).
- IF opponent stacks or retracts arm early,
  THEN return to triangle threat or switch to heist/go-behind when head dips.

###### Guillotine Decision Sub-Tree
- IF head posture is snappable (head-to-head becomes collapsible),
  THEN football snap entry → centerline shift → high-wrist configuration.
- IF opponent’s defensive arm is available to trap,
  THEN overhook/arm-control finish structure to reduce defenses.
- IF opponent attempts rollout/turn-out defenses,
  THEN maintain high-wrist structure and route to follow-up front-headlock control (do not lose head).

##### 4.53.5E Hip-Heist / Go-Behind Gate (Score Conversion Engine)
- IF opponent’s head posture collapses OR they post hands forward,
  THEN hip heist to gain height advantage → go-behind routes:
  - Knee-block go-behind
  - Hand-block go-behind
  - Arm-drag go-behind
  - Misdirectional go-behind
  - Front body-lock go-behind
- Completion rule:
  - IF you can keep a knee down long enough to circle,
    THEN secure behind position → consolidate (top/back).
  - IF opponent recovers posture before you can circle,
    THEN reset to Sumi Gaeshi or re-enter legs.

---

#### 4.53.6 TECHNIQUE-CLUSTER GATES (Tripod / Shin Sweeps / Irimi-Ashi Sweeps)

##### 4.53.6A Tripod / Reverse Tripod / Outside Tripod Cluster
- IF opponent posture/stance permits hand/hip knockdown via tripod mechanics,
  THEN treat tripod as Phase 1 knockdown tool.
- Phase 2 requirement remains WAIST capture:
  - climb/shoot as opponent recovers.
- Failure signature: “tripod knocks but doesn’t score” → fix by immediate waist chase.

##### 4.53.6B Double-Shin Sweep Cluster
- IF both shins can control and separate the opponent’s base,
  THEN use as Phase 1 knockdown to hands/hips → immediate waist hunt.

##### 4.53.6C Irimi Ashi Garami Sweep Cluster
- IF you can enter square and connect to legs safely,
  THEN Irimi Ashi acts as an entry/sweep bridge:
  - toward knockdown,
  - toward X-guard hub,
  - toward leg threat as primary.

---

#### 4.53.7 RESET LOGIC (When to Cycle Back)
- IF your attack fails without positional loss,
  THEN re-establish primary leg threat (default opening gambit).
- IF opponent changes posture to avoid your current lane,
  THEN immediately switch lanes:
  - standing → X-guard/legs,
  - kneeling → Sumi Gaeshi / upper-body triad,
  - head dips → heist/go-behind/guillotine.
- IF you lose initiative and opponent begins pass consolidation,
  THEN retention first, then restart at 4.53.1.

---

#### 4.53.8 FAILURE PATTERNS (System-Level)
- FP1: “Knockdown without waist” → opponent escapes → repeated non-scoring sweeps.
  - Fix: mandatory Phase 2 waist capture (climb/shoot).
- FP2: “Chasing second leg against elite opponents” → day-late finish.
  - Fix: reframe finish target as WAIST.
- FP3: “Overholding transitory grips” → grip breaks → lost tempo.
  - Fix: transitory grips are only for creating instability; upgrade immediately.
- FP4: “Wrong posture lane” (trying standing solutions on kneeling, or vice versa).
  - Fix: posture gate first, then select hub.
- FP5: “Single-channel offense” (only legs or only upper body).
  - Fix: enforce Upper/Lower and Lower/Position dilemmas to maintain forced choices.

## 5.0 SYSTEM OVERVIEW

### 5.1 System Mission
Open Guard 2 is a no-gi open-guard program whose primary deliverables are:
- high-percentage submissions (upper- and lower-body), and
- high-percentage reversals that reliably convert guard to top or back scoring positions.

The program is built to function against skilled opponents in late-match, high-sweat conditions where classical no-gi grips are transient. 

### 5.2 Core Constraint (No-Gi Grip Fragility) → Architectural Solution
- Constraint: no-gi grip connections to wrists/head/ankles are often not durable enough to control the completion window after a classical sweep. 
- Solution: adopt a two-phase scoring model that uses a classical sweep to create a knockdown, then upgrades to robust control (legs/hips/waist) to complete the reversal. 

### 5.3 Primary Control Targets (No-Gi Substitutions)
The system treats legs/hips/waist as the main control groups that replace gi collar/cuff/belt style control, because those are the domains where reliable no-gi connections exist. 

### 5.4 The Main Attack Cycle (How the System Loops)
1) Establish retention baseline (assumed prerequisite from prior work) → initiate offense from open guard.  [oai_citation:0‡NW Open Guard V2 Ch1.docx](file-service://file-PcZD8DxDDbZtGCLc3CvUpH)  
2) Present a constant leg threat (entanglement/ashi-based), which induces predictable defensive reactions.   
3) Feed off reactions into one of three hubs:
   - **Wrestling reversals** (when opponent uses distance/pullback).   
   - **Sumi gaeshi** (when opponent is kneeling).   
   - **Upper-body submissions** (triangle/armbar/guillotine, especially when legs are hidden by kneeling).   
4) Convert knockdowns to scoring via two-phase completion (waist capture), then continue to top/back dominance and finishing.   
5) Use dilemmas (especially Volume 8) as the forcing layer that keeps the opponent choosing between bad options, which restarts the cycle.   

### 5.5 The Two-Phase Engine (Default Scoring Method)
**Phase 1: Knockdown creation.**
- The sweep is treated primarily as a way to put the opponent’s **hands or hips** to the mat.   

**Phase 2: Wrestling completion via the WAIST.**
- The “finish target” is the opponent’s **waist** (preferably from behind), because it is a robust no-gi grip that survives motion and sweat.   
- Typical completion behaviors include climbing from leg connections to the waist, then breaking down/pinning (including shoulder-to-mat behaviors) to consolidate.   

A key motivational model is that the two-phase method “cheats” the neutral wrestling problem by forcing scrambles from an unfair starting position (opponent knocked down, you rising with access to hips/waist).   

### 5.6 X-Guard as a Standing-Opponent Hub
X-guard is treated as a premier method to:
- control the space between the opponent’s knees,
- generate knockdowns to hands/hips, and
- transition into waist capture for scoring.   

### 5.7 Wrestling Reversals as the Distance-Dilemma Hub
When the opponent plays at distance to avoid the legs, the system routes to seated/guard-based wrestling reversals (picks/pulls/doubles) with the same completion rule: knockdown → waist.   

### 5.8 Sumi Gaeshi as the Kneeling-Opponent Hub
Sumi gaeshi is positioned as a centerpiece sweep for opponents on one or two knees, with emphasis on winning the ensuing scramble (not merely tipping the opponent).   

### 5.9 Hip Heist and Go-Behinds as a Parallel Scoring Channel
Hip heisting is treated as a third major attack direction (beyond sweeps/submissions) that:
- forces the opponent to “hold you down” as well as pass,
- creates front-headlock/go-behind scoring opportunities, and
- yields favorable scoring continuations (including back takes).   

Scoring constraint: the heist itself is not a score; you must get behind the elbows and keep at least one knee down long enough to satisfy the scoring window.   

### 5.10 Guillotine and Go-Behind Symbiosis (Integrated Threat Pair)
The system explicitly pairs guillotines with go-behinds as mutually reinforcing threats:
- defending one tends to open the other,
- the pair is especially strong when entered off hip heists.  [oai_citation:1‡NW Open Guard V2 Ch7.docx](file-service://file-1GRHatNrqj6fCx5Aea1bvP)  

### 5.11 Dilemmas as the System “Control Layer”
Volume 8’s five dilemmas are treated as the meta-layer that selects and times routing between:
- legs vs wrestling reversals,
- under vs over routes,
- positional vs submission choices,
- and upper vs lower body attacks.   

### 5.12 Operating Priorities (What the System Optimizes For)
The system optimizes for:
- **initiative** (opponent reacts first),
- **repeatable entry** (attacks available regardless of opponent posture),
- **reliable completion** (conversion to top/back via durable control),
- **cyclic pressure** (failure in one lane routes immediately into another lane).

Practically: “enter → force reaction → convert reaction into a stronger next attack” is the core operating rhythm.

### 5.13 Robust-Control Hierarchy (What Counts as ‘Real Control’ in No-Gi)
The system implicitly ranks control states:
1) **Transitory control**: wrists/head/ankles (good for tempo and openings, poor for long completion windows).
2) **Leg connections**: entanglements that restrict base and directionality (good for forcing posts/knockdowns).
3) **Waist control**: the main “completion” control group (best for finishing reversals to score).

This hierarchy explains why many sweeps are treated as knockdowns rather than finishes.

### 5.14 Scoring Model (What “Completion” Means)
The system treats “completion” as one of two outcomes:
- **Top position** with consolidation (the opponent cannot immediately re-guard or scramble to neutral), or
- **Back exposure/back control** resulting from turn-outs, run-aways, or forced rotations.

Knockdowns are not the score; they are *the condition that makes the score achievable*.

### 5.15 Hub-and-Spoke Structure (Why Certain Positions Matter More Than Others)
Open Guard 2 functions as a hub system:
- **Leg entanglement** is the opening hub (high availability).
- **X-guard** is a standing-opponent hub (between-the-knees control → knockdown → rise).
- **Sumi gaeshi** is a kneeling-opponent hub (principal sweep channel).
- **Hip heist / go-behind** is a conversion hub (height advantage → back/top).
- **Upper-body triad** is the submission hub (triangle/armbar/guillotine) when legs are hidden.

The “spokes” are the specific entries and finishes that feed into and out of each hub.

### 5.16 Completion Doctrine (The “Waist First” Rule)
Whenever a scramble begins after a knockdown, completion doctrine is:
- **Do not** default to chasing the second leg in open space.
- **Do** treat the opponent’s **waist** as the primary finish target.
- Choose a completion method (climb vs shoot) based on how the opponent is escaping.

This is the single biggest “why it works at high level” rule in the system.

### 5.17 Risk Management Doctrine (What the System Avoids)
Because no-gi guard attacks carry submission exposure during stand-ups and shots, the system emphasizes:
- minimizing **head commitment** in seated shots,
- using **posting/brace hands** to create leverage safely,
- avoiding extended, overreaching arm positions (“short arms” logic),
- preferring inside grips when head control is not safely available.

### 5.18 Dilemmas as the Control Layer (How Choices Are Forced)
Dilemmas are not a separate topic; they are the **routing mechanism**:
- They decide *when* to pull vs push,
- *whether* to go under vs over,
- *when* to switch from submissions to positional attacks,
- *when* to switch from legs to scoring (position),
- *when* to rotate between upper- and lower-body threats.

In practice, dilemmas keep the opponent from stabilizing in a single defensive posture.

### 5.19 System Reset Conditions (When to Abandon and Restart)
The system defines fast resets rather than slow, grinding battles:
- If the opponent clears your grips and you lose attack posture → **rebuild open guard posture**.
- If a knockdown fails to convert → **re-enter legs** or switch to **front-head/go-behind** if head posture breaks.
- If opponent drops to knees to hide legs → switch to **sumi gaeshi** or **upper-body triad** immediately.
- If opponent stands and disengages → return to the **distance dilemma** solutions (seated reversals or re-entry to legs).

### 5.20 Practical Implementation Notes (How to Use the System in Training)
- Build competence in each hub independently (entries, mechanics, finishes).
- Then train transitions explicitly (hub-to-hub routing), especially:
  - legs → X-guard → waist,
  - leg threat → distance → wrestling reversal → waist,
  - kneeling defense → sumi gaeshi → scramble win,
  - head posture break → hip heist → go-behind / guillotine.

The skill that usually separates success/failure is not the sweep—it is the **post-knockdown conversion**.

### 6.2 Wrestling Reversals From Guard (Standing or Creating Stand-Like Distance)

#### 6.2.1 Ankle Pick (Seated)
- **Primary Use:** Phase 1 knockdown tool when opponent is upright/at distance.
- **Entry Conditions:** inside access to ankle/foot line; opponent’s weight readable by feel.
- **Key Mechanics:** inside grip preference without head control; pull direction aligned with toes; short arms.
- **Completion:** convert knockdown into waist capture (climb/shoot).
- **Common Counters:** opponent squares stance; pulls leg free; back-steps.
- **Follow-Ups:** knee pull; double leg; re-enter legs/ashi.

#### 6.2.2 Knee Pull (and Switching Knee Pull)
- **Primary Use:** destabilize stance via inside knee tendon control.
- **Entry Conditions:** opponent steps in or gives inside knee line.
- **Key Mechanics:** precision on knee tendon; off-balance first; post-hand as needed.
- **Completion:** knockdown → immediate waist hunt.
- **Common Counters:** opponent retracts leg hard; rotates hips away.
- **Follow-Ups:** switch to other knee; double leg; re-enter legs.

#### 6.2.3 Double Leg (Seated Shot)
- **Primary Use:** high-percentage reversal when opponent squares.
- **Entry Conditions:** opponent’s feet present; you can connect behind knees or lock at hip-line.
- **Key Mechanics:** hand depth behind knees; short arms; angle correction; head-use minimized.
- **Completion:** lock → rise → waist control → breakdown/top.
- **Common Counters:** sprawl; heavy hips; crossface.
- **Follow-Ups:** transition to leg entanglement; return to picks/pulls.

#### 6.2.4 Arm Drag (Guard-Based)
- **Primary Use:** create angle and expose back/go-behind path.
- **Entry Conditions:** opponent posts hands or commits arm forward.
- **Key Mechanics:** elbow line control; pull to hip; immediate angle change.
- **Completion:** go-behind OR knockdown → waist.
- **Common Counters:** opponent retracts elbow; squares quickly.
- **Follow-Ups:** hip heist; front headlock; leg re-entry.

#### 6.2.5 Ashi Waza (Foot Sweeps)
- **Primary Use:** micro-knockdowns that feed two-phase finishes.
- **Entry Conditions:** opponent stepping patterns are readable; timing window present.
- **Key Mechanics:** off-balance first; sweep into the step; don’t overreach.
- **Completion:** treat as Phase 1 (stumble/hand post/hip drop) → waist.
- **Common Counters:** opponent floats foot; withdraws; hops out.
- **Follow-Ups:** picks/pulls; leg entanglement re-entry.

### 6.3 Tripod / Shin Sweep Cluster (Knockdown Families)

#### 6.3.1 Tripod Sweep (Forward Knockdown)
- **Primary Use:** put hands to mat; start Phase 2.
- **Entry Conditions:** ankle/heel line available + upper-body steering.
- **Key Mechanics:** timing; angle; keep opponent’s hands busy.
- **Completion:** heist/climb/shoot to waist; do not settle for a loose tip-over.

#### 6.3.2 Reverse Tripod (Backward Knockdown)
- **Primary Use:** put hips/butt to mat; start Phase 2.
- **Entry Conditions:** opponent weight on heels / retreating energy.
- **Key Mechanics:** controlled directionality; maintain leg connection as opponent turns.
- **Completion:** win race to waist before opponent springs back up.

#### 6.3.3 Outside Tripod
- **Primary Use:** punish outside stepping reactions.
- **Entry Conditions:** opponent steps outside to clear entanglement or avoid picks.
- **Key Mechanics:** align force with opponent’s step; keep connection.
- **Completion:** waist capture remains mandatory.

#### 6.3.4 Double Shin Sweep
- **Primary Use:** separate base and create hands/hips knockdown.
- **Entry Conditions:** shin-on-shin control available; base can be split.
- **Key Mechanics:** control space between knees; timing.
- **Completion:** rise and convert to waist.

### 6.4 Ashi Entries as Bridges (Irimi Ashi Garami as a Routing Tool)

#### 6.4.1 Irimi Ashi Garami (Entry/Sweep Bridge)
- **Primary Use:** safe square entry to legs; bridge to X-guard; bridge to knockdowns.
- **Entry Conditions:** standing opponent; you can stay square and connect to legs.
- **Key Mechanics:** alignment safety (don’t “side-on” too early); preserve inside position.
- **Completion:** transition to X-guard or knockdown → waist.
- **Common Counters:** peel/clear of leg connection; back-step.
- **Follow-Ups:** X-guard hub; leg lock threat; reset to distance-reversal lane.

### 6.5 X-Guard Hub (Standing Opponent Control Platform)

#### 6.5.1 X-Guard (Position)
- **Primary Use:** control space between knees; generate knockdowns.
- **Entry Conditions:** prefer Irimi Ashi → X transition over direct X vs standing.
- **Key Mechanics:** inside positioning; elbow/shoelace controls; base separation.
- **Completion:** knockdown → immediate waist hunt.

#### 6.5.2 X-Guard Heist Reversal (Core)
- **Primary Use:** convert forward knockdown (hands posted) into rise and finish.
- **Entry Conditions:** hands forced to mat; you can turn to a side and build height.
- **Key Mechanics:** foot brace behind knee; move from hip → knee; ear/shoulder tightness.
- **Completion:** finish to waist via climb or shoot; avoid chasing the “second leg.”

#### 6.5.3 Shoelace Heist (De-Athleticizing Variant)
- **Primary Use:** force knee-down to reduce mobility and improve heist success.
- **Entry Conditions:** shoelace-on-shoelace access after knockdown.
- **Key Mechanics:** remove foot contact; force knee to mat; shelf leg to prevent escape.
- **Completion:** step in → hip higher than opponent → waist.

#### 6.5.4 X-Guard Scissor / Double-Leg X / Overclasp X
- **Primary Use:** variations that adjust control and directionality during resistance.
- **Entry Conditions:** opponent posture/clearing patterns dictate which variation stabilizes.
- **Key Mechanics:** preserve between-the-knees control; keep safe alignment.
- **Completion:** all variants must terminate in waist capture for scoring.

#### 6.5.5 Double Kouchi / Shoelace Double Kouchi / De Ashi Harai (from X contexts)
- **Primary Use:** knockdown tools integrated into the X-guard standing game.
- **Completion:** treat as Phase 1 → convert to Phase 2 (waist).

#### 6.5.6 Scramble Control After Two-Phase Reversals
- **Primary Use:** prevent re-guard and win race to hips/back.
- **Key Mechanics:** ear-to-back + tight waist anchoring; shoulder-to-mat where available.
- **Completion:** consolidate top/back (don’t accept “almost”).

- **Entry Conditions:** opponent kneeling; posture can be tilted.
- **Key Mechanics:** forehead-to-mat cue; elbow inside (deny post); two-leg engine; correct roll direction.
- **Completion:** win the scramble and consolidate top (or waist/back if they scramble).

### 6.7 Upper-Body Submission Hub (Triangle / Armbar / Guillotine)

#### 6.7.1 Triangle System (Entry Families)
- **Primary Use:** highest-percentage upper-body submission when elbow line can be controlled.
- **Entry Conditions:** 2-on-1 elbow control; hand-to-hand setups; overties; clamp entries.
- **Key Mechanics:** elbow forward inside thigh line; trap triangle first; convert to figure-four.
- **Completion:** finish OR use reaction to open armbar/legs.

#### 6.7.2 Armbar (Juji Gatame) + Inverted Juji (Clamp Path)
- **Primary Use:** punish posture/extension created by triangle threats.
- **Entry Conditions:** opponent defends triangle by posture or arm exposure.
- **Key Mechanics:** control elbow line; angle; prevent stack where possible.
- **Completion:** finish OR transition back to triangle/legs.

#### 6.7.3 Guillotine System (Football Snap → High Wrist)
- **Primary Use:** primary head attack; integrates with hip heist and go-behind threats.
- **Entry Conditions:** head posture collapsible; football snap available.
- **Key Mechanics:** football catch tight; centerline shift to high wrist; secondary arm overhook control where appropriate.
- **Completion:** finish OR force defense that opens go-behind.

- **Primary Use:** convert guard exchange into height advantage and score.
- **Entry Conditions:** opponent head low / hands posted / pressure forward.

- **Completion:** go-behind or front-headlock control.

## X-GUARD HUB (Standing Opponent)

### 6.20.1 X-Guard (Base Position)
- **Family / Hub:** X-Guard Hub (Standing Opponent)
- **Primary Use:** Between-the-knees control platform to create predictable knockdowns and start two-phase finishes.
- **Trigger(s):** Opponent standing with accessible legs; opponent clearing an initial ashi/leg connection.
- **Preconditions:** You can get one leg threaded inside and maintain inside position without being flattened.
- **Primary Mechanics (Non-Negotiables):**
  1) Control the space between the knees (separate base).
  2) Keep inside alignment (avoid getting rotated side-on without control).
  3) Maintain connection while off-balancing (do not “push away” and lose contact).
- **Completion Doctrine:** Your job is to produce KD-H (hands posted) or KD-HIP (hips down), then immediately start the Waist Capture Module.
- **Failure Mode(s):** Opponent back-steps/clears; you lose between-knees control and get forced flat.
- **Reset / Next Hub:** Re-enter legs (Ashi) or route to Wrestling Reversal Hub if opponent disengages to distance.

---

### 6.20.2 X-Guard Heist Reversal (Core)
- **Family / Hub:** X-Guard Hub → Completion Module
- **Primary Use:** Convert KD-H (hands posted) into rise + waist capture to score.
- **Trigger(s):** Opponent’s hands touch the mat, posture broken forward, or knee forced down.
- **Preconditions:** X-guard connection is still present as the knockdown occurs.
- **Primary Mechanics (Non-Negotiables):**
  1) **Heist correctly:** move from hip → knee → height (don’t “stand up loose”).
  2) **Stay connected:** keep a leg/hip connection so opponent cannot reset base cleanly.
  3) **Angle to the waist:** rise into the corner behind the hips, not straight into their chest.
  4) **Head/shoulder tether:** keep your upper body tight so they cannot turn and face you freely.
- **Completion Doctrine:** Mandatory Phase 2: lock to the **waist** (climb or shoot), then breakdown/consolidate top/back.
- **Failure Mode(s):** You heist but chase the second leg; opponent limp-legs and re-squares.
- **Reset / Next Hub:** Re-enter legs (back to X/Ashi) or switch to go-behind if head posture collapses during the scramble.

---

### 6.20.3 Shoelace Heist Reversal (De-Athleticizing Variant)
- **Family / Hub:** X-Guard Hub (De-Athleticizing)
- **Primary Use:** Force knee-down / remove foot contact to reduce opponent athleticism, then rise to waist.
- **Trigger(s):** Opponent is bouncing, hopping, or repeatedly clearing standard X-guard heists.
- **Preconditions:** You can connect shoelace-to-shoelace and prevent the opponent from posting a strong stepping base.
- **Primary Mechanics (Non-Negotiables):**
  1) Remove their ability to plant the foot (shoelace control prevents stable steps).
  2) Force a knee-down event (KD-K) to reduce mobility.
  3) Shelf the leg long enough to build height (heist without losing the leg line).
- **Completion Doctrine:** KD-K → rise → waist capture; consolidate before they re-stand.
- **Failure Mode(s):** Shoelace control breaks as you rise; opponent steps free and resets stance.
- **Reset / Next Hub:** Return to standard X-guard heist, or re-enter Ashi if they clear.

---

### 6.20.4 Overclasp X-Guard (Stabilizer Variation)
- **Family / Hub:** X-Guard Variations
- **Primary Use:** Stabilize the opponent’s rotation/clearing attempts so you can still produce knockdowns.
- **Trigger(s):** Opponent attempts to peel/turn out of your X-guard by rotating hips away.
- **Preconditions:** You can connect an upper-body clasp that prevents free hip rotation.
- **Primary Mechanics (Non-Negotiables):**
  1) Clamp rotational escape (deny hips turning away).
  2) Keep between-knees control while clasping (don’t trade leg control for a hug).
  3) Re-aim for KD-H or KD-HIP immediately.
- **Completion Doctrine:** Variation is not the finish—knockdown → waist remains the finish.
- **Failure Mode(s):** You clasp but lose leg separation; opponent steps out and disengages.
- **Reset / Next Hub:** Re-enter legs or shift to wrestling reversals if distance opens.

## SUMI GAESHI HUB (Kneeling Opponent)

### 6.20.5 Sumi Gaeshi (Core Mechanics Entry)
- **Family / Hub:** Sumi Gaeshi Hub (Kneeling Opponent)
- **Primary Use:** Principal sweep channel against one/two-knee opponent.
- **Trigger(s):** Opponent drops to knees (often to hide legs); opponent pressure forward with knee(s) down.
- **Preconditions:** You can tilt opponent’s posture and deny their posting arm(s).
- **Primary Mechanics (Non-Negotiables):**
  1) Forehead/head line driven toward the mat (break posture).
  2) Elbow inside / post denial (remove the key base).
  3) Two-leg engine: elevator leg + drive leg (power source).
  4) Correct directionality: roll them over the head-line, not into their base.
- **Completion Doctrine:** Sweep is not “done” until the scramble is won—come up and consolidate; waist capture is acceptable/expected if they scramble.
- **Failure Mode(s):** Opponent posts hand; you roll them into base; you end flat and lose posture.
- **Reset / Next Hub:** If flattened supine → Forward Shift → re-seat; if opponent stands → transition to legs/X-guard.

---

### 6.20.6 Collar-and-Elbow Sumi Gaeshi (Connection-Based Variant)
- **Family / Hub:** Sumi Gaeshi Variants
- **Primary Use:** Apply Sumi gaeshi when you have head/arm tie control.
- **Trigger(s):** Opponent’s head and elbow line are available in kneeling posture.
- **Preconditions:** Stable head control plus elbow line that can be pulled inside.
- **Primary Mechanics (Non-Negotiables):**
  1) Snap head posture down first (don’t sweep an upright spine).
  2) Pull elbow line inside to kill posting.
  3) Maintain tight connection as hips elevate (no separation).
- **Completion Doctrine:** Finish roll → immediately get to top control; if they turn, hunt waist/back.
- **Failure Mode(s):** Weak head control; elbow retracts; opponent bases wide.
- **Reset / Next Hub:** Switch to another Sumi variant (double triceps/overhook) or route to upper-body triad if head stays low.

---

### 6.20.7 Shoulder Crunch / Arm-Trap Sumi Gaeshi (Compression Variant)
- **Family / Hub:** Sumi Gaeshi Variants (Compression)
- **Primary Use:** Make opponent feel “light” and remove post options via compression.
- **Trigger(s):** Opponent drives forward; you can compress shoulder/arm across.
- **Preconditions:** You can keep their shoulder line collapsed and arm trapped across the body.
- **Primary Mechanics (Non-Negotiables):**
  1) Knees-to-chest to lighten opponent.
  2) Keep the trapped arm across (post denial by structure).
  3) Elevate hips and roll with correct directionality.
- **Completion Doctrine:** Win scramble—do not accept a loose roll that turns into neutral.
- **Failure Mode(s):** Crunch loosens; opponent posts or back-steps knee out.
- **Reset / Next Hub:** Forward shift to re-seat; transition to triangle/guillotine if head remains down.

## GUILLOTINE HUB (Head Attacks + Integration)

### 6.20.8 Football Snap Guillotine (Entry)
- **Family / Hub:** Guillotine Hub (Entry)
- **Primary Use:** Cross-centerline snap that “catches” the head tight to your torso to start high-percentage guillotine work.
- **Trigger(s):** Head-to-head contact; opponent’s posture is momentarily vulnerable to a snap.
- **Preconditions:** You can rise enough to snap with structure (not just arm strength).
- **Primary Mechanics (Non-Negotiables):**
  1) Snap with forearm/wrist (not fingers).
  2) Bring the head across your centerline into a tight “catch.”
  3) Close the space: your torso traps the head so it cannot be yanked free.
- **Completion Doctrine:** Football snap is an entry—immediately route to High-Wrist via centerline shift, or to front headlock control if depth is denied.
- **Failure Mode(s):** Catch is loose; head slips out; you end extended and exposed.
- **Reset / Next Hub:** Re-snap if head is still available; otherwise hip heist/go-behind if posture collapses.

---

### 6.20.9 High-Wrist Guillotine (Primary Finish Configuration)
- **Family / Hub:** Guillotine Hub (Finish)
- **Primary Use:** Efficient carotid coverage with reduced hand-fighting access.
- **Trigger(s):** Football snap succeeded; head is tight and you can shift your head across centerline.
- **Preconditions:** Head trapped close; you can perform a centerline shift without losing the head.
- **Primary Mechanics (Non-Negotiables):**
  1) Centerline shift to access depth.
  2) High-wrist depth: hand/wrist positioned for strong strangulation.
  3) Secondary arm routing to control the defensive arm when available (reduce frames/rollouts).
- **Completion Doctrine:** Finish the strangle OR force defense that opens go-behind/back exposure.
- **Failure Mode(s):** You fail to shift; wrist stays low; opponent hand-fights and frees head.
- **Reset / Next Hub:** Convert to front headlock control → go-behind; or re-enter upper-body tie and re-snap.

---

### 6.20.10 Arm-In Guillotine (Situational Variant)
- **Family / Hub:** Guillotine Variants
- **Primary Use:** Maintain a viable strangle when opponent defends by inserting an arm as a frame.
- **Trigger(s):** Opponent frames with an arm inside your choking path during defense.
- **Preconditions:** You can keep head trapped; you can keep your body tight to prevent extraction.
- **Primary Mechanics (Non-Negotiables):**
  1) Keep chest/torso tight over the head/shoulder line.
  2) Prevent the opponent from rotating to face you freely.
  3) Maintain elbow position that keeps pressure while limiting hand access.
- **Completion Doctrine:** Finish if pressure is immediate; otherwise use it to force turn/roll that exposes back/go-behind.
- **Failure Mode(s):** Space opens; head pops out; you lose front headlock.
- **Reset / Next Hub:** Re-snap/high-wrist route; or go-behind if they turn away.

## HIP HEIST / GO-BEHIND HUB (Scoring Conversion)

### 6.20.11 Knee-Block Go-Behind (Primary)
- **Family / Hub:** Go-Behind Families
- **Primary Use:** Convert heist height advantage into a behind-the-hips score by blocking the opponent’s knee.
- **Trigger(s):** Opponent’s knee is planted; opponent drives forward; you can heist and trap the knee line.
- **Preconditions:** You can rise with structure and access the knee as a block point.
- **Primary Mechanics (Non-Negotiables):**
  1) Knee block prevents their “turn and square” recovery.
  2) Circle behind elbows/hips immediately (do not pause in front).
  3) Keep at least one knee down long enough to complete the circling window.
- **Completion Doctrine:** Behind position → consolidate (tight waist/head connection) → top/back control.
- **Failure Mode(s):** You circle too wide; opponent retracts knee and squares; you lose angle.
- **Reset / Next Hub:** If they square → return to legs/X-guard; if head dips → guillotine/front headlock.

---

### 6.20.12 Hand-Block Go-Behind (Post-Hand Punish)
- **Family / Hub:** Go-Behind Families
- **Primary Use:** Exploit opponent’s hand post by blocking it to prevent circling defense.
- **Trigger(s):** Opponent posts a hand to stabilize during your heist or after a knockdown.
- **Preconditions:** You can access and block the posted hand without losing your base.
- **Primary Mechanics (Non-Negotiables):**
  1) Block the post so the opponent cannot pivot/face you.
  2) Circle tight behind the elbow line.
  3) Maintain height advantage during the circle (do not drop your chest).
- **Completion Doctrine:** Secure behind → consolidate; treat it as a scoring finish, not a “moment.”
- **Failure Mode(s):** Miss the post; opponent re-posts elsewhere; you end in front again.
- **Reset / Next Hub:** Re-block or switch to knee-block; if head available, snap to guillotine.

---

### 6.20.13 Arm-Drag Go-Behind (Elbow-Line Route)
- **Family / Hub:** Go-Behind Families
- **Primary Use:** Create angle and back exposure via elbow-line control.
- **Trigger(s):** Opponent reaches/hand-fights forward; elbow line becomes accessible.
- **Preconditions:** You can control the elbow and move your body off-line immediately.
- **Primary Mechanics (Non-Negotiables):**
  1) Drag elbow past your hip line (don’t tug it to center).
  2) Immediate angle change—get your body to the outside lane.
  3) Circle behind before the elbow retracts.
- **Completion Doctrine:** Behind position → waist control → consolidate.
- **Failure Mode(s):** Drag without angle change; opponent retracts elbow and squares.
- **Reset / Next Hub:** If drag fails, route to hip heist again or switch to legs if they stand.

---

### 6.20.14 Misdirectional Go-Behind (Reaction Exploit)
- **Family / Hub:** Go-Behind Families (Reaction-Based)
- **Primary Use:** Use one direction to force a defensive shift, then circle the opposite way into open space.
- **Trigger(s):** Opponent overreacts to your initial circle direction.
- **Preconditions:** You are upright enough to change direction without collapsing.
- **Primary Mechanics (Non-Negotiables):**
  1) Sell the first direction just enough to draw reaction.
  2) Re-route immediately; do not “double fake” and lose time.
  3) Finish tight behind hips, not behind shoulders.
- **Completion Doctrine:** Secure waist/back exposure; consolidate.
- **Failure Mode(s):** Direction change too late; opponent re-squares.
- **Reset / Next Hub:** Re-enter heist/go-behind series or return to legs if posture changes.

## 8.0 APPLICATION SCENARIOS

### 8.1 Scenario Format (How These Are Written)
Each scenario is a “playbook” built from the decision framework:
- **Situation / Start State**
- **Primary Objective**
- **Primary Route (Hub)**
- **Decision Gates**
- **Completion Doctrine**
- **Common Failures**
- **Immediate Resets**

---

### 8.2 Standing Opponent Sits Back to Avoid Legs (Distance Dilemma)
- **Situation / Start State:** Opponent stands and stays at long range, backing hips away from your feet.
- **Primary Objective:** Force a knockdown via seated reversals and complete to waist.
- **Primary Route (Hub):** Wrestling Reversal Hub.
- **Decision Gates:**
  - If ankle/inside access exists → ankle pick family.
  - If inside knee tendon accessible → knee pull family.
  - If opponent squares stance → seated double leg.
- **Completion Doctrine:** Any KD-H or KD-HIP must route immediately to waist capture (climb/shoot).
- **Common Failures:** overreaching; chasing second leg; allowing opponent to fully disengage.
- **Immediate Resets:** re-enter legs if they step in; if they kneel to hide legs, switch to kneeling hub.

---

### 8.3 Standing Opponent Steps In Heavy to Smother (Legs Become Easier)
- **Situation / Start State:** Opponent approaches aggressively to shut down your seated shots.
- **Primary Objective:** Use their forward pressure to enter legs safely and route to X-guard.
- **Primary Route (Hub):** Leg Entanglement → X-Guard Hub.
- **Decision Gates:**
  - If square entry available → Irimi Ashi entry.
  - If they clear initial leg bite → transition to X-guard.
- **Completion Doctrine:** X-guard knockdown → waist capture → consolidate.
- **Common Failures:** entering side-on; losing inside position; accepting a loose knockdown.
- **Immediate Resets:** if they disengage to distance, switch to wrestling reversals; if head collapses during scramble, insert heist/go-behind.

---

### 8.4 Opponent Drops to Two Knees to Hide Legs (Kneeling Hub Trigger)
- **Situation / Start State:** Opponent disengages legs by kneeling close.
- **Primary Objective:** Sweep or submit from kneeling posture; maintain initiative.
- **Primary Route (Hub):** Sumi Gaeshi Hub and Upper-Body Triad Hub.
- **Decision Gates:**
  - If posture tiltable + post denial present → Sumi gaeshi.
  - If elbow line exposed/extended → triangle or armbar.
  - If head posture collapses → guillotine or hip heist to go-behind.
- **Completion Doctrine:** Sweep is not done until scramble is won; submissions must have exit rules.
- **Common Failures:** rolling into base; trying standing solutions on kneeling opponent; overcommitting to one submission.
- **Immediate Resets:** opponent stands again → return to legs/X-guard; opponent shells up → positional off-balance then re-attack.

---

### 8.5 Opponent Posts Hands to Prevent Sweep (KD-H Trigger)
- **Situation / Start State:** You off-balance and opponent catches themselves with hands.
- **Primary Objective:** Convert KD-H into a scored reversal.
- **Primary Route (Hub):** Completion Modules (Heist → Waist).
- **Decision Gates:**
  - If you have leg connection → heist immediately.
  - If head posture collapses during post → hip heist / guillotine insert.
- **Completion Doctrine:** KD-H requires waist capture; ear-to-back + tight waist to stop flight.
- **Common Failures:** pausing after knockdown; chasing second leg; losing connection during rise.
- **Immediate Resets:** if opponent springs back to stand, re-enter legs or switch to distance reversals.

---

### 8.6 Opponent Drops to Hip to Avoid Being Swept (KD-HIP Trigger)
- **Situation / Start State:** Your sweep forces opponent to hips/butt, but they scramble.
- **Primary Objective:** Win race to waist before they stand/square.
- **Primary Route (Hub):** Two-Phase Completion (Waist-first doctrine).
- **Decision Gates:**
  - If contact is close → climb to waist.
  - If contact is separating → shoot angle to waist.
- **Completion Doctrine:** waist is the finish; shoulder-to-mat where available to kill wrestling.
- **Common Failures:** settling into loose top without control; allowing them to face you.
- **Immediate Resets:** if waist missed → re-enter legs; if head collapses → front head/go-behind.

---

### 8.7 Opponent Drives Forward With Head Low (Over Route Trigger)
- **Situation / Start State:** Opponent’s pressure puts head low and hands active.
- **Primary Objective:** Convert head posture break into score or submission.
- **Primary Route (Hub):** Hip Heist / Go-Behind Hub + Guillotine Hub.
- **Decision Gates:**
  - If you can snap and trap head → football snap → high-wrist guillotine.
  - If their knee/hand is planted → knee-block/hand-block go-behind.
- **Completion Doctrine:** finish guillotine OR take the back/top off defense; consolidate behind.
- **Common Failures:** loose head catch; circling too wide; losing height advantage.
- **Immediate Resets:** if head escapes but they remain kneeling → route to Sumi or triangle/armbar.

---

### 8.8 Opponent Contracts Hard (Elbows In, Base Tight) to Avoid Submissions
- **Situation / Start State:** Opponent shells up and prevents clean submission entries.
- **Primary Objective:** Use positional off-balances to force extension or posts.
- **Primary Route (Hub):** Sumi gaeshi variants + knockdown families.
- **Decision Gates:**
  - If kneeling → Sumi gaeshi to force post.
  - If standing/distance → picks/pulls to force post/hip drop.
- **Completion Doctrine:** knockdown → waist; do not over-grip wrists/head.
- **Common Failures:** trying to submit a contracted opponent without first forcing extension.
- **Immediate Resets:** once posts/extension appear → route back to submissions or legs.

---

### 8.9 Opponent Extends Arms to Hand-Fight or Post (Submission Trigger)
- **Situation / Start State:** Opponent reaches, posts, or leaves elbow line exposed.
- **Primary Objective:** Convert extension into the upper-body triad.
- **Primary Route (Hub):** Triangle/Armbar + Guillotine.
- **Decision Gates:**
  - Elbow forward controllable → triangle entries.
  - Arm isolated / posture reaction → armbar.
  - Head available / snap window → guillotine.
- **Completion Doctrine:** finish or use reaction to re-enter legs (upper ↔ lower cycling).
- **Common Failures:** staying on a failed triangle too long; losing elbow control.
- **Immediate Resets:** if opponent postures and legs are now exposed → re-enter leg entanglement.

## 9.0 DRILLING PROGRESSIONS

### 9.1 Drilling Principles (How to Train This System)
- Train by **hub**, then train **transitions between hubs**.
- Prioritize **completion drills** (waist capture and consolidation) over “pretty sweeps.”
- Use **constraints** (rules) to force correct decision-making (e.g., “must finish at waist”).
- Build from **low resistance → patterned resistance → live resistance**.

---

### 9.2 Phase-Based Progression Structure
Progress each cluster through four stages:
1) **Static mechanics** (correct shape)
2) **Cooperative movement** (timing + posture)
3) **Scripted resistance** (predictable counters)
4) **Live resistance** (decision-making under pressure)

---

## 9.3 Core Module Drills (Universal)

### 9.3.1 Two-Phase Engine Drill (Knockdown → Waist)
- **Goal:** automate Phase 2 completion.
- **Round:** 2–3 minutes each; swap roles.
- **Constraint:** partner must give a knockdown (hands or hips), then immediately try to scramble.
- **You:** must reach waist (climb or shoot) and consolidate.
- **Fail Condition:** chasing second leg, losing contact, or allowing re-square.

### 9.3.2 Knockdown Type Drill (KD-H / KD-HIP / KD-K Recognition)
- **Goal:** instant recognition of knockdown type and correct follow-up.
- **Round:** partner randomly gives one of three knockdown outcomes.
- **You:** call it (KD-H/KD-HIP/KD-K) and execute the correct Phase 2 action.

### 9.3.3 Waist Capture Module Drill (Climb vs Shoot)
- **Goal:** select the correct waist method under time pressure.
- **Round:** partner alternates escapes:
  - “close + sticky” (forces climb), then
  - “separating + limp-leg” (forces shoot).
- **You:** must choose correctly and finish to waist.

### 9.3.4 Ear-to-Back + Tight Waist Anchor Drill
- **Goal:** stop opponent’s run/turn during scramble.
- **Round:** start from behind-the-hips scramble; partner tries to face you.
- **You:** keep head tether + tight waist until opponent’s movement stops.

### 9.3.5 Shoulder-to-Mat Consolidation Drill
- **Goal:** learn to kill wrestling in the final phase.
- **Round:** start from a tight waist; partner attempts to build base and stand.
- **You:** rotate shoulder down and stabilize top.

---

## 9.4 Standing-Lane Drills (Legs → X-Guard → Waist)

### 9.4.1 Irimi Ashi Entry Reps (Square Entry)
- **Goal:** safe entry against standing posture.
- **Constraint:** no side-on entry; must remain square until connection is stable.
- **Progression:** slow → moderate → partner begins clearing attempts.

### 9.4.2 Ashi → X-Guard Transition Drill
- **Goal:** treat opponent’s clearing action as the trigger to X-guard.
- **Round:** partner clears the first entry; you must transition to X-guard immediately.

### 9.4.3 X-Guard Knockdown Creation Drill
- **Goal:** consistently produce KD-H or KD-HIP from X-guard.
- **Constraint:** no “resetting to guard” after knockdown—must continue to Phase 2.

### 9.4.4 X-Guard Heist → Waist Drill
- **Goal:** connect heist timing to waist capture.
- **Constraint:** must reach waist within a short count (e.g., 3 seconds) after KD-H.
- **Progression:** partner increases scramble intensity.

### 9.4.5 Shoelace Heist (De-Athleticizing) Drill
- **Goal:** force KD-K (knee down) and complete.
- **Round:** partner hops/clears aggressively; you must force knee-down and heist.

---

## 9.5 Distance-Lane Drills (Wrestling Reversals From Guard)

### 9.5.1 Inside Grip Pick Series
- **Goal:** ankle pick and knee pull with safe posture.
- **Constraint:** no deep head commitment; use post-hand.
- **Progression:** partner alternates between stepping in and stepping out.

### 9.5.2 Knee Pull → Switching Knee Pull Ladder
- **Goal:** attack whichever knee becomes available as opponent turns/retracts.
- **Round:** partner retracts one knee; you switch to the other.

### 9.5.3 Seated Double Leg Completion Drill
- **Goal:** build the grip hierarchy (behind knees → locked double) and finish.
- **Constraint:** finish must become waist control; no “stalling” on the legs.

### 9.5.4 Ashi Waza Timing Drill
- **Goal:** use foot sweeps as knockdown creators.
- **Round:** partner steps in patterns; you must time sweeps to generate KD-H/KD-HIP.

---

## 9.6 Kneeling-Lane Drills (Sumi + Upper Triad + Heist)

### 9.6.1 Sumi Gaeshi Mechanics Stack Drill
- **Goal:** make the five mechanical priorities automatic.
- **Constraint:** if any non-negotiable is missing, restart the rep.

### 9.6.2 Sumi Gaeshi Variant Selector Drill
- **Goal:** choose the correct variant by available connection.
- **Round:** partner presents changing ties (head/arm, triceps, overhooks, etc.).
- **You:** call the variant and execute.

### 9.6.3 Sumi Gaeshi → Scramble Win Drill
- **Goal:** prevent “tip sweeps” that don’t score.
- **Constraint:** every rep must end with consolidation (top or waist/back).

### 9.6.4 Forward Shift Recovery Drill
- **Goal:** restore seated posture when flattened.
- **Round:** partner flattens you; you forward shift to re-seat; then re-attack.

### 9.6.5 Upper-Body Triad Routing Drill
- **Goal:** enforce exit rules and cycling (upper ↔ lower).
- **Round:** partner alternates:
  - kneeling leg hiding,
  - hard posture escapes,
  - head dips.
- **You:** must route triangle/armbar/guillotine appropriately, then cycle to legs when posture opens them.

### 9.6.6 Guillotine Entry Ladder (Football Snap → High-Wrist)
- **Goal:** eliminate “loose catches.”
- **Constraint:** entry must be tight; if head slips, rep is a failure.
- **Progression:** partner adds hand-fighting and roll attempts.

---

## 9.7 Hip Heist / Go-Behind Drills (Conversion Lane)

### 9.7.1 Hip Heist Height Drill
- **Goal:** develop clean rise mechanics.
- **Constraint:** maintain structure; do not stand up without control.

### 9.7.2 Go-Behind Selector Drill
- **Goal:** choose correct go-behind variant based on opponent’s post.
- **Round:** partner alternates knee post, hand post, reaching elbow.
- **You:** choose knee-block, hand-block, or arm-drag go-behind.

### 9.7.3 Go-Behind Consolidation Drill (Knee-Down Window)
- **Goal:** build habit of circling tight and consolidating behind hips.
- **Constraint:** at least one knee down while circling; finish with tight waist.

### 9.7.4 Guillotine ↔ Go-Behind Integration Drill
- **Goal:** treat guillotine defense as a back-take trigger and vice versa.
- **Round:** partner defends guillotine by turning/rolling; you convert to go-behind.
- **Alternate:** partner defends go-behind by squaring; you snap to guillotine entry.

---

## 9.8 Positional Sparring Progressions (System Testing)

### 9.8.1 Hub Sparring
- **Round:** start in each hub (X-guard, kneeling tie, front head/heist, etc.).
- **Win condition:** score via waist/back consolidation or submit.

### 9.8.2 Constraint Sparring
- “No submissions for 2 minutes—only score via two-phase waist finishes.”
- “No legs for 2 minutes—must score via sumi/heist/go-behind.”
- “Opponent must stay standing—only standing-lane solutions.”

### 9.8.3 Full-System Rounds
- Start from open guard; both athletes free.
- Scoring focus: prioritize reversals that end in consolidated top/back.

### 9.9 Metrics and Evaluation (How You Know Training Is Working)

#### 9.9.1 Conversion Metrics (Primary)
Track these as percentages over weekly rounds:
- **Knockdown-to-Waist Conversion Rate:** (# knockdowns that become waist control) / (total knockdowns)
- **Waist-to-Consolidation Rate:** (# waist captures that become stable top/back) / (total waist captures)
- **Hub Entry Rate:** (# times you reach a hub on purpose) / (total attacks)

Target behavior: conversion rates rise first; submissions often rise later as opponents overdefend the scoring game.

#### 9.9.2 Failure Pattern Metrics (Primary)
Log failures with a short tag:
- FP1: knockdown achieved, waist missed
- FP2: chased second leg
- FP3: overheld transitory grips
- FP4: wrong posture lane
- FP5: single-channel offense

Goal: reduce FP1/FP2 first (they are the most score-limiting).

#### 9.9.3 Dilemma Metrics (Control Layer Quality)
- **Forced Reaction Rate:** (# times opponent clearly reacts) / (total initiations)
- **Lane Switch Time:** average time from opponent posture change → your hub switch
Goal: forced reactions increase; lane switch time decreases.

---

### 9.10 Constraint Packs (Plug-and-Play Rule Sets for Rounds)

#### 9.10.1 Two-Phase Pack
- You may only score if you reach **waist control** after a knockdown.
- Any sweep that does not become waist control is a “no score” and forces a reset.

#### 9.10.2 Anti-Second-Leg Pack
- The moment a knockdown occurs, you are prohibited from grabbing a second leg.
- You must either climb/shoot to waist or reset.

#### 9.10.3 Kneeling Pack
- Partner stays kneeling (one/both knees down).
- You must rotate between: Sumi gaeshi, upper-body triad, hip heist/go-behind.

#### 9.10.4 Standing Pack
- Partner must remain standing unless forced down.
- You must rotate between: legs → X-guard → waist, and seated reversals → waist.

#### 9.10.5 Over/Under Pack
- You must attempt one UNDER route and one OVER route each minute.
- Purpose: engrain switching rather than preference-lock.

---

### 9.11 Micro-Progressions (Short Drills With High Yield)

#### 9.11.1 “3-Second Waist” Micro-Drill
- Start at the moment of KD-H (hands posted).
- Objective: reach waist control within 3 seconds.
- If you fail, reset and repeat immediately.

#### 9.11.2 “One-Knee Window” Micro-Drill (Go-Behind)
- Start from hip heist with opponent’s knee posted.
- Objective: keep one knee down and circle tight to behind position.

#### 9.11.3 “Head-Catch Quality” Micro-Drill (Guillotine)
- Start head-to-head.
- Objective: football snap catch must be tight enough that partner cannot yank out.
- If head slips, rep fails.

#### 9.11.4 “Variant Call-Out” Micro-Drill (Sumi / Go-Behind)
- Partner changes tie/post.
- You must call the correct variant out loud before executing:
  - Sumi variant or go-behind variant.
- Purpose: force decision-making, not memorized sequences.

---

### 9.12 Periodization Notes (How to Cycle Training Emphasis)
A practical rotation for most rooms:
- Weeks 1–2: completion modules (waist capture + consolidation)
- Weeks 3–4: hub entries (X-guard / Sumi / heist) under light resistance
- Weeks 5–6: transitions (hub-to-hub routing) under medium resistance
- Weeks 7–8: constraint sparring + full-system rounds with metrics logging

Rule: if conversion metrics drop, return to completion modules before adding new technique.

---

### 9.13 Coach’s Checklist (Fast Diagnostics During Rounds)
Watch for these visible tells:
- After knockdown, do they **pause**? (means Phase 2 is not automatic)
- Do they **chase second leg**? (means waist doctrine not internalized)
- Do they **stay on wrists/head grips too long**? (means grip sequencing not understood)
- Do they **try standing solutions on kneeling opponent**? (posture gate failure)
- Do they **repeat one lane only**? (dilemma control layer not used)

## 10.0 FOUR-WEEK CURRICULUM

### 10.1 Curriculum Design (Intent)
This curriculum is built around the system’s actual bottleneck:
- Most athletes can create knockdowns.
- Fewer athletes can **convert knockdowns into scored reversals** consistently.
Therefore, Weeks 1–2 emphasize completion and conversion, then Weeks 3–4 expand hub entries and transitions.

Format assumption:
- 4 sessions/week (adjustable)
- Each session: 60–90 minutes
- Every session includes: (a) technique, (b) constrained drilling, (c) positional sparring, (d) brief metrics.

---

## WEEK 1 — Completion Doctrine (Two-Phase Engine)

### Session 1: Two-Phase Fundamentals
- **Theme:** Phase 1 is a means; Phase 2 is the score.
- **Tech Focus:** Waist Capture Module (Climb vs Shoot).
- **Drills:**
  - Two-Phase Engine Drill (knockdown → waist)
  - “3-Second Waist” Micro-Drill
- **Positional Sparring:** start at KD-H and KD-HIP.
- **Metric:** Knockdown-to-Waist Conversion Rate.

### Session 2: Scramble Anchors
- **Theme:** Stop the escape; then consolidate.
- **Tech Focus:** Ear-to-Back + Tight Waist; Shoulder-to-Mat.
- **Drills:**
  - Ear-to-Back anchor rounds
  - Shoulder-to-mat consolidation rounds
- **Positional Sparring:** start from waist control with partner attempting to face/stand.
- **Metric:** Waist-to-Consolidation Rate.

### Session 3: Knockdown Recognition
- **Theme:** KD-H, KD-HIP, KD-K recognition → correct action.
- **Tech Focus:** Knockdown Type Map.
- **Drills:**
  - Knockdown Type Drill (randomized)
  - Anti-Second-Leg Pack rounds
- **Positional Sparring:** start from each knockdown type.
- **Metric:** FP1 and FP2 frequency.

### Session 4: Integration Test (Week 1)
- **Theme:** Convert whatever you get.
- **Tech Focus:** Review completion modules only.
- **Drills:** short, high intensity (two-phase sprints).
- **Positional Sparring:** open guard start, but scoring only via waist completion.
- **Metric:** overall conversion rate across live rounds.

---

## WEEK 2 — Standing Lane (Legs → X-Guard → Waist)

### Session 5: Safe Standing Entries
- **Theme:** Get to legs without losing alignment.
- **Tech Focus:** Irimi Ashi entry; inside-position management.
- **Drills:**
  - Irimi Ashi entry reps (square protocol)
  - Ashi → X-guard transition drill
- **Positional Sparring:** opponent must remain standing.
- **Metric:** Hub Entry Rate (X-guard reached on purpose).

### Session 6: X-Guard Knockdowns
- **Theme:** Between-the-knees control creates knockdowns.
- **Tech Focus:** X-guard base position → KD creation.
- **Drills:**
  - X-guard knockdown creation drill
  - KD-H recognition → immediate heist
- **Positional Sparring:** start in X-guard.
- **Metric:** X-guard knockdown success rate.

### Session 7: X-Guard Heist Completion
- **Theme:** Knockdown is the beginning—finish at waist.
- **Tech Focus:** X-guard heist reversal (core).
- **Drills:**
  - Heist → waist capture ladder
  - Ear-to-back anchor immediately after waist capture
- **Positional Sparring:** start from KD-H in X-guard.
- **Metric:** X-guard knockdown-to-waist conversion.

### Session 8: De-Athleticizing Variant
- **Theme:** When they bounce, force knee-down.
- **Tech Focus:** Shoelace heist.
- **Drills:**
  - Shoelace knee-down forcing reps
  - KD-K → waist completion rounds
- **Positional Sparring:** opponent’s goal is to hop/clear; yours is to force KD-K and finish.
- **Metric:** KD-K conversion.

---

## WEEK 3 — Kneeling Lane (Sumi + Upper Triad + Heist)

### Session 9: Sumi Gaeshi Mechanics Stack
- **Theme:** The five non-negotiables.
- **Tech Focus:** Sumi gaeshi core mechanics.
- **Drills:**
  - Mechanics stack reps (restart if any is missing)
  - Sumi → scramble win drill
- **Positional Sparring:** start kneeling tie.
- **Metric:** “Tip without score” rate (should decrease).

### Session 10: Sumi Variants and Selection
- **Theme:** Choose by connection, not preference.
- **Tech Focus:** Variant selector (collar/elbow, double triceps, overhooks, etc.).
- **Drills:**
  - Variant call-out drill
  - Variant reps under light resistance
- **Positional Sparring:** partner rotates ties; you must select correctly.
- **Metric:** correct selection rate.

### Session 11: Upper-Body Triad (Triangle/Armbar)
- **Theme:** Elbow control is the real key.
- **Tech Focus:** Triangle entry and exit rules; armbar off triangle reactions.
- **Drills:**
  - Triangle lock ladder (trap → figure-four)
  - Triangle exit to armbar drills
- **Positional Sparring:** start from 2-on-1 elbow.
- **Metric:** time-to-decision (don’t overinvest in failed locks).

### Session 12: Guillotine + Heist Insert
- **Theme:** Head posture breaks are scoring opportunities.
- **Tech Focus:** Football snap → high-wrist; heist/go-behind pairing.
- **Drills:**
  - Guillotine entry ladder
  - Guillotine defense → go-behind conversion
- **Positional Sparring:** start head-to-head in kneeling posture.
- **Metric:** forced reaction rate (finish or convert).

---

## WEEK 4 — Full-System Routing (Dilemmas + Transitions)

### Session 13: Distance Dilemma Day (Wrestling Reversal Hub)
- **Theme:** Distance routes to picks/pulls/doubles.
- **Tech Focus:** inside grip picks; knee pull ladder; seated double.
- **Drills:**
  - Distance-lane drill set
  - Two-phase finish under time constraint
- **Positional Sparring:** opponent must keep distance and stand.
- **Metric:** reversal-to-waist conversion.

### Session 14: Heist/Go-Behind Day (Over Route)
- **Theme:** Make standing up a permanent threat.
- **Tech Focus:** hip heist mechanics; go-behind selector.
- **Drills:**
  - Go-behind selector drill
  - Knee-down window consolidation
- **Positional Sparring:** start from hip heist with knee/hand posts.
- **Metric:** go-behind consolidation rate.

### Session 15: Dilemma Routing Day (All Five Dilemmas)
- **Theme:** Use dilemmas to switch lanes before you stall.
- **Tech Focus:** decision reps (push/pull → under/over → target selection).
- **Drills:**
  - “must switch hubs” constraint rounds
  - variant call-outs under moderate resistance
- **Positional Sparring:** open guard start with lane-switch constraints.
- **Metric:** lane switch time.

### Session 16: Final Integration Test (Week 4)
- **Theme:** Full system, minimal rules.
- **Tech Focus:** none (review only).
- **Positional Sparring:** full rounds from open guard.
- **Scoring Rule:** bonus points for finishes that pass through waist capture + consolidation.
- **Metrics:** all primary metrics + failure pattern log.

## 11.0 FAILURE PATTERNS (AND CORRECTIONS)

### 11.1 FP1 — Knockdown Achieved, No Score (Failure to Convert to Waist)
- **Signature**
  - You knock the opponent to **hands** or **hip/butt**, but they scramble up and you end back in guard or neutral.  [oai_citation:0‡Nw Open Guard V2 Ch4.docx](file-service://file-PMcS9iJNaSim2XpXT5i78Y)
- **Root Cause**
  - Treating the sweep/knockdown as “the finish,” rather than Phase 1 of a two-phase reversal. 
- **Correction (Non-Negotiable)**
  - Phase 1 (hands/hips down) must immediately become Phase 2: **hunt the waist** (prefer head behind if possible) and break them down. 
- **Default Action**
  - KD-H or KD-HIP occurs → **rise** (heist) with connection → lock to **tight waist** → ear-to-back anchor → breakdown. 
- **Drill Fix**
  - Start at KD-H/KD-HIP; 3-second rule to reach tight waist; reset on failure.

---

### 11.2 FP2 — Chasing the “Second Leg” Against Tough Opponents
- **Signature**
  - After knockdown, you reach for the far/second leg and the opponent **limp-legs** out or re-squares. 
- **Root Cause**
  - Using a completion method that can work at beginner/intermediate levels but degrades at world-class athleticism.  [oai_citation:1‡NW Open Guard V2 Ch3.docx](file-service://file-8MneYpA98K5ENWjgGUjgMz)
- **Correction (Non-Negotiable)**
  - Your real target is the **waist**, not the second leg; use a **climb** strategy or a **shoot** strategy to get there. 
- **Default Action**
  - KD occurs → choose:
    - **Climb** (treat body like a rope) when you have close body contact, or
    - **Shoot** (angle correction) when the opponent is separating.
- **Drill Fix**
  - Constraint rounds: “After KD, no second-leg contact allowed; only waist capture.”

---

### 11.3 FP3 — Overholding Transitory Grips (Wrist/Head/Ankle) Until They Fail
- **Signature**
  - You get initial control (wrist/head/ankle) but stall; grip breaks; you lose tempo and posture.
- **Root Cause**
  - Misunderstanding the gripping pattern: transitory grips are **temporary** and are meant to create openings, not to complete outcomes.  [oai_citation:2‡NW Open Guard V2 Ch3.docx](file-service://file-8MneYpA98K5ENWjgGUjgMz)
- **Correction (Non-Negotiable)**
  - Follow the declared sequence: **transitory upper-body grips → firm leg connections → climb to waist**.  [oai_citation:3‡NW Open Guard V2 Ch3.docx](file-service://file-8MneYpA98K5ENWjgGUjgMz)
- **Default Action**
  - If transitory grip stabilizes the exchange: immediately convert it into leg connection or off-balance; do not “hold and hope.”
- **Drill Fix**
  - Timer drill: any transitory grip must produce a leg connection/off-balance within 2–3 seconds or reset.

---

### 11.4 FP4 — Failure to Control “Space Between Knees” in X-Guard (No Off-Balance, No KD)
- **Signature**
  - You enter X-guard but cannot create a meaningful knockdown; opponent clears and disengages.
- **Root Cause**
  - Losing the core control principle of X-guard: separating and controlling the space between the knees.  [oai_citation:4‡NW Open Guard V2 Ch3.docx](file-service://file-8MneYpA98K5ENWjgGUjgMz)
- **Correction (Non-Negotiable)**
  - Rebuild between-knees control first; knockdowns are downstream of that control.  [oai_citation:5‡NW Open Guard V2 Ch3.docx](file-service://file-8MneYpA98K5ENWjgGUjgMz)
- **Default Action**
  - If knees re-close: re-separate immediately (re-assert between-knees control) before attempting heist/finish.
- **Drill Fix**
  - Positional sparring from X-guard with goal = force KD-H or KD-HIP inside 10 seconds; reset if cleared.

---

### 11.5 FP5 — Athletic Escape Problem (Opponent Too Fast to Control During Heist)
- **Signature**
  - You create KD-H but opponent bounces away; you cannot “stick” to them long enough to rise.
- **Root Cause**
  - Insufficient de-athleticizing measures during Phase 1/transition.
- **Correction (Preferred Tool)**
  - Force one knee to the mat to reduce athleticism and lower height before coming up. 
- **Default Action**
  - If opponent is hopping/accelerating away: apply the knee-down forcing variant → then rise to tight waist.
- **Drill Fix**
  - “Hopper drill”: partner’s only goal is to hop/clear; your goal is knee-down event → immediate waist capture.

---

### 11.6 FP6 — Missing the Ear-to-Back Anchor (Opponent Escapes During Rise)
- **Signature**
  - You reach the waist but the opponent turns/faces and peels you off during the scramble.
- **Root Cause**
  - Head connection not established; you are “hanging” from arms only. 
- **Correction (Non-Negotiable)**
  - Ear-to-back + tight waist is the anchor that makes the opponent’s heist carry you up (rather than shedding you). 
- **Default Action**
  - As soon as waist contact is achieved: head connects (ear-to-back) before you attempt the breakdown.
- **Drill Fix**
  - Start from tight waist with no head contact; partner attempts to face/peel; your only objective is to re-attach ear-to-back and stop the turn.

---

### 11.7 FP7 — Failure to Put Shoulder to the Mat (Opponent Wrestles Too Effectively)
- **Signature**
  - You get close to consolidation but opponent remains too effective with shoulders free; they build/stand or re-square.
- **Root Cause**
  - Missing an anti-wrestling consolidation step: shoulder-to-mat creates a major reduction in wrestling effectiveness. 
- **Correction**
  - Whenever available during breakdown: drive the shoulder to the mat before advancing. 
- **Default Action**
  - Tight waist established → step/angle to put shoulder down → then continue breakdown/advance.
- **Drill Fix**
  - Start from tight waist; partner tries to build; your win condition is shoulder-to-mat within 5 seconds.

---

### 11.8 FP8 — Good Sumi Gaeshi, “Zero Points” (Completion Failure)
- **Signature**
  - You execute a clean sumi gaeshi but do not control the end; opponent returns to top or neutral.  [oai_citation:6‡Nw Open Guard V2 Ch4.docx](file-service://file-PMcS9iJNaSim2XpXT5i78Y)
- **Root Cause**
  - Treating the rotation as scoring; failing to finish with dominant alignment and control.
- **Correction (Non-Negotiable)**
  - Complete with **hips over torso** and **chest down**, so the end position is controlled and the opponent cannot immediately recover.  [oai_citation:7‡Nw Open Guard V2 Ch4.docx](file-service://file-PMcS9iJNaSim2XpXT5i78Y)
- **Default Action**
  - At the end of the throw: chase chest-down alignment and hip-over-torso, then consolidate.
- **Drill Fix**
  - Sumi gaeshi reps where the rep is invalid unless you end chest-down with hips over torso and stabilize before reset.

---

### 11.9 FP9 — Wrong Lane for Opponent Posture (Standing Tools on Kneeling, or Vice Versa)
- **Signature**
  - You repeatedly attempt standing-lane entries when opponent is kneeling, or you insist on kneeling-lane attacks when opponent is upright and distancing.
- **Root Cause**
  - Posture gate not enforced before technique selection.
- **Correction**
  - Enforce posture gate:
    - Standing → legs/X-guard and distance reversals.
    - Kneeling → sumi gaeshi / upper-body triad / heist insert.
- **Default Action**
  - First question every exchange: “standing or kneeling?” then route immediately.
- **Drill Fix**
  - Call-out rounds: partner changes posture; you must name the hub out loud and execute one correct entry within 3 seconds.

---

### 11.10 FP10 — Dilemma Layer Not Used (Single-Channel Offense)
- **Signature**
  - You attack only legs or only upper-body; opponent stabilizes defenses and you stall.
- **Root Cause**
  - Failure to apply dilemmas as the control layer that forces reactions rather than “solving problems.” 
- **Correction**
  - Use dilemmas to force two bad outcomes; switch lanes on posture/defense changes rather than persisting in one channel. 
- **Default Action**
  - If opponent neutralizes your current lane for >2–3 seconds: switch via a dilemma (push/pull, under/over, upper/lower).
- **Drill Fix**
  - Constraint sparring: you must switch hubs at least once per minute; stalling resets the round.

## 13.0 TERMINOLOGY

### 13.1 System Terms

- **Hub**
  A central position/family (legs, X-guard, sumi gaeshi, heist/go-behind, upper-body triad) that many branches enter/exit.

- **Lane**
  A posture- and distance-dependent pathway through the system (standing lane, kneeling lane, distance lane, over-route/heist lane).

- **Dilemma**
  A forcing framework where the opponent must choose between two bad outcomes; used to route between hubs.

- **Control Layer**
  The dilemma set functioning as a routing and timing mechanism for the whole system.

- **Completion Doctrine**
  The system’s definition of “success,” especially: knockdown → waist capture → consolidation.

- **Reset**
  A preplanned return to a hub (often leg threat) when the current lane fails without positional loss.

---

### 13.2 Two-Phase / Scoring Terms

- **Two-Phase Reversal**
  Phase 1: create a knockdown (hands/hips/knee).  
  Phase 2: convert to a durable control (waist) and consolidate to score.

- **Phase 1 (Knockdown)**
  Any event where the opponent must base on hands, drop to hips/butt, or is forced to a knee.

- **Phase 2 (Waist Capture)**
  The finishing phase where you reach the waist/tight-waist control to prevent escape and consolidate top/back.

- **Waist-First Doctrine**
  Post-knockdown priority is the waist—not chasing the second leg.

- **Climb**
  A completion method where you “rope climb” from legs/hips to the waist using tight contact.

- **Shoot**
  A completion method where you angle-correct and “race” to the hips/waist when the opponent is separating.

- **Consolidation**
  The control step after waist capture: stopping opponent’s motion, establishing a stable top/back control.

- **Ear-to-Back (ETB)**
  Head/neck connection on the opponent’s back during waist control to prevent turning and to anchor scrambles.

- **Tight Waist**
  A locked, close waist grip used to control hips and prevent facing/re-square.

- **Shoulder-to-Mat**
  A consolidation action that reduces the opponent’s wrestling effectiveness during finishing sequences.

- **Go-Behind Scoring Window**
  The practical requirement to keep at least one knee down long enough to circle behind and consolidate.

---

### 13.3 Posture / Distance Terms

- **Standing Opponent**
  Opponent on feet; higher mobility; system routes primarily to legs/X-guard and distance reversals.

- **Kneeling Opponent**
  Opponent on one or two knees; system routes primarily to sumi gaeshi and upper-body triad, with heist/go-behind inserts.

- **Distance Dilemma**
  The idea that increased distance often improves your ability to rise/reverse, while crowding often improves leg entries.

- **Inside Position**
  Safer foot/leg alignment preference, especially relevant to leg-entry safety and X-guard access.

---

### 13.4 Position / Technique Family Terms

- **Ashi Garami (Ashi)**
  Leg entanglement family used as the primary “opening threat” and as a bridge to X-guard and reversals.

- **Irimi Ashi Garami**
  A square, safer entry protocol into leg connections that can bridge into X-guard.

- **X-Guard**
  A “between-the-knees” control platform used to off-balance and create knockdowns for two-phase completion.

- **Heist / Hip Heist**
  A stand-up/height-building movement from guard that converts bottom into front-head/go-behind and scoring opportunities.

- **Sumi Gaeshi**
  Butterfly-style sweep family treated as a principal attack on a kneeling opponent; success requires scramble win/consolidation.

- **Upper-Body Triad**
  The narrowed set of primary upper-body submissions used in the system: triangle, armbar, guillotine.

- **Triangle (Sankaku)**
  Choke system emphasizing elbow-line control; often executed as trap triangle → figure-four finish.

- **Juji Gatame**
  Armbar system; frequently linked to triangle threats and clamp-style control.

- **Guillotine**
  Front-headlock choke family; emphasized entry via football snap and finishing via high-wrist configuration.

- **Football Snap**
  A cross-centerline snap/catch mechanism to trap the head tightly and set up high-wrist guillotine depth.

- **High-Wrist Guillotine**
  Preferred finishing configuration emphasizing carotid coverage and reduced hand-fighting access.

- **Arm-In Guillotine**
  Guillotine variant used when the defender inserts an arm; can be used to finish or to force turn-outs/back exposure.

- **Arm Drag**
  Elbow-line redirection used to create angle and open go-behind/back exposure pathways.

- **Ashi Waza**
  Foot-sweep family used as stumble/knockdown creators that feed Phase 2 completion.

- **Tripod / Reverse Tripod / Outside Tripod**
  Knockdown families that are treated primarily as Phase 1 tools requiring Phase 2 waist completion.

- **Double Shin Sweep**
  Base-separation sweep used to create knockdowns that feed waist completion.

---

### 13.5 KD (Knockdown) Codes

- **KD-H**
  Hands-posted knockdown.

- **KD-HIP**
  Hips/butt-down knockdown.

- **KD-K**
  Knee-down event (one knee forced to mat).

## 14.0 ADDITIONAL NOTES

### 14.1 Prerequisite Assumption (Open Guard 1 Dependency)
Open Guard 2 assumes you already have:
- functional open-guard retention,
- the ability to maintain inside position without being flattened,
- baseline leg-entry literacy (enough to threaten, not necessarily to finish),
- and the ability to sit up and hand-fight without immediately conceding passes.

If these are missing, Open Guard 2 becomes “technique rich, conversion poor.”

---

### 14.2 What This System Is (and Is Not)
- **Is:** a no-gi open-guard scoring system built around knockdowns that become waist control.
- **Is not:** a “catalog of sweeps” where the sweep itself is considered a complete outcome.

The primary competency is conversion and consolidation.

---

### 14.3 The Real Skill Stack (Practical Order of Learning)
If you learn in the wrong order, you get lots of motion and few scores.

Recommended order:
1) Two-phase completion (waist capture + consolidation)
2) X-guard knockdowns
3) Sumi gaeshi core + scramble win
4) Hip heist → go-behind conversion
5) Upper-body triad entries + exit rules
6) Dilemma routing under resistance

---

### 14.4 Coaching Heuristics (Fast Correctives)
- If you hear: “I swept him but didn’t get on top,” translate as: “Phase 2 failed.”
- If you see: “hands on mat and athlete pauses,” treat as a major system error.
- If you see: “chasing second leg,” intervene immediately; enforce waist-first.

---

### 14.5 Match Strategy Notes (When to Bias Toward Which Hub)
- If opponent is aggressively standing and disengaging: bias to **distance reversals**.
- If opponent crowds and drives in: bias to **legs/X-guard entries**.
- If opponent kneels to hide legs: bias to **sumi gaeshi + upper-body triad**.
- If head posture repeatedly breaks: bias to **heist/go-behind + guillotine pairing**.

---

### 14.6 “System Integrity” Rules (Avoid Dilution)
To keep the system coherent:
- Do not add additional submission families unless they improve routing.
- Do not add sweeps that end without a predictable conversion to waist/top/back.
- Do not overinvest in transitory grips; they exist to trigger the next connection.

---

### 14.7 Integration With a Wider Game
Open Guard 2 integrates cleanly with:
- leg-attack systems (as an opener and as a reset),
- front headlock systems (as an “over route” conversion),
- wrestling-based top games (because the finish target is waist/back).

It also naturally supports a “score-first” style that later opens submissions.

---

### 14.8 Common Misread (Why People Underperform This Material)
The common misread is treating the instruction as “guard technique.”
It should be treated as “guard-to-score conversion,” where:
- the knockdown is a checkpoint,
- the waist is the real finish,
- and consolidation is non-negotiable.

---

### 14.9 Suggested Implementation Constraint (One Rule That Fixes Most Problems)
For 2–4 weeks, adopt this hard rule in training:
- “If you create a knockdown, you must reach waist control before you may attempt any other control or submission.”

This single constraint rapidly aligns training behavior with the system.
