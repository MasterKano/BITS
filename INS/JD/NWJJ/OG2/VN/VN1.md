## 3.0 VOL 1 — CHAPTER NOTES (Expanded + Index Tags)

### 3.1 Chapter 1.1 — Introduction (0:00–11:04)
**Index tags:** LOWER_BODY_INITIATIVE, RESET_LOGIC, LANE_SWITCH

**Purpose**
- Define NW no-gi open guard as a forcing system that manufactures reactions and converts them into scoring (top/back) and finishing opportunities.

**Core claims**
- No-gi open guard should create initiative, not merely defend.
- Legs/feet are the most reliable always-available threatening surface.
- Attacks should be evaluated by conversion quality, not by visual “sweep success.”

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you are not forcing reaction, THEN adjust distance/attachments until you are.
- IF opponent begins stable pass-building, THEN re-create instability first (movement + threat) before attempting a “move.”

**Common failure modes**
- Static guard → opponent disengages and passes in stages.
- Early upper-body hunting without forcing posture change → low yield.

**Training implication**
- Practice “reaction creation” as a skill: feet engagement + distance management + forcing cues.

**Links forward**
- Sets up the logic later operationalized by Volume 2–3 (reversals/X-guard) and formalized in Volume 8 (dilemmas).

### 3.2 Chapter 1.2 — Three Key Insights (11:04–40:18)
**Index tags:** CONTROL_SUBSTITUTION, TRANSITORY_TO_FIRM, TWO_PHASE_ENGINE, WAIST_FIRST

**Insight 1: Control substitution**
- Replace durable gi control groups with no-gi reliable control groups: legs/hips/waist.

**Insight 2: Grip/connection sequencing**
- Transitory ties create openings; firm leg connections create knockdowns; waist control completes outcomes.

**Insight 3: Conversion bias**
- Sweeps are frequently Phase 1; Phase 2 conversion is the differentiator at high level.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF knockdown occurs, THEN waist hunt begins immediately.
- IF grip is transitory and progress stalls (seconds), THEN upgrade connection or reset.

**Common failure modes**
- “Sweep admiration pause” after knockdown.
- Overholding weak grips waiting for them to become strong.

**Training implication**
- Training time must bias toward conversion modules (waist + consolidation), not only entries.

### 3.3 Chapter 1.3 — Gi vs No-Gi Differences (40:18–1:11:28)
**Index tags:** CONTROL_SUBSTITUTION, TRANSITORY_TO_FIRM, CONSOLIDATION, RESET_LOGIC

**Key points**
- No-gi completion windows are shorter because grips degrade faster.
- You often must “bring yourself to them” rather than pull them into you.
- Strong outcomes require faster upgrades to durable control.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF your plan requires holding a single hand grip to finish, THEN assume it fails against strong opponents; seek control upgrade.
- IF opponent is regaining feet/hips, THEN reconnect to legs or switch lanes immediately; do not chase late.

**Common failure modes**
- Using gi tempo and gi expectations (slow build, static ties).
- Finishing sweeps in positions that don’t stabilize (no durable control).

---

### 3.4 Chapter 1.4 — Distance Dilemma (1:11:28–1:18:31)
**Index tags:** DISTANCE_DILEMMA, LANE_SWITCH, LOWER_BODY_INITIATIVE

**Core model**
- Retreating opponent to avoid legs opens seated reversals/come-ups.
- Crowding opponent to stop reversals opens leg entries and off-balances.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent stays far, THEN attack with seated reversals and/or close distance with feet.
- IF opponent crowds, THEN attack with legs/entries/off-balances.
- IF distance behavior flips, THEN switch lanes immediately (seconds, not minutes).

**Common failure modes**
- Fighting to keep one preferred distance rather than exploiting the opponent’s choice.
- Missing the reversal lane when opponent retreats.

---

### 3.5 Chapter 1.5 — Seated Shots vs Standing Shots (1:18:31–1:33:24)
**Index tags:** NO_HEAD_SHOTS, DISTANCE_DILEMMA, TRANSITORY_TO_FIRM

**Core constraints**
- Guard-based shots are not classic wrestling shots: head-first commitment carries submission risk.
- Structure (posts, angles, connection) replaces head-drive.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF finishing requires deep head commitment, THEN change entry/angle; do not accept head-first risk.
- IF you can brace/post, THEN entries become safe and repeatable.

**Common failure modes**
- Reaching without base.
- Head-first commitment leading to front-head/submission danger.

---

### 3.6 Chapter 1.6 — 3-Step Pattern of Wrestling Reversals (1:33:24+)
**Index tags:** TWO_PHASE_ENGINE, KNOCKDOWN_TYPES, WAIST_FIRST, CLIMB_VS_SHOOT, CONSOLIDATION

**Template**
- Step 1: Connection that destabilizes stance.
- Step 2: Knockdown event (hands/hips).
- Step 3: Immediate conversion to waist and consolidation.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF knockdown occurs, THEN Step 3 begins immediately; no pause.
- IF knockdown cannot be created, THEN adjust Step 1 or switch lanes.
- IF opponent separates during conversion, THEN SHOOT; if sticky contact exists, THEN CLIMB.

**Common failure modes**
- Creating knockdowns without conversion.
- Chasing second leg instead of waist.
- Failing to consolidate after waist capture.

## 4.0 VOL 1 — ONE-PAGE RULESET (Extracted Non-Negotiables)

These rules are intended to be copy/paste front matter for training constraints.

R1 — LOWER BODY INITIATIVE
- IF you want initiative from open guard, THEN begin with lower-body threat.

R2 — POSTURE FIRST
- IF opponent is standing, THEN standing-lane solutions.
- IF opponent is kneeling, THEN kneeling-lane solutions.

R3 — DISTANCE DILEMMA
- IF opponent distances, THEN seated reversals/come-ups become the primary lane.
- IF opponent crowds, THEN leg engagement becomes the primary lane.

R4 — TRANSITORY GRIPS ARE TEMPORARY
- IF grip is transitory and does not improve position within seconds, THEN upgrade or reset.

R5 — TWO-PHASE ENGINE
- IF you want consistent scoring reversals, THEN treat sweeps as Phase 1 and conversions as Phase 2.

R6 — KNOCKDOWN IS NOT THE FINISH
- IF hands or hips touch the mat, THEN the “real work” starts now.

R7 — WAIST FIRST
- IF a knockdown occurs, THEN hunt waist control (not the second leg).

R8 — CLIMB VS SHOOT
- IF contact is close/sticky, THEN CLIMB to waist.
- IF opponent separates/limp-legs, THEN SHOOT to waist.

R9 — CONSOLIDATION IS REQUIRED
- IF you capture the waist, THEN consolidate before advancing (tight waist + head tether concept + breakdown).

R10 — NO-HEAD SHOTS (Guard Safety)
- IF your shot requires head-first commitment, THEN it is a bad selection from guard; change entry/angle.

R11 — LANE SWITCHING
- IF opponent changes distance/posture, THEN you must switch lanes quickly; stalling is failure.

R12 — RESET LOGIC
- IF conversion fails without positional loss, THEN reset to lower-body threat rather than wrestling a dead position.

## 5.0 VOL 1 — DRILL MAP (Rule → Drill Prescription)

Each drill has: start, constraint, win condition, fail condition.

### D1 — Reaction Creation Rounds (R1)
- **Start:** open guard, neutral.
- **Constraint:** you must force a visible reaction within 5 seconds using legs/feet.
- **Win:** opponent’s posture/distance changes or they post/retreat.
- **Fail:** static grip fighting; opponent freely settles to pass.

### D2 — Posture Gate Call-Out (R2)
- **Start:** partner alternates standing/kneeling every 10–15 seconds.
- **Constraint:** you must say “standing lane” or “kneeling lane” and enter correct hub within 3 seconds.
- **Win:** correct lane entry.
- **Fail:** using wrong-lane technique.

### D3 — Distance Dilemma Switch Drill (R3, R11)
- **Start:** opponent cycles far ↔ crowded on coach cue.
- **Constraint:** you must switch attack family on every cue.
- **Win:** appropriate lane engaged each time.
- **Fail:** continuing prior lane for more than 3 seconds after cue.

### D4 — Transitory Grip Timer (R4)
- **Start:** any upper-body tie or ankle grip from seated guard.
- **Constraint:** within 2–3 seconds you must either (a) enter legs, (b) create knockdown, or (c) reset.
- **Win:** upgrade achieved.
- **Fail:** grip breaks and you lose posture/initiative.

### D5 — Two-Phase Sprint (R5, R6, R7)
- **Start:** partner gives you a Phase 1 knockdown (KD-H or KD-HIP) from any entry.
- **Constraint:** you have 3 seconds to begin waist hunt.
- **Win:** tight waist achieved.
- **Fail:** pause, second-leg chasing, or loss of contact.

### D6 — Climb vs Shoot Selector (R8)
- **Start:** after knockdown; partner alternates “sticky” vs “separating” escape styles.
- **Constraint:** you must choose the correct method (climb on sticky; shoot on separating).
- **Win:** waist achieved with correct method.
- **Fail:** wrong selection or missed waist.

### D7 — Consolidation Lock-In (R9)
- **Start:** you have waist control; opponent’s goal is to face/stand.
- **Constraint:** you cannot advance position until you stop their movement for 3 seconds.
- **Win:** movement stopped (stable top/back).
- **Fail:** opponent re-squares/re-guards.

### D8 — No-Head Shot Constraint Rounds (R10)
- **Start:** seated reversal entries.
- **Constraint:** any head-first commitment is an automatic reset.
- **Win:** clean entry using posts/angles.
- **Fail:** head dive or front-head exposure.

### D9 — Reset Discipline (R12)
- **Start:** you miss a conversion (knockdown but no waist).
- **Constraint:** you must immediately reset to lower-body threat (no stubborn wrestling).
- **Win:** re-establish primary threat within 5 seconds.
- **Fail:** extended neutral scramble without initiative.
