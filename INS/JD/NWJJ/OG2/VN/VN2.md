## 3.0 VOL 2 — CHAPTER NOTES (Best Version: Tags + Decision Rules + Failure Modes + Drill Hooks)

### 3.1 Chapter 2.1 — Ankle Picks (0:00–7:45)
**Index tags:** WRESTLING_REVERSALS_FROM_GUARD, INSIDE_GRIP_PREFERENCE, TOES_DIRECTION_RULE, POST_HAND_BRACE, NO_HEAD_SHOTS

**Primary use**
- High-availability seated reversal when opponent is standing and keeping distance.

**Entry conditions**
- You can touch the ankle line without diving head-first.
- You can establish a stable post/brace hand to support the pull.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Use bracing/posting for leverage (not head drive).
- Prefer safer grip alignments when head control is not available.
- Pull in the direction the toes point (reduces friction; improves finish).

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent gives long stance and retreat energy, THEN ankle pick is high value.
- IF ankle pick starts but opponent retracts hard, THEN transition to knee pull or switch-knee pull rather than “reaching longer.”
- IF knockdown occurs, THEN immediate Phase 2 (waist) begins.

**Common failure modes**
- Reaching without a base → you get flattened.
- Pulling “across” toe direction → friction stalls the finish.
- Knockdown achieved but you pause or chase the far leg → no score.

**Drill hook**
- “Ankle pick → KD → 3-second waist” (pass/fail).

### 3.2 Chapter 2.2 — Knee Pull (7:45–12:16)
**Index tags:** WRESTLING_REVERSALS_FROM_GUARD, KNEE_TENDON_HANDLE, POST_HAND_BRACE, NO_HEAD_SHOTS, TWO_PHASE_ENGINE

**Primary use**
- Destabilize stance via inside knee line; often higher reliability than ankle line under resistance.

**Entry conditions**
- Access to inside knee line (tendon “handle”) and enough angle to pull without head commitment.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Precision: inside knee-tendon grip is the “sticky handle.”
- Short, compact pulling mechanics (avoid extended arms).
- Convert immediately to knockdown rather than tug-of-war.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF inside knee line is available, THEN knee pull is preferred.
- IF opponent rotates to save the knee, THEN switch-knee pull becomes primary (next chapter).
- IF opponent squares stance to defend, THEN double leg often rises in value.

**Common failure modes**
- Grabbing too high/too general (hamstring) → slips under rotation.
- Pulling with long arms → breaks posture and loses base.
- Failure to convert to waist after KD.

**Drill hook**
- “Knee pull ladder: right knee → left knee → finish to waist.”

---

### 3.3 Chapter 2.3 — Switching Knee Pull (12:16–16:19)
**Index tags:** KNEE_TENDON_HANDLE, LANE_SWITCH, RESET_LOGIC, TWO_PHASE_ENGINE

**Primary use**
- Solve the opponent’s primary knee-pull defense: turning/retracting one knee by switching to the other.

**Entry conditions**
- Opponent rotates or retracts one knee to escape.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Switch fast; do not persist on a defended knee.
- Maintain your base while switching (post-hand discipline).

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent turns out to save one knee, THEN switch-knee pull immediately.
- IF switching causes opponent to square, THEN transition to double leg.

**Common failure modes**
- Slow switch (you chase what’s leaving).
- Switching without base; you get compressed.

**Drill hook**
- “Partner rotates every rep → you must switch knees on cue → finish to waist.”

---

### 3.4 Chapter 2.4 — Double Leg (16:19–26:21)
**Index tags:** SQUARE_STANCE_TRIGGERS_DOUBLE, POST_HAND_BRACE, NO_HEAD_SHOTS, WAIST_FIRST, CONSOLIDATION

**Primary use**
- Highest value reversal when opponent’s stance squares and knees align.

**Entry conditions**
- Opponent squared; both legs accessible enough to connect without head-first drive.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Secure both legs with compact arms; avoid overextension.
- Use angle and posts, not head drive.
- Think “get them down, then get waist,” not “finish like wrestling.”

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent squares to defeat picks/pulls, THEN double leg becomes the default.
- IF you knock them down but they scramble up, THEN immediately shoot/climb to waist.

**Common failure modes**
- Treating it like a classic wrestling penetration step with deep head commitment.
- Stalling on legs after knockdown; opponent limp-legs out.

**Drill hook**
- “Double leg entry → KD-HIP → shoot to waist” (separating escape scenario).

---

### 3.5 Chapter 2.5 — Arm Drag (26:21–32:36)
**Index tags:** ARM_DRAG_ANGLE, OVER_ROUTE_INSERT, GO_BEHIND_PATH, RESET_LOGIC

**Primary use**
- Create angle and expose behind-the-elbows/hips pathways; also a bridge into heist/go-behind logic.

**Entry conditions**
- Opponent’s arm reaches/hand fights forward; elbow line becomes accessible.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Drag elbow past your hip line (angle creation).
- Immediate body angle change; do not “drag and stay square.”

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF elbow line is extended, THEN arm drag is a high-speed angle tool.
- IF arm drag creates behind access, THEN route to go-behind/waist (do not return to neutral).
- IF arm retracts hard, THEN return to legs or seated reversal lane immediately.

**Common failure modes**
- Dragging with no angle change.
- Holding the arm instead of using it to create behind position.

**Drill hook**
- “Arm drag → angle change → touch waist” (time-limited).

---

### 3.6 Chapter 2.6 — Ashi Waza / Foot Sweeps (32:36–39:15)
**Index tags:** ASHI_WAZA_TIMING, KNOCKDOWN_TYPES, TWO_PHASE_ENGINE

**Primary use**
- Timing-based knockdown creation (stumbles) that feed two-phase conversions.

**Entry conditions**
- Opponent stepping patterns are readable; you can align sweep with step timing.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Sweep into the step; do not chase the foot after it lands.
- Maintain connection so the stumble becomes a knockdown event.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent is light on feet and hard to “catch,” THEN sweeps become a strong Phase 1 creator.
- IF sweep yields only a stumble, THEN treat it as KD-K potential and immediately rise to waist.

**Common failure modes**
- Sweeping late (after weight is already placed).
- Losing connection so you get a stumble but no conversion.

**Drill hook**
- “Step-pattern drill: partner steps; you must create KD-K then waist.”

### 3.7 Chapter 2.7 — Reversals on a Kneeling Opponent (39:15–52:00)
**Index tags:** POSTURE_GATE, KNEELING_OPPONENT, REVERSAL_ADAPTATIONS, TWO_PHASE_ENGINE

**Primary use**
- Adapt seated reversal logic when the opponent reduces height by kneeling (often to hide legs).

**Core concept**
- Even with knees down, reversal logic can apply: destabilize base → create knockdown → convert to control.
- In practice, this chapter bridges Vol 2 reversals into the kneeling lane that Vol 4 will dominate with sumi gaeshi.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent kneels to hide legs, THEN:
  - use kneeling-compatible reversals OR
  - switch to kneeling hubs (sumi/upper body) when available.
- IF you can force a knee-down / hand-post event, THEN treat it as Phase 1 and go to waist.

**Common failure modes**
- Using standing-distance reversal shapes on a kneeling base (wrong geometry).
- Creating movement but not converting to control.

**Drill hook**
- “Kneeling opponent reversal: start with opponent on knees; goal = KD + waist.”

### 3.8 Chapter 2.8 — Two-Phase Reversals (52:00–56:13)
**Index tags:** TWO_PHASE_ENGINE, KNOCKDOWN_TYPES, WAIST_FIRST, CLIMB_VS_SHOOT

**Purpose**
- Formalize the engine: Phase 1 knockdown is deliberately used to start Phase 2 conversion.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF Phase 1 happens, THEN Phase 2 must begin immediately.
- IF opponent separates, THEN shoot; IF sticky, THEN climb.
- IF you chase second leg, THEN accept failure at high level.

**Failure modes**
- “One-phase mindset” (trying to finish the sweep itself).
- “Leg collecting” instead of waist finishing.

**Drill hook**
- “KD randomizer: partner gives KD-H or KD-HIP; you must choose climb vs shoot correctly.”

### 3.9 Chapter 2.9 — Thought Experiment (56:13–1:11:49)
**Index tags:** TWO_PHASE_ENGINE, OUTLOOK_SHIFT, CONSOLIDATION, WAIST_FIRST

**Purpose**
- Reframe no-gi sweeps/reversals as a geometry advantage: you engineer an “unfair” starting position for the scramble.

**Operational takeaway**
- Phase 1 is designed to put the opponent in a compromised base state while you are in a position to rise with access to hips/waist.
- The “win condition” is not the knockdown; it is the inability of the opponent to regain feet/hips before you secure control.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you do not have a conversion plan, THEN the sweep choice is premature.
- IF a sweep reliably produces KD but not waist, THEN fix conversion rather than replacing the sweep.

**Drill hook**
- “Conversion-only rounds” (no new moves allowed; only improve Phase 2 until it scores).

**Index tags:** TRIPOD_FAMILY, KNOCKDOWN_TYPES, WAIST_FIRST

**Primary use**
- Phase 1 knockdown creation against a standing base.

**Non-negotiable mechanics (conceptual)**
- Tripod is treated as a knockdown generator; connection must persist long enough to rise.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF tripod produces KD-H, THEN heist immediately to waist.
- IF tripod produces only a stumble, THEN treat it as KD-K and rise.

**Common failure modes**
- Treating tripod as a finish and pausing.
- Losing connection during the knockdown.

**Drill hook**
- “Tripod → KD-H → waist” with a 3-second conversion constraint.

**Primary use**
- Backward/hip-based knockdown (KD-HIP) that often creates a race condition.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF hips/butt down occurs, THEN win the race to waist before they spring up.
- IF separation happens quickly, THEN shoot; if you are glued, then climb.

**Common failure modes**
- Accepting “they sat down” as success.
- Chasing far leg while they stand.

**Drill hook**
- “Reverse tripod → KD-HIP → shoot-to-waist” (separating escape scenario).

**Index tags:** DOUBLE_LEG_ASHI, LEG_CONNECTION_STABILITY, KD_CREATION, RESET_LOGIC

**Primary use**
- Stabilize both legs to reduce escape options and create a controlled knockdown pathway.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent is too athletic to control on single-leg connections, THEN double-leg ashi stabilizers become high value.
- IF opponent clears, THEN reset to reversal lane (picks/pulls/doubles) or transition to X-guard lane (Vol 3).

**Common failure modes**
- Over-investing in leg collection without moving toward knockdown/conversion.
- Losing base while re-pummeling legs.

**Drill hook**

## 4.0 VOL 2 — ONE-PAGE RULESET (Non-Negotiables)

R1 — DISTANCE = REVERSAL LANE
- IF opponent is standing and keeping distance, THEN seated reversals are primary.

R2 — NO-HEAD SHOTS
- IF a reversal requires head-first commitment, THEN change entry/angle; guard shots must be posture-safe.

R3 — POST-HAND DISCIPLINE
- IF you are pulling/picking from seated, THEN you must build base (post/brace) or you will be flattened.

R4 — INSIDE HANDLE PRIORITY
- IF inside knee/inside lines are available, THEN they are preferred because they remain sticky through rotation.

R5 — TOES-DIRECTION RULE
- IF you are finishing an ankle-line pull, THEN pull in the direction the toes point.

R6 — SQUARE STANCE → DOUBLE
- IF opponent squares stance to defend picks/pulls, THEN route to double-leg entries.

R7 — KNOCKDOWN TYPES ARE CHECKPOINTS
- IF KD-H / KD-HIP / KD-K occurs, THEN Phase 2 is now mandatory.

R8 — WAIST FIRST
- IF knockdown occurs, THEN waist is the finish target; do not chase second leg by default.

R9 — CLIMB VS SHOOT
- IF sticky/close contact, THEN climb to waist.
- IF separation/limp-leg, THEN shoot to waist.

R10 — KNOCKDOWN FAMILIES ARE PHASE 1 TOOLS
- Tripod/double shin/ashi-waza are validated only by how reliably they lead to waist conversion.

R11 — FIX CONVERSION BEFORE REPLACING MOVES
- IF a technique creates knockdowns but doesn’t score, fix Phase 2 first.

## 5.0 VOL 2 — DRILL MAP (Rule → Drill Prescription)

### D1 — Post-Hand Pick Series (R2, R3)
- **Start:** seated guard, opponent standing at distance.
- **Constraint:** any pick/pull must include a brace/post hand; no head-first drive.
- **Win:** knockdown created without losing posture.
- **Fail:** you get flattened or forced to turtle/front head.

### D2 — Ankle Pick Direction Drill (R5)
- **Start:** ankle line grip; opponent gives light resistance.
- **Constraint:** finish must follow toe direction; wrong direction = reset.
- **Win:** clean KD event.
- **Fail:** friction stall, grip breaks.

### D3 — Knee Tendon Ladder (R4)
- **Start:** inside knee access; partner alternates rotations.
- **Constraint:** you must maintain “handle” precision and switch as needed.
- **Win:** KD created within time limit.
- **Fail:** handle slips; you reach long.

### D4 — Square-to-Double Trigger Rounds (R6)
- **Start:** partner alternates staggered stance and squared stance.
- **Constraint:** you must call the trigger (“square”) and immediately enter double.
- **Win:** double entry → KD.
- **Fail:** you persist with pick/pull into square.

### D5 — KD Randomizer → Waist (R7, R8, R9)
- **Start:** partner gives KD-H or KD-HIP (random).
- **Constraint:** 3 seconds to start waist hunt; choose climb vs shoot correctly.
- **Win:** tight waist achieved and held for 3 seconds.
- **Fail:** pause, second-leg chase, missed waist.

### D6 — Tripod Family Conversion (R10)
- **Start:** tripod / reverse tripod / outside tripod entries.
- **Constraint:** rep only counts if it ends in waist control (not knockdown alone).
- **Win:** KD → waist → stabilize.
- **Fail:** knockdown with no conversion.

### D7 — “Fix Conversion First” Constraint Rounds (R11)
- **Start:** any technique that regularly creates KD but “doesn’t score” for you.
- **Constraint:** you are prohibited from changing the technique for the round; only improve Phase 2.
- **Win:** conversion rate increases within the session.
- **Fail:** you abandon technique instead of fixing conversion.
