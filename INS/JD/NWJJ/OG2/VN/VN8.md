## 3.0 VOL 8 — CHAPTER NOTES (Best Version)

### 3.1 5 Dilemmas That Will Change Your Guard Game - 1. The Push/Pull Dilemma (0:00 - 17:13)
**Index tags:** PUSH_PULL_DILEMMA, REACTION_FORCING, READ_AND_ROUTE

**Purpose**
- Install the most universal standing posture “sensor”: push vs pull creates immediate posture/weight reactions that select your next action.

**Operational model**
- Push and pull are not “moves.” They are tests that force weight distribution and stepping responses.
- Your job is to recognize which side of the dilemma the opponent chooses (or is forced into) and route accordingly.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent’s weight is committed backward (heels / recoil), THEN “push” outcomes (backward knockdowns / off-balances / entries that capitalize on retreat) rise in value.
- IF opponent’s weight is committed forward (toes / drive), THEN “pull” outcomes (drawing forward into posts, snaps, go-behinds, leg connections) rise in value.
- IF your first push/pull attempt produces no meaningful reaction, THEN your setup is wrong (connection, angle, or timing), not “the dilemma.”

**Common failure modes**
- Forcing your favorite direction regardless of the opponent’s weight state.
- Confusing “motion” with “reaction” (they move, but not in a way that opens a hub).

**Drill hook**
- “Heels vs toes read drill”: partner changes weight bias; you must call “heels/toes” and select the matching route within 2 seconds.

### 3.2 2. Under/Over Dilemma (17:13 - 23:09)
**Index tags:** UNDER_OVER_DILEMMA, HUB_ROUTING, SYSTEM_INTEGRITY

**Purpose**
- Establish a second universal sensor: under vs over positioning creates predictable counters and predictable openings.

**Operational model**
- Under routes tend to create lifting, elevation, and inside-line access.
- Over routes tend to create wrapping, head/arm controls, and go-behind opportunities (especially as the opponent turns or posts).

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent blocks under routes by heavy downward pressure and frames, THEN over routes become easier (wrap, snap, angle, behind).
- IF opponent blocks over routes by retracting arms/keeping posture tight, THEN under routes become easier (inside legs, elevation entries, base separation).
- IF you commit to one side (under or over) and it stalls for seconds, THEN switch to the other side before the opponent stabilizes.

**Common failure modes**
- Persisting with under when frames are fully established.
- Persisting with over when the opponent has fully retracted and denied contact.
- Treating under/over as “techniques,” rather than as routing choices that must switch.

**Drill hook**
- “Under/over alternation rounds”: you must attempt one under-route and one over-route every 20 seconds; score only if you convert to a hub entry.

### 3.3 3. Contraction/Extension Dilemma (23:09 - 29:00)
**Index tags:** CONTRACTION_EXTENSION_DILEMMA, LANE_SWITCH, READ_AND_ROUTE

**Purpose**
- Formalize distance as a dilemma: crowding (contraction) and retreating (extension) each open different hubs.

**Operational model**
- Contraction/crowding often suppresses clean stand-ups but opens leg engagement and close-range off-balancing.
- Extension/retreat often denies leg contact but opens come-ups, seated reversals, and standing-up threats.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent contracts (crowds), THEN prioritize leg engagement hubs and close-range knockdown creation.
- IF opponent extends (retreats), THEN prioritize seated reversals and heist/come-up threats.
- IF opponent oscillates between crowding and retreating, THEN your success is defined by switching lanes quickly, not by “winning” one lane.

**Common failure modes**
- Treating distance as a “comfort preference” rather than a routing signal.
- Continuing to attack the same hub when the opponent’s distance choice makes it low-probability.

**Drill hook**
- “Contraction/extension cue rounds”: partner cycles crowd/retreat; you must switch hubs on each cue within 3 seconds.

### 3.4 4. Lower Body/Position Dilemma (29:00 - 36:08)
**Index tags:** LOWER_BODY_POSITION_DILEMMA, TWO_PHASE_ENGINE, COMPLETION_BIAS

**Purpose**
- Clarify a critical trade: chasing lower-body attacks vs taking positional advancement/score. The system’s answer is not “either/or,” but “threat creates movement; position completes.”

**Operational model**
- Lower-body threats are frequently the best way to force reactions.
- Positional control is frequently the best way to secure scoring permanence.
- This dilemma is resolved by recognizing when the opponent’s defense gives you positional completion (waist/behind) and taking it.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF lower-body threat forces the opponent into posts, knees-down, or compromised base, THEN shift immediately to positional completion (waist/behind) rather than staying on the legs.
- IF positional completion is not available (no knockdown, no behind access), THEN return to lower-body threats to force the next reaction.
- IF you are “winning” the leg battle but not scoring, THEN you are failing the lower-body/position dilemma (you’re not cashing out reactions).

**Common failure modes**
- Over-investing in leg attacks when the opponent is already giving up behind-the-hips access.
- Abandoning leg threats too early before the opponent reacts.

**Drill hook**
- “Cash-out constraint”: you may initiate lower-body threats freely, but you may only score the round by waist/behind consolidation.

### 3.5 5. Upper Body/Lower Body Dilemma (36:08 - 42:31)
**Index tags:** UPPER_BODY_LOWER_BODY_DILEMMA, REACTION_FORCING, HUB_ROUTING

**Purpose**
- Install the final major routing sensor: upper-body threats and lower-body threats each force defenses that expose the other.

**Operational model**
- Lower-body threats pull attention downward and often create arm extension, head drop, and posting.
- Upper-body threats often cause posture retreat, arm retraction, and disengagement—opening legs and come-ups.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent commits resources to defending legs (hands low, hips back, posture changes), THEN look for upper-body entries (triangle/guillotine/go-behind windows).
- IF opponent commits resources to defending upper body (posture up, arms retract, disengage), THEN re-enter lower-body threats and/or come-up lanes.
- IF one lane is defended hard, THEN the other should be attempted quickly; “staying in the defended lane” is the error.

**Common failure modes**
- Treating upper-body attacks as a separate mini-game (no resets back to legs).
- Treating lower-body attacks as the only initiative (missing head/elbow windows created by defense).

**Drill hook**
- “Upper/lower alternation rounds”: coach calls “upper” or “lower” on the fly; you must create a credible threat in that lane within 3 seconds, then switch when opponent reacts.

### 3.6 Guard Play: Putting it All Together (42:31 +)
**Index tags:** DILEMMA_CONTROL_LAYER, HUB_ROUTING, SYSTEM_INTEGRITY, TWO_PHASE_ENGINE

**Purpose**
- Integrate dilemmas into full-system operation: not collecting techniques, but operating a routing engine that leads to reliable completion.

**Operational workflow (default loop)**
1) Establish primary threat (often lower body).
2) Force a dilemma and observe the opponent’s choice.
3) Route to the hub that punishes that choice.
4) Create knockdown/post event.
5) Convert immediately to waist/behind control.
6) Consolidate briefly to stabilize score.
7) Reset to primary threat if completion fails cleanly.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you are stalled, THEN you are missing a dilemma; create one and switch hubs.
- IF you got a knockdown or post event, THEN you must cash out to waist/behind—do not admire the moment.
- IF you are switching hubs but not scoring, THEN your Phase 2 completion is the bottleneck (return to conversion modules).

**Common failure modes**
- Technique hopping with no dilemma logic (randomness).
- Dilemma creation without follow-through (you force a reaction, but fail to route/complete).

**Drill hook**
- “Full-system routing rounds”: each exchange must include (a) forced dilemma, (b) hub switch, and (c) attempt at completion. No exchange may remain in one lane for more than ~3 seconds without switching.

## 4.0 VOL 8 — ONE-PAGE RULESET (Non-Negotiables)

R1 — DILEMMAS ARE THE CONTROL LAYER
- IF you want reliable outcomes, THEN force dilemmas rather than “solving problems” directly.

R2 — PUSH/PULL IS A SENSOR
- IF weight is on heels → punish backward bias.
- IF weight is on toes → punish forward bias.

R3 — UNDER/OVER MUST SWITCH
- IF under is blocked → go over.
- IF over is blocked → go under.
(Time-based switching; do not persist in a dead lane.)

R4 — CONTRACTION/EXTENSION ROUTES HUBS
- IF opponent crowds → legs/close hubs.
- IF opponent retreats → reversals/heist/come-ups.

R5 — LOWER BODY CREATES, POSITION CASHES OUT
- IF lower-body threat creates a post/knockdown → take waist/behind and consolidate.

R6 — UPPER/LOWER RECIPROCITY
- IF one lane is defended hard, THEN the other lane is likely open; switch quickly.

R7 — COMPLETION BIAS
- IF you get a knockdown/post window, THEN waist/behind completion is mandatory.

R8 — SYSTEM INTEGRITY
- IF you add techniques, they must improve routing and/or completion. If they do not, they dilute the system.

## 5.0 VOL 8 — DRILL MAP (Rule → Drill Prescription)

### D1 — Push/Pull Read-and-Route (R2)
- **Start:** opponent standing; you have light contact.
- **Constraint:** you must call “heels” or “toes” and initiate a matching route within 2 seconds.
- **Win:** visible reaction + correct route choice.
- **Fail:** forcing a preferred route regardless of weight bias.

### D2 — Under/Over Switch Timer (R3)
- **Start:** seated guard with hand-fighting.
- **Constraint:** if your chosen route stalls for 3 seconds, you must switch under↔over.
- **Win:** hub entry achieved after the switch.
- **Fail:** persisting in the defended route.

### D3 — Contraction/Extension Lane Switch (R4)
- **Start:** partner alternates crowd/retreat on cue.
- **Constraint:** you must switch hubs within 3 seconds of each change.
- **Win:** correct hub engaged each cycle.
- **Fail:** attacking a hub that the distance choice has made low-probability.

### D4 — Cash-Out Constraint Rounds (R5, R7)
- **Start:** open guard, live.
- **Constraint:** you may threaten legs/upper freely, but you only score by waist/behind stabilization (3 seconds).
- **Win:** conversion to waist/behind + stabilization.
- **Fail:** repeated reactions created but not cashed out.

### D5 — Upper/Lower Alternation (R6)
- **Start:** live open guard.
- **Constraint:** coach calls “upper” or “lower”; you must create a credible threat in that lane within 3 seconds.
- **Win:** opponent reacts in the called lane.
- **Fail:** no credible threat or wrong-lane persistence.

### D6 — Full-System Routing Round (R1–R7)
- **Start:** live open guard.
- **Constraint:** each exchange must include:
  1) a dilemma forced,
  2) a hub switch,
  3) an attempt to complete (waist/behind) if a post/knockdown appears.
- **Win:** clean routed completion.
- **Fail:** technique hopping without dilemma logic, or dilemma without completion.
