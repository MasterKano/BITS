## 3.0 VOL 4 — CHAPTER NOTES (Best Version)

### 3.1 Sumi Gaeshi - Sumi Gaeshi as the Principal Attack On a Keeling Opponent (0:00 - 3:48)
**Index tags:** KNEELING_LANE, SUMI_GAESHI_HUB, POSTURE_GATE

**Primary use**
- Default scoring hub when opponent kneels (often as a leg-hiding strategy).

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent drops to one/two knees to hide legs, THEN sumi gaeshi becomes the primary attack family.
- IF opponent’s hands/arms are available for control, THEN commit quickly once the correct connection exists (no grip procrastination).

**Common failure modes**
- Treating kneeling opponent like standing opponent (wrong geometry).
- Waiting too long after obtaining the correct tie; opponent stabilizes base.

**Drill hook**
- “Kneeling trigger drill”: partner kneels on cue; you must enter a sumi connection and begin mechanics within 3 seconds.

---

### 3.2 The Mechanics of Sumi Gaeshi (3:48 - 21:21)
**Index tags:** POSTURE_BREAK, POST_DENIAL, TWO_LEG_ENGINE, DIRECTIONALITY, FOLLOW_THROUGH

**Non-negotiable mechanics stack**
1) **Posture break**: head/forehead line down (you do not roll an upright spine).
2) **Post denial**: remove the posting arm (elbow inside / control line).
3) **Two-leg engine**: one leg elevates, the other drives (power source).
4) **Directionality**: roll over the head-line; your force direction should match the opponent’s spine line.
5) **Connection**: stay attached; separation produces “pretty roll, zero control.”

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF posture is not broken, THEN do not sweep yet; fix posture first.
- IF the opponent can freely post, THEN remove the post before committing.
- IF your legs are not doing the work, THEN the technique will degrade under resistance.

**Common failure modes**
- Rolling “around” a stable base rather than through compromised posture.
- Failing to remove the post (hand catches, knee widens, no score).
- Creating rotation but losing connection on landing.

**Drill hook**
- “Mechanics stack reps”: each rep must visibly show (1) posture break, (2) post denial, (3) two-leg engine, (4) correct direction, before the sweep counts.

---

### 3.3 Crucial Insight Regarding Your Shoulder for Sumi Gaeshi (21:21 - 26:19)
**Index tags:** SHOULDER_LINE_INSIGHT, DIRECTIONALITY, CONNECTION_STABILITY

**Primary use**
- Shoulder positioning as a power, safety, and directionality control point.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF shoulder line is misused (wrong side / wrong orientation), THEN you lose leverage and may expose yourself to flattening or failed rotation.
- IF you want reliable power transfer, THEN align shoulder usage with the intended roll direction (shoulder supports posture break + directional force).

**Common failure modes**
- Shoulder drifting “open” (loss of compression).
- Driving the sweep with arms/upper body instead of shoulder line + legs.

**Drill hook**
- “Shoulder check drill”: partner gives moderate resistance; reps only count if shoulder line stays aligned through the roll.

### 3.4 Winning the Scramble After Sumi Gaeshi (26:19 - 32:01)
**Index tags:** SCRAMBLE_WIN, FOLLOW_THROUGH, CONSOLIDATION, RESET_LOGIC

**Primary use**
- Convert the rotation into a score (top/waist/back), rather than a neutral roll.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF the sweep rotates them but you do not come up, THEN it is not a scoring action.
- IF opponent turns during the aftermath, THEN route to waist/back exposure rather than chasing a “clean” pin.
- IF you lose alignment and get flattened, THEN reset via forward shift (later chapter) rather than insisting on a dead position.

**Common failure modes**
- Admiring the sweep and pausing.
- Ending under the opponent or allowing immediate re-kneel/re-guard.
- Trying to “hold them down” without establishing stable top control.

**Drill hook**
- “3-second aftermath rule”: reps only count if you stabilize top (or waist/back control) for 3 seconds after the rotation.

### 3.5 Collar and Elbow Sumi Gaeshi (32:01 - 41:48)
**Index tags:** COLLAR_ELBOW_CONNECTION, VARIANT_SELECTOR, POSTURE_BREAK, POST_DENIAL

**Primary use**
- Variant when you can control head/upper body tie plus elbow line sufficiently to remove the post.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you have head control and elbow line access, THEN collar-and-elbow is a fast-entry variant.
- IF elbow line retracts, THEN switch to a different connection variant rather than forcing the same one.

**Common failure modes**
- Head tie without real posture break (opponent upright).
- Elbow line not controlled; opponent posts and kills rotation.

**Drill hook**
- “Connection-to-commit drill”: once collar/elbow is set, you must initiate within 2 seconds (no stalling).

---

### 3.6 Double Triceps Sumi Gaeshi (41:48 - 49:32)
**Index tags:** DOUBLE_TRICEPS_CONNECTION, POST_DENIAL, VARIANT_SELECTOR

**Primary use**
- Variant when both triceps lines are available for post denial and steering.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF both triceps are available, THEN double triceps offers strong post denial and steering.
- IF one triceps breaks free, THEN rotate to collar/elbow or over/under rather than clinging to half the variant.

**Common failure modes**
- Pulling arms without breaking posture.
- Losing one arm and continuing anyway (post returns).

**Drill hook**
- “Triceps clamp drill”: partner attempts to free one arm; you must either re-secure both or switch variants immediately.

---

### 3.7 Double Overhook Sumi Gaeshi (49:32 - 1:00:46)
**Index tags:** DOUBLE_OVERHOOK_CONNECTION, COMPRESSION_CONTROL, VARIANT_SELECTOR

**Primary use**
- Variant when opponent’s arms are forward/inside enough to be overhooked; emphasizes compression and post denial.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent drives forward with arms committed, THEN double overhook variant becomes high value.
- IF the overhooks slip and arms retract, THEN transition to shoulder crunch or arm trap routes rather than forcing overhooks.

**Common failure modes**
- Loose overhooks (no compression) → opponent backs out.
- Attempting to roll without a real base break.

**Drill hook**
- “Pressure entry drill”: partner pressures in; you must acquire double overhooks and initiate before posture rebuild.

---

### 3.8 Over Under Sumi Gaseshi (1:00:46 - 1:10:30)
**Index tags:** OVER_UNDER_CONNECTION, POST_DENIAL, VARIANT_SELECTOR

**Primary use**
- Variant when you have over/under-style structure; uses asymmetric control to remove posting options and steer rotation.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you have stable over/under structure, THEN commit to the roll path that best matches that asymmetry (do not force symmetrical mechanics).
- IF opponent squares out of over/under, THEN switch to double triceps or collar/elbow connections.

**Common failure modes**
- Trying to execute symmetrical mechanics from asymmetrical ties.
- Losing the under/over connection mid-commitment.

**Drill hook**
- “Asymmetry drill”: partner tries to square; you must either preserve over/under and sweep or switch connections within 2–3 seconds.

---

### 3.9 Important Note 'on Butterfly Sumi Gaeshi: Double Directionality (1:10:30 - 1:17:30)
**Index tags:** DOUBLE_DIRECTIONALITY, DIRECTIONALITY, DECISION_TREE

**Primary use**
- Install the decision model that butterfly-style sumi can attack in two directions depending on base reaction.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent bases/reacts to one direction, THEN the opposite direction becomes available (do not insist on the blocked direction).
- IF you feel their base widen to kill one angle, THEN redirect to the other angle while maintaining connection.

**Common failure modes**
- “One-direction stubbornness” (forcing the chosen side into a posted base).
- Redirecting too late after the base is fully established.

**Drill hook**
- “Direction cue drill”: partner chooses to post/shift base left or right; you must redirect and complete within the same beat.

---

### 3.10 Arm Trap Sumi Gaeshi (1:17:30 - 1:25:36)
**Index tags:** ARM_TRAP, POST_DENIAL, COMPRESSION_CONTROL

**Primary use**
- Variant when you can trap an arm across and reduce posting capacity structurally.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you can trap the arm across the body, THEN you can commit with higher confidence (post denial is built-in).
- IF the trapped arm begins to free, THEN switch immediately to shoulder crunch or collar/elbow rather than “holding and hoping.”

**Common failure modes**
- Arm trap without posture break (opponent still strong-spined).
- Losing the trap mid-roll (post returns at the worst moment).

**Drill hook**
- “Arm-trap retention reps”: partner tries to free the trapped arm; you must maintain trap through initiation or switch variants instantly.

---

### 3.11 Shoulder Crunch Sumi Gaeshi (1:25:36 - 1:40:33)
**Index tags:** SHOULDER_CRUNCH, COMPRESSION_CONTROL, POSTURE_BREAK, SCRAMBLE_WIN

**Primary use**
- High-compression variant that makes opponent feel light and reduces their ability to post/withdraw.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent drives in or keeps arms tight and you can compress shoulder line, THEN shoulder crunch becomes a primary variant.
- IF compression is strong but opponent refuses to tip, THEN redirect (double directionality logic) rather than forcing one line.

**Common failure modes**
- Crunch that isn’t truly compressive (space exists; opponent posts).
- Treating crunch as a hold rather than a trigger to sweep.

**Drill hook**
- “Compression-to-sweep drill”: reps only count if you initiate within 2 seconds of achieving a real crunch.

---

### 3.12 Forward Shift and Sumi Gaeshi (1:40:33 - 1:46:01)
**Index tags:** FORWARD_SHIFT_RECOVERY, RESET_LOGIC, POSTURE_REBUILD

**Primary use**
- Recovery pathway when you get flattened or lose the ability to sit up and attach; forward shift restores your ability to attack.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you are flattened supine and cannot re-attach, THEN forward shift to re-seat and re-enter the sumi system.
- IF forward shift restores posture but connection is gone, THEN select the quickest re-connection variant available rather than waiting.

**Common failure modes**
- Staying flat and trying to “arm wrestle” for grips.
- Re-seating but pausing long enough for opponent to re-stabilize.

**Drill hook**
- “Flattened recovery loop”: start flattened → forward shift → immediate sumi connection → initiate.

---

### 3.13 Body Lock Butterfly Sweep (1:46:01 +)
**Index tags:** BODY_LOCK_BUTTERFLY, COMPRESSION_CONTROL, SCRAMBLE_WIN, CONSOLIDATION

**Primary use**
- When you can lock the torso, the sweep becomes a high-control rotation with strong follow-through potential.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you can secure a body lock from butterfly, THEN this becomes a high-percentage sweep path.
- IF the rotation happens but opponent turns/escapes, THEN route immediately to scramble win (waist/back exposure acceptable).

**Common failure modes**
- Body lock without breaking posture (opponent heavy-spines and stalls).
- Rolling but failing to come up (same universal failure as all Vol 4 content).

**Drill hook**
- “Body lock → 3-second stabilization” (rep does not count without consolidation).

## 4.0 VOL 4 — ONE-PAGE RULESET (Non-Negotiables)

R1 — POSTURE GATE (KNEELING)
- IF opponent is kneeling, THEN sumi gaeshi is the principal scoring hub.

R2 — GRIP-TO-COMMIT (NO PROCRASTINATION)
- IF the correct connection is acquired, THEN initiate; waiting improves nothing.

R3 — MECHANICS STACK (IN ORDER)
- IF posture is not broken, THEN do not roll.
- IF post is not denied, THEN do not roll.
- IF legs are not the engine, THEN expect failure under resistance.
- IF directionality is wrong, THEN you roll into base.
- IF connection is lost, THEN you get “movement without control.”

R4 — FOLLOW-THROUGH IS THE SCORE
- IF the sweep rotates but you do not come up and stabilize, THEN it is a failed rep.

R5 — VARIANTS ARE SELECTORS BY CONNECTION
- IF collar/elbow available → use it.
- IF double triceps available → use it.
- IF double overhooks available → use it.
- IF over/under available → use it.
- IF arm trap available → use it.
- IF shoulder crunch available → use it.
(Do not force a variant that lacks its connection.)

R6 — DOUBLE DIRECTIONALITY
- IF opponent blocks one direction with base/post, THEN redirect and sweep the other way.

R7 — RECOVERY PATH
- IF flattened, THEN forward shift to re-seat and re-enter; do not dead-wrestle flat.

R8 — SCRAMBLE WIN IS ACCEPTABLE END STATE
- IF opponent turns during aftermath, THEN waist/back exposure routes are acceptable; insist on control, not aesthetics.

## 5.0 VOL 4 — DRILL MAP (Rule → Drill Prescription)

Each drill includes: start, constraint, win condition, fail condition.

### D1 — Kneeling Trigger Entry (R1, R2)
- **Start:** partner kneels (one/two knees) on cue.
- **Constraint:** establish a valid sumi connection and initiate within 3 seconds.
- **Win:** initiation occurs on time with posture break.
- **Fail:** hesitation or static grip fight.

### D2 — Mechanics Stack Validator (R3)
- **Start:** chosen sumi connection.
- **Constraint:** coach/partner checks the 4 visible gates (posture break, post denial, leg engine, direction).
- **Win:** sweep executed with all gates present.
- **Fail:** any missing gate resets the rep.

### D3 — Post Denial Rounds (R3)
- **Start:** kneeling opponent with active posting hand.
- **Constraint:** you must deny the post before sweeping; sweeping with a live post is invalid.
- **Win:** post denied → sweep.
- **Fail:** posted base kills the roll.

### D4 — 3-Second Aftermath Stabilization (R4)
- **Start:** immediately after the rotation.
- **Constraint:** rep only counts if you stabilize top/waist/back for 3 seconds.
- **Win:** stabilized control.
- **Fail:** opponent returns to knees/guard/neutral.

### D5 — Variant Call-Out Selector (R5)
- **Start:** partner presents changing ties (collar/elbow, triceps, overhooks, over/under, etc.).
- **Constraint:** you must call the variant and initiate within 2 seconds.
- **Win:** correct selection and initiation.
- **Fail:** forcing wrong variant or stalling.

### D6 — Double Directionality Drill (R6)
- **Start:** butterfly sumi position; partner chooses to base left/right.
- **Constraint:** you must redirect and sweep the open direction without resetting grips.
- **Win:** successful redirection and sweep.
- **Fail:** pushing into the posted base.

### D7 — Flattened → Forward Shift → Re-Attack Loop (R7)
- **Start:** you are flattened supine.
- **Constraint:** within 5 seconds you must forward shift to seated posture and initiate a sumi entry.
- **Win:** re-attack initiated.
- **Fail:** prolonged flat hand-fighting.

### D8 — Scramble Route Acceptance (R8)
- **Start:** after sweep attempt, partner turns/tries to run.
- **Constraint:** you must choose a control end state (top stabilization or waist/back route) within 3 seconds.
- **Win:** control secured.
- **Fail:** chasing aesthetics (trying to pin without control) and losing scramble.
