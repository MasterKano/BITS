## 3.0 VOL 6 — CHAPTER NOTES (Best Version)

### 3.1 Football Snapping Across The Centerline (0:00 - 4:20)
**Index tags:** FOOTBALL_SNAP_ENTRY, HEAD_POSTURE_TRIGGER, FOREARM_WRIST_SNAP

**Primary use**
- Create a tight head capture when the opponent’s head posture drops or crosses centerline.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Snap with wrist/forearm (whole unit), not fingers.
- Snap across the centerline so the head is “caught” on the wrong side for the opponent.
- The snap is not a slap; it is a capture mechanism.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent’s head posture drops into range, THEN snap immediately (do not wait for “perfect”).
- IF you miss the catch, THEN reset posture/frames rather than reaching longer.

**Common failure modes**
- Finger-only snap (no capture).
- Snapping but not across centerline (head slips back to safety).

**Drill hook**
- “Centerline snap reps”: partner gives light head entries; you must snap/catch across centerline repeatedly.

---

### 3.2 Football Catching and The Centerline Shift (4:20 - 6:17)
**Index tags:** FOOTBALL_CATCH, CENTERLINE_SHIFT, HIGH_WRIST_PATHWAY

**Primary use**
- Secure the head like a football and shift your control across centerline to gain depth.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Catch quality: tight forearm/hand wrap that resists yanking out.
- Centerline shift creates depth without needing a “big squeeze” early.
- The shift is a positional improvement step, not an afterthought.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF catch is loose, THEN do not advance to finish; re-catch tighter.
- IF the opponent begins immediate hand-fighting, THEN accelerate shift to depth and add secondary-arm policy.

**Common failure modes**
- Loose catch leading to immediate escape.
- Trying to finish without depth (shallow guillotine).

**Drill hook**
- “Catch → shift ladder”: rep only counts if you stabilize catch, then visibly shift to deeper control.

---

### 3.3 High Wrist Guillotine (6:17 - 6:56)
**Index tags:** HIGH_WRIST_GUILLOTINE, FINISH_MECHANICS

**Primary use**
- Preferred configuration because it covers both carotids and hides your hand from hand-fighting.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Wrist position is high; coverage is symmetrical.
- Hand position should be hard to reach/peel.
- Depth is prerequisite: high-wrist without depth is cosmetic.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you have depth and centerline control, THEN upgrade to high-wrist as default.
- IF hand-fighting becomes dominant, THEN your first fix is hand hiding + secondary-arm policy, not “squeeze harder.”

**Common failure modes**
- Shallow high-wrist attempt.
- Squeeze-first instead of depth-first.

**Drill hook**
- “High-wrist finish rounds”: start already deep; partner’s only defense is hand-fighting; you must keep hand hidden.

---

### 3.4 Arm in Guillotine (6:56 - 9:07)
**Index tags:** ARM_IN_GUILLOTINE, DEFENSE_BRANCH, UPPER_LOWER_CYCLE

**Primary use**
- Configuration when the defender inserts an arm; can be a finish or a control that forces turnouts and exposes go-behind.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Maintain head control; the inserted arm changes finishing details but not the requirement for depth and control.
- Treat arm-in as a branch, not a failure.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF defender inserts an arm, THEN commit to arm-in mechanics (do not insist on no-arm finish).
- IF arm-in prevents finish but forces a turnout, THEN use it as a conversion to go-behind/back exposure.

**Common failure modes**
- Treating arm-in as “ruined guillotine.”
- Losing head control while adjusting.

**Drill hook**
- “Arm-in branch drill”: partner purposely inserts arm; you must either finish or force a turnout and take behind.

---

### 3.5 Overhooking The Arm for Rollout Escapes (9:07 - 9:38)
**Index tags:** SECONDARY_ARM_POLICY, OVERHOOK_VS_ROLLOUT, DEFENSE_DENIAL

**Primary use**
- Secondary-arm policy to neutralize rollout-style defenses and stabilize finishing control.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Overhook (or equivalent control) of the defensive arm prevents the opponent from rolling out cleanly and reduces hand-fighting access.
- Think: primary arm = head control; secondary arm = defense control.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent threatens rollout/turn, THEN overhook the defensive arm as policy.
- IF you cannot secure secondary-arm control, THEN you must expect escapes; either re-catch deeper or convert to go-behind.

**Common failure modes**
- Ignoring the secondary arm and losing finish to rollouts.
- Trying to “muscle finish” without defense control.

**Drill hook**
- “Rollout defense rounds”: partner’s only defense is rollout; you must apply secondary-arm policy and finish/convert.

### 3.6 Hip Heisting from Open Guard (9:38 - 17:43)
**Index tags:** HIP_HEIST_LANE, STAND_UP_THREAT, DISTANCE_DILEMMA_REINFORCED

**Primary use**
- Stand-up threat from open guard that converts bottom into a scoring lane (go-behinds, back exposure, top), and also forces reactions that reopen legs.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Hip heist is a structured stand-up: build height while maintaining protective frames/connection.
- Your goal is not “standing”; it is “standing with advantage” (angle + behind access).

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent retreats to avoid legs, THEN heist/rise lane becomes stronger.
- IF opponent crowds to stop your heist, THEN legs become easier again (cycle).
- IF opponent posts hands or plants a knee during heist defense, THEN go-behind windows appear (expanded in Vol 7).

**Common failure modes**
- Heisting straight up into the opponent (no angle).
- Standing without protection (getting snapped/front-headed).

**Drill hook**
- “Heist-to-angle drill”: stand to a safe angle and either touch behind hips or re-seat to legs.

### 3.7 The Case for Hip Heisting (17:43 - 24:19)
**Index tags:** STAND_UP_THREAT, OUTLOOK_SHIFT, DISTANCE_DILEMMA_REINFORCED

**Purpose**
- Establish why heisting is central in a modern no-gi environment: it forces the opponent to solve a difficult dilemma (allow stand-up vs crowd and expose legs).

**Operational takeaways**
- Heist is the “third option” that prevents the opponent from playing a purely leg-defense game.
- It makes the opponent choose: engage close (legs open) or disengage (rise open).

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent’s strategy is “hide legs by distance,” THEN heist is the punisher.
- IF opponent’s strategy is “smother to stop stand-up,” THEN legs become the punisher.

**Common failure modes**
- Treating heist as occasional trick rather than a persistent threat.

**Drill hook**
- “Dilemma rounds”: partner must choose either crowd or retreat; you must punish with legs or rise accordingly.

---

### 3.8 The Most Neglected Aspect of Guard Play (24:19 - 33:37)
**Index tags:** HIP_HEIST_LANE, NEGLECTED_SKILL, TRAINING_PRIORITY

**Purpose**
- Declare hip heisting as a highly undertrained skill and argue it should be trained as a primary competency.

**Operational takeaways**
- Many guard players become excellent at leg engagement but cannot translate that into scoring because they cannot safely build height.
- Heist competence increases conversion rates for both reversals and go-behinds.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF your reversals fail because opponents scramble up, THEN improving heist + behind-the-hips control will often fix it.
- IF you cannot heist safely, THEN your open guard will remain “threatening but non-scoring” at higher levels.

**Common failure modes**
- Overreliance on bottom retention with no stand-up pressure.
- Poor heist mechanics leading to front headlock exposure.

**Drill hook**
- “Heist literacy block”: 5-minute block each session of heist entries, angles, and re-seating discipline.

---

### 3.9 Hip Heisting When Opponents are Standing (33:37 +)
**Index tags:** HIP_HEIST_LANE, STANDING_OPPONENT, ANGLE_AND_BEHIND, RESET_LOGIC

**Primary use**
- Apply heist against a standing opponent while managing distance and preventing snapdowns.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Do not heist into the centerline; heist to an angle.
- Use frames/hand positioning to prevent head control.
- The goal is behind-the-hips access or forcing the opponent to crowd (which reopens legs).

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent is standing and far, THEN heist is viable; build height to threaten behind.
- IF opponent rushes forward to stop heist, THEN immediately re-enter legs or snap head (if posture drops).
- IF you touch behind hips but cannot finish, THEN reset to guard with initiative, not neutral.

**Common failure modes**
- Heisting too tall too early (exposes posture/head).
- Staying upright with no behind access (stagnation).

**Drill hook**
- “Standing heist rounds”: partner stands at distance; your goals are (a) touch behind hips, (b) force crowd and enter legs, or (c) snap to guillotine if head drops.

## 4.0 VOL 6 — ONE-PAGE RULESET (Non-Negotiables)

R1 — HEAD POSTURE TRIGGERS THE LANE
- IF head posture drops/crosses centerline, THEN guillotine lane becomes primary.

R2 — SNAP QUALITY FIRST
- IF football snap is finger-only or not across centerline, THEN you do not “have” the guillotine.

R3 — CATCH → SHIFT → DEPTH
- IF catch is loose, THEN re-catch.
- IF catch is stable, THEN centerline shift to gain depth.

R4 — HIGH-WRIST DEFAULT
- IF depth is present, THEN high-wrist is default finish configuration.

R5 — SECONDARY ARM POLICY
- IF opponent threatens rollout/hand-fight, THEN control the defensive arm (overhook or equivalent).

R6 — FINISH OR CONVERT
- IF guillotine defense forces turnout/roll, THEN convert to go-behind rather than chasing a dying choke.

R7 — HIP HEIST IS A PRIMARY SKILL
- IF opponent retreats to avoid legs, THEN heist/rise is the punisher.
- IF opponent crowds to stop heist, THEN legs become the punisher (cycle).

R8 — HEIST TO ANGLE
- IF you heist, THEN you must build height to a safe angle with frames; straight-up centerline heists are low quality.

R9 — RESET DISCIPLINE
- IF you miss guillotine depth or lose heist advantage, THEN reset to lower-body threat rather than dead neutral.

## 5.0 VOL 6 — DRILL MAP (Rule → Drill Prescription)

### D1 — Football Snap Across Centerline (R1, R2)
- **Start:** partner gives head entries (light).
- **Constraint:** snap must be wrist/forearm unit and across centerline.
- **Win:** tight catch achieved.
- **Fail:** head slips out immediately.

### D2 — Catch → Shift Ladder (R3)
- **Start:** you have a catch.
- **Constraint:** rep counts only if you stabilize catch, then shift to deeper control.
- **Win:** depth improved without losing catch.
- **Fail:** catch loosens or resets.

### D3 — High-Wrist Hand-Hiding Rounds (R4)
- **Start:** deep guillotine position.
- **Constraint:** partner’s only defense is hand-fighting; you must keep hand hidden and posture controlled.
- **Win:** finish or 3-second “unpeelable” control.
- **Fail:** hand is peeled; depth lost.

### D4 — Secondary Arm Policy vs Rollouts (R5)
- **Start:** guillotine locked; partner attempts rollout every rep.
- **Constraint:** you must secure secondary-arm control before finishing.
- **Win:** finish or forced stop of rollout.
- **Fail:** rollout escape.

### D5 — Finish-or-Convert (R6)
- **Start:** guillotine entry; partner defends by turning/rolling.
- **Constraint:** within 3 seconds you must either finish or secure behind-the-hips/go-behind control.
- **Win:** finish or behind control.
- **Fail:** prolonged chasing.

### D6 — Hip Heist Angle Builder (R7, R8)
- **Start:** open guard; opponent standing at distance.
- **Constraint:** heist must produce (a) behind-hips touch or (b) forced crowding that opens legs.
- **Win:** one of the two outcomes.
- **Fail:** you stand square with no advantage.

### D7 — Dilemma Rounds: Crowd vs Retreat (R7)
- **Start:** open guard.
- **Partner rule:** must choose crowd or retreat each exchange.
- **Constraint:** punish crowd with legs, punish retreat with heist/rise.
- **Win:** correct punishment selection within 3 seconds.
- **Fail:** wrong lane persistence.

### D8 — Reset Discipline (R9)
- **Start:** you miss snap/catch depth or lose heist angle.
- **Constraint:** within 5 seconds you must return to legs/guard initiative.
- **Win:** re-established threat.
- **Fail:** dead neutral.
