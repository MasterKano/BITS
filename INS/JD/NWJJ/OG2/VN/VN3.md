## 3.0 VOL 3 — CHAPTER NOTES (Best Version)

### 3.1 Chapter 3.1 — X Guard (0:00–10:41)
**Index tags:** X_GUARD_HUB, BETWEEN_KNEES_CONTROL, BASE_SEPARATION, TWO_PHASE_ENGINE

**Primary use**
- Standing-opponent hub that reliably produces knockdowns because it controls the “space between the knees.”

**Entry conditions**
- You can thread inside position and establish between-knees control before being flattened or cleared.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Base separation first (knees cannot be allowed to re-close).
- Maintain connection while off-balancing (no “push away and lose contact”).
- Think in outputs: your job is to create KD-H / KD-HIP / KD-K, not to “hold X.”

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you cannot control the space between the knees, THEN do not attempt finishes; rebuild X alignment first.
- IF opponent is bouncing/clearing quickly, THEN prepare to de-athleticize (shoelace/knee-down pathway).

**Common failure modes**
- Entering X but letting knees re-close (no leverage; no KD).
- Losing connection during off-balance (KD without conversion opportunity).

**Drill hook**
- “Between-knees control → KD within 10 seconds” positional rounds.

### 3.2 Chapter 3.2 — X Guard Heist Reversal (10:41–22:33)
**Index tags:** HEIST_MECHANICS, WAIST_FIRST, KNOCKDOWN_TYPES, TWO_PHASE_ENGINE

**Primary use**
- Default Phase 2 converter after KD-H (hands posted) or forward base breaks.

**Entry conditions**
- You created KD-H (hands posted) or compromised posture from X.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Heist with structure (hip → knee → height), not a loose stand.
- Keep a tether during the rise (leg/hip contact) so they cannot reset base.
- Rise into the corner behind hips (don’t rise square into their chest).
- Finish target is waist (not “collect another leg”).

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF KD-H happens, THEN heist immediately; do not pause.
- IF opponent separates during heist, THEN switch to shoot-to-waist rather than chasing a far leg.
- IF opponent turns to face during your rise, THEN prioritize head tether + tight waist to stop the turn before advancing.

**Common failure modes**
- The pause after KD (most common scoring killer).
- Chasing second leg while opponent limp-legs and re-squares.
- Rising tall with no tether (they turn and face).

**Drill hook**
- “KD-H → 3-second waist start” conversion sprints (pass/fail).

### 3.3 Chapter 3.3 — Shoelace Heist Reversal (22:33–30:44)
**Index tags:** SHOE_LACE_CONTROL, DE_ATHLETICIZE, KD-K, HEIST_MECHANICS, WAIST_FIRST

**Primary use**
- De-athleticizing converter when opponent is too mobile for standard heists.

**Entry conditions**
- Opponent is bouncing/hopping/clearing; you can access shoelace-to-shoelace contact and deny clean foot plant.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Remove stable foot plant (deny the “spring”).
- Force a knee-down event (KD-K) to reduce athletic scramble.
- Rise while maintaining the leg tether; convert immediately to waist.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent repeatedly clears standard heist by hopping, THEN use shoelace heist.
- IF knee-down is achieved, THEN treat it as Phase 2 start (waist-first) even if the opponent is not “fully down.”

**Common failure modes**
- Losing shoelace control during the rise (they re-plant and sprint out).
- Treating KD-K as “not enough” and hesitating.

**Drill hook**
- “Hopper drill”: partner’s only goal is to hop/clear; your only goal is KD-K → waist.

### 3.4 Chapter 3.4 — X Guard Scissor (30:44–41:03)
**Index tags:** SCISSOR_X, BASE_SEPARATION, KNOCKDOWN_TYPES

**Primary use**
- Variation to create sharper base separation and steer knockdown direction.

**Entry conditions**
- Opponent’s clearing pattern suggests you need stronger lateral separation.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Scissor creates separation, separation creates KD.
- Maintain alignment so the scissor doesn’t become a push-away.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent closes knees to kill X leverage, THEN scissor variation becomes a stabilizer.
- IF scissor creates KD-H or KD-HIP, THEN immediate heist/waist conversion.

**Common failure modes**
- Scissor without controlling the follow-through (KD but no tether).
- Over-separating and losing your own alignment.

**Drill hook**
- “Scissor → KD recognition → waist” (randomized KD outcomes).

---

### 3.5 Chapter 3.5 — Double Leg X Guard (41:03–52:41)
**Index tags:** DOUBLE_LEG_X, LEG_CONNECTION_STABILITY, DE_ATHLETICIZE, KNOCKDOWN_TYPES

**Primary use**
- Stabilize both legs to prevent athletic clearing and increase KD reliability.

**Entry conditions**
- Opponent is too strong/fast on a single leg-line; you can connect both legs into a stable structure.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Use double-leg control to deny step-out and re-square.
- Convert stability into KD, then waist; do not “collect legs and stall.”

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent is repeatedly clearing single-leg X, THEN upgrade to double-leg X.
- IF double-leg X produces KD-K (knee-down) rather than KD-H, THEN still proceed to waist.

**Common failure modes**
- Treating double-leg control as an endpoint.
- Losing base while re-pummeling legs.

**Drill hook**
- “Double-leg X: hold for 3 seconds → force KD → waist” (time-limited).

---

### 3.6 Chapter 3.6 — Overclasp X Guard (52:41–1:03:19)
**Index tags:** OVERCLASP_X, ROTATION_DENIAL, BASE_SEPARATION, WAIST_FIRST

**Primary use**
- Prevent rotational clear-outs by clamping the opponent’s ability to turn hips away while maintaining between-knees control.

**Entry conditions**
- Opponent’s primary defense is hip rotation/turn-out to clear X.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Clamp rotation *without* sacrificing between-knees control.
- Use the clamp to create KD, not to “hug and stall.”

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent wins by rotating hips away, THEN overclasp becomes the stabilizer.
- IF overclasp stalls KD creation, THEN re-emphasize base separation first.

**Common failure modes**
- Overclasping so hard you lose leg separation (you trade the hub for a hug).
- Staying square in front; failing to convert to KD/waist.

**Drill hook**
- “Turn-out defense rounds”: partner’s goal is hip rotation escape; your goal is overclasp → KD → waist.

### 3.7 Chapter 3.7 — Double Kouchi Gari (1:03:19–1:12:19)
**Index tags:** KOUCHI_GARI_FROM_X, TAKEDOWN_STYLE_KD, FORWARD_KNOCKDOWN_PREFERENCE, TWO_PHASE_ENGINE

**Primary use**
- Takedown-style knockdown tool integrated into X-guard contexts.

**Entry conditions**
- Opponent’s stepping pattern and weight distribution make inside reaps available.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Off-balance first; reap into the step.
- Treat the reap as Phase 1; plan for Phase 2 waist immediately.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent’s weight shifts allow inside reap, THEN double kouchi becomes a high-percentage KD creator.
- IF KD-H occurs (hands post), THEN heist to waist immediately.

**Common failure modes**
- Reaping without prior off-balance (they step out).
- Creating a stumble but failing to convert.

**Drill hook**
- “Kouchi timing rounds”: partner steps; you must create KD then waist within time.

### 3.8 Chapter 3.8 — Shoelace Double Kouchi (1:12:19–1:19:14)
**Index tags:** SHOE_LACE_CONTROL, KOUCHI_GARI_FROM_X, DE_ATHLETICIZE, KD-K

**Primary use**
- Combine reap mechanics with shoelace-based de-athleticizing to force knee-down or hands-post outcomes.

**Entry conditions**
- Opponent is quick stepping; you need to deny foot plant.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Shoelace control reduces stepping ability; reap becomes easier.
- Convert knee-down events into waist finishes.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent is too mobile for standard kouchi timing, THEN add shoelace control to slow them.
- IF knee-down occurs, THEN proceed as Phase 2 trigger.

**Common failure modes**
- Shoelace control breaks mid-reap; opponent re-plants and escapes.
- Treating knee-down as “not enough” and pausing.

**Drill hook**
- “Shoelace reap → KD-K → waist” (repeat until automatic).

### 3.9 Chapter 3.9 — De Ashi Harai (1:19:14–1:23:23)
**Index tags:** DE_ASHI_HARAI_FROM_X, ASHI_WAZA_TIMING, KNOCKDOWN_TYPES

**Primary use**
- Timing sweep to exploit stepping rhythm; another Phase 1 KD creator that feeds heist-to-waist.

**Entry conditions**
- You can read step timing and align the sweep into the step.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Timing over force; sweep into weight transfer.
- Maintain connection so the stumble becomes KD rather than a reset.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF opponent’s defense is constant stepping and clearing, THEN timing sweeps rise in value.
- IF sweep yields only a stumble, THEN treat it as KD-K and rise.

**Common failure modes**
- Sweeping late; missing weight transfer window.
- No tether; stumble becomes a disengage.

**Drill hook**
- “Step-pattern harai drill → KD recognition → waist.”

### 3.10 Chapter 3.10 — Taking Opponents Forward Rather Than Backwards While Scrimmaging (1:23:23–1:33:05)
**Index tags:** FORWARD_KNOCKDOWN_PREFERENCE, KD-H, HEIST_MECHANICS, WAIST_FIRST

**Purpose**
- Explain why forward knockdowns (hands posted) frequently produce cleaner heist-to-waist conversions than purely backward tip-overs.

**Operational takeaway**
- Bias toward outcomes that make the opponent post hands (KD-H) because it gives you a predictable rise window and predictable hips exposure.
- “Directionality” is a strategic choice: not all KDs are equally convertible.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you can choose KD direction, THEN prefer KD-H (forward, hands posted) because Phase 2 becomes simpler.
- IF you produce KD-HIP frequently but struggle to finish, THEN adjust to produce more KD-H (or improve shoot-to-waist response).

**Common failure modes**
- Backward knockdowns that create race conditions you are not trained for.
- Continuing to select KD directions that don’t match your conversion skill.

**Drill hook**
- “Directional KD drill”: produce 5 KD-H conversions before allowing KD-HIP reps.

### 3.11 Chapter 3.11 — Controlling the Scramble From Two-Phase Reversals (1:33:05+)
**Index tags:** SCRAMBLE_CONTROL, CONSOLIDATION, WAIST_FIRST, TWO_PHASE_ENGINE

**Primary use**
- Make Phase 2 reliable under elite resistance by anchoring the scramble (tight waist + head tether) and denying re-square.

**Non-negotiable mechanics**
- Waist capture is necessary but not sufficient; consolidation stops the opponent’s “face and flee” options.
- Head tether concept (ear-to-back) turns a loose waist grip into a control system.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you capture the waist but the opponent turns to face, THEN your first job is to stop the turn (head tether + tight waist), not to advance.
- IF opponent sprints away after KD, THEN connection plus head tether beats “arm strength chasing.”

**Common failure modes**
- Winning KD and even waist, but losing the scramble because head tether is missing.
- Advancing prematurely before movement is stopped.

**Drill hook**
- “Waist + head tether lock-in”: start from tight waist; partner’s goal is to face/sprint; you must stop movement for 3 seconds before advancing.

## 4.0 VOL 3 — ONE-PAGE RULESET (Non-Negotiables)

R1 — X-GUARD IS A HUB, NOT AN END
- IF you enter X-guard, THEN your job is to create a knockdown output, not to hold position.

R2 — BETWEEN-KNEES CONTROL COMES FIRST
- IF you cannot control the space between the knees, THEN rebuild base separation before attacking.

R3 — KD OUTPUTS ARE CHECKPOINTS
- IF KD-H / KD-HIP / KD-K occurs, THEN Phase 2 begins immediately.

R4 — WAIST IS THE FINISH
- IF KD occurs, THEN finish target is waist (not far/second leg collection).

R5 — HEIST MUST BE STRUCTURED
- IF you heist, THEN rise hip→knee→height with tether; loose stands lose scrambles.

R6 — DE-ATHLETICIZE WHEN NEEDED
- IF opponent clears by hopping/stepping fast, THEN force knee-down via shoelace/double-leg stabilizers.

R7 — VARIATIONS ARE STABILIZERS, NOT SEPARATE GAMES
- IF opponent’s defense is knee re-closing, THEN scissor/double-leg X stabilizes.
- IF defense is hip rotation, THEN overclasp stabilizes.
(Then return to KD → waist.)

R8 — FORWARD KDs ARE OFTEN EASIER TO CONVERT
- IF you can choose direction, THEN bias toward KD-H (hands posted) unless your KD-HIP conversion is equally trained.

R9 — CONSOLIDATION IS MANDATORY
- IF waist is captured, THEN head tether + tight waist must stop movement before advancing.

R10 — RESET FAST
- IF X breaks cleanly, THEN re-enter legs or switch to distance-lane reversals; do not wrestle dead neutral.

## 5.0 VOL 3 — DRILL MAP (Rule → Drill Prescription)

### D1 — Between-Knees Control Timer (R2)
- **Start:** in X-guard.
- **Constraint:** maintain base separation for 10 seconds while partner tries to re-close knees.
- **Win:** separation maintained.
- **Fail:** knees re-close or you lose alignment.

### D2 — KD Production From X (R1, R3)
- **Start:** in X-guard.
- **Constraint:** must produce KD-H or KD-HIP within 10 seconds.
- **Win:** KD event occurs with tether maintained.
- **Fail:** opponent clears or you lose contact.

### D3 — KD-H → Heist → Waist Sprint (R4, R5)
- **Start:** KD-H moment (hands posted) from X.
- **Constraint:** begin waist hunt within 3 seconds.
- **Win:** tight waist achieved.
- **Fail:** pause, second-leg chase, or lost tether.

### D4 — Hopper / Shoelace De-Athleticize (R6)
- **Start:** X entry vs partner whose only goal is hopping/clearing.
- **Constraint:** must force KD-K (knee-down) before attempting waist.
- **Win:** KD-K → waist.
- **Fail:** shoelace control breaks and partner re-plants.

### D5 — Variation Selector Rounds (R7)
- **Start:** X-guard.
- **Partner behavior:** alternates (a) knee re-close, (b) hip rotation turn-out.
- **Constraint:** you must choose scissor/double-leg X for (a) and overclasp for (b), then KD → waist.
- **Win:** correct selection and conversion.
- **Fail:** wrong stabilizer or stalled conversion.

### D6 — Directional KD Training (R8)
- **Start:** X-guard.
- **Constraint:** produce 5 KD-H conversions (KD-H → waist) before allowing KD-HIP reps.
- **Win:** quota met.
- **Fail:** repeated KD-HIP with poor conversion.

### D7 — Scramble Lock-In (R9)
- **Start:** tight waist achieved, opponent attempting to face/sprint.
- **Constraint:** you cannot advance until movement is stopped for 3 seconds.
- **Win:** movement stopped; then advance.
- **Fail:** opponent re-squares/re-guards.

### D8 — Reset Discipline (R10)
- **Start:** opponent clears X cleanly to standing distance.
- **Constraint:** within 5 seconds you must re-enter legs or initiate a seated reversal.
- **Win:** re-engagement achieved.
- **Fail:** dead neutral wrestling.
