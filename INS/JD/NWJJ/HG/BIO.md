## 2.0 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This series presents half guard bottom as a *connected, underneath guard* whose purpose is to convert bottom position into (a) a stand-up/takedown finish, (b) a back exposure, (c) a dominant top-pin sequence, or (d) a leg entanglement—while denying the opponent stable crossface/flattening control.

The system is governed by a single positional law: **win the outside shoulder and outside hip relationship**. When this is maintained, half guard becomes a 3-directional platform with reliable kuzushi and predictable transitions. When it is lost (crossface + hip collapse), the objective immediately shifts to **elbow-escape recovery** back to a functional half guard framework.

### Primary Strategic Objectives
1. **Prevent flattening**: preserve frames, knee shield integrity, and head/shoulder alignment.
2. **Create kuzushi under connection**: off-balance from underneath where the opponent’s base is compromised.
3. **Convert to standing or back exposure**: tight waist / single leg / body lock pathways dominate the “get-up” layer.
4. **Exploit leg-lock access**: knee shield + scoop elevation and deep half entries provide structured leg attacks.

### System Priorities
- **Connection before movement**: establish meaningful attachment (hip/shoulder alignment + inside leg control) before attempting elevation or stand-up.
- **Frames as steering, not stalling**: frames exist to re-route pressure and re-acquire the outside shoulder/hip condition.
- **Kuzushi as the engine**: the repeated use of scissor/bump/scoop/elevators/knee lever/tilt/windmill is what forces reactions that open the stand-up and submission layers.

### Win Conditions (Most Common)
- **Stand-up conversion**: tight waist → body lock / single leg finishes.
- **Back exposure**: knee lever/heist/arm-drag continuations.
- **Top position**: sweeps that land into pins rather than “reset” scrambles.
- **Leg entanglement**: controlled entries to inside sankaku / 50-50 variants once the opponent’s base is disrupted.

### Loss Conditions (Must Be Prevented)
- Opponent establishes **crossface + hip collapse** leading to flattening.
- Knee shield is defeated without immediate elbow-escape recovery.
- Chasing leg locks without control, resulting in disengagement or passing exposure.

### Practical Interpretation
Half guard bottom is treated less like a “guard to stall” and more like a **platform for forced transitions**: connect → off-balance → stand/back/legs/top—repeating under the outside shoulder/hip rule.
