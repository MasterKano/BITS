## Volume 1 — Underhook Acquisition From Side Control (Core Entries)

> VN01 focus: Gate 1 (Acquire far-side underhook) + Gate 2 (Closed-loop retention).

---

## 1. Introduction (0:00–3:17)

### Objective
Frame Side Crucifix as a structured pipeline beginning with underhook acquisition.

### Exit Condition
You can initiate underhook attempts without lifting chest or losing base.

### Micro-Drill 1 — “No Reach” Entry Reps
- **Start:** Static side control.
- **Objective:** Initiate underhook entry without lifting chest.
- **Constraints:** Partner gives light resistance but no escape attempt.
- **Win Condition:** 10 clean entries with zero chest lift.
- **Rounds:** 2 x 10 reps per side.

---

## 2. Reflections On Side Crucifix (3:17–9:33)

### Objective
Understand elbow displacement as the bridge between pin and finish.

### Exit Condition
You recognize elbow control as the primary metric of progress.

### Micro-Drill 2 — Elbow Displacement Awareness
- **Start:** Side control with partner elbows tight.
- **Objective:** Manually displace far elbow 2–3 inches without advancing to full underhook.
- **Constraints:** No wrist grabbing; focus on elbow line only.
- **Win Condition:** 15 successful elbow displacements per side.
- **Rounds:** 2 x 2 minutes.

---

## 3. Ten-Finger Method (9:33–16:50)

### Objective
Force underhook against hand-high defensive posture.

### Micro-Drill 3 — Ten-Finger Isolation
- **Start:** Partner holds far hand high and elbow tight.
- **Objective:** Insert palm-up and secure underhook.
- **Constraints:** No switching to other methods.
- **Win Condition:** Underhook achieved + 3-second closed loop.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 30 seconds (switch each round).

### Micro-Drill 4 — Immediate Retention
- **Start:** Begin already mid-insertion (ten-finger grip).
- **Objective:** Complete underhook and seal closed loop instantly.
- **Constraints:** Partner attempts immediate re-pummel.
- **Win Condition:** Maintain retention 15 seconds.
- **Rounds:** 4 x 30 seconds per side.

---

## 4. Reverse Overhook Method (16:50–22:27)

### Objective
Create space using wedge-first insertion.

### Micro-Drill 5 — Wedge Precision
- **Start:** Partner clamps elbow tightly.
- **Objective:** Insert wrist edge only (no full forearm drive initially).
- **Constraints:** If elbow enters before wedge is set, reset.
- **Win Condition:** 8 clean wedge-first entries per side.
- **Rounds:** 2 x 8 reps.

### Micro-Drill 6 — Angle Walk Control
- **Start:** Mid-wedge with partial insertion.
- **Objective:** Walk toward north–south without losing chest pressure.
- **Constraints:** Partner tries to retract elbow.
- **Win Condition:** Complete underhook + closed loop.
- **Rounds:** 5 x 30 seconds.

---

## 5. Wrist Pass Method (22:27–29:42)

### Objective
Convert active shoulder post into underhook.

### Micro-Drill 7 — Timed Wrist Pass
- **Start:** Partner actively bench-presses on your shoulder.
- **Objective:** Time push → pass wrist fully → convert.
- **Constraints:** Must use wrist-pass branch only.
- **Win Condition:** Underhook + 3-second hold.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 45 seconds.

### Micro-Drill 8 — Full-Pass Verification
- **Start:** Wrist partially passed.
- **Objective:** Identify incomplete pass and correct it.
- **Constraints:** Partner retracts immediately if pass incomplete.
- **Win Condition:** 10 fully displaced passes per side.
- **Rounds:** 2 x 10 reps.

---

## 6. Mount Threat to Underhook (29:42–36:57)

### Objective
Use alignment shift to create underhook opportunity.

### Micro-Drill 9 — Mount-Fake Entry
- **Start:** Side control.
- **Objective:** Threaten mount → insert underhook during defensive block.
- **Constraints:** Must dismount back to side control with underhook preserved.
- **Win Condition:** Underhook retained after dismount.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 45 seconds.

### Micro-Drill 10 — Parallel Alignment Awareness
- **Start:** Perpendicular side control.
- **Objective:** Move to parallel alignment before inserting hand.
- **Constraints:** No underhook attempt until alignment achieved.
- **Win Condition:** 10 clean alignment shifts leading to successful underhook.
- **Rounds:** 2 x 5 reps per side.

---

## 7. Fake American Lock (36:57–39:21)

### Objective
Use credible Americana threat to provoke structural concession.

### Micro-Drill 11 — Threat-to-Conversion Timing
- **Start:** Partner arm bent, elbow tight.
- **Objective:** Threaten Americana → capture underhook on reaction.
- **Constraints:** Underhook must occur within 2 seconds of opponent’s push.
- **Win Condition:** 6 clean conversions per side.
- **Rounds:** 2 x 3 reps each side.

### Micro-Drill 12 — Delayed Reaction Punishment
- **Start:** Partner delays reaction.
- **Objective:** Maintain threat pressure without losing base.
- **Constraints:** No rushing; wait for real reaction.
- **Win Condition:** Underhook secured without chest lift.
- **Rounds:** 4 x 30 seconds.

---

## Volume 1 Final Operator Checklist

- Read hand height and posting behavior.
- Select correct acquisition method.
- Insert without lifting chest.
- Close loop immediately.
- Do not advance to pinning until retention is secure.

# VN02 — Master the Move: Side Crucifix
## Volume 2 — Underhooks Prior To Side Control + Underhook Retention + Near-Arm Pinning

> VN02 focus: Win underhook earlier (pre-pin), then retain it, then complete near-arm pin with correct selector.

---

## V2 Master Decision Tree (Applies to All Chapters)

### A) When to win the underhook
- If underhook is available during passing/neutral → **take it early** (easier than in settled side control).
- If you land in side control without it → revert to V1 methods to force it.

### B) What to do immediately after winning it
- Underhook is not “done” until retention loop is closed:
  - chin-to-hand
  - ear-to-bicep
  - shoulder-line sealed
  - post available

### C) Near-arm pin selector
- Long arm (wrist reachable) → **wrist-control pathway**
- Short arm (elbow glued) → **inside-hand/bicep pathway**
- Safe rise available → **tripod completion**
- If the selector changes mid-rep (arm shortens/lengthens) → switch pathways immediately.

---

## 1. Getting Underhooks Prior To Side Control (0:00–2:41)
### Objective
Reprioritize the system: underhook should often be won **before** you settle side control.

### When
Against competent opponents who shell elbows tightly once pinned.

### Mechanics (operational)
- Treat the underhook as part of the pass, not a post-pass task.
- Arrive in side control already “inside” the elbow structure.

### Quality checks
- You land with underhook already present.
- You do not need prolonged hand-fighting after pinning.

### Failure → Fix
- **Failure:** you pass then immediately stall fighting elbows.  
  **Fix:** hunt underhook earlier in the pass/ride.

### Exit
You consistently arrive in side control with underhook established (or you know when you didn’t).

#### Micro-Drill 1 — “Underhook Before Settle”
- **Start:** Top half guard or knee-cut staging position.
- **Objective:** Win underhook before you declare the pass complete.
- **Constraints:** No chest-to-chest settle until underhook is in.
- **Win Condition:** Pass completed with underhook + closed loop.
- **Rounds:** 8 reps each side.

---

## 2. Getting To An Underhook From Half Guard Top (2:41–6:16)
### Objective
Create a reliable pre-side-control underhook from top half guard.

### When
Top half guard with shoulder access and a controllable wrist line.

### Entry conditions
You can create a small “rise” without being re-guarded.

### Mechanics (operator sequence)
1. Establish head/shoulder pressure so the opponent cannot sit up.
2. Create slight rise to open the wrist-pass lane.
3. Wrist pass across the centerline (fully, not halfway).
4. Convert immediately to far-side underhook.
5. Close loop retention (chin/ear/shoulder seal).
6. Complete the pass into side control with underhook preserved.

### Quality checks
- Wrist pass is complete (wrist displaced, not merely moved).
- Underhook survives the finishing phase of the pass.

### Failure → Fix
- **Failure:** you try to wrist-pass while driving forward (no space).  
  **Fix:** rise first; pass second.
- **Failure:** underhook disappears during pass completion.  
  **Fix:** close loop before dropping weight to settle.

### Exit
Side control achieved with underhook retained and base stable.

#### Micro-Drill 2 — Half-Guard Wrist Pass to Underhook
- **Start:** Top half guard; defender frames lightly.
- **Objective:** Wrist pass → underhook → close loop → finish pass.
- **Constraints:** If underhook breaks, reset immediately.
- **Win Condition:** 3-second underhook retention in side control.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 45s (switch each round).

---

## 3. Bodylock Tight Waist And Tripod Into Underhook (6:16–13:23)
### Objective
Use tight waist/bodylock structure and tripod posture to produce the underhook early.

### When
You are passing with a tight waist/bodylock and can build height.

### Entry conditions
Opponent is flattened enough that your head pressure can dictate shoulders.

### Mechanics (operator sequence)
1. Secure a deep tight waist (wrap as far as possible).
2. Use head into shoulder to flatten and prevent sit-up.
3. Tripod: build height so you can step around the knee line.
4. Step past the knee line while keeping shoulder pressure.
5. Convert tight waist to underhook by climbing up the lat line.
6. Close loop retention as soon as the underhook is established.
7. Settle side control with underhook already secured.

### Quality checks
- Tight waist is deep (not shallow around ribs).
- Head pressure prevents the opponent from turning into you.
- Underhook is established before chest-to-chest settle.

### Failure → Fix
- **Failure:** shallow waist grip → opponent re-guards.  
  **Fix:** deepen the wrap and drive head into shoulder line.
- **Failure:** tripod without shoulder flattening → opponent sits up.  
  **Fix:** head/shoulder flatten first, then build height.

### Exit
Pass concludes with underhook secured and closed loop intact.

#### Micro-Drill 3 — Tight Waist to Underhook Conversion
- **Start:** Top passing with tight waist.
- **Objective:** Step knee line → convert to underhook.
- **Constraints:** Defender attempts to sit up once you tripod.
- **Win Condition:** Underhook + 3-second closed loop in side control.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 60s.

---

## 4. Underhook From Turtle Top (13:23–17:16)
### Objective
Win underhook during turtle conversions rather than after the opponent is flattened.

### When
Opponent turtles and must post/shift to base.

### Entry conditions
You control posture enough to force posting reactions.

### Mechanics (operator sequence)
1. Maintain control so opponent must post to prevent being tipped/flattened.
2. Insert underhook during the posting transition (when elbow leaves ribs).
3. Collapse posture and convert to side control with underhook preserved.
4. Close loop immediately upon landing.

### Quality checks
- Underhook is inserted during transition, not after stabilization.
- Landing is stable (no scramble) and retention is immediate.

### Failure → Fix
- **Failure:** waiting until side control is “settled” to hunt underhook.  
  **Fix:** hunt the underhook during the post/shift.

### Exit
Side control achieved with underhook already sealed.

#### Micro-Drill 4 — Turtle Transition Underhook
- **Start:** Partner in turtle; top has ride control.
- **Objective:** Capture underhook during post/shift and land in side control.
- **Constraints:** Defender must post at least once per rep.
- **Win Condition:** Underhook closed loop for 10 seconds after landing.
- **Rounds:** 6 reps each side.

---

## 5. Knee Cut Passes To Underhook (17:16–20:14)
### Objective
Land knee cut with underhook already present.

### When
Knee-cut passing phase where opponent is turning and framing.

### Entry conditions
You can pummel underhook during the cut before pinning becomes elbow-locked.

### Mechanics (operator sequence)
1. Initiate knee cut with head/shoulder pressure.
2. As you cut, pummel for far-side underhook (take it before you settle).
3. Close loop as soon as underhook is won.
4. Finish knee cut into side control with underhook retained.

### Quality checks
- Underhook won during the cut, not after.
- Closed loop is present on landing.

### Failure → Fix
- **Failure:** “finish pass first, then fight elbows.”  
  **Fix:** underhook during the cut.

### Exit
Side control achieved with retention intact.

#### Micro-Drill 5 — Knee Cut to Underhook Landing
- **Start:** Knee cut staging position.
- **Objective:** Cut and land with underhook + closed loop.
- **Constraints:** If you land without underhook, reset.
- **Win Condition:** 8 clean landings each side.
- **Rounds:** 2 x 8 reps.

---

## 6. Keeping An Underhook (20:14–24:42)
### Objective
Prevent re-pummeling and keep the system alive.

### When
Immediately after underhook capture (always).

### Entry conditions
You already have the underhook; now you must lock retention.

### Mechanics (retention engine)
- Chin connected to the underhooking hand (close loop).
- Ear glued to opponent bicep (deny elbow travel).
- Shoulder line sealed (no daylight).
- Post/widen base so head stays higher than hips.

### Quality checks
- Partner cannot reinsert elbow to ribs.
- You can withstand active pummel attempts without moving to pin.

### Failure → Fix
- **Failure:** chin floats, ear floats → re-pummel occurs.  
  **Fix:** close loop first; only then progress.

### Exit
You can hold closed loop under active pummeling for a timed interval.

#### Micro-Drill 6 — Retention-Only Timer
- **Start:** Underhook already established from side control.
- **Objective:** Maintain closed loop.
- **Constraints:** No pin attempts; retention only.
- **Win Condition:** 20-second hold.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 30s (switch each round).

---

## 7. Long Arm/Wrist Control (24:42–27:59)
### Objective
Use wrist control to lengthen the near arm and complete the pin.

### When
Near arm is long enough that wrist is reachable (wrist exposed / arm extended).

### Entry conditions
Underhook is retained (closed loop present).

### Mechanics (operator sequence)
1. Open hands and rise only enough to create stepping/pinning angle.
2. Freeze the near side of opponent’s head/shoulder line (so your step gains position).
3. Step across/over the head line to lengthen the near arm.
4. Take inside-wrist control (end of lever).
5. Walk backward to prevent the elbow from following.
6. Insert knee/shin to pin the near arm and complete side crucifix structure.

### Quality checks
- Head/shoulder line is frozen before stepping.
- Wrist control prevents elbow following on walk-back.
- Pin is achieved without losing base.

### Failure → Fix
- **Failure:** you step but gain nothing.  
  **Fix:** head freeze first.
- **Failure:** elbow follows you back.  
  **Fix:** inside-wrist control mandatory after lengthening.

### Exit
Near arm is pinned and elbow cannot return to hip-line.

#### Micro-Drill 7 — Long-Arm Pin Selector
- **Start:** Underhook present; defender presents long arm.
- **Objective:** Wrist control → walk-back → pin.
- **Constraints:** Must freeze head before stepping; if not, reset.
- **Win Condition:** 3-second pin hold.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 45s.

---

## 8. Short Arm Bicep Control (27:59–33:21)
### Objective
Pin a short, glued elbow when wrist chasing fails.

### When
Near elbow is tight to torso; wrist is not realistically reachable.

### Entry conditions
Underhook retained; you can rise without giving reversal.

### Mechanics (operator sequence)
1. Begin rise/tripod but keep safety: knee comes inside early.
2. Head drops low enough to deny bench-press leverage (while keeping base).
3. Insert inside hand to bicep/upper-arm junction.
4. Use knee-over mechanics (like an elbow escape joining knee to elbow line) to trap/pin the arm.
5. Settle into pinned structure without giving elbow extraction space.

### Quality checks
- Knee enters before you rise too high.
- Bicep control leverages elbow away from torso.
- Pin prevents elbow returning to hip-line.

### Failure → Fix
- **Failure:** rising high early → roll/bench press.  
  **Fix:** knee inside first; rise second.
- **Failure:** chasing wrist on short arm.  
  **Fix:** switch immediately to bicep pathway.

### Exit
Near arm pinned from short-arm configuration.

#### Micro-Drill 8 — Short-Arm Pin Selector
- **Start:** Underhook present; defender clamps elbow and hides wrist.
- **Objective:** Inside-hand/bicep → knee-over pin.
- **Constraints:** No wrist grabbing allowed.
- **Win Condition:** 3-second pin hold.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 45s.

---

## 9. Open Handed Lead Leg (33:21–38:54)
### Objective
Use open-handed control with lead-leg positioning to complete pinning while preserving base.

### When
You need posting freedom and don’t want over-committed grips during the rise/pin phase.

### Entry conditions
Underhook is retained and your posture is safe to rise.

### Mechanics (operator cues)
- Use open hands as posts and guides while your lead leg enters the pinning line.
- Preserve elbow–hip separation: near elbow must stay away from hip line.
- If instability appears, post and widen base before progressing.

### Quality checks
- You can post at any moment without losing the underhook.
- The near elbow never collapses back to hip-line during entry.

### Failure → Fix
- **Failure:** elbow stays glued while you step → instability and no progress.  
  **Fix:** create elbow–hip separation first; then step.

### Exit
Pin completion achieved with stable base and posting capacity preserved.

#### Micro-Drill 9 — Post-and-Pin Reps
- **Start:** Underhook established.
- **Objective:** Enter pin while maintaining a live post.
- **Constraints:** One hand must remain available to post.
- **Win Condition:** Pin established without losing underhook.
- **Rounds:** 8 reps each side.

---

## 10. Underhook Method (38:54–43:06)
### Objective
Use near-side underhook logic to simplify pin completion once alignment is parallel.

### When
You can become parallel (pre-pass double underhooks, or mount-fake pathway).

### Entry conditions
Parallel alignment exists, or you can create it.

### Mechanics (operator sequence)
- Two reliable sources:
  1) **Double underhooks before passing** (neutral/guard phase).
  2) **Mount fake** to become parallel, then insert near-side underhook.
- Once near-side underhook exists:
  - Join elbow to knee line and complete pin with high mechanical certainty.

### Quality checks
- Alignment is parallel before you insist on near-side underhook.
- Pin completion is mechanical (not a strength contest).

### Failure → Fix
- **Failure:** attempting near-side underhook while perpendicular.  
  **Fix:** create parallel alignment first.

### Exit
You can complete near-arm pin via near-side underhook pathway when parallel is achieved.

#### Micro-Drill 10 — Parallel First, Underhook Second
- **Start:** Perpendicular side control.
- **Objective:** Create parallel alignment (mount fake) → insert near-side underhook.
- **Constraints:** No insertion attempts until parallel.
- **Win Condition:** Near-side underhook achieved + 10s retention.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 45s.

---

## 11. The Three Methods Of Pinning The Near Side Arm (43:06–46:34)
### Objective
Define the three canonical pins and enforce pin-cycling ability.

### The three pins
- Front leg pin
- Back leg shin pin
- Arm scissor

### Core rules
- Pins are a cycle, not isolated moves.
- Continuous coverage: never release elbow pressure during transitions.

### Quality checks
- You can switch pins without giving arm extraction.
- You can choose the pin type that best routes to your desired hub.

### Failure → Fix
- **Failure:** gaps during switching → arm escapes.  
  **Fix:** continuous shin coverage and brief ankle crossing where needed.

### Exit
You can complete each pin and cycle between them under resistance.

#### Micro-Drill 11 — No-Gap Pin Cycling
- **Start:** Underhook established; near arm pinned in front pin.
- **Objective:** Front → back → arm scissor with no gaps.
- **Constraints:** Any moment of “no pin” = reset.
- **Win Condition:** 3 full cycles each side.
- **Rounds:** 3 cycles per side x 2 sets.

# VN03 — Master the Move: Side Crucifix
## Volume 3 — Chancery Hub + Kimura Family + Yoko Sankaku

> VN03 focus: Convert Side Crucifix (especially arm scissor) into **Chancery**, stabilize, then finish via **Kimura** and **Yoko Sankaku**.

---

## V3 Master Decision Tree (Applies to All Chapters)

### A) Priority order inside the hub
1) **Capture integrity** (elbow trapped correctly)
2) **Stabilization** (post + elbow pin + anti-rise controls)
3) **Attack selection** (kimura family first; yoko sankaku next)

### B) Stabilization window rule
- If the opponent is still in “max output,” do not commit both hands to breaking force.
- Stabilize until resistance decays, then attack.

### C) Attack selector (quick)
- Opponent connects hands / follows you → change base (2 knees → 1 knee → sit-out split)
- Opponent turns into you during triangle attempts → use elbow-first entry and stay tall until locked

---

## 1. Chancery: The Best Side Pin Control For Upper Body Submissions — What Is Chancery (0:00–3:53)

### Objective
Define chancery as the premier control hub from side crucifix for upper-body submissions.

### When
After near-arm pin is strong (ideally arm scissor), and you want the most stable conversion to finishes.

### Mechanics (conceptual)
- Chancery is a **leg-driven head-and-arm capture**:
  - Legs dominate the shoulder/head line.
  - Opponent’s ability to rotate and reinsert elbows collapses.
- This creates a “helplessness effect” that makes upper-body breaks reliable.

### Quality checks
- Opponent’s shoulder line cannot freely rotate.
- You can post and adjust without losing the capture.

### Failure → Fix
- **Failure:** treating chancery as “attack now” instead of “secure then attack.”  
  **Fix:** stabilize first; the hub is a platform, not a finish itself.

### Exit
You can hold chancery for a timed interval without using submissions.

#### Micro-Drill 1 — Chancery Hold (No Attacks)
- **Start:** Begin in chancery capture.
- **Objective:** Maintain capture integrity and base.
- **Constraints:** No submissions; stabilization only.
- **Win Condition:** 15-second hold without opponent turning or rising.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 30s (switch each round).

---

## 2. How To Properly Transition Into Chancery — Securing The Elbow Properly (3:53–9:37)

### Objective

## 2. Putting It All Together: Summarizing The Side Crucifix System (9:26–26:05)

### Objective
Compress the entire system into an executable pipeline with clear contingencies.

### When
Anytime you want to apply the system live without “thinking through moves.”

### Entry conditions

- Back crucifix.

#### Gate 1 — Acquire the far-side underhook
- If free (hand low) → take it.
- If forced (hand high/defensive) → use V1 methods.
- If active shoulder post → wrist pass branch.
- If underhook-first stalls → reverse-order (pin first) branch (V5).

- chin-to-hand
- ear-to-bicep
- shoulder-line sealed
- post for base
**If loop breaks:** do not advance; rebuild.

#### Gate 3 — Pin the near arm (selector)
- Long arm → wrist control pathway.
- Short arm → inside-hand/bicep pathway.
- Safe rise → tripod completion.

#### Gate 4 — Choose and cycle pin variants
- Front pin ↔ back shin pin ↔ arm scissor
- Continuous coverage rule (no gaps).

#### Gate 5 — Route to hub by pin type
- Arm scissor → **Chancery hub** (primary axis).

- Front pin → **Triangle hub**.
- Reverse-order pin-first → wrist pass restore OR north–south strangle.

#### Gate 6 — Stabilize then finish
- In chancery: stabilization window first (post + elbow pin + anti-rise).
- Choose finish family:
  - Kimura family (base selector: 2 knees → 1 knee → sit-out split).
  - Yoko sankaku (elbow-first entry).
  - Balance breaks route to juji/reverse triangle continuations.

### Quality checks
- You never skip a gate to “rush” a finish.
- You can return to the last owned gate instantly when something fails.
- You can cycle pin variants under resistance.

### Failure → Fix
- **Failure:** treating the system as isolated techniques → fragile control in live rounds.  
  **Fix:** score by gates (underhook, retention, pin, hub).
