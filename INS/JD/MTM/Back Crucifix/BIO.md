## 4.0 CORE CONCEPTS

### 4.1 Elbow Structure Is the Primary Defensive Frame
- In side pins, the opponent’s main defense is elbow position: elbows tight to the torso enable framing, elbow escapes, and guard recovery.
- The Side Crucifix system is built to break elbow structure by drawing both elbows away from the body.

### 4.2 Side Crucifix = Two-Elbow Displacement
- A true side crucifix exists only when both elbows are displaced:
  - Far elbow controlled via far-side underhook.
  - Near elbow controlled via leg-based pin.
- If either elbow can return to the ribs or hip-line, control is incomplete.

### 4.3 Primary Series Doctrine (Default Order)
- The highest-percentage sequence is:
  **Far-side underhook → Underhook retention → Near-arm pin → Finishing hub.**
- Reversing the order is a contingency, not the baseline strategy.

### 4.4 Underhook Retention: The Closed Loop
- An underhook without retention is temporary.
- Retention requires:
  - Chin connected to the underhooking hand,
  - Ear glued to opponent’s bicep,
  - Shoulder line sealed,
  - A post available for base.
- Do not advance to pinning until the loop is closed.

### 4.5 Underhooks Are Easier Before Full Side Control
- In neutral and passing phases, opponents defend primarily with legs.
- Once pinned in side control, defense becomes elbow-centric and underhooks become harder to obtain.
- Whenever possible, win the underhook before settling chest-to-chest.

### 4.6 Hand–Elbow Height Heuristic
- If the opponent’s hand is at or below elbow height, the underhook is often freely available.
- If the hand is high, assume you must force the underhook.
- If there is an active shoulder post, prefer the wrist-pass branch.

### 4.7 Win the Elbow Before the Wrist
- Against disciplined opponents, wrist chasing without elbow displacement fails.
- Establish elbow separation first; then control the wrist.
- Control hierarchy: elbow → wrist → break.

### 4.8 Near-Arm Pinning Is Selector-Based
- The correct pin entry depends on arm presentation:
  - Long arm (wrist available) → wrist control pathway.
  - Short arm (elbow glued) → inside-hand/bicep pathway.
  - Safe rise posture → tripod-assisted completion.
- Choosing the wrong pathway wastes time and destabilizes base.

### 4.9 Pin Cycling Is Retention, Not Preference
- Front leg pin, back leg shin pin, and arm scissor are a cycle.
- Switching between them preserves elbow displacement.
- There must never be a moment without shin or knee coverage on the near elbow.

### 4.10 Arm Scissor Feeds the Premier Hub
- Arm scissor provides the strongest structural control and best access to chancery.
- It is the preferred platform when available.

### 4.11 Stabilization Window Doctrine
- After a major capture (especially chancery), expect maximal resistance.
- Posting and elbow pinning precede breaking attempts.
- Stability first, torque second.

### 4.12 Base Before Breaking
- When both hands commit to an attack, base narrows.
- If the head drops below the hips, reversal risk increases.
- Stabilize posture and widen base before applying force.

### 4.13 Direction-of-Failure Routing
- Structural failure (re-pummel, elbow returns to ribs) → return to retention and re-displace the elbow.
- Rotational or balance failure (roll threat) → post and route to armbar or triangle continuations.
- Instability should become a branch, not a loss.

### 4.14 Reverse-Order Contingency
- If underhook-first stalls, pin the near arm first.
- Restore classic structure via wrist pass or route directly to north–south.
- The order may change; the two-elbow requirement does not.

### 4.15 Dilemma Logic Governs Transitions
- From back crucifix:
  - If head slip is available → transition to side crucifix.
  - If head slip is denied by bridging/arching → switch to rear triangle.
- Correct branching prevents wasted effort.

### 4.16 Gate Discipline
- The system operates through gates:
  1) Underhook  
  2) Retention  
  3) Pin  
  4) Hub  
  5) Finish  
- Skipping a gate produces fragile control.

## 5.0 SYSTEM OVERVIEW

### 5.1 System Objective (What the system produces)
Build a control state where the opponent’s elbows are forcibly displaced away from the torso so that:
- Their elbow-escape and frame recovery are heavily degraded.
- Upper-body submissions become mechanically easier and faster.

This is achieved by controlling **both elbows simultaneously**:
- Far elbow: far-side underhook.
- Near elbow: leg-based pin (front leg pin, back leg shin pin, or arm scissor).

---

### 5.2 The Default Route (Primary Series: Underhook → Pin → Hub)
**Step 1 — Acquire the far-side underhook**
- If the opponent’s hand is low (hand ≤ elbow), take the underhook opportunistically.
- If the opponent’s hand is high/defensive, assume you must force the underhook with a dedicated method.
- If the opponent is actively posting/pushing on your shoulder, prefer the wrist-pass branch.

**Step 2 — Retain the underhook (closed-loop retention)**
- Close the pummel window: chin-to-hand connection + ear-to-bicep contact.
- Maintain base with a post so your head stays higher than hips.

**Step 3 — Pin the near-side arm (hand-assisted completion)**
Choose based on arm length/discipline:
- Long-arm case: isolate wrist (end of lever) → leg pin.
- Short-arm case (common vs good opponents): inside-hand/bicep control → lever elbow out → pin.
- Tripod-based completion when hands are open and posture can rise safely.

**Step 4 — Choose the control variant (pin type)**
- Front leg pin
- Back leg shin pin
- Arm scissor (highest control; best feed to chancery)
These are cycleable; switching pins is part of control maintenance.

**Step 5 — Route to a finishing hub**
- If arm scissor: route to **Chancery** (primary hub).
- If back leg shin pin: route to **Kimura / Juji lines**.
- If front leg pin: route to **Triangle lines**.

---

### 5.3 Chancery Hub (Primary Control-to-Finish Bridge)
**Purpose:** Convert side crucifix control into a head-and-arm capture that suppresses escape attempts and enables high-percentage finishes.

**Stability-first doctrine**
- Expect a brief “max resistance” phase right after capture.
- Stabilize with posting and ride control before initiating high-commitment breaks.

**Primary attack families from Chancery**
- Kimura family (multiple basing options; sit-out variants increase breaking leverage).
- Side triangle (yoko sankaku) entered with a “stay tall until strangle is on” approach.
- If balance breaks or opponent’s movement forces it: armbar and triangle follow-ups become structured continuations rather than accidents.

---

### 5.4 Alternative Route (Secondary Series: Pin → Underhook)
When the default order is blocked or when contingencies appear:
- Pin the near-side arm first (typically via reverse crossface / near-arm control)
Then choose:
- Wrist pass back into the classic side crucifix structure (re-establish underhook and proceed to chancery), or
- North–south strangle as an immediate submission route.

---

### 5.5 Back Crucifix Link (Unifying Chest-to-Back and Chest-to-Chest)
The system includes a bridge from back crucifix into side crucifix:
- If you can build height and hollow to let the opponent’s head slip through: transition to side crucifix → chancery.
- If the opponent bridges/arches and denies the head slip: switch direction and route to rear triangle (ushiro sankaku).

This creates a dilemma: head slips → side crucifix; head blocks → rear triangle.

---

### 5.6 Global Gates (What decides your branch)
**G1 — Underhook availability trigger**
- Hand low: take underhook now.
- Hand high: force underhook.
- Active shoulder post: wrist-pass branch.

**G2 — Underhook retention gate**
- If you cannot close the chin-to-hand loop, do not advance to pinning; re-secure retention first.

**G3 — Pin-selection gate**
- Long arm exposed: wrist control routes.
- Short arm glued: inside-hand/bicep routes.
- If posture can rise safely: tripod-based completions.

**G4 — Base gate**
- If your head drops below hips or you feel roll risk: post and widen base before attacking.

**G5 — Chancery stabilization gate**
- If opponent is in peak escape output: stabilize and ride; delay high-commitment finishing attempts until output decays.

---

### 5.7 Quality Checks (What “good” feels like)
**Side crucifix quality**
- Far elbow cannot reinsert to ribs (underhook is retained).
- Near elbow cannot return to hip-line (pin denies elbow escape mechanics).
- You can switch pin variants without losing elbow displacement.

**Chancery quality**
- Head/shoulder capture prevents the opponent from turning freely.
- Your base and posts prevent being rolled or forced off the capture.

---

### 5.8 Where each volume fits (System map to curriculum)
- Vol 1: forcing underhooks from side control + mount-based setups.
- Vol 2: underhooks from neutral/passing/turtle + retention + near-arm pinning methods.
- Vol 3: chancery construction + kimura family + side triangle routes.

## 6.0 TECHNIQUE LIBRARY

### 6.1 Volume 1 — Underhook Acquisition From Side Control

#### 6.1.1 Introduction (0:00–3:17)
- **Objective:** Position the Side Crucifix as a short “pin → submission” bridge with quick learning ROI.
- **Primary decision rule:** Build the bridge with a compact sequence (underhook → near-arm pin → attack hub).
- **Constraint:** The system is “small” and compartmentalized—treat it as a repeatable pipeline, not a loose collection of moves.

#### 6.1.2 Reflections On Side Crucifix (3:17–9:33)
- **Objective:** Explain why Side Crucifix rapidly increases upper-body submission rate.
- **Key idea:** The gap between pinning skill and submission skill is hardest under pressure; Side Crucifix collapses that gap by locking the elbows away from the torso.
- **Quality check:** If you can reliably displace both elbows, submissions become easier than “normal” top attacks.

#### 6.1.3 How To Get A Underhook From Side Control — Ten Finger Method (9:33–16:50)
- **Objective:** Force a far-side underhook against a defensively responsible arm position.
- **Primary decision rule:** Use when opponent’s hand is **higher than the elbow** (pummel is not freely available).
- **Gates/constraints:**
  - Do **not** lift chest pressure to “reach” the hand (you’ll open guard recovery/underhook counters).
  - Keep chin close to opponent’s shoulder line to deny movement while inserting your hand.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Start in a solid side pin with crossface pressure.
  2) Transition to a **one-handed crossface** momentarily.
  3) Turn free hand **palm up**; feed it into the space between opponent’s **wrist and bicep**.
  4) Lock a **ten-finger grip**.
  5) Use your elbow to **lever opponent’s elbow outward** (open the door).
  6) Post forehead on your own bicep; **head-pummel** around opponent’s forearm to complete the path.
  7) Re-lock into a true underhook; immediately close retention (chin-to-hand/ear-to-bicep concept).
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Chest lifts to insert the hand.  
    **Fix:** Insert with narrow hand entry while maintaining chest and chin proximity.
  - **Error:** You get the underhook but leave space.  
    **Fix:** Close the loop immediately (chin down, ear tight, shoulder line sealed).

#### 6.1.4 Reverse Overhook Method (16:50–22:27)
- **Objective:** Use a wrist “knife wedge” under the elbow to create the underhook.
- **Primary decision rule:** Use when ten-finger entry is difficult and opponent’s elbow is extremely tight.
- **Gates/constraints:** Wedge must be **thin-first** (wrist edge) like a knife; don’t try to “force” the whole arm through at once.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Maintain pin; bring elbow across to compress their structure.
  2) Drive your **wrist edge** under the tight elbow line (knife edge opens space for the “spine”).
  3) Once your elbow/bicep is under, **lift their elbow outward**.
  4) Add a **thumb wedge** under tricep to lock the opening.
  5) Walk around toward north–south to consolidate.
  6) Roll the wrist and punch through to complete the underhook.
  7) Re-square into side pin with underhook and anti-turn bar as needed.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Trying to insert the whole forearm at once.  
    **Fix:** Wrist edge first; elbow follows after space opens.
  - **Error:** Losing control while walking.  
    **Fix:** Keep hip/shoulder blocks in place while you re-angle.

#### 6.1.5 Wrist Pass Method (22:27–29:42)
- **Objective:** Convert opponent’s defensive hand/shoulder post into a clean underhook entry.
- **Primary decision rule:** Use when opponent is actively pushing/bench-pressing on your shoulder or framing hard.
- **Gates/constraints:** The pass must go **fully across** the centerline until the wrist is “behind” and pinned by your structure.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Create a small amount of space (rise slightly) so the wrist can be passed.
  2) Pass opponent’s wrist across behind their line.
  3) Lock in underhook and immediately apply underhook retention.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Passing partially (wrist still in front).  
    **Fix:** Pass until their wrist line is truly displaced and cannot re-pummel.

#### 6.1.6 Using The Mounted Position To Get To Your Underhook (29:42–36:57)
- **Objective:** Use mount-threat alignment (more parallel) to make underhooks realistic—even when elbows are closed.
- **Primary decision rule:** If near-side underhook angle is wrong from perpendicular side control, **go more parallel** via mount fake.
- **Gates/constraints:** The “window” occurs when opponent becomes obsessed with stopping mount.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Start in side control with a strong crossface.
  2) Drive your knee across belly as a **mount threat**.
  3) As opponent commits arms to block mount, **pummel your hand inside** for underhook.
  4) Dismount back to your original side control, now with underhook secured.
  5) Apply retention (chin on shoulder, ear to bicep), then proceed to near-arm pinning.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Forcing the underhook while still perpendicular.  
    **Fix:** Create the parallel alignment first (mount fake).

#### 6.1.7 Fake American Lock (36:57–39:21)
- **Objective:** Provoke a defensive reaction that *gives you* the underhook.
- **Primary decision rule:** Use against responsible opponents with short/bent arm (elbow tight, hand on shoulder/ribs).
- **Gates/constraints:** You must be anchored to their shoulder line; threat must be believable.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Anchor to opponent’s shoulder; turn to shoulder line.
  2) Partial sit-out to align and pressure forward.
  3) Control the end of lever and threaten Americana.
  4) Typical reaction: opponent bridges/pushes the arm away → they **surrender the underhook**.
  5) Take the underhook immediately and lock retention.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Threat without shoulder anchoring (they retract without consequence).  
    **Fix:** Shoulder-line anchor first, then threaten.

### 6.2 Volume 2 — Underhooks Before Side Control + Underhook Retention + Near-Arm Pinning

#### 6.2.1 Getting Underhooks Prior To Side Control (0:00–2:41)
- **Objective:** Make underhook acquisition easier by winning it before chest-to-chest pins.
- **Primary decision rule:** Against skilled opponents, obtain underhook **as early as possible** (guard/neutral), not after settling side control.
- **Concept gate:** Neutral defense is leg-based; side-control defense is elbow-based (harder).

#### 6.2.2 Getting To An Underhook From Half Guard Top (2:41–6:16)
- **Objective:** Use the wrist-pass concept to secure underhook while still in half guard passing.
- **Primary decision rule:** Create space (rise) before passing the wrist; do not try to wrist-pass while driving forward.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) From half guard top, cup/anchor shoulder.
  2) Rise slightly to create wrist-pass space.
  3) Pass the wrist across, place chin to shoulder, lock hands.
  4) Finish the half-guard pass (knee down, leg free) with underhook already present.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Pushing forward while attempting the pass (no space).  
    **Fix:** Rise first, then pass.

#### 6.2.3 Bodylock Tight Waist And Tripod Into Underhook (6:16–13:23)
- **Objective:** Convert tight waist/bodylock passing into an underhook before side control is finalized.
- **Primary decision rule:** Use head/shoulder pressure to flatten and step across the knee; convert the tight waist to an underhook by climbing the lat line.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Head into shoulder; arm wraps deep for **tight waist** (as far around waist as possible).
  2) Use head to push shoulders down; step forward until elbow can get inside opponent’s knee line.
  3) Step over the knee line; convert tight waist to underhook:
     - Hand exits and climbs up the lat; switch head position to **chin-on-shoulder**.
     - Shoulder-to-shoulder tightness denies their pummel.
  4) Complete pass into side control with underhook secured.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Loose waist grip (shallow) leads to re-guarding.  
    **Fix:** Tight waist deep + head pressure as the driver.

#### 6.2.4 Underhook From Turtle Top (13:23–17:16)
- **Objective:** Build underhook access from top turtle to feed side control and side crucifix.
- **Primary decision rule:** Use turtle rides to create underhook angle early; don’t wait until you “settle” side control.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  - Control the turtle posture, then insert underhook as the opponent shifts or posts.
  - Convert to side control with underhook already present (so Side Crucifix assembly is immediate).
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Chasing control only after flattening.  
    **Fix:** Hunt underhook during transitions when arms are posting/moving.

#### 6.2.5 Knee Cut Passes To Underhook (17:16–20:14)
- **Objective:** Land knee-cut with underhook already established.
- **Primary decision rule:** If you can force underhook during the knee-cut phase, you prevent the “elbow lockdown” that appears once pinned.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  - Underhook first → knee-cut structure → settle side control with underhook in place.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Knee-cut to settle first, then fight for underhook.  
    **Fix:** Win the underhook during the cut.

#### 6.2.6 Keeping An Underhook (20:14–24:42)
- **Objective:** Prevent the re-pummel that resets your entire pipeline.
- **Primary decision rule:** After you acquire the underhook, immediately “close the loop.”
- **Gates/constraints:**
  - Chin on/near shoulder.
  - Ear on bicep.
  - Shoulder line sealed; recognize that pummeling out would often expose them to Americana-type risk.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Underhook achieved but head is floating (space).  
    **Fix:** Chin-to-shoulder and ear-to-bicep immediately.

#### 6.2.7 Long Arm/Wrist Control (24:42–27:59)
- **Objective:** Use arm-lengthening + wrist control to enable a reliable knee-over pin.
- **Primary decision rule:** If the arm can be made long, control **the end of the lever** (inside wrist) and walk it where you need it.
- **Gates/constraints:** If you simply walk “into” them, their head moves away and you don’t gain position—freeze the head side first.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Underhook is secured (step 1 complete).
  2) Open hands and perform a **partial sit-out** (turn hips, do not fully sit out).
  3) Freeze the near side of opponent’s head with a grip/block.
  4) Step across the centerline over the head (1–2–3–4) to **lengthen the arm**.
  5) Grip the **inside of the wrist** and walk backward—wrist control prevents elbow from following.
  6) Insert your knee efficiently to pin the near arm and complete Side Crucifix structure.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** No head-freeze → you walk but gain nothing.  
    **Fix:** Block/freeze head side before stepping.
  - **Error:** Wrist not controlled → elbow follows you back.  
    **Fix:** Inside-wrist control is mandatory once you lengthen.

#### 6.2.8 Short Arm Bicep Control (27:59–33:21)
- **Objective:** Pin a short, defensively tucked arm that cannot be lengthened for wrist control.
- **Primary decision rule:** When wrist is not realistically available, go **inside-hand to bicep** and use knee-over mechanics similar to an elbow escape.
- **Gates/constraints:** If you “come up” too high too early, you can be bench-pressed/rolled.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Begin the rise/tripod action but keep stability.
  2) Put head down and bring a knee across the stomach early to deny bench-press leverage.
  3) Insert hand inside to contact the bicep/upper arm line.
  4) Bring knee over top to trap/pin the near arm.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Rising high before knee is inside the bicep line → roll risk.  
    **Fix:** Knee inside first; rise second.

#### 6.2.9 Open Handed Lead Leg (33:21–38:54)
- **Objective:** Use open-hand posting/controls with lead-leg positioning to complete pinning without over-committing grips.
- **Primary decision rule:** Open hands are permissible, but don’t allow opponent’s elbow to collapse back close to hips.
- **Mechanics (operator cues):**
  - Widen the distance between opponent’s elbow and hip line.
  - Be prepared to post for base during the rise/pin completion.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Allowing elbow to stay glued while you step → instability/roll risk.  
    **Fix:** Prioritize elbow-hip separation before committing.

#### 6.2.10 Underhook Method (38:54–43:06)
- **Objective:** Use near-side underhook logic to make knee–elbow joining a simple pin completion.
- **Primary decision rule:** Near-side underhook is easiest when you are **parallel**, not perpendicular.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  - Two reliable pathways:
    1) Get **double underhooks before passing** (guard/neutral).
    2) Use a **mount fake** to become parallel, then insert near-side underhook.
  - Once double underhooks exist, join elbow to knee and pin the near arm.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Trying near-side underhook while perpendicular.  
    **Fix:** Create parallel alignment first (pre-pass or mount fake).

#### 6.2.11 The Three Methods Of Pinning The Near Side Arm (43:06–46:34)
- **Objective:** Establish and cycle the three canonical near-arm pins.
- **Primary decision rule:** You must be competent in all three and be able to switch between them.
- **Mechanics (high-level):**
  - Front leg pin ↔ back leg pin (pommeling/crossing feet briefly).
  - Transition to arm scissor by pinching knees around shoulder line and sitting out.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Treating the pins as separate “moves.”  
    **Fix:** Treat them as a cycle; switching is part of retention.

### 6.3 Volume 3 — Chancery Hub + Kimura Family + Yoko Sankaku

#### 6.3.1 Chancery: What It Is (0:00–3:53)
- **Objective:** Define Chancery as the strongest control hub from Side Crucifix for upper-body submissions.
- **Primary decision rule:** If you can reach arm scissor control, prioritize building Chancery as your main finishing platform.
- **Concept:** Chancery produces a “helpless” feeling by controlling head/arm line with the legs.

#### 6.3.2 Transition Into Chancery — Securing The Elbow Properly (3:53–9:37)
- **Objective:** Enter Chancery without allowing elbow extraction.
- **Primary decision rule:** The direction of your lower leg during the rise is decisive.
- **Gates/constraints:**
  - **Do not** come up with your foot pointing in the same direction as opponent’s arm (it becomes easy for them to extract elbow).
  - Lower leg should point in the same direction as opponent’s head.
  - Keep knees pinched tight around opponent while rising.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Start from arm scissor: bottom knee wedges shoulder; top knee controls elbow.
  2) Rise to knees with lower leg oriented toward opponent’s head.
  3) Keep knees tight; progress into the full Chancery structure.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Foot points down the opponent’s arm line.  
    **Fix:** Re-orient lower leg toward head direction before you rise.

#### 6.3.3 Building A Rock Solid Control From Chancery (9:37–15:28)
- **Objective:** Stabilize Chancery so the opponent cannot turn, build posture, or free the arm line.
- **Primary decision rule:** Stability first; submission second.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  - Post a hand wide for base.
  - Pin opponent’s elbow down toward hip line (deny explosive extraction).
  - If opponent builds to knees, use a forearm/hand “bar” across the back to return them to the mat.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Attacking immediately and losing base.  
    **Fix:** Post and pin elbow first; then attack.

#### 6.3.4 Conversion To Kimura (15:28–19:58)
- **Objective:** Translate Chancery control into Kimura capture.
- **Primary decision rule:** Pull the trapped arm into a controllable line before attempting the break.
- **Mechanics (operator cues):**
  - Maintain Chancery clamp while you bring the opponent’s arm into Kimura configuration.
  - Keep the opponent’s elbow controlled (don’t allow elbow to drift back toward ribs).
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Reaching for Kimura while opponent can rotate shoulders.  
    **Fix:** Re-pin elbow/shoulder line, then re-attack.

#### 6.3.5 Kimura Variations From Chancery (19:58–24:31)
- **Objective:** Provide stable basing options for finishing the Kimura.
- **Primary decision rule:** Choose your base based on balance and distance needs.
- **Three variations (base choices):**
  1) **Two knees down** (max stability, tight finish).
  2) **One knee up** (more height/mobility to travel up the lever).
  3) **Full sit-out / legs split wide** (very strong, stable, powerful break).
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Choosing a mobile base when you’re unstable.  
    **Fix:** Default to two-knees-down until control is unquestionable.

#### 6.3.6 Dorsal Kimura And Sit Out Kimura (24:31–29:33)
- **Objective:** Increase breaking leverage and reliability with dorsal angle and sit-out mechanics.
- **Primary decision rule:** If opponent resists by following you or turning, use sit-out geometry to maintain elbow capture while you create leverage.
- **Mechanics (operator cues):**
  - Maintain elbow pinning while you rotate your body into a sit-out.
  - Split your legs wide to prevent being toppled and to increase torque.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Sitting out without elbow isolation.  
    **Fix:** Elbow pinned first; sit-out second.

#### 6.3.7 Chancery To Yoko Sankaku (Side Triangle) (29:33–40:43)
- **Objective:** Convert Chancery into a high-percentage side triangle.
- **Primary decision rule:** Don’t drop flat too early—use an elbow post to prevent explosive turn-ins.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) From Chancery, transition down to an **elbow** (not shoulder-flat).
  2) Keep posture controlled while legs thread to lock the triangle.
  3) Once lock is secure, finalize the strangle with controlled angle and pressure.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Dropping to shoulder immediately → opponent turns into you and scrambles.  
    **Fix:** Elbow first, triangle second, then settle.

### 6.4 Volume 4 — Balance Loss Follow-Ups + Troubleshooting

#### 6.4.1 What To Do If You Lose Your Balance — Juji Gatame (0:00–4:57)
- **Objective:** When balance is compromised during Chancery/Kimura attempts, route to a structured armbar rather than losing the position.
- **Primary decision rule:** If hands are committed (Kimura grip) and you lose posting ability, transition into armbar mechanics that restore control.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  - As you fall to your side, reconfigure legs to separate opponent’s arms.
  - Move opponent’s hand to the “top armpit” configuration for controlled breaking pressure.
  - Apply back-heel pressure through the legs to create armbar pressure.
  - If opponent rolls defensively, follow into a tight finishing configuration (shoulder/triangle-style follow-up as shown).
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Holding Kimura while falling without leg reconfiguration.  
    **Fix:** Use legs to separate grips and re-center the breaking line.

#### 6.4.2 ¾ Juji Gatame (4:57–9:05)
- **Objective:** Provide an armbar variant when full classical alignment is blocked.
- **Primary decision rule:** Use when you can’t square fully but can still control elbow line and prevent turning escape.
- **Mechanics (operator cues):**
  - Keep the opponent’s arm wedged and use leg position to deny rotation.
  - Maintain “double-control” feel—don’t allow the free arm to peel your control.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Sitting for the arm without controlling rotation.  
    **Fix:** Control the turn first; then extend.

#### 6.4.3 Falling Into Reverse Triangle/Kimura (9:05–12:50)
- **Objective:** Use a reverse triangle as a control/finish platform when you lose balance during Chancery/Kimura.
- **Primary decision rule:** When falling occurs, lock the triangle as best you can, then use Kimura pressure to force predictable movement (often a forward roll).
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  - Lock reverse triangle; keep opponent wedged between legs.
  - Use Kimura to pull arm behind back; expect forward roll.
  - Follow roll to re-enter Chancery control or finish via a stable base variant.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Triangle loose and opponent turns out.  
    **Fix:** Prioritize triangle lock quality before breaking mechanics.

#### 6.4.4 Alternative To Chancery Reverse Triangle (Gyaku Sankaku) (12:50–18:22)
- **Objective:** A full triangle-lock alternative (rather than just ankles crossed) for extreme tightness.
- **Primary decision rule:** If you can step over the head and rake the knee down tight, lock a true figure-four triangle and anchor for adjustment.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Start: underhook far side + arm scissor near side (classic Side Crucifix).
  2) Step over head; **rake the knee back to the floor** in a chest-down tight position (stay close).
  3) Lock a true triangle (figure four), not just crossed ankles.
  4) Post and hunt the opponent’s foot as an **anchor**.
  5) Adjust triangle: pump/turn until the triangle is truly locked; apply strong finishing pressure.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Staying too high/too far away while locking.  
    **Fix:** Chest-down tight; rake knee down before locking.

#### 6.4.5 Troubleshooting — Opponent Grips Inside His Own Leg (18:22–22:33)
- **Objective:** Solve a common Kimura defense: opponent hides hand deep inside their own thigh/leg and closes knees.
- **Primary decision rule:** Don’t muscle the Kimura against the hidden grip—strip the grip first.
- **Gates/constraints:** Legal grip stripping is **all four fingers together** (no single-digit peeling).
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Release Kimura to a controlled one-hand hold.
  2) Go palm-up and actively locate the opponent’s **four-finger line**.
  3) Grip all four fingers along the knuckles; pull 1–2–3 to extract.
  4) Once extracted, prevent re-insertion and re-lock Kimura.
  5) Finish by putting the hand behind the back; once behind, re-gripping inside the thigh becomes impossible.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Trying to “rip” the Kimura without stripping.  
    **Fix:** Strip the grip first, then re-lock.

### 6.5 Volume 5 — Shin Pin Options + Reverse-Order Entry + North–South

#### 6.5.1 Back Leg Shin Pin Into Kimura (0:00–4:11)
- **Objective:** Use shin pins as a practical alternative when Chancery is difficult (short legs / broad shoulders).
- **Primary decision rule:** Maintain continuous elbow coverage—there must never be a moment where no shin is pinning the elbow/bicep.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Establish shin pin (front leg), then switch to back leg shin pin by briefly crossing ankles.
  2) Ensure one shin is always “sitting on” the bicep/elbow—continuous coverage.
  3) Draw opponent up onto their side while elbow remains pinned.
  4) Lock Kimura; because elbow is pinned, opponent can’t easily follow your hand movements.
  5) Use a quick sit-out; split legs wide; finish with strong Kimura mechanics.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Releasing one shin before the other covers → opponent pulls arm free.  
    **Fix:** Cross ankles briefly; maintain constant pin coverage.
  - **Error:** Trying to finish without sit-out stability.  
    **Fix:** Default to sit-out finish from shin pins.

#### 6.5.2 Back Leg Shin Pin Into Armbar (4:11–10:33)
- **Objective:** Switch from Kimura preference to a strong armbar when Kimura is blocked.
- **Primary decision rule:** Use “double trouble” logic: control both elbows to make the armbar much harder to escape.
- **Mechanics (operator cues):**
  - Step over the head with elbow still pinned.
  - Use legs to separate/control arms as you sit for the armbar.
  - Expect a strong bridge reaction—your dual-elbow control is what makes it tight.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Sitting to armbar while only controlling the attacked arm.  
    **Fix:** Control both elbows (“double trouble”) before committing.

#### 6.5.3 Front Leg Shin Pin Into Triangle (10:33–17:18)
- **Objective:** Triangle-based attacks from a front-leg pin configuration.
- **Primary decision rule:** Don’t put the knee down in the step-over phase—step the **foot** to the floor to preserve alignment.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Widen distance between opponent’s elbow and hips; avoid being toppled.
  2) Step over with knee first, then **pull shoulder off the mat**.
  3) Step the **foot** onto the floor (not the knee) to create space and correct angle.
  4) Head down, foot up; curl knee into armpit to set triangle structure.
  5) Attack options:
     - **Asphyxiation** by lifting head and knees for a tight choke pressure.
     - Fall forward to shin, grab your own shin, forehead to floor to lock a tight triangle; walk hands down to finish.
     - Fall back to lock a tight reverse/angled triangle finish (hantai sankaku style) and walk forward to strangle.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Putting knee on the floor (kills alignment).  
    **Fix:** Foot to floor; keep shoulder lifted.

#### 6.5.4 Alternative Method To Entering Side Crucifix — Pin Nearside Arm First (17:18–23:38)
- **Objective:** Reverse the order when opponent denies far-side underhook.
- **Primary decision rule:** If step 1 (underhook) stalls, switch to **reverse crossface** and pin near arm first.
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Convert crossface to **reverse crossface**.
  2) Partial sit-down toward hips (not a deep sit that invites bridging).
  3) Get hip inside the opponent’s elbow line; rise and pin near arm with shin/knee.
  4) From shoulder to bicep: place your elbow onto opponent’s sternum; rise into position.
  5) Hit a **wrist pass** to re-enter classic side crucifix (underhook restored).
  6) Transition from shin pin → arm scissor via sit-out scissor action.
  7) Post for stability; come up into Chancery; proceed with standard Chancery attacks.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Sitting too deep (bridged over).  
    **Fix:** “Nothing crazy” partial sit only.
  - **Error:** Staying in shin pin when you want Chancery.  
    **Fix:** Sit-out to arm scissor first; then rise.

#### 6.5.5 Entry To North South Strangle (23:38–29:06)
- **Objective:** When wrist-pass return isn’t available, use pinned near arm to remove the primary north–south defense and finish.
- **Primary decision rule:** North–south is easier because the near-side defensive arm is pinned under your shin.
- **Gates/constraints:**
  - Ribcage placement must force opponent’s face to look **away** from you.
  - Your strangle arm must be hand-in with **elbow out** (not elbow tucked near their head).
- **Mechanics (operator sequence):**
  1) Reverse crossface; partial sit-out; slide shin across the major defensive arm to remove it.
  2) Move closer to north–south; set strangle arm hand-in/elbow-out.
  3) Roll ribcage over cheekbone to prevent them turning toward you.
  4) Hand comes tight above shoulder; ear to sternum/chest pressure.
  5) Post inside their remaining defensive arm.
  6) Walk down; connect hands palm-to-palm; settle toward the mat into true north–south.
  7) Shoulder pressure finalizes an exceptionally tight strangle.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Allowing them to turn their face toward you.  
    **Fix:** Ribcage-to-cheekbone blockade.
  - **Error:** Elbow close to head.  
    **Fix:** Hand in, elbow out.

### 6.6 Volume 6 — Back Crucifix Link + System Summary (Technique Implementations)

#### 6.6.1 Back Crucifix Into Side Crucifix — 2 Directions Of Attack (0:00–9:26)
- **Objective:** Link chest-to-back control (Back Crucifix) into chest-to-chest attacks (Side Crucifix), creating a unified system.
- **Primary decision rule (dilemma):**
  - If opponent’s body is low/hollowed → build height and let head slip through → Side Crucifix.
  - If opponent bridges/arches to deny head slip → switch direction → rear triangle (ushiro sankaku).
- **Mechanics (operator cues):**
  - Retract feet tight for elbow/arm control.
  - Build to elbow/knees for height.
  - Hollow body to create the head-slip pathway when available.
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Forcing head slip when posture denies it.  
    **Fix:** Take the rear triangle branch immediately.

#### 6.6.2 Putting It All Together: Summarizing The Side Crucifix System (9:26–26:05)
- **Objective:** Implement the 3-step system with correct branching based on near-arm control type.
- **Primary series (default):**
  1) **Get far-side underhook**:
     - Free underhook if opponent is naive/tired (hand low).
     - Forced underhook via fake Americana / wrist pass / chain-finger (preferred).
  2) **Pin near-side arm** (hand-assisted preference):
     - Make arm long → expose wrist → pin with leg.
     - If arm is short → hand inside to bicep → knee over to pin.
     - Choose one of the three pins and be able to switch:
       - Front leg pin ↔ back leg pin ↔ arm scissor (scissor, don’t squeeze).
  3) **Enter attacks based on pin type**:
     - **Arm scissor → Chancery**:
       - Stability first (post wide; pin elbow to hip).
       - Kimura as first attack; choose 3 base variants (two knees / one knee / sit-out split).
       - Side triangle next; use elbow-down method to prevent explosive turn-ins.
     - **Back leg pin**:
       - Use “double trouble” to control both arms and draw into a tight judogatame variation.
     - **Front leg pin**:
       - Route into triangles: step foot to floor; shoulder off mat; fall forward or back to lock tight finishes.
- **Secondary series (reverse order):**
  - Reverse crossface → pin near arm first → wrist pass back to Side Crucifix → arm scissor → Chancery.
  - If wrist pass isn’t there, route directly to north–south strangle (near defensive arm removed by shin pin).
- **Common errors / fixes:**
  - **Error:** Not being able to switch between pin types.  
    **Fix:** Drill pin cycling as a retention skill, not as “three different moves.”
  - **Error:** Attacking before stability in Chancery.  
    **Fix:** Post + pin elbow first; then apply submission mechanics.

## 8.0 APPLICATION SCENARIOS

### 8.1 Side Control vs Defensive Elbows (Hand High, Elbow Tight)
- **Start:** You have chest-to-chest side control; opponent keeps elbow tight and hand high, denying easy underhook.
- **Read:** Far hand above elbow; pummel lane is not available.
- **Default:** Force the underhook (ten-finger or reverse-overhook family) → close-loop retention → proceed to near-arm pin.
- **Opponent reactions → responses:**
  - Opponent stiffens and refuses elbow separation → deepen wedge (wrist “knife edge” concept) before inserting elbow.
  - Opponent turns slightly toward you to block insertion → re-seal crossface and re-angle, then reattempt.
- **Win condition:** Far-side underhook established with closed loop; opponent cannot re-pummel.

### 8.2 Side Control vs Active Shoulder Posting (Bench-Press / Hard Frame)
- **Start:** Side control; opponent posts/pushes on your shoulder, creating an active frame.
- **Read:** Active shoulder post is present; opponent is giving you a lever.
- **Default:** Wrist pass branch → pin wrist across centerline → convert to underhook → close-loop retention.
- **Opponent reactions → responses:**
  - Opponent retracts arm quickly → follow the retraction into forced underhook methods (ten-finger/reverse-overhook).
  - Opponent bridges to create space after the pass → prioritize base (post) before advancing to pinning.
- **Win condition:** Underhook secured and retained; opponent’s frame is removed.

### 8.3 Passing Finish: You Land With Underhook Already Won
- **Start:** You complete a pass (knee cut/bodylock/tripod) and land with far-side underhook.
- **Read:** Step 1 is already done; immediate priority is retention and pin.
- **Default:** Close-loop retention immediately → select near-arm pin entry (long vs short) → choose pin type.
- **Opponent reactions → responses:**
  - Opponent tries to pummel immediately on landing → chin-to-hand + ear-to-bicep, then settle hips before pin.
  - Opponent turns away to recover guard → keep shoulder line sealed and start near-arm pinning early.
- **Win condition:** Two-elbow displacement achieved; side crucifix assembled quickly.

### 8.4 Half Guard Top: Underhook Before You Settle Side Control
- **Start:** Top half guard; you are in passing posture with access to shoulder and wrist.
- **Read:** You can win underhook here with less elbow lockdown resistance.
- **Default:** Create slight rise → wrist pass to underhook → close-loop retention → complete pass into side control with underhook.
- **Opponent reactions → responses:**
  - Opponent clamps half guard and stalls → continue head/shoulder pressure and advance knee line, then reattempt.
  - Opponent frames hard to stop shoulder line → switch to forced underhook methods once side control is reached.
- **Win condition:** You arrive in side control already holding underhook (shortens the system).

### 8.5 Turtle Top: Converting Ride to Underhook-Based Top Control
- **Start:** Opponent turtles; you have top control behind the elbows/hips.
- **Read:** Transition moments (posts, shifts) expose underhook lanes.
- **Default:** Insert underhook during transition → convert to side control with underhook secured → proceed to near-arm pin.
- **Opponent reactions → responses:**
  - Opponent sits back into you to deny angle → prioritize height and re-angle rather than forcing arm insertion.
  - Opponent posts far arm to stand → use the post moment to capture underhook and collapse posture.
- **Win condition:** Underhook is won before opponent re-establishes elbow-tight shell.

### 8.6 Near Arm Long vs Short (Choosing the Pin Entry Correctly)
- **Start:** Underhook is secured; you are beginning near-arm pinning.
- **Read:** Near arm is either long/exposed (wrist reachable) or short/glued (wrist unavailable).
- **Default:**
  - Long arm → wrist control → walk back → knee/leg pin.
  - Short arm → inside-hand/bicep control → knee-over completion.
- **Opponent reactions → responses:**
  - Opponent bends arm to “shorten” it mid-entry → switch to bicep-control pathway immediately.
  - Opponent tries to pull elbow back to hip-line → increase elbow-hip separation before stepping over.
- **Win condition:** Near elbow cannot return to hip-line; pin is established without giving up base.

### 8.7 Pin Variant Selection: Front Pin vs Back Pin vs Arm Scissor
- **Start:** Near arm is pinned; side crucifix is forming.
- **Read:** Your leg configuration determines your best hub.
- **Default:**
  - If you can get arm scissor safely → prioritize it (best feed to chancery).
  - If body type/space limits arm scissor → stay in shin pin variants (front/back) and route to their hubs.
- **Opponent reactions → responses:**
  - Opponent tries to slide elbow out as you switch pins → maintain continuous shin coverage; switch without gaps.
  - Opponent bridges to roll you → post before switching; switch only with base.
- **Win condition:** You can cycle pins without loss of elbow displacement.

### 8.8 Chancery Entry: Stabilize First vs Immediate Finish Temptation
- **Start:** You have arm scissor and are converting to chancery.
- **Read:** Opponent outputs maximal escape effort in the first moments.
- **Default:** Post wide + pin elbow to hip; ride out the stabilization window; only then attack.
- **Opponent reactions → responses:**
  - Opponent tries to turn belly-down / rise → bar across back; return them to mat; re-seat capture.
  - Opponent locks hands to block kimura → switch base (two knees → one knee → sit-out) until lever line clears.
- **Win condition:** Chancery remains stable; you can initiate kimura/triangle without losing position.

### 8.9 Balance Disruption During Chancery Attacks (Planned Continuations)
- **Start:** You attack kimura/triangle and your base is compromised.
- **Read:** Rotational/balance failure, not structural failure.
- **Default:** Route immediately to the planned fallbacks: juji / 3⁄4 juji / reverse triangle / gyaku sankaku depending on leg position.
- **Opponent reactions → responses:**
  - Opponent turns into you during triangle attempts → use elbow-first triangle doctrine; re-lock before settling.
  - Opponent rolls to relieve pressure → follow the roll to re-establish a stable finishing line.
- **Win condition:** You convert instability into a finish without giving up control.

### 8.10 Reverse-Order Entry When Underhook-First Is Stalled
- **Start:** Side control, but underhook is difficult to force.
- **Read:** Underhook-first is stalling; time-cost rising.
- **Default:** Reverse crossface → pin near arm first → wrist pass to restore classic side crucifix → arm scissor → chancery.
- **Opponent reactions → responses:**
  - Wrist pass is blocked → route to north–south strangle (near defensive arm already removed).
  - Opponent bridges to disrupt the sit/raise phase → keep partial sit; post; then re-pin.
- **Win condition:** You re-enter the primary series or finish via north–south without burning time.

### 8.11 North–South Strangle Off the Pinned Defensive Arm
- **Start:** You have near arm pinned under shin (reverse-order setup).
- **Read:** Their primary defensive arm is removed; north–south becomes unusually fast.
- **Default:** Ribcage-to-cheek pressure → hand-in elbow-out strangle arm → walk to north–south → connect and settle to finish.
- **Opponent reactions → responses:**
  - Opponent tries to look toward you → increase cheek/ribcage blockade.
  - Opponent uses remaining arm to frame → post inside that arm; keep elbow-out alignment.
- **Win condition:** Head cannot rotate; strangle pressure is applied before they can rebuild frames.

### 8.12 Back Crucifix → Side Crucifix vs Rear Triangle (The Dilemma)
- **Start:** You have back crucifix from turtle attacks.
- **Read:** Can you slip the head through by gaining height and hollowing?
- **Default (dilemma):**
  - If head slip is available → transition to side crucifix → chancery.
  - If opponent bridges/arches to deny head slip → switch to rear triangle immediately.
- **Opponent reactions → responses:**
  - Opponent tries to wiggle elbow free → keep feet retracted and elbow close to your shoulder (deny extraction).
  - Opponent wins the height battle → don’t insist; change direction to rear triangle.
- **Win condition:** You always get an upper-body finishing platform (side crucifix hub or rear triangle).

## 9.0 DRILLING PROGRESSIONS

### 9.1 Progression Ladder (Skill Acquisition Order)
1) **Underhook acquisition (forced vs free)**  
2) **Underhook retention (closed loop) under movement**  
3) **Near-arm pin completion (long-arm vs short-arm)**  
4) **Pin cycling (front ↔ back ↔ arm scissor) without gaps**  
5) **Chancery entry + stabilization window**  
6) **Primary finishes (kimura / side triangle) from stabilized chancery**  
7) **Balance-loss continuations (juji / reverse triangle variants)**  
8) **Reverse-order entry (pin first → wrist pass / north–south)**  
9) **Back crucifix link (side crucifix vs rear triangle dilemma)**

---

### 9.2 Isolation Drills (Low Variability, High Reps)

#### 9.2.1 Underhook Forcing Reps (Static Resistance)
- **Goal:** Build reliable entries vs defensive elbow.
- **Method:** 10–20 reps per side per method; increase resistance gradually.
- **Constraint:** If chest pressure breaks, reset immediately (no “reach” reps).

#### 9.2.2 Closed-Loop Retention Holds (Timed)
- **Goal:** Make retention automatic before pinning.
- **Method:** 5 x 20s holds each side with partner actively trying to pummel.
- **Constraint:** If chin-hand loop breaks, restart the timer.

#### 9.2.3 Near-Arm Pin Completions (Long vs Short)
- **Goal:** Correct selector choice under mild resistance.
- **Method:** Partner alternates long-arm vs short-arm posture every rep.
- **Constraint:** If you choose wrong path, abort and restart (don’t “force” it).

#### 9.2.4 Pin Cycling (No-Gap Switches)
- **Goal:** Switch front ↔ back ↔ arm scissor without releasing elbow displacement.
- **Method:** Slow switches first; speed only after zero-gap proficiency.
- **Constraint:** Any moment of “no pin” = fail and reset.

---

### 9.3 Game Cards (Runnable Training Games)

#### 9.3.1 Game — Forced Underhook From Side Control
- **Start:** Top side control; defender has far arm high/defensive, elbow tight.
- **Defender wins:** Re-pummels and clears the underhook attempt (elbow returns tight) OR recovers guard.
- **Passer wins:** Establishes far-side underhook with closed loop and holds 3 seconds.
- **Rules:** Passer must start from chest-to-chest; no jumping to mount unless using mount fake as part of underhook entry.
- **Reset:** On any guard recovery, underhook clear, or 3-second hold.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 45s each, switch roles each round.

#### 9.3.2 Game — Wrist Pass Branch (Active Shoulder Post)
- **Start:** Side control; defender must maintain a strong shoulder post/frame.
- **Defender wins:** Keeps post and escapes to knees or reguards.
- **Passer wins:** Completes wrist pass to underhook + closed loop, hold 3 seconds.
- **Rules:** Defender must actively push; passer may not switch to other underhook methods until one wrist-pass attempt is made.
- **Reset:** Underhook hold 3s or defender escapes position.
- **Rounds:** 5 x 60s each side.

#### 9.3.3 Game — Underhook Retention Only
- **Start:** Passer already has underhook from side control.
- **Defender wins:** Re-pummels (underhook cleared) or inserts elbow back to ribs.
- **Passer wins:** Maintains closed-loop retention for 20 seconds.
- **Rules:** Passer may not progress to pinning; only retention.
- **Reset:** Any re-pummel or 20s hold.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 30s, switch each round.

#### 9.3.4 Game — Long vs Short Pin Selector
- **Start:** Underhook secured; defender chooses long-arm (wrist exposed) or short-arm (elbow glued) at start.
- **Defender wins:** Gets elbow back to hip-line or clears pin attempt.
- **Passer wins:** Pins near arm (front pin or back pin) and holds 3 seconds.
- **Rules:** Passer must choose the correct pathway (wrist control for long; bicep control for short).
- **Reset:** 3-second pin hold or defender clears elbow line.
- **Rounds:** 8 reps each, switch every 4 reps.

#### 9.3.5 Game — Pin Cycling Triangle
- **Start:** Near arm pinned (front leg pin), underhook already secured.
- **Defender wins:** Extracts pinned arm or recovers elbow-to-hip defensive shell.
- **Passer wins:** Achieves arm scissor and holds 3 seconds.
- **Rules:** Passer must switch pins at least once before winning (front → back → scissor or front → scissor).
- **Reset:** Arm extracted or 3-second scissor hold.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 45s each side.

#### 9.3.6 Game — Chancery Stabilization Window
- **Start:** Passer begins in chancery capture position.
- **Defender wins:** Turns belly-down and frees head/shoulder line OR stands to knees and exits the capture.
- **Passer wins:** Maintains chancery with stable base for 15 seconds.
- **Rules:** Passer may not attack submissions; stabilization only (post + ride).
- **Reset:** Exit or 15-second hold.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 30s, switch each round.

#### 9.3.7 Game — Chancery to Kimura Finish
- **Start:** Chancery stabilized (partner starts at “calm” output).
- **Defender wins:** Connects hands and clears kimura line OR escapes the capture.
- **Passer wins:** Secures kimura configuration and forces a clear break position (or tap).
- **Rules:** If defender connects hands, passer must change base (two knees → one knee → sit-out) before retrying.
- **Reset:** Finish or escape/clear.
- **Rounds:** 5 x 60s each.

#### 9.3.8 Game — Chancery to Side Triangle (Elbow-First Rule)
- **Start:** Chancery stabilized; passer begins on elbow (not shoulder-flat).
- **Defender wins:** Turns into passer and recovers frames/guard.
- **Passer wins:** Locks triangle and holds 3 seconds with strangle line present.
- **Rules:** Passer must stay on elbow until triangle is locked (no early shoulder drop).
- **Reset:** 3-second lock hold or defender escapes.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 45s each side.

#### 9.3.9 Game — Balance-Loss Continuations
- **Start:** Passer attacks kimura from chancery; defender is allowed to buck/turn hard.
- **Defender wins:** Clears capture entirely.
- **Passer wins:** Converts to juji or reverse-triangle family and holds control 3 seconds (finish optional).
- **Rules:** If passer loses base, they must convert within 5 seconds (no stalling).
- **Reset:** Conversion hold 3s or escape.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 45s, switch each round.

#### 9.3.10 Game — Reverse-Order Entry (Pin First)
- **Start:** Side control; underhook is declared “blocked” (passer cannot pursue underhook-first for first 10 seconds).
- **Defender wins:** Escapes to knees/guard or clears the near-arm pin.
- **Passer wins:** Pins near arm first, then completes wrist pass to re-enter classic side crucifix and holds 3 seconds.
- **Rules:** Must use reverse crossface as the entry theme; no direct underhook attempts for first 10 seconds.
- **Reset:** 3-second hold or defender escape.
- **Rounds:** 5 x 60s each.

#### 9.3.11 Game — Reverse-Order to North–South Strangle
- **Start:** Reverse-order setup with near arm pinned under shin.
- **Defender wins:** Turns face toward passer and rebuilds frames or escapes.
- **Passer wins:** Achieves a north–south strangle configuration and holds 3 seconds (finish optional).
- **Rules:** Passer must use ribcage-to-cheek “face-away” control before connecting hands.
- **Reset:** 3-second hold or defender escape.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 45s each side.

#### 9.3.12 Game — Back Crucifix Dilemma (Side Crucifix vs Rear Triangle)
- **Start:** Back crucifix position.
- **Defender wins:** Extracts an elbow or returns to turtle with arms free.
- **Passer wins:** Either (A) transitions to side crucifix and holds 3 seconds, or (B) locks rear triangle and holds 3 seconds.
- **Rules:** Passer must choose branch based on posture read: head slip available vs denied by bridge/arch.
- **Reset:** 3-second hold or defender escapes.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 60s, switch each round.

---

### 9.4 Integration Rounds (From Side Control to Finish)
- **Phase 1 (control):** Underhook acquisition + retention only (2 minutes).
- **Phase 2 (assembly):** Add near-arm pin + pin cycling (2 minutes).
- **Phase 3 (hub):** Add chancery entry + stabilization (2 minutes).
- **Phase 4 (finish):** Add kimura/triangle/armbar finishes (2 minutes).
- **Scoring:** Passer scores only on 3-second holds at each phase gate; defender scores on clear escapes to guard/knees.

### 9.5 Benchmarks (Exit Criteria)
- **Underhook forcing:** 8/10 successful entries vs moderate resistance.
- **Retention:** 20s hold maintained 4/5 rounds vs active pummel.
- **Pin completion:** correct long/short choice 8/10 reps.
- **Pin cycling:** front→back→scissor completed with no-gap 5 times each side.
- **Chancery stabilization:** 15s hold 4/5 rounds vs explosive output.
- **Finish conversion:** reach a finish-ready kimura/triangle configuration 3 times per 5-minute integration round.

## 11.0 FAILURE PATTERNS

### 11.1 Underhook Acquisition Failures

#### F1 — “Reaching Underhook” (Chest Lifts, Space Opens)
- **Signature:** You lift your chest to insert the hand; opponent re-guards or re-pummels.
- **Root cause:** Attempting insertion without maintaining shoulder-line pressure.
- **Immediate fix:** Insert with palm-up narrow entry while keeping chest and chin tight.
- **Prevention rule:** Underhook entry must never increase space at the torso.

#### F2 — Wrist Pass Stops Midline
- **Signature:** Wrist is passed but remains in front of opponent’s torso; they re-pummel.
- **Root cause:** Partial pass; elbow not displaced behind centerline.
- **Immediate fix:** Pass fully until wrist is pinned by your structure.
- **Prevention rule:** If wrist is visible in front of their body, the pass is incomplete.

#### F3 — Forcing Underhook Against Wrong Read
- **Signature:** You attempt forced entry when hand is already low (easy pummel available).
- **Root cause:** Ignoring hand–elbow height heuristic.
- **Immediate fix:** Take free underhook when available; reserve forcing methods for defensive arms.
- **Prevention rule:** Always read hand height before committing to a method.

---

### 11.2 Underhook Retention Failures

#### F4 — Open Loop (Chin–Hand Gap)
- **Signature:** Opponent re-pummels despite you “having” the underhook.
- **Root cause:** Space between chin and underhooking hand.
- **Immediate fix:** Chin-to-hand contact; ear-to-bicep seal.
- **Prevention rule:** Do not advance to pin until the loop is closed.

#### F5 — Head Below Hips (Roll Risk)
- **Signature:** Opponent bridges and rolls you during pin attempt.
- **Root cause:** No post; head drops below hip line.
- **Immediate fix:** Post wide before advancing to near-arm pin.
- **Prevention rule:** Base before breaking.

---

### 11.3 Near-Arm Pin Failures

#### F6 — Wrong Selector (Chasing Wrist on Short Arm)
- **Signature:** You grab at wrist; elbow stays glued; opponent retracts arm.
- **Root cause:** Failure to switch to inside-hand/bicep pathway.
- **Immediate fix:** Abandon wrist; insert hand inside to bicep–shoulder junction.
- **Prevention rule:** Win elbow position before wrist control.

#### F7 — No Head Freeze During Long-Arm Walk-Back
- **Signature:** You walk backward but gain no arm length.
- **Root cause:** Opponent’s head/shoulder line not blocked.
- **Immediate fix:** Freeze near side of head before stepping across.
- **Prevention rule:** Head freeze precedes arm lengthening.

#### F8 — Gap During Pin Switching
- **Signature:** Elbow slips free during front ↔ back ↔ scissor transition.
- **Root cause:** Releasing one shin before the other covers.
- **Immediate fix:** Cross ankles briefly; maintain continuous shin coverage.
- **Prevention rule:** There must never be a moment without elbow pressure.

---

### 11.4 Arm Scissor & Chancery Entry Failures

#### F9 — Lower Leg Points Down Arm Line
- **Signature:** Opponent extracts elbow as you rise into chancery.
- **Root cause:** Foot orientation incorrect during rise.
- **Immediate fix:** Lower leg must point toward opponent’s head, not along arm.
- **Prevention rule:** Leg orientation determines elbow capture integrity.

#### F10 — Attacking Before Stabilization
- **Signature:** You attempt kimura/triangle immediately and lose balance.
- **Root cause:** Ignoring stabilization window.
- **Immediate fix:** Post wide; pin elbow to hip; wait for output drop.
- **Prevention rule:** Stabilize first, attack second.

---

### 11.5 Kimura Family Failures

#### F11 — Base Too Narrow During Break
- **Signature:** Opponent follows your movement and neutralizes torque.
- **Root cause:** Inappropriate base choice (mobile when unstable).
- **Immediate fix:** Default to two-knees-down or sit-out split for stability.
- **Prevention rule:** Base selection precedes breaking force.

#### F12 — Grip War vs Hidden Hand (Inside Thigh)
- **Signature:** You pull hard but opponent’s hand remains hidden.
- **Root cause:** Not stripping grip first.
- **Immediate fix:** Release to controlled hold; strip all four fingers; re-lock kimura.
- **Prevention rule:** Strip before torque.

---

### 11.6 Triangle & Armbar Failures

#### F13 — Early Shoulder Drop in Side Triangle
- **Signature:** Opponent turns into you and escapes before triangle is locked.
- **Root cause:** Dropping to shoulder before elbow-first stabilization.
- **Immediate fix:** Stay on elbow until triangle is secured.
- **Prevention rule:** Elbow first, shoulder second.

#### F14 — Armbar Without Dual Elbow Control
- **Signature:** Opponent rolls or stacks you during juji attempt.
- **Root cause:** Only attacking arm controlled; opposite elbow free.
- **Immediate fix:** Reassert double-elbow control before extending.
- **Prevention rule:** Double trouble before extension.

---

### 11.7 Reverse-Order Entry Failures

#### F15 — Sitting Too Deep on Reverse Crossface
- **Signature:** Opponent bridges and reverses you.
- **Root cause:** Over-committed sit without base.
- **Immediate fix:** Partial sit only; maintain post.
- **Prevention rule:** “Nothing crazy” sit—just enough to clear elbow line.

#### F16 — Forcing Wrist Pass When It Isn’t There
- **Signature:** You stall attempting wrist pass; opponent reguards.
- **Root cause:** Ignoring north–south branch.
- **Immediate fix:** Transition immediately to north–south strangle.
- **Prevention rule:** If wrist pass window closes, change branch.

---

### 11.8 North–South Strangle Failures

#### F17 — Face Turns Toward You
- **Signature:** Opponent frames and recovers as they turn into you.
- **Root cause:** Ribcage not blocking cheek/face line.
- **Immediate fix:** Ribcage pressure over cheekbone; force face away.
- **Prevention rule:** Control head rotation before connecting hands.

#### F18 — Elbow Tucked Near Head
- **Signature:** Strangle arm weak; no choking pressure.
- **Root cause:** Elbow positioned too close to opponent’s head.
- **Immediate fix:** Hand in, elbow out alignment.
- **Prevention rule:** Elbow-out is mandatory for finishing geometry.

---

### 11.9 Back Crucifix Link Failures

#### F19 — Forcing Head Slip Against Bridge
- **Signature:** You try to enter side crucifix but get shaken off.
- **Root cause:** Ignoring posture read (arch denies slip).
- **Immediate fix:** Switch immediately to rear triangle branch.
- **Prevention rule:** Dilemma logic: read first, then commit.

#### F20 — Loose Foot Retraction (Elbow Escapes)
- **Signature:** Opponent extracts elbow during back crucifix transition.
- **Root cause:** Feet not retracted tightly for elbow control.
- **Immediate fix:** Pull heels tight; keep elbow near your shoulder.
- **Prevention rule:** Foot tension maintains elbow capture.

---

### 11.10 Meta-Failure: Treating the System as Isolated Moves
- **Signature:** Techniques work in isolation but collapse in live rounds.
- **Root cause:** Not respecting the pipeline (underhook → retention → pin → hub).
- **Immediate fix:** Train and score by gates (Section 9 + 10 benchmarks).
- **Prevention rule:** Never skip a gate to “rush” a finish.

## 12.0 KEY PASSAGES

### 12.1 The Elbow Is the Gatekeeper
The opponent’s defensive system in side control is elbow-based.  
If the elbows stay close to the torso, escapes and frame recovery remain viable.  
If both elbows are displaced, upper-body submissions accelerate.

---

### 12.2 Side Crucifix Is a Bridge, Not a Position
Side Crucifix is not an aesthetic control—it is a functional bridge between pinning and finishing.  
Its value lies in shortening the path from positional dominance to submission pressure.

---

### 12.3 Two-Elbow Control Is the Core Metric
You are not “in the system” unless both elbows are displaced:
- Far elbow via underhook.
- Near elbow via leg pin.  
Anything less is transitional.

---

### 12.4 Underhook First (Default Doctrine)
The highest-percentage pathway is:
Underhook → Retention → Near-arm pin → Hub.  
Reversing the order is a contingency, not the baseline.

---

### 12.5 Read Before You Force
Hand height determines action:
- Hand low → take underhook.
- Hand high → force underhook.
- Active post → wrist pass branch.  
Decision precedes insertion.

---

### 12.6 Close the Loop Before Advancing
An underhook without a closed loop is temporary.  
Chin-to-hand and ear-to-bicep seal the structure.  
Advancing without retention invites re-pummel resets.

---

### 12.7 Win the Elbow Before the Wrist
Against skilled opponents, wrist chasing fails.  
Elbow displacement precedes reliable wrist control.  
Control hierarchy: elbow → then wrist → then break.

---

### 12.8 Pin Cycling Is Retention, Not Preference
Front pin, back pin, and arm scissor are not three separate moves.  
They are a retention cycle.  
Switching pins is how you preserve elbow displacement under resistance.

---

### 12.9 Arm Scissor Feeds the Strongest Hub
Arm scissor provides the cleanest route to chancery.  
Chancery provides the most stable control-to-finish platform.  
This is the system’s primary finishing axis.

---

### 12.10 Stabilize First, Break Second
After chancery capture, expect explosive resistance.  
Posting and elbow pinning precede torque.  
Premature breaking attempts cost position.

---

### 12.11 Base Before Breaking
Whenever both hands leave the mat, base narrows.  
If your head drops below hips, you are vulnerable to reversal.  
Stability is a prerequisite to finishing force.

---

### 12.12 Balance Loss Is a Branch, Not a Mistake
If you lose balance during a break attempt, the system routes to:
- Juji variants
- Reverse triangle variants  
Instability becomes continuation, not failure.

---

### 12.13 Reverse-Order Is a Time-Saving Contingency
If underhook-first stalls, pin near arm first.  
Restore underhook via wrist pass or route to north–south.  
The order can change, but the two-elbow requirement cannot.

---

### 12.14 Remove the Defensive Arm, Then Strangle
North–south strangles become fast when the near defensive arm is already pinned.  
Eliminate defense first; apply choke second.

---

### 12.15 Dilemma Logic Governs Transitions
From back crucifix:
- Head slips → side crucifix.
- Head denied → rear triangle.  
Correct branching prevents wasted effort.

---

### 12.16 Never Skip a Gate
Underhook.  
Retention.  
Pin.  
Hub.  
Finish.  

Skipping gates produces fragile control.  
Sequential integrity produces reliable finishes.

## 13.0 TERMINOLOGY

### 13.1 Core Positions & States
- **Side Control / Side Pin:** Chest-to-chest top pin where opponent’s hips/shoulders are controlled and frames are suppressed.
- **Side Crucifix:** A two-component control state where both opponent elbows are displaced:
  - Far elbow controlled via far-side underhook.
  - Near elbow controlled via leg-based pin.
- **Back Crucifix:** Chest-to-back control where legs and/or hooks trap an arm while controlling rotation; used here as a bridge to side crucifix or rear triangle.
- **Chancery:** A leg-based head-and-arm capture (“legs chancery”) used as a premier hub for upper-body submissions from side crucifix.

### 13.2 Primary Control Concepts
- **Elbow Structure:** The opponent’s defensive frame where elbows stay tight to torso/hip-line; breaking elbow structure is the system’s main control objective.
- **Two-Elbow Displacement:** The success condition of the system: both elbows pulled away from torso so escapes and frames collapse.
- **Closed Loop (Underhook Retention):** The retention state where chin-to-hand and ear-to-bicep eliminate the pummel window and seal shoulder line.
- **Pummel Window:** The space/opportunity that allows the opponent to reinsert an arm, recover elbow structure, or clear the underhook.

### 13.3 Underhook Acquisition Methods (Names Used Here)
- **Free Underhook:** Underhook available because opponent’s hand is low (hand ≤ elbow).
- **Forced Underhook:** Underhook acquired against defensive hand-high posture using a dedicated forcing method.
- **Ten-Finger Method:** Palm-up insertion to wrist–bicep space with ten-finger grip to lever elbow outward and complete the underhook.
- **Reverse Overhook Method:** Wrist “knife wedge” under elbow line to open space and convert into underhook.
- **Wrist Pass:** Passing the opponent’s wrist across centerline (especially vs active shoulder post) to create underhook access.
- **Mount Fake / Mount Threat:** Using mount entry attempts to create parallel alignment and provoke defensive posting that opens underhook lanes.
- **Fake American Lock:** A believable Americana threat used to provoke arm positioning that surrenders underhook access.

### 13.4 Near-Arm Pinning Terms
- **Near-Side Arm:** The opponent arm closest to you in side control; the arm you pin with the legs to complete side crucifix.
- **Long-Arm Case:** Near arm is extended/wrist available; wrist control is viable.
- **Short-Arm Case:** Near arm is glued/tucked; wrist is not realistically available; bicep-control pathway is required.
- **Wrist Control (Long-Arm Control):** Controlling inside wrist (end of lever) to prevent elbow following and enable pin insertion.
- **Bicep Control (Short-Arm Control):** Inside-hand control to bicep/upper-arm junction to lever elbow away from torso for pin completion.
- **Tripod Pin Completion:** Rising posture to drive knee/leg over the arm while maintaining base with posts.

### 13.5 Pin Variants
- **Front Leg Pin:** Near arm pinned primarily by lead/front leg configuration.
- **Back Leg Shin Pin:** Near arm pinned by the back leg’s shin; often accessed by briefly crossing ankles to maintain continuous coverage.
- **Arm Scissor:** Knee pinch / scissor configuration that traps arm and shoulder line; highest control and best feed to chancery.
- **Pin Cycling:** Switching between pin variants to maintain elbow displacement without gaps; treated as retention, not preference.
- **Continuous Shin Coverage:** The rule that at least one shin must maintain pressure on the pinned arm during transitions to prevent extraction.

### 13.6 Submission Families (As Used Here)
- **Kimura Family:** Kimura grip and variations used as primary finishes from chancery or shin pin structures.
- **Dorsal Kimura:** A kimura breaking configuration emphasizing dorsal/behind-the-back pressure line.
- **Sit-Out Kimura:** Kimura finished by sitting out and splitting legs wide for stability and torque.
- **Juji Gatame (Armbar):** Straight armbar family used especially as a balance-loss continuation.
- **¾ Juji Gatame:** Armbar variant used when full square alignment is blocked.
- **Yoko Sankaku (Side Triangle):** Side triangle strangle; entered with “elbow-first” stabilization doctrine.
- **Gyaku Sankaku (Reverse Triangle):** Reverse/alternative triangle configuration used as a continuation when balance shifts.
- **Ushiro Sankaku (Rear Triangle):** Rear triangle used as the alternative branch when head-slip entry from back crucifix is denied.
- **North–South Strangle:** Choke finished from north–south alignment; emphasized here when the near defensive arm has been removed/pinned.

### 13.7 Tactical / Diagnostic Terms
- **Stabilization Window:** The short period after a major capture (especially chancery) where opponent outputs maximal escape effort.
- **Base Before Breaking:** Priority rule: stabilize posture and posts before committing both hands to a break attempt.
- **Direction-of-Failure Routing:** Troubleshooting rule:
  - Structural failure (re-pummel / elbow returns) → return to retention and elbow displacement.
  - Rotational/balance failure (roll threat) → post and route to armbar/triangle continuations.
- **Gate:** A required condition in the pipeline (underhook, retention, pin, hub) that should not be skipped.
- **Win Condition (Scenario):** The concrete outcome that ends a drill/game (e.g., 3-second hold, lock achieved, finish-ready configuration).
