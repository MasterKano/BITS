## 2.0 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### System thesis
- **Position-first defense:** win **inside position (feet + knees)** to deny clamp + rotation before chasing “escape.”
- **Leg locks = control problem:** solve **legs/hips structure** first; grips are secondary unless they change structure.

### Universal defensive workflow (repeats across all volumes)
- **Identify**
  - Determine **Ashi family** (Irimi / Outside / Top Oshiro / Cross / Inside / 50/50 / Mutual).
  - Determine **live sub line** (Achilles / Toe Hold / Heel Hook / Knee Bar).
  - Determine **stage** (early lock vs bite forming vs breaking pressure).
- **Stabilize safety**
  - **Hide heel**, **deny rotation**, **protect knee line**.
  - **Build base** only when it does not feed rotation/knee-line loss.
- **Break structure**
  - **Re-pummel** to reclaim inside position.
  - **Separate crossed ankles** / disrupt clamps.
  - **Second-leg interference** (free-foot blocks bite/rotation).
  - **Sit-over-a-leg** to collapse lever and finishing angles.
- **Extract**
  - Use the lowest-risk ladder: **re-pummel -> build to knee -> build to stand**.
  - **Rolling is a separation tool**, not a default:
    - **Half-roll** first; **full/multiple rolls** only when separation is predictable.
- **End game**
  - **Clear entanglement + reset** (neutral or stable base).
  - Avoid “half-out” states where knee line/heel can be re-captured.

### Decisive variables (what the system optimizes)
- **Inside-position “real estate”** (feet + knees).
- **Knee-line depth** (threshold that spikes risk across HH/Toe/KB).
- **Rotational alignment** (especially heel hook mechanics).

### Multi-leg hierarchy (Cross Ashi + 50/50 emphasis)
- **Primary leg:** current breaking target (knee line + rotation + heel/foot exposure).
- **Secondary leg:** clamp/lever enabling control.
- Operational rule: **solve the secondary-leg control to free the primary leg** (uncross ankles, disrupt top-leg clamp, free-foot block, sit-over-a-leg).

### Dilemma handling (recurring decision rule)
- When dilemmas appear (e.g., toe hold vs knee bar from Top Oshiro; Achilles hand-position dilemmas; post/reap modifiers):
  - Prioritize the **leg/hip control hub** first.
  - Use hand fighting **only** insofar as it breaks structure.

### Submission-line rules (high-level)
- **Achilles**
  - Keep opponent **belly-up**; deny belly-down finishing mechanics.
  - Manage **boot/forward pressure**; use **step-over / roll-through** to separate and exit.
  - Treat **reap/post-ashi** as structural modifiers to undo before “standard” extraction.
- **Toe hold**
  - Protect foot early; disrupt **figure-four** + wrist line.
  - Use **free-foot interference**; then **sit-over-a-leg** to collapse leverage.
  - Roll only when it **creates separation** (half-roll first).
- **Heel hook**
  - **Hide heel + deny rotation** immediately.
  - **Heel slip early** when timing exists; **second-leg interference** to deny a stable bite.
  - Manage **inverted HH threats** (notably from Outside Ashi).
- **Knee bar**
  - Treat **orientation (wrong vs correct side)** + knee-line preservation as decisive.
  - Use **Top Oshiro** logic (knee slip + dilemma counters) to resolve before extraction.

### Preferred outcomes (what “success” looks like)
- **Re-pummel to neutral** when inside recovery is available.
- **Build to knee -> stand** to clear when base is stable.
- **Transfer to safer Ashi** (often **Outside**) when a direct exit is not clean.
- In **50/50 + Mutual**, emphasize:
  - disciplined heel hiding,
  - center-line extraction mechanics,
  - separation of crossed ankles,
  - exiting when opponent unlocks (forced via controlled counter-threats).

## 4.0 CORE CONCEPTS (EXPANDED / HIGH DENSITY)

### 4.1 Inside-position dominance (feet + knees = “real estate”)
- Core thesis: **position defense beats submission defense**; if you lose inside position you are forced into late-stage hand/foot saving.
- “Inside” is not abstract: it is **where your knees and feet live relative to their hip line and knee line** (their clamp + rotation engine).
- Inside-position recovery is the **default win condition**: re-pummel your legs back inside so their structure collapses.
- Inside position governs everything: it dictates whether **heel access, toe exposure, knee-line capture, and rotation** are possible.
- Practical objective: **reclaim your knees/feet inside their legs** before attempting to stand/step/roll.

### 4.2 Heel safety (heel hiding + rotation denial)
- Priority order on HH lines: **hide heel -> stop rotation -> separate legs** (not the reverse).
- Heel hiding means: **remove heel presentation** and deny the “bite” that creates rotational leverage.
- Rotation denial is proactive: do not allow your hip/knee/ankle to be aligned into finishing rotation.
- **Heel slip timing rule:** attempt slips early (before bite is sealed); if bite is sealed, shift to **control-break + separation** rather than frantic slipping.
- Safety metric: if they can freely rotate you and your heel is exposed, you are in “late defense.”

### 4.3 Knee line management (threshold concept)
- Knee line is the decisive threshold: once your knee is captured deep past their knees/hips, your exposure increases across **HH/Toe/KB**.
- Primary goal: **keep knee line out or moving out** while you break their clamps.
- Your escape choices must respect knee line: do not roll/step in ways that **feed the knee deeper**.
- Knee line is managed by:
  - **separation** (uncrossing their ankles / removing their clamp),
  - **wedges/frames** (leg positioning, sit-over-a-leg),
  - **base** (knee/stand when safe) to prevent being folded.

### 4.4 Primary vs secondary leg (control hierarchy)
- Multi-leg entanglements are solved by hierarchy:
  - **Primary leg:** the leg they are actively breaking (knee line + rotation + heel/foot exposure).
  - **Secondary leg:** the lever/clamp that enables control (crossed ankles, top leg clamp, lace, etc.).
- Rule: **solve the secondary leg problem to free the primary leg.**
- This explains recurring tactics:
  - separating crossed ankles,
  - free-foot interference (second-leg block),
  - sit-over-a-leg to collapse leverage,
  - re-pummeling to change which leg is “primary.”

### 4.5 Separation-first rule (structural win condition)
- “Escape” is not a single move; it is **creating separation** such that their legs can no longer maintain clamp + rotation.
- Separation targets are consistent:
  - **separating crossed ankles**, and/or
  - **getting your legs on different lines** so their clamp cannot travel with you.
- Decision rule: **do not roll/step “with the lock”** if the motion is not actively producing separation.
- Late defense becomes safe only when separation is achieved; until then, treat everything as **positional firefighting**.

### 4.6 Base-building ladder (safety engine)
- Default ladder repeats throughout the system: **re-pummel -> build to knee -> build to stand**.
- Base is not “stand up at all costs”; it is **standing/kneeing only when you can base without conceding heel rotation or knee-line capture**.
- Building to a knee is a transitional base that:
  - stabilizes hips,
  - reduces rotational freedom,
  - gives stepping angles (high-step/forward/back-step) without feeding the knee line.
- Standing becomes the final clearing phase **after** enough control is broken.

### 4.7 Control-breaking tools (mechanical primitives)
- The technique set is built from a small set of primitives:
  - **re-pummel** (inside-position recovery),
  - **separate crossed ankles** (remove clamp),
  - **second-leg interference** (free foot blocks bite/rotation),
  - **sit-over-a-leg** (collapse lever + deny finishing angles),
  - **step-out mechanics** (high-step / forward-step / back-step),
  - **transfer mechanics** (move into a safer Ashi rather than “force an exit”).
- Conceptual rule: **break the clamp before you chase the leg.**

### 4.8 Submission-line priorities (what “safe” looks like per line)
- Achilles:
  - Primary rule: keep opponent **belly-up**; deny belly-down finishing mechanics.
  - Manage boot/forward pressure; use **step-over / roll-through** to separate, then clear.
  - Reap/post variants are **structural modifiers**: undo them before relying on standard exits.
- Toe hold:
  - Early: protect foot; deny clean figure-four + wrist line.
  - Mid: **free-foot interference** to disrupt finishing leverage.
  - Late: **sit-over-a-leg** to reduce torque; roll is used to separate, not to “win a spin.”
- Heel hook:
  - Hide heel immediately; deny rotational alignment.
  - Use **second-leg interference** and early heel slip when timing exists.
  - Separation is the win; do not accept prolonged rotation in exchange for “motion.”
- Knee bar:
  - Orientation + knee line decide outcome; treat “wrong side vs correct side” as a core classification.
  - Solve hips/leg alignment (Top Oshiro logic) before hand-fighting.

### 4.9 Roll taxonomy (when and why to roll)
- Rolls are ranked by commitment and risk:
  - **Half-roll**: first-choice roll when it creates separation without prolonged exposure.
  - **Full roll**: higher commitment; used when separation is predictable.
  - **Multiple rolls**: last resort; used when continued rotation is the only path to break clamp/separate.
- Roll rule: **roll only when you can separate legs during/after the roll**; otherwise you are rotating inside a finishing structure.
- “Attempting to roll with the lock” is treated as a common error unless it changes the structural relationship.

### 4.10 Transfer-as-defense (move to safer Ashi)
- The system treats transfers as a legitimate defensive outcome:
  - Move from high-risk alignment into a **safer entanglement** where you can re-pummel/extract.
- Preferred pattern: **transfer to Outside Ashi** as a stabilizing “safety valve” when heel threats persist.
- Transfer is not stalling; it is **re-positioning into a configuration where separation and base are attainable**.

### 4.11 Symmetry management (50/50 + Mutual Ashi)
- Symmetric positions are not “neutral”; they are **two-way threat environments** requiring discipline.
- 50/50 core mechanics:
  - **heel hiding** remains first priority,
  - **center-chest extraction** is a primary mechanical cue (extract along the center line rather than sideways fighting),
  - **separate crossed ankles** to remove clamp,
  - **extract when they unlock** (unlock events create the safest exit window),
  - build to knee/stand only when stability is real.
- Mutual Ashi:
  - Avoid stalemate turning into a race; use **controlled threats** to induce unlocks and create separation.
  - Objective remains: **force structural separation**, then clear and reset.

### 4.12 Dilemma management (control hub > hand fighting)
- When faced with dilemmas (e.g., toe hold vs knee bar options, hand-up/hand-down Achilles problems), the principle is:
  - solve **legs + hips control hub** first,
  - then address grips as a secondary layer.
- Hand fighting without changing the leg/hip structure is treated as **low-leverage** and time-wasting.

### 4.13 End-game definition (what “success” is)
- Success is not “survived the moment”; it is:
  - **clear the entanglement**, then
  - **reset to neutral** or establish a stable base/position (knee/stand),
  - avoid remaining in a compromised half-exit where knee line/heel can be re-captured.
- End-game discipline: do not chase offense while still entangled unless the threat is specifically used to **force unlock/separation**.


## 5.0 SYSTEM OVERVIEW (HIGH DENSITY / OPERATIONAL)

### 5.1 Defensive workflow template (no-sub -> sub-on -> extraction/end game)
- **Stage 0 — No-sub (position forming)**
  - **Goal:** prevent clamp+rotation engine from forming.
  - **Win conditions:** inside position reclaimed; knee line not captured; heel not presented.
  - **Default actions:** foot/heel concealment + re-pummel early; deny knee-line entry; build posture/base.
- **Stage 1 — Sub-on (bite forming / pressure starting)**
  - **Goal:** stop escalation from “control” to “finish.”
  - **Priority order (non-negotiable):**
    1. **Hide heel / protect foot** (deny bite alignment)
    2. **Deny rotation** (stop hip/knee line being turned)
    3. **Protect knee line** (do not feed depth while moving)
    4. **Break structure** (clamp/ankles/top leg/secondary lever)
  - **Structure breakers (repeat across volumes):**
    - **Re-pummel** (reclaim inside real estate)
    - **Separate crossed ankles** (remove clamp)
    - **Second-leg interference** (free foot blocks bite/rotation)
    - **Sit-over-a-leg** (collapse lever + reduce finishing angles)
- **Stage 2 — Extraction (escape mechanics)**
  - **Goal:** remove legs from their system with lowest exposure.
  - **Preferred ladder:** **re-pummel -> build to knee -> build to stand**
  - **Leg hierarchy rule:** when two-leg control exists, **extract secondary -> extract primary** (creates window).
  - **Locomotion tools:** high-step / forward-step / back-step when they *change structure* (don’t “run with the lock”).
  - **Roll policy (separation-only):**
    - **Half-roll** first (lowest commitment roll that can separate)
    - **Full roll** only if separation is predictable
    - **Multiple rolls** last resort
    - **Hard rule:** never roll if it preserves the clamp and rotates you inside the finish.
- **Stage 3 — End game (clear + reset)**
  - **Goal:** do not remain half-free; prevent immediate re-capture.
  - **Exit criteria:** clamp broken + bite cleared + knee line safe + legs disentangled.
  - **If no clean exit:** **transfer to safer Ashi** (often Outside) and restart workflow.

### 5.2 Positional progression: Irimi -> Outside -> Top Oshiro -> Cross -> Inside -> 50/50 -> Mutual
- **Irimi (foundation):** inside-position battle + base ladder; primary exits via re-pummel and knee/stand clears.
- **Outside (hub):** ankle separation + inverted HH threat management; decisions between extract vs re-pummel; feed/defeed symmetry if forced.
- **Top Oshiro (KB hub):** knee slip + orientation classification (wrong vs correct side); solve dilemmas (toe hold vs KB) structurally.
- **Cross (2-leg hierarchy hub):** primary vs secondary leg definition; leg lace defense; secondary-first extraction; transfer to 50/50 as pathway.
- **Inside (HH priority hub):** heel hide immediately; separate/extract; common safety valve is transfer to Outside; roll-to-change-leg-position when needed.
- **50/50 (symmetry hub):** heel hiding + center-line extraction; separate ankles; exit on unlock events; passover to Outside.
- **Mutual (symmetry escalation):** avoid stalemate becoming a race; force unlock/separation; clear/reset.

### 5.3 State transitions: seated -> build-to-knee -> build-to-stand (where applicable)
- **Seated**
  - **Use when:** clamp not yet broken enough to stand safely.
  - **Seated goals:** re-pummel; separate ankles; free-foot interference; sit-over-a-leg; deny rotation/knee-line feed.
- **Build to knee**
  - **Gate:** heel/foot protected + rotation controlled + knee line not feeding deeper.
  - **Purpose:** create stable base that reduces rotational freedom and enables step clears.
  - **Common outputs:** high-step/forward/back-step; knee-down escape options; safer transfers.
- **Build to stand**
  - **Gate:** structure broken such that standing clears rather than amplifies finish.
  - **Purpose:** final clearance phase (step out, disengage, reset).
  - **Caution:** standing while clamp/rotation remains can increase finishing force against you.

### 5.4 Counter-offense integration (threats to force unlocks/extractions)
- **Principle:** counter offense is a **structure-change tool**, not a submission race while entangled.
- **Valid counter outcomes (must create one quickly):**
  - opponent **uncrosses ankles** / loosens clamp
  - opponent **repositions feet** (loses bite alignment)
  - opponent **changes hips** (knee-line window opens)
  - opponent **posts/rebases** (control integrity drops)
- **Common use cases:** controlled toe-hold threats in symmetry (esp. 50/50) to induce unlocks and open center-line extraction.
- **Risk rules:**
  - never sacrifice heel safety or knee line to “threaten”
  - if threat does not change structure fast, revert to break-control -> extract -> clear

- **Key actions:** transfer cleanly -> immediately prioritize heel hiding + ankle separation -> extract on unlock windows.

#### 6.8.17 Attempting to roll with the lock
- **Trigger:** temptation to roll while clamp remains intact.
- **Objective:** define as a caution case: rolling without separation is low percentage/high risk.
- **Rule:** only roll if the roll **breaks structure and separates legs** during/after the motion.

#### 6.8.18 Countering far-hip ashi
- **Trigger:** opponent shifts to far-hip control variant that changes angles and blocks standard exits.
- **Objective:** re-orient hips/legs to remove far-hip leverage and reopen separation pathways.
- **Key actions:** address the hip-control lever first; then re-pummel/separate/extract.

#### 6.8.19 End game (Cross Ashi overall)
- **Trigger:** separation achieved and primary/secondary legs cleared (or transferred safely and cleared).
- **Objective:** reset to neutral or stable base; prevent immediate re-entry to Cross/Inside.
- **Key actions:** disengage; stand/step clear when stable; re-pummel if any re-attachment occurs.

## 8.0 APPLICATION SCENARIOS

### 8.1 Early defense (before lock is established)
- **Scenario:** opponent is entering Ashi but has not consolidated clamp/bite (your heel/foot still mostly “free”).
- **Primary objective:** deny attachment points (heel/foot + knee line) so the position never becomes “sub-on.”
- **Operational priorities:**
  - Win **inside position** immediately (feet + knees back inside their frame).
  - Keep **heel hidden** and foot “quiet” (do not rotate into their bite path).
  - Deny **knee-line depth** (do not allow knee to travel deep past their knees/hips).
  - Choose movement that breaks structure: re-pummel first; step only if it changes clamp.
- **Primary outputs:** re-pummel to neutral; build to knee/stand only after clamp integrity drops.

### 8.2 Late defense (breaking pressure present)
- **Scenario:** bite is established and pressure/rotation is live (Achilles extension pressure; toe hold figure-four torque; heel hook bite + rotation; knee bar alignment).
- **Primary objective:** stop escalation (rotation/extension) and create separation without compounding damage.
- **Operational priorities (in order):**
  1. **Protect heel/foot** (deny bite alignment or reduce it if present).
  2. **Deny rotation/extension** (do not move in a way that accelerates the break).
  3. **Recover knee line** (knee slip/structure break before “escape motion”).
  4. **Break clamp** (sep ankles, second-leg interference, sit-over-a-leg).
  5. **Extract** via lowest-risk ladder (re-pummel -> knee -> stand; roll only to separate).
- **Primary outputs:** separation achieved -> immediate clear/reset; if not achievable -> transfer to safer Ashi (often Outside) and restart.

### 8.3 Slippery/greasy opponent adjustments
- **Scenario:** opponent’s legs are hard to “catch” structurally; they re-attach quickly; transitions are fast.
- **Primary objective:** increase your structural reliability (wedges/base) rather than chasing with hands.
- **Operational priorities:**
  - Emphasize **base-building** earlier (knee base) once heel/rotation safety is established.
  - Use **sit-over-a-leg** and **ankle separation** as primary clamp breakers (higher friction, less precision).
  - Reduce “big movement” attempts that allow re-attachment; prefer re-pummel loops and short step clears.
  - End game discipline: clear fully before disengaging posture (no half-outs).

### 8.4 Staying “in the pocket” vs disengage-to-neutral
- **Scenario:** you can either (a) stay engaged to solve structure, or (b) disengage/reset if separation opens.
- **Decision rule:** disengage only after **separation** and **knee-line safety** exist; otherwise you remain in the pocket.
- **Stay in the pocket when:**
  - clamp is still intact or tracking,
  - heel exposure/rotation is still live,
  - knee line is still captured (movement risks amplifying finish).
- **Disengage-to-neutral when:**
  - ankles separated / clamp broken,
  - bite disrupted and rotation denied,
  - leg(s) extracted cleanly (or you have stable stand with legs clear).

### 8.5 Defensive-to-offensive transition (counter locks to force unlocks)
- **Scenario:** you are entangled (often in 50/50 or mutual-like symmetry) and need the opponent to unlock to extract.
- **Primary objective:** use controlled threats to create a **structural change** (unlock, hip shift, clamp loosening), not to race submissions.
- **Operational constraints:**
  - Threat only if it preserves **heel safety** and **knee-line safety**.
  - If threat does not cause immediate structural change, revert to break-control -> extract.
- **Typical outputs:**
  - opponent unlocks/adjusts -> you extract on the unlock window,
  - passover to Outside -> clear/reset.



## 9.0 DRILLING PROGRESSIONS

### 9.1 Inside-position pummeling (static -> resisted)
- **Goal:** make inside-position recovery (re-pummel) a default reflex under pressure.
- **Phase 1 — Static patterning**
  - Start: controlled Irimi-style entanglement with minimal resistance.
  - Task: pummel knees/feet back inside; reset when inside is won.
  - Constraint: no stepping/rolling until inside is re-established.
- **Phase 2 — Positional resistance**
  - Start: same position; partner actively clamps to deny inside recovery.
  - Task: win inside + separate clamp (ankles/top leg) using interference/sit-over mechanics.
  - Constraint: score only when inside is regained and clamp integrity is reduced.
- **Phase 3 — Live re-attachment cycles**
  - Start: partner allowed to re-clamp during your exit attempts.
  - Task: re-pummel loop (exit attempt -> re-attach -> re-pummel -> continue ladder).
  - Constraint: reset if you “run with the lock” (step without structure change).

### 9.2 Positional starts by ashi + objective constraints
- **Positions:** Irimi, Outside, Top Oshiro, Cross, Inside, 50/50, Mutual.
- **Objective constraints (force correct priorities):**
  - Defender can only “win” by: separation + clear/reset (or transfer to specified safer node then clear).
  - Attacker can only “win” by: consolidating clamp + progressing to a defined submission stage.
- **Round design:**
  - Short rounds (20–60s) focused on one escape engine (e.g., ankle separation in Outside; knee slip in Top Oshiro).
  - Reset criteria: once defender clears or attacker achieves the defined consolidation.

### 9.3 Build-to-knee / build-to-stand ladders
- **Goal:** link seated structure breaks to safe base transitions.
- **Knee ladder drill**
  - Start: seated entanglement with clamp partially intact.
  - Task: break structure (re-pummel/sep/interference/sit-over) -> build to knee -> hold stability for count -> clear.
  - Failure reset: knee line deepens or heel becomes exposed during transition.
- **Stand ladder drill**
  - Start: clamp already weakened (simulate late-stage success).
  - Task: build to stand -> step clear -> reset to neutral.
  - Constraint: partner tries to re-clamp during the stand; defender must re-pummel rather than force stand.

### 9.4 Timed survival + escape-to-neutral rounds
- **Goal:** preserve safety under pressure while executing the workflow.
- **Format A — Survival gate**
  - Attacker applies progressive pressure (Achilles/Toe/HH/KB lines).
  - Defender must maintain: heel hiding, rotation denial, knee-line protection for a time window.
- **Format B — Escape gate**
  - After survival window, defender must produce separation + clear/reset within time.
  - Scoring: survival maintained + clean exit = full; survival but no exit = partial; any “do-not” violation = fail.
- **Do-not enforcement:**
  - “Run with the lock” (stepping while clamp intact) = immediate reset.
  - “Roll with the lock” (rolling without separation) = immediate reset.

### 9.5 Counter-offense prompts (toe-hold/HH threats as tools)
- **Goal:** train controlled threats as structure-change tools (unlock creation), especially in symmetry.
- **Prompt design (symmetry-focused):**
  - Start: 50/50 or Mutual with stable clamp.
  - Defender goal: create an **unlock event** then extract on the window.
  - Defender allowed: controlled toe-hold/HH threats only if heel/knee-line safety is preserved.
- **Scoring:**
  - Win = opponent unlocks/adjusts -> defender extracts and clears.
  - Lose = defender threatens but sacrifices heel/knee line; or threat fails to produce structural change and defender stalls.
- **Constraint:** threats must be followed immediately by extraction attempts (no threat “hunting” while still trapped).

  - Scoring: clear+reset = win; transfer to safer node then clear = win; any do-not violation = loss.


## 11.0 FAILURE PATTERNS

### 11.1 Losing inside position (feet/knees)
- **Pattern:** defender accepts outside feet/knees while trying to step/roll away.
- **Why it fails:** opponent’s clamp can track; isolation and rotation become easy; defender is forced into late-stage defense.
- **Common triggers:**
  - stepping before re-pummeling
  - attempting to stand without inside recovery
  - chasing grips instead of reclaiming leg position
- **Corrections:**
  - reset priority to inside real estate: re-pummel immediately
  - treat “inside won” as a gate before base-building or movement
  - use second-leg interference to reopen pummel windows

### 11.2 Heel exposure errors (toe/hip alignment, dangling foot)
- **Pattern:** heel is presented during transitions; defender rotates foot/hip in a way that feeds the bite.
- **Why it fails:** HH bite forms early; rotation becomes live; late heel slips become low-percentage.
- **Common triggers:**
  - turning while heel is exposed
  - rolling without first hiding heel
  - standing with foot line uncontrolled
- **Corrections:**
  - hide heel as the first response (before “escape motion”)
  - deny rotation, then break clamp, then separate
  - use second-leg interference to prevent bite consolidation

### 11.3 Wrong-hip alignment vs controlled leg
- **Pattern:** hips align to the opponent’s finishing corridor (Achilles belly-down drift; knee bar correct-side extension lane).
- **Why it fails:** defender’s body orientation amplifies finish mechanics.
- **Common triggers:**
  - allowing belly-down rotation on Achilles lines
  - failing to classify knee-bar orientation (wrong vs correct side) in Top Oshiro
  - stepping/rolling into the extension lane
- **Corrections:**
  - enforce belly-up rule in Achilles contexts
  - classify orientation early; prioritize knee slip and hip re-alignment
  - treat alignment correction as a prerequisite to extraction

### 11.4 Over-rolling / rolling without separating ashi
- **Pattern:** defender rolls while clamp/bite remains intact (“roll with the lock”).
- **Why it fails:** rotation happens inside the finish; damage risk increases; opponent rides the roll.
- **Common triggers:**
  - panic response to pressure
  - misunderstanding rolls as “escape moves” rather than separation tools
  - rolling when ankles are still crossed or structure is intact
- **Corrections:**
  - roll only on a separation pathway; half-roll before full/multi
  - break clamp first (ankle separation, sit-over, interference)
  - if roll does not separate, stop and return to structure-breaking

### 11.5 “Defend-only” mindset (no threats -> opponent cycles attacks freely)
- **Pattern:** defender hand-fights and survives but never forces structural change; opponent re-clamps and cycles attacks.
- **Why it fails:** defense becomes reactive; attacker controls tempo and re-attachment opportunities.
- **Common triggers:**
  - refusing to transfer when needed (staying in bad symmetry)
  - never using controlled counter threats to induce unlocks
  - clearing “almost” then relaxing (half-out end games)
- **Corrections:**
  - define success as clear+reset; enforce end-game discipline
  - use counter-offense only to force unlock/separation (especially in 50/50)
  - prefer transfer-to-safer-Ashi when direct exit is not clean, then restart workflow


## 12.0 KEY PASSAGES

### 12.1 High-leverage rules/quotes (inside position, belly-up rule, etc.)

**Position-first axioms**
- “Everything starts with inside position: feet and knees inside their frame.”
- “Solve the position before you solve the submission.”
- “If you can re-pummel, re-pummel. It is the safest reset.”
- “Break the clamp before you chase the leg.”
- “Separation is the win condition; extraction is the result.”

**Inside-position and structure control**
- “Inside ‘real estate’ is your knees and feet—if they own it, they own your rotation.”
- “When the clamp can track you, movement doesn’t escape; it just moves the problem.”
- “If you’re outside with both feet, you’re late—go back inside or expect to defend the finish.”

**Heel safety and rotation denial**
- “Heel safety is first: hide the heel before you move.”
- “Stop rotation before you try to leave.”
- “Early heel slip is timing; late heel slip is hope—change structure instead.”

**Knee-line threshold**
- “Knee line is a threshold—once it’s deep, everything gets riskier.”
- “Recover knee line before you commit to steps or rolls.”

**Achilles orientation rule**
- “Achilles rule: keep them belly up; belly down is where they finish.”
- “Step-over and roll-through are separation tools—use them to break structure, then clear.”

**Two-leg hierarchy (Cross / complex entanglements)**
- “Primary leg is the break target; secondary leg is the lever that enables it.”
- “Solve the secondary leg to free the primary.”
- “If you can extract secondary first, do it—windows appear immediately.”

**Symmetry (50/50 + Mutual)**
- “Symmetry is not neutral; discipline beats speed.”
- “Create an unlock and extract on the unlock.”
- “Passover to Outside is the clean channel when the structure allows it.”

**Transfer-as-defense**
- “Transfers are not failure; they are safer restarts.”
- “Move to a safer Ashi when direct exit isn’t clean—then run the ladder.”

### 12.2 Safety warnings (roll risk, knee protection)

**Roll discipline (separation-only)**
- “Do not roll with the lock.”
- “Roll only if the roll produces separation.”
- “Half-roll first; full/multiple rolls are last-resort tools.”

**Standing/base discipline**
- “Do not stand while bite + rotation is still live; standing can amplify the finish.”
- “Build to knee and standing are gates: if structure isn’t broken, base becomes a liability.”
- “If the clamp is intact, stepping is often just ‘running with the lock.’”

**Heel-hook specific hazards**
- “If heel is exposed and rotation is live, you are already late—hide heel and deny rotation first.”
- “If the bite is sealed, stop trying to ‘slip’ and start breaking structure.”

**Toe-hold specific hazards**
- “If the figure-four consolidates, your job is leverage collapse (sit-over + interference), not hand fighting alone.”
- “Rolls that preserve the figure-four are rotations inside the finish.”

**Knee-bar specific hazards**
- “If you don’t know orientation (wrong vs correct side), assume danger and solve alignment immediately.”
- “Knee line first: if it’s captured, treat extraction attempts as secondary.”

**End-game hazards**
- “Half-out is not out.”
- “Most failures happen after the escape—when you stop clearing and they re-capture.”

### 12.3 Strategy frames (pocket vs disengage; attack/defense balance)

**Pocket vs disengage**
- “Stay in the pocket until structure breaks; disengage only after separation.”
- “If the clamp tracks you, you’re still in the pocket even if you moved.”
- “Disengage is earned: separation + knee-line safety + heel safety.”

**The ladder frame (default plan)**
- “Defense is a ladder: re-pummel -> knee base -> standing clear.”
- “When exits fail, the system resets to re-pummel, not improvisation.”

**Control-hub over hand-fighting**
- “Treat the opponent’s clamp as the real enemy; grips matter only insofar as they maintain clamp and rotation.”
- “Solve legs/hips first; hands finish the cleanup.”

**Counter-offense as a structural tool**
- “Threats are tools, not goals.”
- “Use counter offense only if it forces a structural change (unlock, hip shift, clamp loosening).”
- “If the threat doesn’t create an unlock quickly, return to structure breaking.”

**Symmetry strategy**
- “In 50/50 and Mutual, discipline beats speed: hide heel, separate ankles, extract on unlock.”
- “When the exit channel appears, take it immediately—don’t keep negotiating.”

**Success definition**
- “Success is clear + reset, not surviving a moment.”
- “A clean reset denies re-entry and stops the next attack cycle before it starts.”


## 13.0 TERMINOLOGY

### 13.1 Position names (Irimi/Outside/Cross/Inside/50-50/Mutual)
- **Ashi Garami / “Ashi”**: generic term for leg entanglement families where legs control hips/knee line to enable submissions.
- **Irimi Ashi Garami (Irimi Ashi)**: foundational entanglement used to teach the core defensive engine (inside-position recovery + base ladder).
- **Outside Ashi Garami (Outside Ashi)**: entanglement characterized by a strong clamp (often crossed-ankles) and common inverted HH threat pathways; “separate ankles” is the first structural task.
- **Inside Ashi Garami (Inside Ashi)**: entanglement where heel exposure/rotation threats are persistent; defense begins with heel hiding and often transfers to Outside as a safety valve.
- **Cross Ashi**: entanglement with two-leg control and frequent hierarchy decisions; defense formalizes **primary vs secondary leg** and often runs **secondary-first extraction**.
- **50-50**: symmetric entanglement (both parties have mirrored leg access); defense emphasizes heel hiding, ankle separation, center-line extraction, and “extract on unlock” windows.
- **Mutual Ashi**: symmetry escalation state (both have meaningful entanglement); goal is to induce separation/unlock and exit without racing.
- **Butterfly Ashi**: variant where butterfly-style leg positioning becomes the central structural problem (often blocks simple base-building/exits); may require “pass a leg to escape.”
- **Post Ashi**: variant where an opponent “posts” a leg as an anchor to preserve bite/control; treated as a modifier that must be undone before standard exits.
- **Far-hip Ashi**: variant where control lever shifts to the far hip line (changes angles and blocks standard escapes); solved by addressing the hip-control lever first.
- **Top Oshiro**: positional hub associated with knee-bar orientation management and knee-slip solutions (terminology used in the curriculum structure even when transcript shorthand truncates).

### 13.2 Control terms (knee line, wedge, primary/secondary leg, “real estate”)
- **Inside position / “inside real estate”**: your knees/feet being inside the opponent’s leg frame; the system’s dominant control objective because it collapses clamp tracking and reduces rotation.
- **Outside position**: your knees/feet remain outside their leg frame; increases risk of isolation, rotation, and late-stage bite consolidation.
- **Knee line**: the depth threshold where your knee is considered “captured” (deep past their knees/hips); once lost, rotation/extension threats accelerate.
- **Heel exposure / heel line**: whether your heel is available to be “bitten” (HH) or aligned for torque; primary early-warning variable in Inside/Cross/Outside HH contexts.
- **Rotation (live vs denied)**: whether the opponent can rotate your knee/hip line around the heel/foot; “live rotation” means the finish corridor is open.
- **Clamp**: opponent’s leg configuration that immobilizes your hip/knee line (often via crossed ankles/top-leg control); until the clamp is structurally broken, movement “travels with the lock.”
- **Crossed ankles (ankle cross)**: a common clamp state (especially Outside/50-50); separating ankles is treated as a first-order structural breaker.
- **Primary leg**: the leg currently targeted as the breaking limb (knee line + rotation + bite alignment define it).
- **Secondary leg**: the opponent-controlled leg that provides the clamp/lever enabling the primary attack; often must be solved first to free the primary.
- **Leg lace**: high-control threading/entangling modifier (not just a “position”); treated as a priority problem because it blocks standard separation/extraction.
- **Figure four (leg/foot figure-four)**: finishing structure (notably toe hold, and sometimes 50-50 control) that locks torque mechanics; disruption is prioritized over hand-fighting alone.
- **Bite**: the attacker’s effective attachment on heel/foot (HH/Toe) or pressure line (Achilles/KB) that enables finishing mechanics.
- **Modifier**: overlays that change escape math (reap, post-ashi, lace); system rule: undo modifiers before standard exits.
- **Unlock event**: moment the opponent loosens/unlocks their legs (commonly in 50-50); treated as a high-value trigger to extract immediately.

### 13.3 Mechanics terms (heel slip, re-pummel, high step, backstep, leg interference)
- **Pummel / pummeling**: leg-inside recovery exchanges to win inside position (feet/knees).
- **Re-pummel**: repeat pummeling after re-attachment; treated as the default “reset” action when exits fail or clamp re-forms.
- **Heel hiding (hide heel)**: foot/hip orientation and positioning that denies heel presentation and delays/denies bite consolidation.
- **Heel slip**: timing-based escape of the heel from a forming bite; highest value early, low percentage late.
- **Second-leg interference (2nd-leg block)**: using the free leg/foot as a wedge to block bite alignment, disrupt clamp, or deny rotation.
- **Sit-over-a-leg (sit over a leg)**: collapsing the opponent’s lever by sitting over the controlled leg line; reduces finishing angle and helps break structure.
- **Ankle separation / “separate ankles”**: uncrossing/peeling the opponent’s ankles to break the clamp; foundational Outside/50-50 clearance task.
- **Build to a knee**: intermediate base transition used only when heel/rotation/knee-line are sufficiently protected; enables step clears and posture stability.
- **Build to standing**: final base transition used only when structure is broken enough that standing clears rather than amplifies finishing force.
- **High step**: step-clear method that elevates the leg to clear the controlling leg line once clamp is weakened.
- **Forward step / backstep**: angle-changing steps used when they change structure (remove knee line from frames); not used if clamp still tracks.
- **Knee slip**: knee-line recovery mechanic (notably in knee-bar hub contexts); moves knee out of extension corridor.
- **Step-over**: separation action (notably Achilles contexts) to break the opponent’s leg line/clamp and clear.
- **Roll-through**: rolling method used as a separation tool (not spinning); used when it predictably breaks structure and creates exits.
- **Half-roll**: lowest-commitment roll category; preferred roll option when it yields separation and time to stabilize.
- **Full roll / multiple rolls**: higher-commitment roll categories; last resort and only when separation is predictable.
- **Knee-down escape**: ending posture/phase where defender lands knee-down to stabilize and complete separation/clear.
- **Transfer (as defense)**: moving to a safer Ashi family to reduce immediate risk (e.g., Inside -> Outside; Cross -> 50-50; 50-50 -> Outside via passover).
- **Passover**: 50-50 clearance route described as “pass over to Outside” in the curriculum; used as a clean exit channel after heel safety and ankle separation cues are met.
- **End game**: the “clear + reset” phase; requires full disengagement from entanglement (no half-out lingering that permits immediate re-capture).
