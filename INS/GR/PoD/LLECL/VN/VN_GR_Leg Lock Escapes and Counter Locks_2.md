### 2.1 Achilles Locks — Belly Up Rule
- Objective
  - Prevent catastrophic Achilles breaks by enforcing orientation control: keep attacker belly-up; treat belly-down as emergency.
- Gates
  - Orientation gate: “can’t see their face / eyes” = they’re turning belly-down.
  - Risk gate: belly-down + time = catastrophic break (bodyweight + elongation).
- Controls
  - Orientation enforcement: pull/rock/roll-through to return them belly-up.
  - Awareness control: immediate “sirens” when they start turning.
- Primary line
  - Identify Achilles lock → monitor attacker’s orientation → if they start turning belly-down, immediately force belly-up (pull them back / rock them / roll-through) before they settle.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent turns belly-down slowly → pull/rock to re-square belly-up.
  - Opponent drops to two knees belly-down → escalate to roll-through/high-leg-over mechanics (see 2.5).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - You accept belly-down orientation even briefly → catastrophic finish probability spikes.
- Drills
  - Explicit/implied: partner alternates belly-up pressure vs belly-down turn; defender must “catch the turn” every rep.
- System tags
  - [Rule: Keep Belly-Up] [Orientation Alarm] [Achilles Catastrophic Condition]

### 2.2 Initial Booting And Pressure
- Objective
  - Buy time and reduce immediate finishing power by booting and pressuring “around the corner” (not into the wedge).
- Gates
  - Grip gate: once Achilles is tight, do NOT attempt to yank foot out.
  - Space gate: Irimi wedge limits straight-in pressure; you must angle your scoot.
- Controls
  - Boot: toes curled toward shin.
  - Scoot mechanics: scoot around behind their back; “push heel to butt” by cornering.
  - Optional anchors: far trap / hip grab to help scoot (if reachable).
- Primary line
  - Feel Achilles tighten → boot foot → scoot your hips around the corner (behind their back line) rather than into their knee wedge → create space to boot more effectively → set conditions to keep them belly-up and begin separation.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent tightens Ashi wedge hard → don’t scoot into wedge; angle around corner to create space.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Pulling foot straight out under Achilles tension (“knot at end of rope”) → you tighten their finish and break risk increases.
- Drills
  - Implied: partner applies progressive Achilles tension; defender trains “boot + corner-scoot” without pulling.
- System tags
  - [Boot + Corner-Scoot] [No-Yank Rule] [Space Creation Under Irimi Wedge]

### 2.3 Separating And Stepping Over
- Objective
  - Break the lower-body structure and clear the Achilles grip via rotation mechanics; exit to inside position/high-step.
- Gates
  - Separation gate: you cannot reliably pull out until you separate/sit-over/step-over to reduce structure.
  - Mechanics gate: Achilles grip stops linear motion; rotation is the exploitable weakness.
- Controls
  - Sit-over / step-over their foot (physically sit on it).
  - Foot placement: foot to their hamstring as you begin to extend/rotate.
  - Rotation detail: rotate heel toward opponent’s elbow to clear grip.
- Primary line
  - Under Achilles: don’t pull → separate their feet / step over / sit on the foot → rotate your heel toward their elbow as you pull → foot clears → immediately take both feet inside → high-step and stabilize.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent clamps linear pull attempts → rotate (don’t fight linear).
  - Opponent maintains strong grip but legs compromised → heel-to-elbow rotation produces clearance.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Linear yank attempts = zero progress; increases damage risk.
- Drills
  - Implied: sit-over → rotate heel-to-elbow → clear → re-establish inside feet (high reps).
- System tags
  - [Separate Feet] [Rotation Clears Achilles] [Heel-to-Elbow Cue] [High-Step Exit]

### 2.4 Keeping Them Belly Up (Crisis Rock)
- Objective
  - When you can’t peel/step-over fast enough and they start turning belly-down, use rocking momentum to bring them back belly-up.
- Gates
  - Crisis gate: step-over/peel failing; opponent initiates belly-down turn.
  - Heel-exposure gate: rocking can expose heel to heel hook if you don’t manage foot orientation.
- Controls
  - Cup/hold partner’s knee (same cup used in build-to-knee).
  - Rocking mechanics: head to one side → rock to the other; move whole body (not just lean).
  - Heel management: hide heel; connect heel to their ribcage when turning them back; put weight on their heel once belly-up.
- Primary line
  - They turn belly-down → tuck foot → cup their knee → rock side-to-side to reverse their turn → once they’re belly-up, immediately build to knee to reduce heel-hook switch risk.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent switches to heel hook during recovery → preempt by hiding heel + connecting heel to ribcage during the belly-up restoration.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Trying to “lean them up” without momentum → too slow; you get held belly-down.
- Drills
  - Implied: partner repeatedly initiates belly-down; defender must recover belly-up using rock, then immediately build-to-knee.
- System tags
  - [Belly-Up Enforcement] [Rocking Momentum] [Heel-Hide During Turn] [Build-to-Knee Follow]

### 2.5 Roll Through (High-Leg-Over) To Force Belly Up
- Objective
  - When they reach two-knees belly-down and you cannot restore belly-up by rocking, rotate/roll so they must follow or your leg slips.
- Gates
  - Threshold gate: opponent’s knees on floor belly-down = imminent catastrophic.
  - Rotation gate: Achilles grip cannot hold you if orientation changes and they don’t follow.
- Controls
  - High-leg-over / full rotation.
  - Principle: if you go belly-down, they must rotate with you or the leg slips.
- Primary line
  - Opponent reaches two-knees belly-down → high-leg-over / roll through → you invert/rotate so they must follow to keep grip → they return belly-up (or lose grip) → you end on knee on top with foot weighted and safe.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent tries to stay belly-down as you rotate → leg slips; grip fails (escape condition).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Staying belly-up while they go belly-down = you get broken; you must match orientation change to force follow.
- Drills
  - Implied: timed reps where partner must reach knees; defender must roll-through before “count” ends.
- System tags
  - [Roll-Through to Belly-Up] [Forced Rotation] [Two-Knees Emergency Protocol]

### 2.6 End Game (Achilles: Orientation Race)
- Objective
  - Define the end condition: can you return them belly-up before they hold you belly-down long enough to break.
- Gates
  - Time gate: if you cannot restore belly-up quickly enough, tapping/break occurs.
- Controls
  - None beyond previously defined mechanisms; this is the decision boundary.
- Primary line
  - Attempt belly-up restoration; if restoration fails and they hold belly-down static long enough → tap (or accept break).
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent achieves static belly-down with control → end state; no technical escape guaranteed.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Delayed reaction to belly-down = you lose the race.
- Drills
  - Implied: “orientation race” rounds (partner aims for belly-down hold; defender aims for belly-up restoration).
- System tags
  - [End Game Definition] [Time-to-Belly-Up] [Catastrophic Threshold]

### 2.7 Stopping/Undoing The Reap (Anti-Reap Wipe + Leg Capture)
- Objective
  - Prevent the reap from blocking your ability to bring them belly-up; clear the reaping leg or roll-through if you can’t.
- Gates
  - Reap gate: if reap is across, you cannot safely explode/turn back (you break your own leg).
  - Recognition gate: reap is often “cast over the top” rather than shot through when your wedge is present.
- Controls
  - Catch the casting leg as it comes over.
  - Wipe/peel the reap to the outside.
  - Secondary control: hold one of their legs (reach under your own leg to catch) to prevent them firing Outside Ashi.
- Primary line
  - Begin belly-up restoration → opponent casts reap over → catch the leg → wipe reap off to outside → immediately secure their leg(s) so they cannot pummel to Outside Ashi → now you can pummel your free leg inside / rotate out / counterattack to create hand separation → then extract or attack.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent casts reap over top → catch and wipe.
  - Opponent tries to pummel to Outside Ashi after reap wipe → hold their leg under your own leg; deny the pummel; you pummel instead.
  - If you miss the reap wipe and feel break imminent → roll through back to belly-up protocol (see 2.8).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Attempting belly-up restoration without addressing the reap → you’re structurally blocked and get broken.
- Drills
  - Implied: partner repeatedly attempts cast-reap during belly-up restoration; defender trains catch→wipe→leg capture.
- System tags
  - [Reap Modifier] [Catch-and-Wipe] [Outside-Ashi Denial] [Free-Leg Pummel Advantage]

### 2.8 Roll Through (With Reap Present)
- Objective
  - If the reap is in and you can’t clear it fast enough, use roll-through mechanics to force them to follow or lose grip.
- Gates
  - Emergency gate: reap + belly-down trajectory + knees approaching floor = immediate roll-through.
- Controls
  - Put yourself belly-down; they must follow or leg slips.
  - Tuck heel into butt during high-leg-over as they follow.
- Primary line
  - Strong reap + pressure building → you go belly-down to force follow → rise to knee → rotate; they must rotate with you → high-leg-over with heel tucked → land on knee on top, foot weighted; proceed to follow-ups.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent fails to follow rotation → your leg slips free.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Trying to “fight the reap” while staying belly-up when pressure is already catastrophic.
- Drills
  - Implied: partner applies reap and belly-down pressure; defender must trigger immediate roll-through to knee-top end state.
- System tags
  - [Reap Emergency Roll] [Forced Follow] [Heel Tuck] [Knee-Top Stabilization]

### 2.11 Re-Pummel (Achilles Context; Tactical Counter)
- Objective
  - Re-pummel into offense; Achilles commitment can expose their heel tactically as they go belly-down.
- Gates
  - Tactical gate: opponent believes Achilles is winning → over-commits belly-down → exposes heel.
- Controls
  - Re-pummel mechanics; transition to mutual ashi; heel-hook acquisition when opponent turns.
- Primary line
  - Re-pummel from knee → fall to mutual ashi → allow/accept their Achilles attempt as bait → as they go belly-down, expose/attack heel hook (tactical trade described).
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent turns belly-down to finish Achilles → you gain heel hook access (offense-from-defense).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Purely defensive mindset when opponent gives offensive openings by over-commitment.
- Drills
  - Implied: re-pummel entries where attacker is instructed to go belly-down on Achilles; defender transitions to heel exposure/attack.
- System tags
  - [Offense-from-Defense] [Re-Pummel] [Opponent Overzeal → Exposure]

### 2.12 Build To Standing (Achilles On; Sensitivity Notes)
- Objective
  - Stand-up mechanics are the same; but escape selection (forward vs backstep) depends on Achilles “stickiness”.
- Gates
  - Sensitivity gate: forward step only if Achilles loose enough; sticky Achilles can hold you in place.
- Controls
  - Head position: head leans over your foot (standing stability cue).
  - Escape menu: re-pummel / forward step / backstep.
- Primary line
  - Build to standing using same posture → test Achilles grip tightness implicitly → if sticky, choose backstep or re-pummel; if not, forward step may work.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Sticky Achilles holds you during forward run → abandon forward; backstep or re-pummel instead.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Treating forward run as universal; it is not under tight Achilles.
- Drills
  - Implied: standing escapes with variable grip tightness; defender must select correct exit.
- System tags
  - [Base Ladder: Knee→Stand] [Sensitivity: Grip Tightness] [Escape Selection]

### 2.13 Central Problem Of Butterfly Ashi
- Objective
  - Diagnose why butterfly ashi changes Achilles defense: blocks step-over and forward pressure; creates wedge that pins hip and enables extension.
- Gates
  - Butterfly hook gate: attacker has hook under you + leg fired over hip line.
- Controls
  - Identify constraints
    - Cannot pressure forward (hook creates space and moves attacker away).
    - Cannot step-over (leg fired over blocks).
    - Hip is blocked by inside leg chopping over the hip.
- Primary line
  - Recognize butterfly ashi wedge as the problem; prepare to remove the hip-blocking leg or pass it across (see 2.14).
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent uses hook to extend away/tighten Achilles → you must address hip-blocking leg (not just “boot and scoot”).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Repeating standard Irimi Achilles responses when step-over/pressure are structurally denied.
- Drills
  - Implied: partner switches Irimi Achilles → butterfly ashi; defender must immediately identify blocked hip and change line.
- System tags
  - [Butterfly Ashi Modifier] [Hip Block Problem] [Extension Threat]

### 2.14 Passing A Leg To Escape (Butterfly Ashi)
- Objective
  - Remove the leg that blocks your hip so you can scoot/escape or convert to mutual ashi.
- Gates
  - “Hip stuck” gate: you try to scoot and can’t because inside leg blocks hip.
- Controls
  - Lean back briefly → pass their leg across to opposite side → free hip.
  - After pass: reclaim inside knees; foot to hamstring; rotate heel-to-elbow to clear.
- Primary line
  - Butterfly ashi wedge blocks hip → lean back → pass the blocking leg across → choose path:
    - Escape path: bring both knees inside → hip out/away → post/turn → rotate foot → out.
    - Pocket path: V-grip and sit up → enter (more-or-less) mutual ashi to attack.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent tries to maintain wedge after leg pass → wedge removed; you now have hip mobility; proceed to exit.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Trying to “muscle” scoot while hip is blocked instead of removing the blocker.
- Drills
  - Implied: butterfly ashi starts; defender must leg-pass within 1–2 seconds then execute chosen finish (escape vs attack).
- System tags
  - [Remove Hip Block] [Leg Pass Across] [Inside Knees Reclaim] [Dual Finish: Escape/Attack]

### 2.15 Preventing Belly Down (Butterfly Ashi Context)
- Objective
  - Keep them belly-up by covering inside knee; deny their rotation to belly-down.
- Gates
  - Opponent initiates belly-down from butterfly ashi.
- Controls
  - Cover inside of opponent’s knee.
  - Weight forward into your foot once belly-up is restored.
- Primary line
  - As they go belly-down → cover inside knee → take them back belly-up → pressure forward; stabilize.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - If you cannot keep belly-up → roll-through/full rotation (see 2.5 analog) to force follow and end on knee-top.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Letting them rotate while you remain static.
- Drills
  - Implied: butterfly ashi belly-down attempts; defender must knee-cover belly-up restoration or roll-through.
- System tags
  - [Belly-Up Enforcement] [Knee Cover] [Butterfly Context]

### 2.16 End Game (Butterfly Ashi Achilles)
- Objective
  - Restate the end condition under butterfly ashi: belly-up = safer; belly-down = break risk; butterfly can increase extension even belly-up.
- Gates
  - Butterfly extension gate: they may legitimately apply strong pressure even belly-up due to hook + figure-four.
- Controls
  - Must get foot to floor and reduce extension (expanded next).
- Primary line
  - Keep them belly-up where possible; if they keep you belly-down → end; if they extend hard belly-up → solve extension by getting foot to floor (2.18).
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent achieves heavy extension with figure-four Achilles in butterfly ashi → transition to elbow-grab wedge to get foot down.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Assuming belly-up automatically safe in butterfly ashi; extension can still be real.
- Drills
  - Implied: controlled rounds where attacker uses butterfly extension; defender must immediately “foot-to-floor” solution.
- System tags
  - [Butterfly Extension] [Belly-Up Not Always Safe] [Foot-to-Floor Priority]

### 2.17 Build To A Knee (Butterfly Ashi)
- Objective
  - Build to knee the same way, but acknowledge butterfly hook increases off-balance and extension threats immediately after you rise.
- Gates
  - Butterfly hook gate: opponent can lift + push to tighten Achilles as you rise.
- Controls

- Primary line
  - Cup → rock to knee → “fly” into kneeling posture; prepare to prevent extension and get foot to floor.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent lifts/pushes with hook as you come up → anticipate tighter Achilles and go straight to extension counter (2.18).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Rising and pausing while hook-driven extension is available.
- Drills
  - Implied: build-to-knee into immediate extension defense (no pause).
- System tags
  - [Base Ladder: Seat→Knee] [Butterfly Hook Off-Balance] [Immediate Extension Threat]

### 2.18 Gripping And Elbow-to-Boot (Foot-To-Floor Solution)
- Objective
  - Prevent being broken by butterfly-as-hi extension by using the opponent’s elbow as a wedge to pull yourself/foot to the floor.
- Gates
  - Your hand is off the ground (working hand available) and you cannot place the foot down because extension tightens immediately.
- Controls
  - Grab opponent’s elbow with free hand.
  - Pull elbow toward you as you pull yourself through; shoot foot down to floor.
- Primary line
  - Opponent extends; foot floats on their body → free hand grabs their elbow → pull elbow forward to create wedge and space → shoot foot down to floor → now you’re safer and can proceed to escape sequence.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent pushes you away while extending → elbow grab converts their push into your pull-through to floor placement.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Trying to stomp foot to floor directly while they extend → you can’t win that race; you stay on their ribcage and get tightened.
- Drills
  - Implied: attacker applies butterfly extension; defender must elbow-grab and place foot to floor repeatedly.
- System tags
  - [Foot-To-Floor] [Elbow Wedge] [Butterfly Extension Counter]

### 2.19 Hand-Up / Hand-Down Dilemma
- Objective
  - Solve the dilemma where your hand must either post (to avoid being tipped/belly-down) or work (to grab elbow); answer is a positional change: pop to feet.
- Gates
  - Dilemma gate: reaching for elbow forces you to post (they tilt you); not posting worsens to belly-down; posting still allows them to break you.
- Controls
  - Option A (working hand available): grab elbow and execute foot-to-floor.
  - Option B (forced to post): two post hands → pop up to feet (hips up) to reduce their ability to put hands to floor; hop/limp-leg as needed.
- Primary line
  - If you can keep a working hand → elbow grab (2.18).
  - If they force two post hands → immediately pop up to feet → now harder to force your hands down; begin peeling legs and exiting.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent tilts to force your hand to floor as you reach → stop reaching; post and pop up instead.
  - Opponent tries to keep you broken while you stand → hop/limp-leg; Achilles grip limits their ability to grab second leg immediately (as stated).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Staying on knee in a forced-post scenario = you remain in the breaking lane.
- Drills
  - Implied: attacker’s job is to force defender’s hand to floor; defender must choose elbow-grab vs pop-up correctly.
- System tags
  - [Dilemma Resolution] [Pop-Up Transition] [Post vs Work Hand] [Butterfly Standing Shift]

### 2.20 Backstepping (Standing; Foot Off Floor)
- Objective
  - Backstep out even when the trapped foot is elevated; prerequisite is clearing the leg that blocks your hip by posturing up (not leaning back).
- Gates
  - Awkwardness gate: your backstep target foot starts off the floor; if you backstep without clearing blocker you fall.
  - Hip-block gate: opponent’s foot blocks your hip; prevents backstep.
- Controls
  - Posture up (not lean back) to expose and clear the blocking foot.
  - After clear: your elevated foot can drop to become a post during the backstep.
  - Control both opponent ankles after backstep to force release.
- Primary line
  - Standing with foot elevated → posture up → wipe/clear opponent’s blocking leg across → now backstep; your foot drops to floor as post → control both ankles → exit past hip line; proceed to attacks/position.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent continues extension while you attempt backstep → posture up first to remove their ability to push effectively with only one leg.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Backstepping while hip is still blocked → off-balance/fall (no post foot).
- Drills
  - Implied: standing butterfly/post-ashi style elevation; defender must posture→clear→backstep with ankle control.
- System tags
  - [Standing Backstep] [Posture-Up First] [Hip-Block Removal] [Ankle Control Exit]

### 2.21 Addressing A Post Ashi
- Objective
  - Emergency response: post-ashi creates immediate catastrophic extension potential; priority is getting feet off hips by moving yourself (scoot) more than trying to push feet away.
- Gates
  - Recognition gate: two feet posted on hips = post ashi; treat as immediate danger.
  - Force gate: if attacker glues feet and extends, pushing feet off is hard; moving your hips is easier.
- Controls
  - Any grip that dislodges feet (push or pull acceptable), but preference for pushing grip for corner-scoot mechanics.
  - Principle: wedge their feet in place and scoot your hips around the corner; let the bridge/extension slip their foot off.
- Primary line
  - See post ashi → immediately address feet-on-hips → establish push (preferred) or pull grip → instead of trying to “bench press” feet off, wedge feet position and scoot your hips around and away → as they bridge/extend, their foot slips off → transition out of catastrophic line (outside-Ashi Achilles context deferred to later volumes).
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent extends hard and you can’t push feet off → keep feet “fixed” and move your hips away (corner-scoot).
  - Opponent is much heavier / high force → do not hesitate; prioritize immediate foot-off-hip outcome.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Trying to push glued feet straight off hips under full extension → you lose force battle and tap/break.
- Drills
  - Implied: post-ashi start; attacker extends progressively; defender trains “hip-move vs foot-push” decision and execution.
- System tags
  - [Post-Ashi Emergency] [Feet-Off-Hips Priority] [Hip-Not-Feet Movement] [Catastrophic Prevention]
