## 5.1 Heel Hooks - Intro To Heel Hooks

- Objective
  - Establish defensive priorities vs heel hook threats in the Outside/Irimi-adjacent context: protect heel first, then create separation, then clear/reset.
- Gates
  - Heel line risk present (heel can be exposed during belly-up recovery / rotations).
  - Opponent can shorten/withdraw the attacked leg (control issue) → you must secure control before committing to step-over/clear.
- Controls
  - Knee-inside-first control when you only have control of one leg (vs “splitting legs” where you control two).
  - Hip-lift/bridge to unstick a planted foot and access heel (if needed to reorient/transition).
- Primary line
  - Separate legs / threaten heel access → if opponent shortens leg: bring knee inside first → then sit-over/step-over → extract to neutral.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Defender shortens the attacked leg to block your hip/step-over → knees come together / knee comes inside first → regain control → proceed to step-over/extract.
  - Defender plants foot to deny heel access → lift hips/bridge forward to make the foot rise → access heel/decision (attack vs transition).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - If you attempt to “jump/step” before knee-inside control and they shorten the leg, their leg blocks your hip and stalls the clear.
  - If you chase heel while their structure prevents heel access (foot pinned), you waste the window; lift hips to solve access first.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - “Shortening leg” reaction drill: separate/attack cue → partner shortens leg → knee-inside-first → control → step-over.
  - “Foot pinned” drill: partner pins foot → bridge to lift → re-access heel → finish transition/extraction.
- System tags
  - [heel-hook-defense] [knee-inside-first] [separation-first] [hip-lift-access] [outside/irimi-bridge]

## 5.2 Initial Heel Slip/Second Leg Interference

- Objective
  - Prevent clean heel exposure/connection; buy time to create separation or transition (including symmetry if chosen).
- Gates
  - You do not control both legs (heel hook scenario) → control acquisition must precede certain clears.
  - Opponent’s “shorten leg” defense blocks hip line.
- Controls
  - Knee-inside-first to gain control of the leg before sit-over/step-over.
  - Second-leg interference is referenced as a general tool, but TRN5 emphasizes control timing more than a discrete interference sequence.
- Primary line
  - Threaten heel hook → opponent shortens leg → knee inside first → control → sit-over/step-over → move out/away to neutral.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Defender shortens leg → knee-inside-first (mandatory) → then sit-over.
  - Defender’s foot is planted (heel access denied) → lift hips/bridge → heel becomes accessible → proceed.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Trying to sit-over/step-over without first controlling the leg when they can shorten it.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Timing ladder: (1) threaten heel (2) partner shortens leg (3) knee-inside-first (4) sit-over (5) clear.
- System tags
  - [heel-slip-timing] [control-before-clear] [knee-inside-first]

## 5.3 Half Roll To Knee Down Escape

- Objective
  - Use a lower-commitment roll to reach a stabilizing knee-down position when immediate separation is not available.
- Gates
  - High urgency (submission pressure fast; reinforced wedge/crossed ankles may prevent “fiddle with legs”).
  - Knee-down landing is available as a stabilization endpoint.
- Controls
  - Knee-down conversion: start roll → bring knee down → scoot knee forward → push and move out/away.
- Primary line
  - Start roll → force knee-down → stabilize → scoot/drive knee forward → clear.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If pressure persists at knee-down → post/attack the grip line (e.g., post elbow) to separate → then complete clear.
  - If knee-down attempt fails to free knee/line → escalate to full/multiple rolls.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - If you try to separate legs first in a “fast-break” scenario, you may not have time; roll commitment becomes necessary.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Knee-down conversion drill: toe hold locked from knee → half-roll → knee-down → scoot/push → exit.
- System tags
  - [half-roll] [knee-down-escape] [stabilize-then-clear]

## 5.4 Full/ Multiple Rolls

- Objective
  - Force separation through sustained rolling when neither upper-body interference nor lower-body separation is available in time.
- Gates
  - Reinforced wedge / crossed ankles / fast finishing mechanics → you cannot reliably separate legs before damage.
- Controls
  - Rolling sequence as separation engine (not “spin to win”): roll repeatedly until structure loosens enough to clear.
  - Optional adjunct: second-leg interference during rolls (only if time exists).
- Primary line
  - Immediate roll → continue multiple rolls until separation window → finish by pushing off/clearing to neutral.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Defender rolls with you (expected) → continue sequence; do not expect static inversion where you can calmly strip grips.
  - Defender “stays” and exposes elbow (unlikely per TRN5) → if it occurs, post elbow/strip grip → clear.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Betting on the opponent staying static/inverted so you can strip grips is low-probability; plan for them rolling with you.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - “Roll-with-me” drill: partner commits to following rolls; defender must maintain roll cadence until a separation window.
- System tags
  - [multiple-rolls] [separation-only-roll] [high-urgency-defense]

## 5.5 End Game (Heel Hooks)

- Objective
  - Convert “survival” into a clean reset: separation achieved → clear legs → return to safe neutral (no re-entry).
- Gates
  - Time is the constraint variable (how long they can maintain finishing alignment vs how fast you can break structure).
- Controls
  - Clear to a position that splits their legs and denies immediate re-pummel/re-entry.
  - Avoid kicking leg far back/away if it invites sticky re-entry; finish in a position that denies easy follow-up.
- Primary line
  - Separation → extract → reset to neutral with inside-position safety.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If re-entry threat persists → prioritize inside recovery (re-pummel framing/positioning) before disengaging fully.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - “Escaping one entanglement into another” due to poor end positioning after the clear.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - End-position discipline: clear → land in split-leg denial position → immediate inside recovery posture.
- System tags
  - [endgame-reset] [re-entry-denial] [inside-recovery]

## 5.7 End Game (Post-Defense)

- Objective
  - Standardize the win condition: deny prolonged belly-down finishing mechanics; return opponent belly-up or clear entirely.
- Gates
  - Opponent tries to stay belly-down long enough to finish.
- Controls
  - Belly-up recovery via stopping belly-down, or via full rotations if forced.
  - Heel hiding during belly-up recovery to avoid a heel hook switch.
- Primary line
  - If they go belly-down → recover belly-up (or land to knee base) → then proceed to extraction ladder.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If you can’t undo reap/structure in time → “go with it” into rotation to return belly-up rather than fight a losing static.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Allowing a static belly-down finishing window to persist.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Belly-down denial drill: partner commits to belly-down finishing attempt → defender rotates/recovers belly-up → immediate clear attempt.
- System tags
  - [belly-up-rule] [rotation-recovery] [time-as-constraint]

## 5.8 Defending From A Knee

- Objective
  - Survive and clear when caught from knee base (not seated), where structure is reinforced and time is short (notably toe hold risk).
- Gates
  - Outside Ashi + toe hold locked while you are on a knee → you may not have time to separate legs.
- Controls
  - Immediate roll commitment may be required; lower-body separation is not always available fast enough.
  - If time appears: knee-down conversion + scoot/push; if not: multiple rolls.
  - Post elbow option exists after you’ve created enough space (not assumed early).
- Primary line
  - Toe hold on from knee → roll (half-roll to knee-down if possible) → if knee-down achieved: scoot/push/clear → else multiple rolls until separation.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Knee-down attempt works → stabilize → post/elbow separation if needed → clear.
  - Knee-down attempt fails → multiple roll sequence until separation.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Trying to “separate partner’s legs” first when toe hold pressure is immediate and wedge is reinforced.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Knee-base emergency: partner locks toe hold immediately from outside ashi while you’re knee-based → defender must choose half-roll vs full/multi based on time.
- System tags
  - [knee-base-defense] [toe-hold-urgent] [roll-to-separate]

## 5.9 Defend From Standing

- Objective
  - Safely open/separate the entanglement while standing without getting off-balanced into re-entry or amplifying the lock.
- Gates
  - You cannot “fiddle” too long while upright; posture/weight distribution must manage off-balancing.
- Controls
  - Weight lean and posting: do not stand tall/straight while they can off-balance.
  - Posture-up timing: posture only when you are ready to open/separate legs.
  - Stuff/pass options once legs are opened: stuff between legs for forward step/backstep; or pass to a mutual/symmetry configuration as a defensive stabilization.
  - If opening legs is repeatedly punished by off-balance: hip thrust forward to change configuration and bring knee down/sit-back on legs.
- Primary line
  - Standing in ashi → maintain lean/post → acquire grip timing → posture/open legs → choose (stuff + forward step) or (stuff + backstep) or (pass to mutual) → clear.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Defender off-balances whenever you try to separate → hip thrust forward → bring legs across/reaping-like alignment → take knee down → sit back on legs → extract.
  - Defender’s leg can be stuffed to far hip or between legs → choose based on exit preference (disengage vs stay in pocket).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Standing upright too long while trying to separate legs; getting off-balanced before you can open structure.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Standing separation timing: partner actively off-balances; defender must maintain lean until the “posture-and-open” moment.
- System tags
  - [standing-defense] [posture-timing] [off-balance-management] [stuff-and-step]

## 5.10 Recapping Irimi And Outside Ashi

- Objective
  - Reassert shared endgame logic across Irimi and Outside: take feet off hip/post ashi, deny belly-down time, apply same base-building exits.
- Gates
  - Post ashi (foot on hip) appears; belly-down attempts appear; reap attempts appear.
- Controls
  - Clearing post ashi is “almost identical” between Irimi and Outside (TRN5 claim).
  - Endgame is time-based: opponent’s belly-down static vs your ability to restore belly-up or clear.
- Primary line
  - Clear post ashi → apply same ladder (knee/stand if safe) → extract/re-pummel → reset.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If achilles grip is super tight → forward running step becomes harder; other ladder elements remain consistent.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Treating post ashi as “new”; failing to apply the same clearing mechanics and then getting stuck under belly-down time.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Irimi vs Outside compare drill: partner alternates post ashi + belly-down attempts in each position; defender runs the same endgame checklist.
- System tags
  - [irimi-outside-merge] [post-ashi-clear] [endgame-time]

## 5.17 End Game (Outro)

- Objective
  - Wrap and reinforce: if you cannot interfere upper body, cannot slip grip, and cannot separate legs in time, you must roll to create separation; end in a clean reset.
- Gates
  - Catastrophic break is prevented only if you create separation before their finishing window closes.
- Controls
  - Decision clarity: interference/slip/separate → if not available → roll ladder; do not stall.
- Primary line
  - Choose the highest-probability separation method available under time constraints → clear → reset.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If roll does not produce separation → continue rolling ladder; if separation occurs → stop and clear immediately.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Hesitation/stalling under a fast-breaking wedge.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Timed rounds: partner locks toe hold quickly; defender must commit to the correct separation strategy without hesitation.
- System tags
  - [decision-ladder] [time-compression] [clear-and-reset]
