## 6.1 Cross Ashi - Intro To Cross Ashi

- Objective
  - Define Cross Ashi as a two-leg control problem with constrained base-building; establish the priority: protect feet/heel, solve the clamp/levers, then extract.
- Gates
  - Standing up is described as “pretty much almost impossible” on good Cross Ashi control; default to seated/knee-based solutions.
- Controls
  - Maintain foot safety while opponent tries to wedge and adjust for exposure.
  - Early recognition of when opponent is “occupied” adjusting position → opportunistic slip windows.
- Primary line
  - Protect feet/heel → hunt slip windows (primary/secondary) → if no window: move into structured separation/roll/transfer logic.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent transitions/adjusts to expose heel → use the transition moment to slip the controlled leg, then immediately deny re-entry.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Treating Cross Ashi like Irimi/Outside and trying to “just stand” → low-percentage and feeds control.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - “Adjustment window” drill: partner cycles Cross Ashi adjustments; defender practices identifying the slip moment.
- System tags
  - [cross-ashi] [two-leg-control] [no-stand-bias] [timing-windows]

## 6.2 Initially Protecting Your Feet

- Objective
  - Establish baseline foot/heel safety so you can operate (slip, separate, roll, transfer) without catastrophic exposure.
- Gates
  - Any moment your heel is available during Cross Ashi adjustments is a priority emergency.
- Controls
  - “Point toes away / bring heel in” style protection is repeatedly referenced across the system; in Cross Ashi it is the entry requirement to everything else.
- Primary line
  - Hide/protect foot and heel → only then attempt slips/exits.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent focuses on heel exposure → tighten heel safety first, then re-enter your escape decision.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Attempting movement (slip/roll/step) while heel is exposed.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Heel-hide first reps: partner’s only goal is heel exposure; defender’s only goal is heel safety maintenance.
- System tags
  - [heel-safety] [foot-protection] [precondition]

## 6.3 Opportunistic Slips Of Primary Leg

- Objective
  - Slip the primary (most-controlled) leg during opponent adjustments, then immediately prevent re-attachment/re-entry.
- Gates
  - Opponent is adjusting/fiddling/transitioning rather than fully wedging the primary leg (timing/sensitivity requirement).
- Controls
  - Thumb post on partner’s hamstring (or two-hand posting) + quick leg extension + toes pointed to slip.
  - Immediate follow control after slip: find partner’s hamstring with your other leg so they cannot climb back in easily.
- Primary line
  - Feel looseness → post (thumb/2 hands) → extend/point toes → slip primary leg → immediately place your leg to partner hamstring line → extract fully to neutral.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent tries to climb back in by bringing foot to hip → hamstring contact makes the climb hard → continue extraction to neutral.
  - Opponent immediately switches to attack the other leg → either fully move away (deny knee access) or palm off to inside position and come up.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Slipping primary leg but leaving it “out there” → opponent re-enters on the other leg.
  - Overextending after slip → opponent can scoop and enter Cross Ashi on the opposite side.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Slip-and-deny drill: slip primary leg → immediate hamstring find → partner attempts immediate re-entry on other leg.
- System tags
  - [primary-leg-slip] [timing-slip] [hamstring-denial] [re-entry-prevention]

## 6.4 Opportunistic Slips Of Secondary Leg

- Objective
  - Exploit opportunities to slip the secondary leg (the enabling lever/clamp side) to reduce control and open the path to primary extraction.
- Gates
  - Secondary control is not fully locked (or opponent is transitioning and neglects it).
- Controls
  - Use the moment you peel/strip grip pressure to slip the secondary leg (referenced conceptually in TRN6).
- Primary line
  - Identify secondary control softness → slip secondary leg → immediately transition to primary extraction or safer configuration.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If secondary slip succeeds but primary remains trapped → do not pause; immediately address the remaining control line.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Treating secondary slip as “done” and stalling → opponent rebuilds control and re-wedges.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Secondary-first reps: partner gives intermittent secondary looseness while keeping primary threatened; defender trains immediate conversion.
- System tags
  - [secondary-leg-slip] [control-hierarchy] [conversion-immediately]

## 6.5 Defending A Leg Lace

- Objective
  - Survive the lace by removing the conditions that make it break-capable, then transition into your broader escape set (slips / re-pummel / rolls / transfers).
- Gates
  - Lace threat is detected (TRN6 repeatedly references “the second I feel the leg lace…” as the trigger).
- Controls
  - Shortening the top leg is referenced as making it harder for the lace to break you (“not enough mass inside the lock” is cited).
  - If hips are pinned / trouble increases: bring hips off the floor (hip elevation appears as a recurring access/pressure reducer).
  - Posting to the floor (“two hands to the floor… push feet to the ground…”) is referenced as a structural response.
- Primary line
  - Detect lace early → shorten top leg / reduce break mechanics → elevate hips if needed → post to stabilize → transition to your next escape (slip/roll/transfer).
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If you cannot pull the leg free after shortening → do not stall; transition immediately into your other escapes and re-pummels (explicitly referenced).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Late recognition: you only respond after lace mechanics are fully set.
  - Trying to “muscle pull” instead of changing structure (shorten / elevate / post) first.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Lace detection drill: partner alternates Achilles pressure ↔ lace entry; defender must react on the first “lace feel” cue.
- System tags
  - [leg-lace-defense] [structure-first] [shorten-top-leg] [hip-elevation] [post-and-stabilize]

## 6.6 End Game (Post Lace Defense)

- Objective
  - Convert lace survival into clear/reset rather than remaining in a compromised entanglement.
- Gates
  - Lace threat reduced but entanglement remains.
- Controls
  - Transition immediately into “other escapes and re-pummels” once the lace’s break conditions are blunted.
- Primary line
  - Stabilize → escape conversion → clear legs → reset.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If opponent switches to another submission line during the scramble → return to “protect feet/heel first,” then re-run decision ladder.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Treating “survived the lace” as the end; failing to clear and allowing re-lock.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Lace-to-reset rounds: win condition is full disengagement, not just “no tap.”
- System tags
  - [endgame-reset] [conversion] [clear-and-reset]

## 6.7 Defending Secondary Leg Achilles Locks

- Objective
  - Manage Achilles pressure on the secondary leg while preserving primary-leg survival and opening extraction order.
- Gates
  - Secondary leg is targeted with Achilles mechanics while Cross Ashi structure persists.
- Controls
  - TRN6 frames the need to use opportunistic slips (including of secondary) and to prevent being statically wedged.
- Primary line
  - Protect foot/heel → prevent static wedge → create slip/separation opportunity → extract with correct hierarchy.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If Achilles pressure increases faster than you can separate → prioritize structure change (shorten/elevate/post) before attempting to pull free.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Ignoring the secondary attack while focusing only on primary extraction.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Secondary-Achilles surprise reps: partner alternates between holding Cross Ashi and attacking secondary Achilles; defender must preserve both legs.
- System tags
  - [achilles-defense] [secondary-leg-attack] [hierarchy-awareness]

## 6.8 End Game (Post Secondary-Achilles Defense)

- Objective
  - Finish by clearing the entanglement rather than cycling between legs.
- Gates
  - You have reduced immediate Achilles danger but remain in Cross Ashi family.
- Controls
  - Re-establish inside safety and deny immediate re-entry before disengaging.
- Primary line
  - Clear → reset to neutral with re-entry denial.
- Branches (defender action → your response)

- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Clearing one leg into immediate re-catch.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Clear-to-deny drill: partner’s goal is immediate re-catch; defender’s goal is clean neutral.

## 6.9 Extracting Secondary Then Primary Leg

- Objective
  - Apply two-leg hierarchy: solve the enabling lever/clamp first (secondary) to free the breaking target (primary).
- Gates
  - Both legs are controlled; direct primary pull fails.
- Controls
  - Explicit hierarchy language appears in TRN6 (primary vs secondary framing is directly discussed).
- Primary line
  - Identify secondary control → remove/escape it → immediately extract primary → reset.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If primary slip window appears first during opponent adjustment → take it, but immediately deny re-entry to the other leg.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Pulling primary leg against an intact secondary clamp.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Hierarchy rounds: partner maintains secondary clamp as the main defense; defender must solve secondary first.
- System tags
  - [control-hierarchy] [secondary-first] [primary-extraction]

## 6.10 Re-Pummel

- Objective
  - Recover inside position/leg alignment so opponent cannot keep a closed circle around your hips/legs.
- Gates
  - You have enough space/structure to reinsert legs to inside safely.
- Controls
  - “Pummel to the inside” is explicitly referenced; used as a reset and as a way to stop re-entry lines.
- Primary line
  - Create minimal space → pummel inside → stabilize → proceed to clear/extract.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If opponent’s control tightens mid-repummel → revert to heel safety + structure break, then repummel again.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Attempting to repummel without first protecting heel/foot.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Repummel reset drill: after each partial escape, force repummel before continuing.
- System tags
  - [re-pummel] [inside-recovery] [reset-engine]

## 6.11 Half Roll To Separate Legs

- Objective
  - Use a low-commitment roll to create leg separation when direct peeling fails.
- Gates
  - Separation by hand/structure is not achievable in time; roll produces separation (not motion).
- Controls
  - Roll is framed as a separation tool; not a “spin inside an intact lock.”
- Primary line
  - Initiate half-roll → create separation → stabilize → clear.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If separation not achieved → do not continue aimlessly; escalate to full/multiple rolls or transition/transfer logic.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Rolling without a separation pathway.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Half-roll test: partner gives just enough resistance that only a correct half-roll yields separation.
- System tags
  - [half-roll] [separation-only-roll]

## 6.12 Knee Down Escape

- Objective
  - Land in a knee-down base that stabilizes and allows final clearing actions.
- Gates

## 6.13 Full/Multiple Rolls

- Branches (defender action → your response)

- Failure-first diagnosis

- Drills (explicit or implied)

## 6.17 Attempting To Roll With The Lock

- Objective
  - Clarify the constraint: roll only if it creates separation; rolling “with the lock” is typically low percentage and high risk.
- Gates
  - You are considering rolling while the lock/clamp remains meaningful.
- Controls
  - TRN6 explicitly frames “roll with the lock” as last-ditch and warns of low percentage; roll is for separation, not for spinning.
- Primary line
  - If roll creates separation → roll; if not → do not roll; instead seek elbow/second-leg interference/structure break.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If roll does not change structure → stop and switch tools (interference / slip / transfer).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Rolling as your first answer, rather than after confirming it produces separation.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Constraint drill: defender must call “separation” before rolling; otherwise the rep is invalid.
- System tags
  - [no-roll-with-lock] [separation-only-roll] [decision-discipline]

## 6.19 End Game

- Objective
  - Cleanly exit entanglement with re-entry denial; do not accept “partial wins.”
- Gates
  - You have achieved partial structure break (slip/separation/transfer) and must finish clearing.
- Controls
  - Reset logic: clear → get legs to safe line → deny immediate re-ashi attempts.
- Primary line
  - Clear legs → establish neutral safe base → re-entry denial posture.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent re-enters on the other leg → distance disengage or inside-position palm-off, then come up.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Exiting one control into immediate re-catch due to poor end positioning.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Endgame-only rounds: start in “almost escaped” positions; goal is full reset with no re-entry.
- System tags
  - [endgame-reset] [re-entry-denial] [system-closure]
