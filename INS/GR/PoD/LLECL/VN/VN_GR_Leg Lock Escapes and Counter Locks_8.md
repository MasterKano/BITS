## 8.1 50/50 - Intro To 50/50
- Objective
  - Define 50/50 as a symmetry position where both legs are exposed; install “balanced offense + defense” as the governing mindset.
- Gates
  - If you are overly offensive with no defense, you get counter-locked.
  - If you are overly defensive with no threat, your partner attacks freely.
- Controls
  - Maintain credible attack threat while running extraction/defense; symmetry punishes single-minded posture.
- Primary line
  - Establish defensive heel safety + positional stability → develop separation/extraction → keep threat presence so opponent must respect you.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent attacks aggressively because you have no threat → introduce counter-attack framing (toe hold / passover / outside transition threats) while extracting.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - “Defend, defend, defend” with no threat; or “attack, attack, attack” while exposing your own heel.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Balanced rounds: win condition is (a) heel safety maintained AND (b) at least one credible counter-attack threat created before full extraction.
- System tags
  - [50-50] [symmetry] [balanced-mindset] [threat-and-extract]

## 8.2 Initial Leg Positions
- Objective
  - Recognize common 50/50 leg configurations and their practical separation difficulty.
- Gates
  - Foot-to-foot is “pretty easy to separate.”
  - Crossed ankles and full figure-four are meaningful structural variants (full figure-four typically harder than crossed ankles).
- Controls
  - Configuration recognition informs whether you separate directly or use threat/transfer logic.
- Primary line
  - Identify configuration → choose matching separation/extraction method.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If configuration escalates to full figure-four → expect harder separation; shift to toe-hold threat or structured extraction.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Treating all 50/50 configurations as equivalent.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Config ID reps: partner alternates foot-to-foot, crossed ankles, figure-four; defender calls config and executes first action.
- System tags
  - [config-recognition] [crossed-vs-figure4] [decision-gate]

## 8.3 Hiding The Heel
- Objective
  - Maintain heel safety while still enabling heel slips and extraction.
- Gates
  - Two heel-hiding positions are referenced; both must address leg extension problems.
  - If your leg gets extended and your toe line points wrong, heel safety degrades.
- Controls
  - Keep heel “in” (close to butt) as a default.
  - Pull butt in toward yourself to counter extension; do not accept an extended leg with poor toe/heel alignment.
- Primary line
  - Set heel-in posture → counter extension by pulling butt in → transition into heel slip / center-line extraction.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent extends your leg → immediate counter: pull butt in to re-hide heel and re-enter your escape plan.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Allowing leg extension to place you “out there” with compromised toe/heel alignment.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Extension resistance drill: attacker repeatedly extends; defender must restore heel-in posture before attempting any slip/extraction.
- System tags
  - [heel-hide] [extension-counter] [butt-in] [toe-line-discipline]

## 8.4 Center Chest Leg Extraction
- Objective
  - Extract the leg by putting it straight on the center line; avoid the “bent-leg trap.”
- Gates
  - Bent leg holds you in place; straight leg on center line is easier to pull away.
- Controls
  - Do not “pull then let them swoop back” to reclaim knee; extract while denying knee-reclaim.
  - Straighten leg on center line → then proceed into inside-position recovery and/or splitting legs.
- Primary line
  - Straighten leg on center line → extract away → deny knee reclaim → come up to inside position / begin splitting legs → exit.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent attempts to reclaim your knee as you extract → keep the extraction aligned on center line; do not drift into a bent-leg hold.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Attempting to pull a bent leg free (you’re held “in place”).
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Center-line extraction reps: start with bent leg; defender must straighten to center line before any pull is allowed.
- System tags
  - [center-line-extraction] [straight-vs-bent] [deny-knee-reclaim]

## 8.5 Separating Crossed Ankles
- Objective
  - Open crossed ankles by shearing with sole+heel control; escalate to full-body involvement if needed.
- Gates
  - Crossed-ankle connection may be “uber strong”; hands alone may lose to legs.
- Controls
  - Catch sole → catch heel → shear by push/pull to separate.
  - If connection is too strong, recruit full body: walk/shift weight instead of relying only on hands.
- Primary line
  - Sole+heel catch → shear open → once legs separate, proceed to extraction/transition.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If hands lose to legs → use bodyweight/walking mechanics to add force until separation occurs.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Prying without controlling both sole and heel; trying to win leg strength with only hand strength.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Strong-cross drill: attacker maximizes crossed-ankle strength; defender must separate using sole+heel and full-body shear.
- System tags
  - [crossed-ankle-separation] [sole-heel-control] [full-body-shear]

## 8.8 Toe Hold Vs Full Figure Four
- Objective
  - Use toe hold threat to either finish or force the opponent to unlock the full figure-four so you can extract.
- Gates
  - Full figure-four increases stability for attacks/controls; toe hold provides a forcing lever.
- Controls
  - Toe hold finish requires coupled shearing force; bridge hips forward plus strong back-heel and elbow-to-Achilles alignment are referenced.
  - Core idea: threat changes opponent behavior (unlock), enabling extraction.
- Primary line
  - Apply toe hold threat against figure-four → opponent unlocks or you finish → on unlock, transition immediately to extraction.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent refuses to unlock despite toe hold threat → toe hold becomes a finish attempt (if mechanics achieved).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Attempting to extract while opponent maintains full figure-four without applying any forcing threat.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - “Threat-to-unlock” rounds: attacker holds figure-four; defender must apply toe hold threat to induce unlock, then extract instantly.
- System tags

## 8.11 Building To A Knee
- Objective
  - Build to a knee safely from 50/50 when direct extraction/counter locks are not available; do not “build later” after being submitted.
- Gates
  - You must tuck the foot/heel to avoid elongation/extension as you rise.
- Controls
  - Tuck foot; pull heel in; rock up.
  - Place weight over partner’s knee/over your foot to stabilize.
  - Knee-up + foot-down produces an “athletic” position with reduced immediate danger.
- Primary line
  - Tuck foot/heel in → rock up → weight over knee/foot → establish knee-up/foot-down base → proceed to either leg extraction or positional play (including passing/back take options referenced).
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If opponent begins attacking as you unlock legs to rise → heel/foot tuck first, then continue building.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Unlocking legs to build without tucking heel/foot (elongation/extension risk).
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Build-to-knee timing reps: defender must tuck heel/foot before any rock-up is allowed.
- System tags
  - [build-to-knee] [athletic-base] [tuck-first] [no-late-building]

## 8.12 Extracting When They Unlock Legs
- Objective
  - Exploit the unlock event for immediate extraction, with and without an Achilles grip present.
- Gates
  - Two cases: (1) opponent has no Achilles, (2) opponent has an Achilles.
- Controls
  - If grip is low you can push it off; if hips are high it’s harder to strip, but high hip does not prevent lifting your leg.
  - If opponent weight is too heavy to step the leg up, you can scoop their leg to alleviate pressure.
  - The transcript includes additional micro-actions (elbow opening/strip) but portions are truncated; treat unclear segments as flagged.
- Primary line
  - Detect unlock → extract immediately; if Achilles grip interferes, strip/push if low, or use scooping/pressure alleviation if too heavy → complete extraction.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Grip low → push off.
  - Grip high/hips high → adjust: lifting/scooping instead of trying to “just push.”
  - Weight too heavy to step → scoop partner’s leg to reduce load, then extract.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Hesitating during the unlock window; trying to brute-step when weight is too heavy instead of scooping.
- Drills (explicit or implied)

## 8.13 Build To Standing Position
- Objective
  - Stand from 50/50 without being knocked over or inverted under; use grips to deny inversion and off-balancing.
- Gates
  - Standing creates two primary dangers: being knocked backward/off-balanced, and opponent inverting underneath you.
- Controls
  - Post hand on the floor to resist knockdowns.
  - Avoid “shoulders over hips” posture for extended time (knockdown risk).
  - Use a V-grip on opponent’s throat/head line so they can’t move head between your legs (denies key inversion/knockdown mechanics).
  - Lean/weight-shift to one side to make backward knockdown difficult.
- Primary line
  - Post hand → establish V-grip control → stand with weight leaned/shifted → prevent inversion/knockback → proceed to separation and extraction from standing.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If you posture and get knocked backward → return to post-hand + V-grip and re-stand with lean.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Standing tall with shoulders over hips; standing without controlling head/inversion line.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Stand-under-knockdown drill: attacker tries to knock back/invert; defender must keep post-hand + V-grip and maintain lean while standing.
- System tags
  - [build-to-stand] [post-hand] [v-grip-head-control] [deny-inversion] [no-shoulders-over-hips]

## 8.14 Extracting Legs From Standing Position
- Objective
  - Extract safely while standing without being sat down backward; open legs then stuff/pass to exit.
- Gates
  - Main danger: opponent sits you down backward; avoid shoulders-over-hips posture.
- Controls
  - Options stated:
    - If opponent unlocks: stuff leg between yours → step forward and pivot.
    - Keep head leaned back and post as needed; reach through/catch.
    - Separate legs with “one-two” action; once legs open, knockdown becomes harder.
    - Hip thrust forward to pass legs across; hands guide while hips do the work.
- Primary line
  - Maintain safe head/hip alignment → create separation (one-two) → once legs open: stuff leg between legs → step forward/pivot → extract to safety.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent resists separation strongly → hip thrust forward to present/push legs across; then extract from the new alignment.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Trying to separate while staying upright and centered; using hands-only separation instead of hips doing the work.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Standing extraction flow: separate → stuff → step/pivot; second round forces the hip-thrust branch (attacker resists separation).

## 8.15 Initial Heel Slip
- Objective
  - Execute the early heel slip by pulling partner close, exposing far elbow, and slipping heel during extension attempt.
- Gates
  - If you can reach elbow right away, do so; if not, go to head first (including two-hand head pull if needed).
- Controls
  - Pull partner toward you to create heel slip conditions.
  - Target: far elbow is “what ultimately is going to break me”; control/expose it.
  - As they extend, pull heel to butt and slip in the opposite direction; re-lock legs after initial slip to restore safety.
- Primary line
  - Pull partner close (head/elbow sequence) → expose/control far elbow → as they extend, pull heel to butt and heel slip → re-lock legs into safer posture.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If elbow is not reachable → head control first, then elbow; use two hands on head if needed to pull close.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Attempting heel slip without pulling partner close and without addressing the far elbow line.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - “Head-first vs elbow-first” reps: attacker distances elbow; defender must choose correct entry (head pull → elbow) then slip.
- System tags

## 8.16 Heel Slip To Counter Leg Extension
- Objective
  - Heel slip in crisis when opponent extends your leg; use hand-pulldown, and if needed, knee-control sit-over to stop reinversion.
- Gates
  - Crisis condition: heel grip established + leg extended.
- Controls
  - Core: push heel in toward partner; if too tight, pull hands down a few times before slipping.
  - If knee is on your hip and too tight to slip: grab partner’s knee → sit/cover knee with rib cage → prevent reinversion → then heel slip.
- Primary line
  - Extension occurs → attempt heel slip if loose enough; if tight, pull hands down → if still tight with knee-on-hip, grab knee and sit/cover with rib cage → then heel slip.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If early grip occurs before extension: get to head/elbow early and prevent full extension (preferred).
  - If heel slip feels too tight and you can’t get forward: use the knee-grab sit-over cover to stop them reinverting the knee, then slip.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Heel slipping into a fully tight, extended configuration; ignoring the knee-on-hip wedge that blocks forward motion.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Crisis ladder reps: attacker extends immediately; defender must run (1) slip attempt, (2) hands-down, (3) knee-grab sit-over cover, then slip.
- System tags
  - [crisis-heel-slip] [hands-down] [knee-grab] [rib-cage-cover] [extension-counter]

## 8.17 Attempting To Roll With The Lock
- Objective
  - Use full-rotation rolling as a low-percentage crisis tool to create motion when head/elbow access and heel slip are not available.
- Gates
  - Preconditions: cannot get to head/elbow, cannot heel slip, cannot scoot around corner; “none of it’s working.”
- Controls
  - Instructor warning: “relatively low percentage” and “you’re getting broken the whole way through.”
  - Goal: use momentum to loosen Ashi, bring hands lower, then attempt heel slip/escape on the other side.
- Primary line
  - Commit to full rotation roll → create motion to loosen Ashi → if hands lower, attempt heel slip → work toward escape.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If roll creates a moment where head/elbow access appears → take it; if not, continue using motion until a realistic escape tool appears.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Treating roll-with-lock as a standard plan; it’s explicitly framed as crisis-only and risky.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Crisis scenario drill: attacker denies head/elbow and tightens; defender practices full rotation roll to create a heel-slip window (acknowledging risk).
- System tags

## 8.18 2nd Leg Interference
- Objective
  - Add a second-leg interference tool when head/elbow access and single-leg heel slip are not viable, and rolling is too risky.
- Gates
  - Trigger: can’t get head/elbow; heel slip with one leg doesn’t work; rolling feels “almost impossible.”
- Controls
  - Place second leg on inside of opponent’s elbow to block belly-down extension and create time.
  - Alternative: second-leg contact to opponent’s wrist.
  - Use interference as a bridge to re-enable head/elbow access or reduce rotation danger.
- Primary line
  - Insert second leg to elbow/wrist → disrupt belly-down extension and rotation → use the created time to re-establish head/elbow access or proceed to endgame extraction.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent goes belly down and starts extending → elbow-interference blocks extension; then you work back to head/elbow or safer structure.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Forcing one-leg heel slip in a configuration where it “isn’t going to work” and ignoring the available second-leg tool.
- Drills (explicit or implied)

## 8.19 End Game
- Objective

- Gates
  - Belly-up gives you recurring options to scoot around the corner and work; belly-down reduces your options and escalates crisis.
- Controls
  - Late escapes exist but are “not really realistic to do reliably time and time again”; prefer earlier decision layers.
- Primary line
  - If belly-up: use corner-scoot/head-elbow tools to work back to escapes; if belly-down: expect endgame crisis and prioritize preventing prolonged finishing mechanics.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If earlier escapes are available, take them; do not delay into endgame where reliability drops.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Repeatedly relying on late/endgame escapes as a primary strategy.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Stage drill: start at early/mid/late; measure success rate; reinforce “solve earlier” bias.

## 8.25 Recapping 50/50
- Objective
  - Reinforce: many leg configurations exist; success depends on understanding leg-to-body relationships under stress and maintaining balanced offense/defense.
- Gates
  - If you have no threat, opponent has “no respect” and attacks continuously.
- Controls
  - Continuous configuration awareness; do not collapse into single-minded defense.
- Primary line
  - Identify configuration → apply correct tool (center-line extraction, crossed-ankle separation, toe-hold threat, passover, build-up, heel slip) while maintaining threat posture.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
