# VN07 — Volume 7 (TRN_GR_POD_LLECL_7.txt) — Inside Ashi (re-pummel/extract, transfer-to-outside, roll series)

## 7.1 Inside Ashi - Intro To Inside Ashi
- Objective
  - Treat Inside Ashi as an “always-heel-exposed” problem; prioritize separation/extraction over pretending you can fully hide the heel.
- Gates
  - If your partner is moderately competent, the heel is functionally exposed “every single time” in Inside Ashi.
  - Heel hiding is a time-buy only; it does not solve the position.
- Controls
  - Defensive mindset shift: do not build a plan that assumes long-duration heel concealment.
  - Structural priority: remove the Ashi control (separate legs / pummel inside / transfer) so heel hook heat becomes irrelevant.
- Primary line
  - Immediately work to break the Ashi structure (separate legs) → bring knees together → pummel legs to inside → exit to safety/neutral.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent begins exposing/digging heel → treat as an alarm to accelerate separation and knee-inside recovery (do not “hang out” trying to hide).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Planning to “hide the heel” as the core solution in Inside Ashi.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - “Inside Ashi alarm” reps: partner enters Inside Ashi and begins heel exposure; defender’s only goal is immediate separation + knees together + pummel inside.
- System tags
  - [inside-ashi] [heel-exposed] [separation-first] [knees-together] [inside-recovery]

## 7.2 Attempting To Hide The Heel
- Objective
  - Buy minimal time by hiding the heel “the best you can” while you initiate separation/extraction.
- Gates
  - Heel hiding is limited in Inside Ashi; at best you reduce digs (e.g., “three or four digs versus just one”).
- Controls
  - Hide heel to reduce immediate access; use the time-buy to start the real work (separate legs / re-pummel / transfer).
- Primary line
  - Hide heel as a temporary measure → continue directly into separation/extraction pathway.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent continues digging and inevitably exposes heel → you must already be mid-separation; do not restart “hide-only” loop.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Hiding heel without immediately initiating the structural exit.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - “Time-buy to exit” drill: defender must hide heel briefly then immediately transition to separation; reps fail if defender stays in hide-only mode.
- System tags
  - [heel-hide] [time-buy] [no-stall] [inside-ashi]

## 7.3 Separating And Extracting
- Objective
  - Remove the Ashi control so heel hook heat cannot be meaningfully applied; exit via knees-together + inside pummel.
- Gates
  - Inside Ashi configurations encountered: crossed ankles vs full figure-four.
  - If opponent is locked on the far leg, you must first address that control to re-establish knee-inside safety.
- Controls
  - Separation mechanics (as stated): catch heel/foot and split the legs; if crossed ankles, separate by controlling heel/foot; if full figure-four, you must address the figure-four before separation is reliable (detail is partially truncated in transcript; treat as configuration-specific).
  - “Knees together” after separation is the critical stabilizer that prevents immediate heel exposure during movement.
- Primary line
  - Split/separate opponent’s legs → bring your knees together → pummel legs to inside → move out and away to neutral/safety.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent locks far-leg control and blocks clean knee-inside recovery → you must “scoot/pummel” to reinsert the leg inside, then continue.
  - Opponent attempts heel hook once the Ashi structure is removed → heel hook heat is now structurally blunted because “there’s no Ashi Garami in place”; finish the inside pummel and disengage.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Attempting to heel-slip/escape while the Ashi structure is still intact (you are “moments away” from exposure/break).
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Config recognition drill: partner alternates crossed ankles vs figure-four; defender must execute correct separation path, then knees-together, then inside pummel.
- System tags
  - [leg-separation] [knees-together] [inside-pummel] [extract] [configuration-aware]

## 7.4 Re-Pummel
- Objective
  - Convert separation into a full inside-position recovery and (optionally) immediate counter-attacking posture.
- Gates
  - Re-pummel becomes available once a space develops and/or knees are together.
- Controls
  - “Re-pummel into various leg configurations” once the immediate heel exposure is reduced.
  - Offensive pivot exists after successful re-pummel (attack once you’re no longer in immediate danger).
- Primary line
  - Create space → knees together → re-pummel legs to inside → stabilize → transition to attack or disengage.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If space develops mid-scramble → step the knees in and re-pummel immediately (do not delay).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Winning partial separation but failing to re-pummel, allowing re-entry into Inside Ashi.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Re-pummel chaining: separation achieved → must re-pummel into inside configuration before rep ends.
- System tags
  - [re-pummel] [inside-recovery] [chain-to-attack]

## 7.5 Transfer To Outside Ashi
- Objective
  - Use transfer to Outside Ashi as a safer defensive channel when Inside Ashi heel risk remains high.
- Gates
  - Trigger: you cannot produce a safe heel slip from current Inside Ashi configuration, or opponent’s configuration makes heel slip unsafe.
- Controls
  - As described: catch/lean forward; if opponent extends legs, you can bring hips forward and force a reap-like alignment; then knee-down.
  - Works against crossed-ankles and full figure-four entries (treated similarly in transcript).
- Primary line
  - Initiate transfer mechanics → hips forward/lean forward → force opponent into reap alignment → bring knee down → continue into Outside-Ashi defensive exits.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent extends legs to keep you from coming forward → hips forward to force reap alignment → knee-down escape pathway.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Persisting with unsafe heel-slip attempts in configurations the instructor flags as non-slip-capable.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Inside→Outside transfer reps: partner maintains Inside Ashi; defender must transfer and land knee-down under extension resistance.
- System tags
  - [transfer-defense] [inside-to-outside] [reap-alignment] [knee-down]

## 7.6 Knee Down Escape
- Objective
  - Use knee-down as the stable conversion when the opponent is already in a reap alignment and you need a reliable base.
- Gates
  - “Perfect scenario”: opponent is already reaping and feeds the leg; knee-down is available.
- Controls
  - Sequence as stated: heist/come up → slide knee to floor → choose immediate posting or heel slip; if not available, multiple rolls.
- Primary line
  - Heist → knee slides to floor → post/structure → heel slip if available; otherwise transition to multiple rolls.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If posting is available immediately → post and stabilize first.
  - If heel slip is available from knee-down → slip and clear.
  - If neither is available cleanly → multiple rolls.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Trying to force heel slip when configuration makes it unsafe/unavailable; delaying the roll decision when you’re already in a reap.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Reap-start drill: start with opponent already reaping; defender must land knee-down and pick the correct branch (post vs heel slip vs rolls).
- System tags
  - [knee-down] [reap-context] [post-or-slip] [roll-bailout]

## 7.7 Full Roll To Change Leg Position
- Objective
  - Change leg configuration via defensive roll when heel slip is unsafe in current Inside Ashi alignment.
- Gates
  - Instructor warning: attempting heel slip from certain Inside Ashi leg configurations “will break my own leg.”
- Controls
  - Full defensive roll changes the leg positions and can transfer you into Outside Ashi; secondary roll available if needed.
- Primary line
  - Recognize heel-slip is unsafe → full defensive roll to change leg position → land into Outside Ashi pathway → continue escape.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If first roll does not achieve a safer leg relationship → perform secondary roll (opening legs) to continue transition.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - “Heel slipping anyway” from a configuration explicitly identified as self-breaking.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Config gate reps: partner sets “non-slip” Inside Ashi configuration; defender must choose full roll to change leg position (not heel slip).
- System tags
  - [defensive-roll] [config-change] [inside-to-outside] [no-unsafe-heel-slip]

## 7.8 Multiple Rolls
- Objective
  - Use multiple-roll series to create the conditions for heel slip / hand lowering and/or escape when single transitions fail.
- Gates
  - Trigger: heel hook is on; single roll becomes multiple rolls; roll series is required to create separation and/or bring hands down.
- Controls
  - Multiple rolls can (a) bring hands down and allow heel slip, and/or (b) transfer you into Outside Ashi if possible.
- Primary line
  - Commit to full roll → if not resolved, continue into multiple rolls → capitalize when hands lower / position improves → heel slip or transition to Outside Ashi → exit.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - If transfer to Outside Ashi becomes available mid-series → take it as the safer route.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Expecting a single roll to solve Inside Ashi heel hook pressure consistently.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - “Roll-to-hands-lower” drill: attacker maintains heel hook; defender rolls until a clear heel-slip window is created, then must finish the exit immediately.
- System tags
  - [multiple-rolls] [hands-lower] [heel-slip-window] [transfer-option]

## 7.9 Recapping Cross And Inside Ashi
- Objective
  - Distinguish Cross vs Inside threat structures to pick correct default tools.
- Gates
  - Cross: both legs controlled; very hard to build to a knee; hard to apply bodyweight; secondary-leg release is foundational to defenses.
  - Inside: heel exposure is structurally persistent; separation/transfer is urgent.
- Controls
  - Cross priority: free secondary leg (or wait for release) to enable defenses.
  - Inside priority: separate legs + knees together + re-pummel/transfer.
- Primary line
  - Identify family (Cross vs Inside) → apply the correct default ladder (Cross: hierarchy/secondary-first; Inside: separation/transfer) → proceed.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - When moving Ashi-to-Ashi in scrambles, complexity increases; default back to family identification and the appropriate priority stack.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Applying Cross logic to Inside (standing/building) or Inside logic to Cross (assuming you can separate/stand freely).
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Family-switch drill: partner cycles Cross ↔ Inside; defender must call family and run the correct first three actions every rep.
- System tags
  - [family-identification] [cross-vs-inside] [priority-stack] [scramble-management]
