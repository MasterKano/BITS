## VN01 — Volume 1

### 1.1 Leg Lock Defense — Intro
- Objective
  - Set scope: leg lock defense requires prior offensive understanding; defense = extraction + re-entry into offense/position.
- Gates
  - Start-state assumption: opponent already entangled (no entry defense).
  - Mindset gate: composure before movement.
- Controls
  - Conceptual control: understand what the attacker is trying to accomplish (breaking mechanics, transitions).
- Primary line
  - Learn offensive leg locks → recognize breaking mechanics → build defensive sequences that remove breaking mechanics (knee-line, heel exposure, rotation, belly-down).
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent enters legs and you panic → slow down, stabilize, then select escape plan (disengage vs stay-in-pocket).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - “Explode-first” = worsens danger; you move without removing control points.
- Drills
  - Missing/Not explicit in this chapter.
- System tags
  - [Mindset: Calm Under Fire] [Offense↔Defense Coupling] [Static Start Assumption]

### 1.2 Overview
- Objective
  - Establish the instructional traversal: positional families → (no-sub) → (sub-on) → (knee/stand variants).
- Gates
  - Progression gate: seated defense → build-to-knee → build-to-stand (where applicable).
- Controls
  - Macro control: always identify which Ashi family you’re in before selecting line.
- Primary line
  - Run sequence: Irimi Ashi (no-sub) → Achilles → toe hold → heel hook → Outside Ashi → Top Oshiro → Cross Ashi → Inside Ashi → 50/50 → Mutual Ashi (+ minor variants: butterfly/post/backside 50/50).
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent shifts sub-system (post ashi / butterfly ashi / etc) → treat as same family until leg configuration forces a new family call.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Treating “leg locks” as one blob → wrong escape choice; wrong priority.
- Drills
  - Implied: positional identification reps (call the family; pick the correct ladder: sit/knee/stand).
- System tags
  - [Ashi Family Taxonomy] [Base Ladder: Seat→Knee→Stand] [Subsystem Awareness]

### 1.3 Theory
- Objective
  - Define universal defensive priorities: composure → choose goal → remove control points → exit/convert.
- Gates
  - Goal gate: (A) disengage to neutral vs (B) stay in pocket and counter (leg attack / pass / back exposure).
  - Structural gate: knee-line status (knee inside opponent’s knee-line = danger; knee outside = safer).
  - Exposure gate: heel/foot exposure must be solved before big movement.
- Controls
  - Knee-line concept: free knee from opponent’s knee-line to become “safe from knee bars/toe holds/heel hooks”.
  - Wedge assessment: how strong is their structure on your hip/knee/ankle; what do you need to break first.
- Primary line
  - Calm → decide outcome → identify control points (knee-line, heel exposure, wedge/ashi) → apply the smallest motion that breaks a control point → then advance to next stage (seat→knee→stand) as needed.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent stronger in leg locks → bias toward disengage/neutral.
  - You stronger → stay in pocket; counter-threaten to create unlocks.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Thinking about “getting out” without naming what is trapping you (knee-line vs heel vs ashi) = random motion.
- Drills
  - Implied: “name-the-control” reps (partner entangles; you call: knee-line? heel? rotation? then perform first corrective action only).
- System tags
  - [Control-Point Diagnosis] [Knee-Line Priority] [Exposure First] [Stay-in-Pocket vs Disengage]

### 1.4 Battle For Inside Position
- Objective
  - Install the core thesis: inside real estate wins leg exchanges (knees/feet inside = deny access; outside = give access).
- Gates
  - Real-estate gate: your two knees/feet inside theirs = deny access; theirs inside yours = they attack, you cannot.
  - Symmetry gate (50/50-type): whoever steals inside knee position gains advantage.
- Controls
  - Inside position control: pummeling feet/knees to inside; deny opponent’s pummel.
- Primary line
  - Continuously fight to put your knees/feet inside theirs; use pummeling to reverse access and either (A) extract to safety or (B) enter mutual/attacking configurations.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent pummels one leg inside → immediately respond with pummel to recover inside or re-square to deny the second pummel.
  - Opponent gets both inside → treat as emergency: you must re-pummel or exit.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Accepting outside knee/foot position = you are letting them own your legs.
- Drills
  - Implied: inside-position sparring from seated entanglements (score = “double inside”).
- System tags
  - [Inside-Position Thesis] [Pummeling Loop] [Access Denial]

### 1.5 Basic Pummeling Drills
- Objective
  - Build automaticity for inside-position recovery with the legs (analogy: wrestling hand pummel for double-unders).
- Gates
  - Training gate: do this as a daily “base hygiene” drill (high reps; low complexity).
- Controls
  - Leg pummel: feet fighting to inside; prevent opponent from obtaining “double inside” equivalent.
- Primary line
  - Run basic leg-pummeling exchange until you can consistently win inside position without overcommitting posture.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent pummels inside → you immediately pummel back; do not accept outside.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Treating pummeling as “random leg flailing” vs goal-driven inside capture.
- Drills
  - Explicit: basic leg pummeling drill for inside-position habit-building.
- System tags
  - [Drill: Leg Pummeling] [Inside Recovery] [Habit Formation]

### 1.6 Irimi Ashi Garami — Intro To Irimi Ashi Garami
- Objective
  - Frame Irimi Ashi as a “non-catastrophic” control that often transitions to Outside Ashi for breaking; your window is during motion/transition.
- Gates
  - Family gate: Irimi can shift to post ashi / butterfly ashi with Achilles dynamics.
  - Transition gate: opponent’s move Irimi → Outside = opportunity window (their movement = your escape timing).
- Controls
  - Motion principle: motion disrupts breaking mechanics; static = danger.
  - Separation target: break/peel their foot/ashi structure before Outside Ashi is established.
- Primary line
  - Use movement (separate feet, step-over attempts, pummels) to prevent the clean transition to Outside Ashi; exit or counter before “catastrophic territory”.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent starts transitioning to Outside Ashi → accelerate separation/escape sequence immediately (don’t wait until settled).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Staying static inside Irimi while opponent consolidates transition pathways.
- Drills
  - Implied: “transition window” rounds (partner’s goal: move Irimi→Outside; your goal: separate/exit during motion).
- System tags
  - [Irimi Hub] [Transition Window] [Motion vs Breaking] [Separation Priority]

### 1.7 Initially Protecting Your Feet
- Objective
  - Solve the immediate dilemma: foot angle trades Achilles vulnerability vs heel-hook exposure; choose a baseline that minimizes catastrophic outcomes.
- Gates
  - Dilemma gate
    - Boot (toes flexed) → easier heel exposure (heel hooks).
    - Point toes → harder heel exposure but Achilles comes on stronger.
- Controls
  - “Heel glued to hip” concept: positioning that reduces both Achilles and heel exposure (explicitly stated as preferred baseline).
  - Foot orientation management: do not let foot angle become the attacker’s lever.
- Primary line
  - Establish initial foot placement that keeps heel tight to hip line and reduces exposure; then proceed into inside-position recovery and base ladder.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent hunts heel (heel hook threat) → de-boot/point to hide heel; adjust immediately.
  - Opponent commits Achilles → manage without yanking foot; move to separation and belly-up control (expanded in VN02).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Defaulting to “boot hard” under Irimi and giving heel exposure.
- Drills
  - Implied: partner alternates Achilles/heel-hook attempts; you adjust foot angle + heel connection without panicking.
- System tags
  - [Foot-Angle Dilemma] [Heel Safety] [Initial Configuration]

### 1.8 Pummeling Back To Neutral
- Objective
  - Use re-pummel to recover inside and either disengage to neutral or convert into mutual/offense.
- Gates
  - Structural gate: “door” between opponent’s knees must open enough to pummel the foot inside.
  - Engagement gate: choose (A) stay in pocket (mutual ashi / attack) vs (B) full disengage (neutral reset).
- Controls
  - False-grip control on ankle/foot; butterfly hook as upper-leg clamp; knee-behind-knee positioning to prevent re-clamp.
- Primary line
  - Maintain heel as close to opponent’s hip as possible → when top knee opens, pummel foot inside → use false grip + butterfly hook control → pull foot to hip / drive leg → insert knee behind their knee → pull into mutual ashi → either attack or disengage.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent runs away as you pummel inside → you can disengage to neutral (they are already breaking contact).
  - Opponent tries to transition to Outside Ashi as you pull foot → you must control the upper leg (cover knee) before completing the pull.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Pulling the foot without controlling the upper leg → opponent knee-to-chest → fires Outside Ashi.
- Drills
  - Implied: “door opens” reaction drill (partner slightly opens knee; you pummel + establish false grip/hook before they re-close).
- System tags
  - [Re-Pummel Loop] [Inside Recovery] [Pocket vs Disengage] [Upper-Leg Control]

### 1.9 Building To A Knee
- Objective
  - Convert seated defense to kneeling structure to improve stripping, base, and leg mobility while maintaining heel safety.
- Gates
  - Base gate: post hand as needed; don’t build upright without structure.
- Controls
  - Post hand as anti-exposure / anti-off-balance; knee/hip alignment to prevent being knocked back.
- Primary line
  - From Irimi: establish post hand → manage heel exposure → use momentum/structure to come to knee → keep weight over safe side; prepare to strip/separate.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent extends/exposes heel as you build → keep post hand; stay leaned to safe side; strip when their extension gives momentum.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Coming up without a post → knocked back; heel becomes accessible.
- Drills
  - Implied: build-to-knee repetitions with partner attempting to off-balance.
- System tags
  - [Base Ladder: Seat→Knee] [Post-Hand Structure] [Anti-Off-Balance]

### 1.10 High Stepping Out
- Objective
  - Achieve clean leg exit without immediately re-entering a second entanglement; exit into a split-legs passing posture.
- Gates
  - Strip gate: must first disrupt their extension/exposure attempt enough to free the foot.
- Controls
  - Post hand to block heel exposure attempts; weight shift to prevent knockback; plant position between opponent’s legs to deny re-pummel.
- Primary line
  - Post hand in place → lean weight to safe side → catch/strip grip as opponent extends → high-step straight out → plant foot between opponent’s legs (split legs) → immediately connect knee+elbow to steal inside and prevent their re-pummel → initiate passing.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent tries to re-pummel after you step out → knee+elbow connection blocks their pummel; transition to pass.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Kicking leg far back/away → opponent re-pummels both feet inside → immediate secondary attack (leg entry or upper-body attacks).
- Drills
  - Implied: high-step exit with “re-pummel chase” from partner; defender must land in split-legs + knee/elbow clamp.
- System tags
  - [Exit: High-Step] [Split-Legs Denial] [Pass-Ready Finish] [Inside Reclaim]

### 1.11 Re-Pummel (From Knee)
- Objective
  - Use threats and leg pummeling from kneeling to (A) extract, (B) enter mutual ashi/offense, or (C) force unlocks.
- Gates
  - Opportunity gate: attacker’s inside leg becomes “lazy” (heel drifts toward centerline/primary leg).
- Controls
  - Heel catch (one- or two-hand), pull pressure to induce withdrawal; knee coverage to prevent Outside Ashi firing.
- Primary line
  - Identify drifting heel → catch heel → pull to create pressure (not a finish vs competent opponent) → as heel withdraws, guide and feed into mutual ashi → or strip grip and high-step out; always cover upper leg (knee) to stop knee-to-chest → Outside Ashi.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent tries to fire Outside Ashi as you pull heel → you must control the knee/upper leg; don’t allow knee-to-chest.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Stripping grip but not controlling the knee → you trade Irimi for Outside Ashi (worse).
- Drills
  - Implied: “lazy leg punish” drill: partner intentionally drifts heel; you catch → feed mutual ashi or high-step.
- System tags
  - [Re-Pummel as Threat] [Knee Coverage] [Mutual Ashi Entry] [Opportunity Capture]

### 1.12 Build To Standing Position
- Objective
  - Stand without giving off-balance or heel exposure; maintain base so you can clear, backstep, or re-pummel.
- Gates
  - Post gate: keep a post hand on the floor during stand-up to prevent knockback.
  - Base gate: do not stand tall/vertical; keep head and weight oriented over your posted foot.
- Controls
  - Post hand + weighted foot base; head alignment toward own foot; elbow wedge against attacker’s knee as needed.
- Primary line
  - From knee: keep post hand on floor → shift weight to support foot (not onto post hand) → stand with head moving toward your own foot → stay crouched (don’t stand tall) → optionally catch opponent’s knee and wedge with elbow to prevent rotation/exposure → stabilize before clearing.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent tries to knock you backward during stand → weight lean direction prevents; post hand blocks.
  - Opponent tries to expose heel while you’re standing → wedge and keep weight on your foot; adjust before stepping away.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Turning head/weight over the post hand → heel exposure and knockdown.
  - Standing straight up → easy knockback.
- Drills
  - Implied: stand-up reps under active off-balance attempts.
- System tags
  - [Base Ladder: Knee→Stand] [Post-Hand Stand] [Head/Weight Alignment] [Standing Stability]

### 1.13 Forward Stepping
- Objective
  - Pure disengagement method: free the leg by stepping above shoulder line and walking away once connection is disrupted.
- Gates
  - Connection gate: must break Ashi connection; cannot “run away” with closed circle still intact.
  - Achilles tightness gate: forward-run viability is sensitivity-based; sticky Achilles may stop it.
- Controls
  - V-grip peel on ankles/feet to disrupt the circle; step above shoulder line to clear “shoelaces vs shoulder” snag.
- Primary line
  - V-grip attacker’s ankle(s) → strip legs off your leg → as legs drop, take a big step above shoulder line → walk/run away to pull leg free → reset to neutral.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent clamps hard and stays connected → you must peel first; if not peeled you can pick them up and nothing changes.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Attempting forward sprint without breaking connection → no exit; high risk.
- Drills
  - Implied: peel→step-forward timing reps with varying Achilles “stickiness”.
- System tags
  - [Disengage-to-Neutral] [Connection Break First] [Forward Escape]

### 1.14 Backstepping
- Objective
  - Preferred standing exit: remain in pocket, clear past hip line, and immediately enter passing exchanges.
- Gates
  - Pocket gate: choose backstep when you want follow-up offense (pass/back take) rather than reset.
- Controls
  - Posture: backstep puts both your feet to the same side of opponent’s body → denies meaningful guard/leg connection.
- Primary line
  - Strip grip / disrupt Ashi → backstep past hip line → establish both feet on same side of opponent → start passing sequence (camp shoulder line, force guard recovery, etc).
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent tries to re-square to recover guard → you exploit their recovery to pass (direction changes, torreando-type movement, etc as referenced).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Forward stepping when you needed to stay engaged (gives up pocket and follow-up).
- Drills
  - Implied: strip→backstep→immediate pass initiation rounds.
- System tags
  - [Stay-in-Pocket] [Backstep Exit] [Pass Conversion] [Same-Side Safety]

### 1.15 Re-Pummel (Standing)
- Objective
  - Use standing re-pummel to convert defense into offense (mutual ashi entry) or to create easier exits.
- Gates
  - Standing mobility gate: open knee and base foot allow easier hand usage (one hand / two hands).
- Controls
  - Grip options: two-hand control becomes easier due to active base foot; upper-leg control (above knee) before stepping in.
- Primary line
  - From standing: strip grip → pull yourself in while controlling opponent’s top leg above the knee → slide your knee inside → step in and drop → enter mutual ashi → proceed to attacks or use threat to disengage.
- Branches (opponent action → your response)
  - Opponent attempts to reap/shift during re-pummel → use base foot to post and maintain alignment; continue knee-entry.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Entering without controlling above-knee line → opponent can reconfigure legs and re-entangle.
- Drills
  - Implied: standing re-pummel entries with partner actively trying to re-entangle.
- System tags
  - [Standing Re-Pummel] [Mutual Ashi Conversion] [Above-Knee Control] [Offense-from-Defense]
