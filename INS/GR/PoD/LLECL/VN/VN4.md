# VIEWING NOTES — INS/GR/PoD/LLECL
## VN03 — Volume 3: Toe Holds + Heel Hooks (Irimi)

### 3.1 Toe holds — Intro (mechanics + defensive order)
- Objective
  - Define toe hold breaking mechanics and install the defensive “order of operations” from Irimi Ashi.
- Gates
  - Break requires coupled force: toes-to-butt + heel-to-chin, held static through Ashi.
  - Toe hold pressure can come on fast (hands apply lock immediately), so early action matters.
- Controls
  - Control-point framing: remove either (A) upper-body lock (hands connection) or (B) lower-body constraint (Ashi) before static break time accumulates.
- Primary line
  - Attempt 1: slip initial grip via hip/leg/foot turning.
  - Attempt 2: if locked, separate hands (upper-body).
  - Attempt 3: second-leg interference (accept “double trouble” risk vs immediate break risk).
  - Attempt 4: address Ashi (step-over / sit-over) to remove lower-body control.
  - Attempt 5: half-roll to buy time to separate legs; then full roll/high-leg through.
  - Attempt 6: multiple rolls (corkscrew) until knee exits knee line; then final push/post to clear.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Attacker locks quickly and cranks immediately → skip “fiddling” and move to interference/roll ladder without delay.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Treating toe hold like a slow lock; waiting for “tight” before acting.
- Drills (explicit or implied)
  - Progressive escalation rounds: attacker tries fast lock/crank; defender must run order-of-ops without pausing.
- System tags
  - [Irimi] [ToeHold] [OrderOfOps] [UpperVsLowerSeparation] [TimeToCatastrophe]

### 3.2 Toe holds — Hiding feet (baseline safety)
- Objective
  - Deny easy access to toe holds/heel hooks by default foot orientation.
- Gates
  - Exposure rule: toe holds/heel hooks require toes pointing toward attacker + heel away from attacker’s body.
- Controls
  - Heel connected to attacker’s hip/ribcage; toes pointing away (do not “point toes at partner”).
- Primary line
  - Maintain heel connection + toes-away orientation continuously; treat toe-in drift as an alarm.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Toes begin drifting toward attacker torso line → immediately reorient heel in / toes away before addressing grips.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Allowing heel to float away from attacker; toe line points inward and invites one-handed toe hold attempts.
- Drills
  - Position-maintenance drill: partner hunts toe/heel exposure; you continuously re-hide heel/toes without initiating escape.
- System tags
  - [FootOrientation] [HeelConnection] [PreventionLayer]

### 3.3 Toe holds — Slip the initial grip (heel moves, toes don’t)
- Objective
  - Escape during lock formation before hands tighten.
- Gates
  - Works in early stage: as attacker reaches to lock / lock is not yet tight; sensitivity-based threshold.
- Controls
  - Key rule: do not fight the “long end of the lever” (toes). Move heel instead.
  - Target: drag outside blade of foot to floor while scooting around the corner; bring heel and butt closer.
- Primary line
  - As attacker goes to lock: immediately scoot around corner + pull heel inward + drag outside blade to floor → hands slip off → toe hold removed.
- Branches
  - If lock is already too tight to slip (sensitivity says “not happening”) → transition immediately to secondary stages (interference / Ashi address / roll).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Trying to point toes away once toes are grabbed; toes don’t move, heel does.
- Drills
  - “Lock-start trigger” drill: attacker touches toes and begins to lock; defender must slip within 1 beat or reset.
- System tags
  - [EarlySlip] [HeelNotToes] [CornerScoot] [SensitivityGate]

### 3.4 Toe holds — Second-leg interference (elbow post / wrist wedge)
- Objective
  - Break/deny hand connection when lock is tight and you are near break point.
- Gates
  - Use when you feel tight lock + imminent pop/break; accept that it can expose your second ankle.
- Controls
  - Option A: post second foot on attacker’s elbow to disrupt wrist-to-wrist connection.
  - Option B (preferred/robust when available): wedge heel deep on attacker’s wrist (short lever) and scissor legs (pull one leg, push the other) to separate hands.
  - Risk note: attacker may capture both ankles (“double trouble”) and transition (outside ashi / top oshiro); accept as “next problem” vs breaking now.
- Primary line
  - Insert second foot (wrist wedge if possible; otherwise elbow post) → scissor to separate hands → pull trapped leg free → re-hide foot orientation.
- Branches
  - Attacker captures both ankles (double trouble) → proceed to next defensive module (not fully detailed in this volume); priority remains limb safety.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Refusing interference because of future positional risk and getting broken in the present.
- Drills
  - Two-lane drill: attacker alternates elbow-tight vs wrist-accessible; defender must pick elbow post vs wrist wedge and scissor cleanly.
- System tags
  - [SecondLegInterference] [ElbowPost] [WristWedge] [DoubleTroubleRiskTrade]

### 3.5 Toe holds — Address the Ashi (step over + sit-over)
- Objective
  - Remove Ashi constraint so toe hold becomes harder to finish and you can exit immediately.
- Gates
  - Use when upper-body solutions fail and pressure is coming on fast.
- Controls
  - Post on attacker’s foot (any contact that clears it off your hip is acceptable).
  - Step over / sit on attacker’s leg to remove Ashi; then do not remain static (you will break if you stay).
- Primary line
  - Post on foot → step over and sit on their leg (Ashi removed) → immediately straighten/walk out OR pummel inside and post hamstring to rotate out.
- Branches
  - Attacker tries to switch to outside ashi during your step-over → pummel to inside position immediately; expose hamstring; rotate/push to clear knee line and exit.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Sitting over the leg and pausing; toe hold can still break you without Ashi if you stay.
- Drills
  - Step-over urgency drill: defender must step over + exit within 1–2 beats; reset if they pause.
- System tags
  - [AshiRemoval] [StepOver] [SitOverLeg] [NoPause]

### 3.6 Toe holds — Half roll to separate (buy time, then separate legs)
- Objective
  - Use the beginning of a roll to reduce pressure and earn time to separate legs.
- Gates
  - Use when you cannot separate legs immediately and pressure is high.
- Controls
  - Control attacker’s foot while moving your hips (move yourself, not their foot).
  - Half-roll gives “half second” to separate legs; separation is mandatory before full roll/high-leg.
- Primary line
  - Initiate roll → stop halfway → separate legs → continue into full roll with high-leg through → strip hands or scoot/straighten leg to finish exit.
- Branches
  - After separation: Option 1 strip hands immediately; Option 2 straighten/scoot back, then post on elbow and pull foot out.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Rolling without separating Ashi; attacker follows and breaks you.
  - Knee hits floor and you pause; attacker throws leg back over and rolls you through (reconnects).
- Drills
  - Half-roll checkpoint reps: roll-to-half, freeze, separate legs, then finish full roll/high-leg without pausing.
- System tags
  - [HalfRoll] [LegSeparation] [HighLegThrough] [NoRollWithoutSeparation]

### 3.7 Toe holds — Multiple rolls (corkscrew knee-line exit)
- Objective
  - Incrementally pull knee out of knee line when you cannot win upper-body or lower-body separation early.
- Gates
  - Use when you cannot separate hands, cannot separate feet, and must create motion to escape.
- Controls
  - Corkscrew rule: each rotation moves you further away (not just rolling along mat).
  - “Late push” rule: do not push hamstring/butt early; push only when knee is almost free to gain last inch or two.
- Primary line
  - Roll repeatedly (distance increases each rotation) → once knee nearly free → post/push on hamstring/butt (thumb post or foot) → extract knee and exit.
- Branches
  - “Little openings” appear during roll series → if you can separate legs mid-series, convert immediately to knee-down + high-leg through.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Pushing early while knee is still deep in knee line; you cannot push out of deep Ashi.
- Drills
  - Runway roll drill: defender rolls 5–10 reps focusing on distance per rotation; finishes with late push when knee is almost out.
- System tags
  - [MultipleRolls] [Corkscrew] [KneeLineExit] [LatePush]

### 3.8 Toe holds — End game (time model)
- Objective
  - Define terminal condition: can you separate enough before catastrophic break.
- Gates
  - If all steps fail (slip, interfere, separate legs, roll) and attacker maintains static breaking mechanics → end game is break/tap.
- Controls
  - Time framing: joint locks allow “infinite time” only if you accept break; operationally you do not accept break, so time is finite.
- Primary line
  - Run order-of-ops; if you cannot create meaningful separation before break threshold → loss condition.
- Branches
  - If you become too fatigued to roll and are stuck static → loss condition accelerates.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Underestimating time pressure; assuming you can always “just roll out later.”
- Drills
  - Timed end-game rounds: attacker tries to keep defender static; defender must force separation within a short clock.
- System tags
  - [TimeModel] [CatastrophicThreshold] [EndGame]

### 3.9 Toe holds — Defending from a knee (no re-pummel)
- Objective
  - Provide knee-start options where pressure is immediate and re-pummel is not available.
- Gates
  - On a knee with toe hold locked: you are in immediate break corridor.
- Controls
  - Option A: strip grip off hip → knee-drop roll (“highlight through” style).
  - Option B: immediate full roll-through into multiple rolls (if you cannot strip).
- Primary line
  - Choose A if strip is available; otherwise commit to B immediately and roll until knee clears.
- Branches
  - If you can separate Ashi first → one roll may be enough; if not → expect multiple rolls.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Attempting to re-pummel from knee while toe hold is breaking.
- Drills
  - Knee-start decision drill: attacker locks toe hold; defender must pick A or B instantly; reset on hesitation.
- System tags
  - [KneeStart] [ImmediateDecision] [RollThrough]

### 3.10 Heel hooks — Intro (stronger lock, slower pressure)
- Objective
  - Install the tradeoff: heel hooks are mechanically stronger (cleaner breaks), but typically slower to apply pressure than toe holds, giving reaction time.
- Gates
  - Initial heel exposure/hand positioning determines whether heel slip is safe or self-destructive.
- Controls
  - Same baseline hiding rule as toe holds: heel connected to ribcage; toes away.
- Primary line
  - Hide heel → assess hand height/tightness → heel slip only in early stage (hands low) or after you pull hands down → otherwise proceed to separation/roll ladder.
- Branches
  - If you turn into lock when hands are already high/tight → you aid the break (do not do this).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Mis-timing heel slip (turning into a tight heel hook).
- Drills
  - Reaction-time rounds: attacker exposes heel with varying hand height; defender must decide slip vs no-slip.
- System tags
  - [HeelHook] [TimingGate] [HigherCatastrophe] [MoreReactionTime]

### 3.11 Heel hooks — Hiding feet (heel exposure prevention)
- Objective
  - Keep heel unexposed by managing toe line and heel distance.
- Gates
  - Toes point toward attacker torso line → heel exposure becomes easy.
  - Heel floats away from attacker body → exposure becomes easy.
- Controls
  - “Big toe line” rule: if line from big toe points at attacker torso, you are in danger; rotate away.
- Primary line
  - Maintain heel contact + toes away continuously; re-hide immediately if toe line points inward.
- Branches
  - Attacker begins digging for heel as toe line drifts → re-hide first, then continue escape.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Treating heel hiding as optional; it is the first gate.
- Drills
  - Toe-line awareness drill: partner tries to rotate your foot inward; you reorient without opening knee line.
- System tags
  - [HeelHide] [ToeLineRule] [PreventativeControl]

### 3.12 Heel hooks — Initial heel slip (early-stage only)
- Objective
  - Slip heel past attacker wrist by turning into lock safely.
- Gates
  - Works only early when hands are low (no breaking pressure yet) OR after you physically pull hands low.
  - Sensitivity gate: you must know whether hands are “low enough”; visual rule-of-thumb exists but must be trained.
- Controls
  - Raise hips off floor; post foot and hand; drive head toward attacker’s head to bring hands low; then slip heel inside lock.
- Primary line
  - If hands low: post + raise hips + drive forward → heel slip → remove heel exposure and proceed to exit.
  - If hands high: first pull hands down / force them low; if you cannot, do not heel slip—move to roll ladder.
- Branches
  - Attacker hands high and pressure present → heel slip is unsafe; proceed to separation/rolling strategy.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Heel slipping late (turning into tight lock) and breaking yourself.
- Drills
  - Trusted-partner heel-slip drilling: attacker applies controlled progressive pressure; defender learns true “low vs high” threshold.
- System tags
  - [HeelSlip] [HandsLowGate] [HipRaise] [DriveForward]

### 3.13 Heel hooks — Separation + high-leg through (Ashi break → exit)
- Objective
  - Use leg separation to make heel hook grip irrelevant and exit via high-leg step-through.
- Gates
  - You must separate legs; otherwise attacker can follow roll and finish.
- Controls
  - Separate legs while maintaining posture; then immediate “go” signal—no pause after releasing foot control.
  - Exit detail: knee to floor → big high-leg over; then prevent re-entry by bringing knees together.
- Primary line
  - Separate legs → knee down → high-leg through immediately → knees together to deny attacker casting leg over and sliding knee under → disengage to positional play.
- Branches
  - Attacker attempts to re-enter Ashi after your high-leg → knees together denies Ashi; shear legs away to reset.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Knee touches floor and you pause; attacker throws leg back over and rolls you through.
- Drills
  - “Release-and-go” drill: defender practices separation-to-high-leg with zero hesitation; attacker’s job is immediate re-cast.
- System tags
  - [LegSeparation] [HighLegThrough] [NoPause] [ReEntryDenial]

### 3.14 Heel hooks — Multiple rolls (same corkscrew theory)
- Objective
  - Apply the same corkscrew rolling model when upper/lower interference fails.
- Gates
  - Use when you cannot (A) access hands, (B) separate legs, or (C) heel slip safely.
- Controls
  - Roll and look for openings each rotation; attempt to separate legs during roll series.
  - Late push/post when knee nearly free.
- Primary line
  - Roll → search for separation chance → if found, convert to knee-down + high-leg; if not, continue rolls until knee exits.
- Branches
  - If attacker stays in Irimi without transitioning and exposes elbow (unlikely per instructor) → you may be able to strip; do not plan around this.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Expecting attacker to remain inverted/expose elbow so you can strip; good attackers roll with you.
- Drills
  - Attacker “always follows” rule in training; defender must escape under follow pressure (realistic constraint).
- System tags
  - [MultipleRolls] [RealisticFollow] [NoPlanOnMistake]

### 3.15 Heel hooks — Defending from a knee (peel foot / heel slip vs forced roll)
- Objective
  - Define knee-start heel hook options.
- Gates
  - If you can peel attacker’s foot and drop knee → you may high-lunge/high-leg out.
  - If attacker is skilled, they will bump you/off-balance and occupy your hands (post requirement), forcing roll-through.
- Controls
  - If attacker is “not great,” you may access hands to pull down and heel slip.
  - If attacker is “good,” they force your hand to floor; you must roll through and likely multiple roll.
- Primary line
  - Attempt: peel foot → knee down → high lunge.
  - If access hands safely: pull hands down → heel slip.
  - If neither available: roll immediately; roll series until escape or finish.
- Branches
  - Attacker bumps you so your hand posts → heel slip window closes → commit to roll-through.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Reaching for hands without a post; you get reaped/off-balanced and finished.
- Drills
  - Knee-start with “good attacker” constraint: attacker must bump/off-balance; defender must respond with post + immediate roll.
- System tags
  - [KneeStart] [PeelFoot] [PostHandConstraint] [ForcedRoll]

### 3.16 Heel hooks — Defending from standing (more time, still real)
- Objective
  - Use standing base + extra time (no attacker bodyweight) to separate legs and escape; acknowledge standing finishes are real.
- Gates
  - Attacker requires high-quality mechanics to finish standing; you still must act.
- Controls
  - Double V-grips (ankle + sole) to separate legs; once legs open, danger drops sharply.
  - Standing exits after separation: heel slip / backstep (stay in pocket) / forward exit.
- Primary line
  - While standing: establish V-grips → focus on separating legs first → once open, choose preferred exit (backstep/pocket vs forward).
  - If you feel beat even standing → drop knee → high-leg over → roll series.
- Branches
  - If separation cannot be achieved and pressure rises → transition to knee + rolling protocol.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Treating standing as “safe enough” and delaying separation.
- Drills
  - Standing heel-hook defense rounds: attacker tries to finish quickly; defender must separate legs within short clock; reset on failure.
- System tags
  - [StandingDefense] [LegSeparationFirst] [BackstepOption] [DropToKneeIfBeat]

## VN04 — Volume 4: Outside Ashi Garami (separation, inverted HH threats, Achilles/toe-hold defenses)

### 4.1 Outside Ashi — Intro (what changes vs Irimi)
- Objective
  - Identify the structural deltas: crossed feet reinforce wedge; separation is harder; forward pressure into partner is easier (no Irimi wedge between hips).
- Gates
  - Outside Ashi typically uses crossed ankles (or figure-four), creating a stronger closed circle than Irimi.
- Controls
  - Priorities shift: you may need more time/effort to separate ankles; but you can pressure forward more effectively when addressing Achilles.
- Primary line
  - Hide foot → separate ankles (primary unlock) → choose (A) extract/escape or (B) counter-lock/attack to force disengage.
- Branches (defender action → your response)
  - Opponent uses tight crossed ankles/figure-four → anticipate slower separation; use correct mechanical grips (sole + heel) rather than “prying.”
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Assuming Irimi timings apply; outside’s reinforced wedge changes time-to-break.
- Drills
  - Entry recognition drill: start in Outside with crossed ankles; defender must immediately move to correct separation grips.
- System tags
  - [OutsideAshi] [ReinforcedWedge] [SeparationHarder] [ForwardPressureEasier]

### 4.2 Outside Ashi — Separating crossed ankles (sole + heel shear)
- Objective
  - Open the closed circle by shearing ankles apart.
- Gates
  - Must hide foot first (do not leave foot dangling while hand-fighting).
- Controls
  - Grip sequence: catch sole → thread hand through → catch heel → open foot directionally while shearing legs apart in opposite directions.
- Primary line
  - Hide foot → catch sole + heel → shear ankles apart → once legs open, transition to extract, leg-pass, or attack.
- Branches
  - If ankles are crossed in the inverted-HH-exposing configuration → separation still works, but be aware of immediate inverted HH opportunities (next section).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Pulling randomly at the top leg without controlling heel/sole; you do not break the closed circle.
- Drills
  - 20-rep separation drill: partner re-crosses immediately; defender must re-open using sole+heel every rep.
- System tags
  - [CrossedAnkles] [SoleHeelGrip] [ShearOpen] [CircleBreak]

### 4.3 Outside Ashi — Inverted heel hook threat (counter-lock to force disengage)
- Objective
  - Use inverted heel hook threat to (A) finish or (B) create panic disengagement and recover neutral.
- Gates
  - Must hide your foot first; must first separate ankles to access heel.
  - Two ankle configurations noted: crossed ankles (one exposes to inverted HH); full figure-four requires different setup.
- Controls
  - After separation: tuck partner’s foot; catch heel; optional false grip over knee to prevent heel slip; open knee line to lock.
  - To prevent them “coming forward to your head/elbow,” cast your leg over and lock feet on far hip (wedge between bodies).
- Primary line
  - Separate ankles → acquire heel → stabilize knee/heel → cast leg over and lock feet on far hip → threat forces release/disengage; if they disengage, re-pummel to neutral.
- Branches
  - Partner “freaks out” and opens legs/scoots away before you lock legs → you pummel feet inside and return to neutral (threat used for escape).
  - Partner uses full figure-four: keep foot on floor (do not raise and expose heel) → open partner’s knee by levering knees outward → then acquire heel and proceed.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Trying to go belly-down finishing path while partner can dig for your heel (especially from figure-four); losing your own heel.
- Drills
  - Threat-to-neutral drill: defender must create HH threat; attacker’s job is immediate disengage; defender must re-pummel to neutral cleanly.
- System tags
  - [InvertedHeelHook] [ThreatForEscape] [FarHipWedge] [FigureFourVariant]

### 4.4 Outside Ashi — Sit-over-a-leg extraction (pin + hamstring post)
- Objective
  - Convert “legs opened” into a stable extraction route even if partner tries to re-pummel or re-lock.
- Gates
  - Occurs after you separate legs OR after threat makes partner retract leg to avoid HH.
- Controls
  - Pin leg and sit over it; then post on hamstring to rotate/turn out and clear.
- Primary line
  - After legs split: Option A pin leg → sit over → bring knees/feet inside → hamstring post → rotate out and exit.
  - Option B threaten heel hook → partner retracts leg → knees come inside first (deny re-lock) → then sit over and hamstring post.
- Branches
  - If partner re-locks Achilles while you are sitting over → hamstring post + turn-out still clears (they no longer control with closed circle).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Attempting to access hamstring “too high up the ladder” before you’ve stabilized sit-over/pin; distance prevents effective post.
- Drills
  - Split-to-sit drill: start with ankles separated; partner attempts to re-pummel; defender must sit-over + inside knees + hamstring post to exit.
- System tags
  - [SitOverLeg] [PinAndPost] [InsideKnees] [Extraction]

### 4.5 Outside Ashi — Feed to Mutual Ashi / re-pummel inside (escape via inside win)
- Objective
  - Use inside position pummeling to either disengage or convert into mutual ashi (attacking option).
- Gates
  - Trigger: legs split and partner tries to re-pummel/re-lock; you must win inside first.
- Controls
  - Knees to inside as primary denial tool; once inside is won, re-lock is difficult.
- Primary line
  - Split legs → knees inside immediately → choose: disengage/extract OR feed to mutual ashi (standing-over variant mentioned later).
- Branches
  - If stepping over feels too slow/risky → insert knee inside right away (skip the bigger movement).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Allowing partner to win pummeling battle during transition; you separate legs but lose inside and get re-locked.
- Drills
  - “Split then pummel race”: partner tries to re-cross/re-enter; defender must get both knees inside before any exit.
- System tags
  - [RePummel] [InsideWin] [MutualAshiOption]

### 4.6 Outside Ashi — Top-leg interference / semi-sankaku (time-buying shell)
- Objective
  - Deny immediate heel digging and buy time to separate opponent’s legs first.
- Gates
  - Used before submissions are on (positional fighting phase) or when partner is actively digging heel.
- Controls
  - Top-leg interference: place top leg on partner’s hip to interfere with heel access.
  - Cross your own ankles as a “triangle shell” (semi-sankaku), NOT a figure-four; never figure-four your legs here (you wedge yourself and can’t roll).
- Primary line
  - As partner digs heel: either top-leg interfere OR cross ankles semi-sankaku to hide heel → force partner to separate your legs first → you simultaneously work to separate theirs → gain time to decide next move.
- Branches
  - If partner begins separating your semi-sankaku → you prioritize separating their legs in parallel (race condition).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Figure-four your own legs in outside ashi; you cannot roll and you get broken.
- Drills
  - Heel-dig rounds: attacker’s goal is heel exposure; defender’s goal is semi-sankaku time-buy and immediate opponent-leg separation.
- System tags
  - [TopLegInterference] [SemiSankaku] [NoFigureFour] [TimeBuyShell]

### 4.7 Outside Ashi — Build to a knee (no “dilemma” vs Irimi)
- Objective
  - Establish that building to knee is structurally simpler than Irimi because attacker has less ability to control your second leg.
- Gates
  - Decision: either separate feet now OR build to knee and work from there; outside lacks Irimi’s same second-leg dilemma.
- Controls
  - Tuck foot and rock to knee.
- Primary line
  - Tuck foot → rock up to knee → begin working (separate legs / pummel / extract).
- Branches
  - If opponent crosses ankles differently → you can still come up; options remain (separate or build).
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Over-committing to prolonged seated hand-fighting when a knee build is available.
- Drills
  - Knee-build reps from outside ashi with partner trying to maintain crossed ankles; defender must rise cleanly.
- System tags
  - [BuildToKnee] [OutsideNoDilemma] [TuckAndRock]

### 4.8 Outside Ashi — Build to standing (no high-step; posture rules)
- Objective
  - Stand safely to separate legs and extract or re-pummel; high-step escape is not the default because the closed circle sits you down if you lean wrong.
- Gates
  - High-step out is not reliable from fully closed outside circle; stand-up is preferred.
- Controls
  - Post hand on floor during stand.
  - Keep weight over your foot (head over your own foot) to make trapped leg “weightless.”
  - Secondary leg stays far away; stand bent over (not tall) to resist reap/knockback.
- Primary line
  - Post hand → lean head over your foot → pop to standing → keep posture bent and secondary leg away → begin separation attempts.
- Branches
  - If you posture upright too early while legs still closed → opponent can sit you down/off-balance.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Standing tall with shoulders over hips; easy knockback/reap.
- Drills
  - Stand-under-pressure drill: attacker tries to knock you back/reap; defender must maintain head-over-foot and bent posture while separating.
- System tags
  - [BuildToStand] [HeadOverFoot] [BentPosture] [SecondaryLegAway]

### 4.9 Standing: extract leg OR re-pummel (strip/open then choose route)
- Objective
  - Provide standing decision set: extract to neutral/pass or stay in pocket by feeding/entering.
- Gates
  - You cannot “fiddle” long with shoulders over hips; separation attempts must be paced with posture.
- Controls
  - Work low with head over side; when ready to open, posture up to separate (legs open reduces off-balance power).
  - After open: options include stuffing leg between legs (open irimi-like), backstep exit, forward run, or feeding to mutual ashi (standing-over).
  - Alternate bailout: hip thrust forward to bring partner’s legs into a reaping alignment, then knee down/sit back on legs (forces opening).
- Primary line
  - Standing: establish grips while still bent → posture up briefly to open legs → choose exit channel:
    - Stuff between legs → forward/backstep.
    - Feed to mutual ashi (standing-over) → fall back to attacks.
    - If consistently off-balanced before you can open → hip thrust + bring legs across (reap alignment) → knee down and sit back to force opening → then extract.
- Branches
  - If you cannot separate without getting off-balanced → use hip-thrust-to-reap alignment as a forcing function.
- Failure-first diagnosis
  - Staying upright and trying prolonged separation; opponent sits you down.
- Drills
  - Standing-choice rounds: attacker off-balances aggressively; defender must either open-and-exit quickly or switch to hip-thrust force-open route.
- System tags
  - [StandingSeparation] [ExtractVsPocket] [ForceOpen] [HipThrustReapAlign]

### 4.10 Achilles in Outside Ashi — forward pressure is easier (details partially compressed)
- Objective
  - Note key difference vs Irimi: forward pressure into partner to address Achilles is easier (no wedge).
- Gates
  - Achilles grip present; leg circle reinforced by crossed ankles (harder to separate).
- Controls
  - Forward pressure/boot concepts are referenced as easier here; detailed micro-steps are not fully expanded in the available excerpt (flag).
- Primary line
  - Use the same Achilles defensive concepts as prior volumes, with the added note: forward pressure is more available in Outside.
