# VIEWING NOTES – Gordon Ryan: Pillars of Defense — Leg Locks to Back Takes

## 6.0 Volume 6 — Inside Ashi + 50-50

### 6.1 Intro to Inside Ashi
**Key concepts**
- Inside ashi offense constraint: there is a physical wedge between bodies; “coming forward” is largely unavailable, so you must win via transfers/rolls rather than pressure-forward.
- Primary objective remains conversion: use defensive actions (posts/pummels) to exit into passing/backtake dilemma.

**Position–structure**
- Start state: opponent has you in inside ashi with wedge preventing forward pressure.
- Primary exits: pummel leg through to re-square; transfer configuration (notably to outside ashi); roll/half-roll families.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF forward pressure is blocked by the wedge -> THEN do not force; post + pummel or transfer configuration.
- IF opponent extends into your leg (to expose heel / make pummel difficult) -> THEN treat it as time-sensitive; act within the brief window before exposure develops.
- IF you can create space with a post -> THEN pummel your leg through immediately to re-orient.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You attempt to “drive forward” and stall -> switch immediately to post/pummel mechanics or a transfer.
- Extension makes pummel difficult and you pause -> post to create space and pummel as soon as the space exists.

**Completion checks**
- You have re-oriented enough that the wedge is no longer preventing your next action (transfer/roll/come-up).
- Your heel line is not being exposed as you transition.

**System tags**
- Inside ashi wedge; No-forward-pressure rule; Post-and-pummel; Time-window awareness

---

### 6.2 Transfer to Outside Ashi
**Key concepts**
- Transfer trigger: when pummeling is blocked (extension / orientation), you lift/punch across into outside ashi to re-open forward offense and the pass/back dilemma.
- Posting and shin control are used to create the space and alignment needed to pummel/transfer.

**Position–structure**
- Inside ashi (blocked) -> post on partner’s shin / create space -> pummel -> lift partner -> punch across -> outside ashi.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you cannot pummel because of extension -> THEN post (shin) to create space and re-attempt pummel/transfer.
- IF you are “ready to start going” -> THEN lift partner and punch across to outside ashi (don’t linger in inside ashi).
- IF legs remain locked and you cannot separate -> THEN take the leg up and over to force progression into the next phase.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You stay in inside ashi trying to force forward pressure -> transfer instead (outside ashi re-enables forward offense).
- You separate legs and they immediately re-expose your heel -> return to follow-up defenses and re-stabilize before advancing.

**Completion checks**
- You have achieved outside ashi alignment where forward offense is possible.
- You can begin the next dilemma sequence (pass/back) without being stalled by the original wedge.

**System tags**
- Transfer to outside ashi; Shin post; Punch-across; Re-enable forward offense

---

### 6.3 Half Roll to Backstep (inside ashi defensive converter)
**Key concepts**
- Roll family as standard continuation when separation isn’t available: half roll (belly-down) and full roll-through are both used; half roll is always available as a safety converter.
- Crossing ankles is used during roll-through phases to protect your own leg during the transition.

**Position–structure**
- Inside ashi -> attempt separation -> if blocked/exposed -> half roll belly-down -> roll-through options -> re-orient into known top/control lanes.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you cannot separate legs and heel exposure is developing -> THEN initiate half roll (belly-down) as the immediate safety converter.
- IF you can free your knee from partner’s knee line -> THEN you can go directly into a roll (or take time and build first).
- IF you roll-through -> THEN protect your leg line (cross ankles) and land in structure before advancing.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You try to force separation while exposure grows -> half roll immediately (don’t wait).
- You roll without protecting your own legs -> cross-ankle/protect leg line before continuing.

**Completion checks**
- You have transitioned to a safer alignment where heel exposure is reduced.
- You can immediately link the roll to a known continuation (backstep/camping/body lock families).

**System tags**
- Half roll (belly-down); Full roll-through; Cross-ankle safety; Knee-line liberation

### 6.4 Heel Hook to Back (inside ashi conversion)
**Key concepts**
- From inside ashi offense: if you “get a bite” on the heel, opponent’s heist/roll reactions create the window to pendulum up to the hip and begin back-taking sequences.
- When opponent moves away while you’re wedged, you must use the same conversion logic as outside ashi: half roll / knee-down / body lock continuations.

**Position–structure**
- Inside ashi -> initial heel engagement (“bite”) -> opponent heists/rolls -> pendulum up -> reach hip line -> enter upper-body lane (turtle/back) and finish.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you secure the heel bite and they begin heisting/rolling -> THEN pendulum up and work to get to the hip.
- IF they wedge/move away to deny your climb -> THEN use half roll + knee-down entries and convert into body lock / side body lock / full roll options.
- IF the position becomes unstable -> THEN prioritize leg safety (hide foot / extract) before continuing.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You stay wedged and cannot close distance -> pendulum to hip instead of reaching.
- You chase without a structured follow-up (no roll/knee-down) -> select the roll family immediately and land in control.

**Completion checks**
- You have reached hip proximity (distance closed) and can enter the upper-body finishing phase.
- You can continue without your own heel line being exposed.

**System tags**
- Heel bite trigger; Pendulum to hip; Wedged-distance solution; Roll-to-control linkage

### 6.5 Intro to 50-50
**Key concepts**
- 50-50 is neutral: both players can attack; the priority is constant leg-safety management while creating conversions to passing/back.
- “Always on offense until you’re on defense”: the moment you feel your position slip, you transition into defensive converter behavior.
- Safety framing: keep bend in the leg and keep heel connected (heel-to-butt style) to reduce exposure; extension trades must be understood (you want bend; opponent wants extension to expose).

**Position–structure**
- 50-50 neutral entanglement -> safety layer (bend/heel connection) -> creative conversions (belly-down/roll-through, knee-down, transfers).

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you feel your position slipping -> THEN immediately shift into defense (don’t stay “neutral” and hope).
- IF extension battle is developing -> THEN preserve bend and heel connection before initiating bigger transitions.
- IF leg orientation prevents coming forward and pressuring -> THEN you must use roll/transfer creativity rather than direct pressure.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You treat 50-50 as a static leg-lock exchange -> convert to combinations (roll-through, transfers, stand-up) instead.
- You accept extension and lose heel safety -> restore bend + heel connection immediately.

**Completion checks**
- You can maintain heel safety while initiating a converter (roll/stand/transfer).
- You can exit 50-50 into a non-neutral lane (pin/pass or back-entry).

**System tags**
- 50-50 neutrality; Offense-to-defense switch; Bend/heel-connection safety; Creative conversion requirement

### 6.6 Half Roll to Back Step (50-50)
**Key concepts**
- Primary converter: belly-down half roll into the same rolling entry patterns used previously; knee-down + roll-through can link into cross-body-ride style entries.
- Transfer option appears: 50-50 -> roll-through -> backside 50-50; but you cannot “hang out” there—must progress.

**Position–structure**
- 50-50 -> belly-down -> roll-through -> knee-down -> cross-body-ride style entry OR transfer into backside 50-50 -> immediate continuation.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you need to move off neutral -> THEN turn belly-down and roll into the entry pattern.
- IF you transfer to backside 50-50 -> THEN do not hang out; immediately progress (pin ankles / move to the next lane).
- IF partner rolls all the way to the opposite hip and threatens heel exposure -> THEN you must roll with them (match the rotation).

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You stay in backside 50-50 without progression -> force immediate continuation (no “camping” inside the entanglement).
- Partner rolls through and you don’t follow -> roll with them to prevent heel exposure and keep control.

**Completion checks**
- You have left the neutral 50-50 exchange and landed in a structured continuation (entry/transfer with forward progress).
- You can prevent partner from rolling to expose your heel without you matching.

**System tags**
- 50-50 belly-down converter; Backside 50-50 transfer; Roll-with-them rule; No-hangout constraint

### 6.7 Build to Standing (50-50)
**Key concepts**
- Standing build is a controlled heist from 50-50: you stand so partner cannot easily expose heel, then you convert into your passing/back system using familiar grips (Achilles grip / underhook / V-grip).
- Denial focus while standing: deny partner’s ability to “come through” on the far leg to take the back.

**Position–structure**
- 50-50 -> heist up -> stand with heel safety -> deny far-leg backtake route -> select grip system (underhook/Achilles/V-grip) -> begin conversions.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you are standing -> THEN maintain structure that prevents easy heel exposure (don’t stand loose).
- IF partner tries to take the back by coming through on the far leg -> THEN deny that route first before advancing offense.
- IF you want to begin passing/back conversions -> THEN choose your grip lane (underhook/Achilles/V-grip) and proceed to leg separation.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You stand and partner immediately “comes through” -> deny far leg path before you attempt to pass/convert.
- You stand but remain in neutral -> pick a grip lane and start separating legs / moving to camping/north-south.

**Completion checks**
- Standing position does not give partner easy heel exposure or a free backtake path.
- You can initiate leg separation and enter passing/back sequences from standing.

**System tags**
- 50-50 stand-up; Heel-safety standing; Far-leg denial; Grip-lane selection

---

### 6.8 Separating Legs / Passing / Backtakes (50-50 standing phase)
**Key concepts**
- V-grip is a recurring control to separate partner’s legs and begin passing sequences.
- Once legs are separated, you can walk directly into your passing system: camping positions, north-south, and backtakes as reactions appear.

**Position–structure**
- Standing from 50-50 -> establish V-grip -> separate legs -> step forward to block -> enter camping / north-south -> branch to back as reactions appear.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you secure V-grip -> THEN separate legs and begin stepping/positioning into camping/north-south (don’t pause).
- IF you have separated legs and are “ready to go” -> THEN you are ready for the full passing game (camping, north-south, backtake branches).
- IF separation is blocked by hand/structure -> THEN adjust with V-grip control and re-separate before moving forward.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You separate but don’t convert -> walk immediately to camping/north-south and force reaction.
- Hand position blocks your separation/advance -> change angle and re-apply V-grip separation before committing forward.

**Completion checks**
- Partner’s legs are separated enough that they can’t re-lock to keep you in neutral.
- You have entered a stable top layer (camping/north-south) or attached to the back.

**System tags**
- V-grip separation; Step-forward block; Camping entry; North-south readiness; Pass/back continuation

### 6.9 Extraction to Body Lock / Backtakes (50-50 offence continuation)
**Key concepts**
- When partner pulls the leg out / tries to re-guard, the response is immediate: create angle and shoot into side body lock; use front tight waist + hook timing as they turn.
- Continuations include: return partner to floor, pressure forward, switch to back-position progressions.

**Position–structure**
- 50-50 offence -> separation -> partner pulls leg / re-guards -> angle creation -> side body lock OR front tight waist -> hook insertion -> finish (back or pass lanes).

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF partner pulls the leg out -> THEN immediately come up and shoot side body lock (don’t let them re-guard cleanly).
- IF you create the angle -> THEN knees come down and you can lock and advance (front tight waist + hook insertion as they turn).
- IF hook insertion is not available -> THEN return them back to the floor or keep pressuring forward into the next lane.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- Partner re-guards because you hesitated -> angle immediately and enter side body lock/front tight waist.
- You chase without controlling the turn -> time the hook to the turn (insert as they turn, not after).

**Completion checks**
- You have a body lock / tight waist control that prevents clean re-guarding.
- You can either insert hooks (back lane) or consolidate a pin/passing lane.

**System tags**
- Angle-first response; Side body lock entry; Front tight waist; Turn-timed hook; No-hesitation conversion

### 6.10 Far Leg Butterfly Hook to Back Take
**Key concepts**
- Defensive priority while standing/advancing: deny partner’s far-leg route (coming through/hooking to initiate backtaking sequences).
- The far-leg threat is treated as a key backtake pathway you must prevent before committing to your own conversions.

**Position–structure**
- Standing from 50-50 -> partner attempts to access far leg to come through -> you deny and reassert control -> proceed to separation/passing/back sequences.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF partner begins accessing the far leg to come through -> THEN deny far leg first (do not advance while the route is open).
- IF far leg is denied -> THEN proceed with your separation and passing/back branches.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You advance while far-leg access is live -> stop and deny far leg, then continue.
- Partner repeatedly re-enters via far leg -> prioritize that denial as your standing “gate” before any offense.

**Completion checks**
- Partner cannot freely come through on the far leg to start backtaking.
- You can safely return to your offensive lane selection.

**System tags**
- Far-leg denial; Standing gate; Backtake-route prevention

---

### 6.11 Backside 50-50 to Backtake
**Key concepts**
- Backside 50-50 is a transfer state, not a holding state: if partner falls across to opposite hip / begins running away, you must pin ankles and continue immediately.
- If partner rolls through to opposite hip to expose your heel, you must roll with them (or you lose safety).

**Position–structure**
- 50-50 -> roll-through -> backside 50-50 -> partner falls/rolls across -> ankle pin / knee-slide style progressions -> continue to back or pass lanes.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you see backside 50-50 -> THEN do not hang out; progress immediately.
- IF partner falls across to opposite hip -> THEN pin ankles and continue (prevent free escape).
- IF partner rolls through to expose your heel -> THEN roll with them (match rotation) to keep heel safe.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- Partner “runs away in a four point” and you lose them -> you must climb up (do not stay low in the entanglement).
- You try to come up directly and they run away -> re-position first (inside knee position) before climbing.

**Completion checks**
- Ankles are pinned/controlled so partner cannot freely rotate away.
- You can transition upward into a back-entry or a stable top lane.

**System tags**
- Backside 50-50; Ankle pin; Roll-with-them; No-hangout rule; Anti-runaway climbing

---

### 6.12 Far Hip Ashi
**Key concepts**
- When partner runs away, direct hip-grabbing fails; the adjustment is to move back to the inside-knee position, take Achilles grip, put partner’s knee to floor, then pendulum up to the far hip.
- Far hip access is used to take internal position and push partner into an “unathletic” posture for continuation.

**Position–structure**
- Backside/escape attempt -> inside knee position -> Achilles grip -> knee-down -> grab front of ankle -> pendulum up -> far hip -> internal position -> continue to backtake/pass.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF partner runs away and you can’t just grab the hip -> THEN move back to inside-knee position first.
- IF you have Achilles grip -> THEN put partner’s knee down and control the front of the ankle.
- IF ankle control + knee-down is set -> THEN pendulum up to reach the far hip and take internal position.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You try to come up directly and they run -> re-set to inside-knee + Achilles grip before climbing.
- You try to pendulum without knee-down/ankle control -> set knee-down + ankle control first, then pendulum.

**Completion checks**
- You can reach the far hip without losing partner to runaway.
- You can take internal position and proceed into finishing sequences (backtake/pass).

**System tags**
- Far hip ashi; Inside-knee reset; Achilles grip; Knee-down; Pendulum to hip; Internal position

---

### 6.13 Cross Waist Ashi
**Key concepts**
- Cross waist ashi is treated as a constrained state with limited direct backtaking; primary recommendation is to split legs and transition to a different ashi (often back to cross ashi) to restart sequences.
- Emphasis: offense/defense here is “changing the ashi garami to a previous ashi garami,” then returning to extractions/back takes/guard passes.

**Position–structure**
- Cross waist ashi -> split legs -> transition to another ashi (cross ashi / prior hub) -> resume extractions and conversions.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you find yourself in cross waist ashi -> THEN prioritize splitting legs (don’t try to force forward pressure).
- IF you need offense/defense from here -> THEN transition to a different ashi that supports extractions/backtakes/passing.
- IF your leg can come out -> THEN you can go into body locks (transition out as a primary continuation).

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You try to do direct offense from cross waist and stall -> transition to a different ashi immediately.
- You remain locked without splitting legs -> split legs first to enable the transition.

**Completion checks**
- Legs are split enough to permit a transition to a more functional ashi.
- You can re-enter extraction/body-lock and pass/back sequences.

**System tags**
- Cross waist ashi; Split-the-legs priority; Ashi-to-ashi transition; Body-lock exit

---

### 6.14 Criss Cross Ashi
**Key concepts**
- Criss cross ashi is described as having very limited direct offense/defense; the consistent solution is to transition to a different ashi (commonly cross ashi) to run prior sequences.
- Constraints explicitly stated: you cannot invert or come forward effectively from criss cross; therefore you must change configuration to progress.

**Position–structure**
- Criss cross ashi -> transition to another ashi (cross ashi / prior hub) -> resume normal conversions (inverting/standing/pressure phases) from the new configuration.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you are in criss cross ashi -> THEN transition out (do not attempt to build a full game there).
- IF you need to take the back -> THEN change ashi to re-enable inverting/standing up and forward pressure pathways.
- IF there is no viable direct offense -> THEN revert to the known sequence hub (cross ashi) and proceed.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You try to “make it work” inside criss cross -> accept the limitation and transition out immediately.
- You wait for openings that don’t come -> change ashi proactively to create openings.

**Completion checks**
- You have successfully transitioned to a functional ashi where your prior sequences apply.
- You can now invert/stand/come forward (depending on the new ashi) to continue conversions.

**System tags**
- Criss cross ashi limitation; Transition-out rule; Re-enter cross ashi; Restore functional pathways
