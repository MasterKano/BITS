# VIEWING NOTES – Gordon Ryan: Pillars of Defense — Leg Locks to Back Takes

## 2.0 Volume 2 — Irimi conversions (back/pass chains; turtle; camping; north-south)

### 2.1 Losing a back head and arm
**Key concepts**
- Primary failure: you have back-head-and-arm control but lose it as the opponent walks their head out and away during your build.
- Head-line loss collapses the conversion: without head capture, they re-square and you lose both back and pin.

**Position–structure**
- Occurs mid-conversion (not fully stabilized): partial back exposure + head/arm control while transitioning up.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF the head starts walking out and away -> THEN stop advancing and re-close head line immediately.
- IF you cannot keep head capture while climbing -> THEN switch to a pin lane (chest-to-chest) rather than chasing hooks.
- IF head line is re-closed -> THEN resume conversion (back or pass) from the stabilized head position.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You feel the head slipping to the “proper angle” -> fix by closing forehead-to-forehead and compressing distance.
- You keep chasing the back while head escapes -> fix by prioritizing head recapture before any hook/finish work.

**Completion checks**
- Head is controlled such that they cannot freely walk out and re-square.
- You can continue building without losing the head-and-arm connection.

**System tags**
- Head-line gate; Back-head-and-arm retention; Cranial shift; Conversion breaker

### 2.2 Backtake / guard pass combinations
**Key concepts**
- Core loop enforced: backtake attempt -> if denied, pass; pass pressure -> if turn-away, back opens.
- Combinations replace isolated attempts; “leave with something” is the operating standard.

**Position–structure**
- Exiting the leg exchange into top lanes: attachment (back) or pin (pass), then recycling based on reactions.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF backtake is denied/slipping -> THEN convert immediately to a pass lane (tight waist / chest-to-chest / north-south).
- IF pass pressure triggers turn-away -> THEN attach chest-to-upper-back and finish the back lane.
- IF pass pressure triggers turn-toward -> THEN stay chest-to-chest and complete the pass.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You hover between lanes (neither pin nor attachment) -> fix by committing to the correct lane immediately after the reaction.
- You reset to neutral after denial -> fix by treating denial as a trigger (not an endpoint).

**Completion checks**
- Every exchange ends in: (a) back control, (b) stabilized pin/pass, or (c) a stabilization layer that forces the next reaction.
- Branch choice is reaction-driven and immediate (no hesitation).

**System tags**
- Pass/back dilemma; Branch triggers; No-reset rule; Conversion chaining

---

### 2.3 Forward roll backtakes vs inversions
**Key concepts**
- Forward roll is acknowledged but framed as risk-managed: failure can dump you back into guard/bottom.
- Alternative continuations (inversion/angle-change) often preserve top advantage better.

**Position–structure**
- Decision point when distance/rotation appears: choose a completion family that preserves top control.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF the roll does not guarantee top advantage under defense -> THEN do not roll; choose a safer converter.
- IF the defender’s structure is stable and you lack control -> THEN avoid high-risk roll pathways.
- IF you can keep connection and land in a pin/attachment lane -> THEN the roll family becomes viable.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- Roll attempt ends with you re-entering guard -> fix by selecting a converter that lands chest-to-chest (underhook/north-south/camping) instead.
- You roll without enough control -> fix by building connection first (then choose method).

**Completion checks**
- Method chosen preserves top advantage when defended.
- You can land in a control lane (pin/attachment), not a scramble.

**System tags**
- Method-family selection; Risk management; Top-position preservation

---

### 2.4 Half roll -> underhook passing / back take
**Key concepts**
- Half roll used to close distance and land into underhook/crossface passing structure when back chase is too “far.”
- Underhook is the bridge: pin/pass if turn-toward; back if turn-away.

**Position–structure**
- Converter: leg exchange -> half roll -> underhook pin lane -> pass; pressure re-opens back on turn-away.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF the back line is too far to finish cleanly -> THEN half roll into underhook passing structure.
- IF you win underhook + head position -> THEN commit chest-to-chest pin and pass.
- IF the defender turns away under pressure -> THEN re-attach to the back from the pin.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- Half roll lands loose and they square -> fix by treating the half roll as a pin entry (underhook + head control), not a movement.
- Underhook becomes a pummel battle -> fix by stabilizing (camping/north-south) then re-enter.

**Completion checks**
- Underhook + head position established (pin is real).
- You can either progress the pass or force turn-away to open the back.

**System tags**
- Half-roll converter; Underhook bridge; Distance management; Pin-first conversion

### 2.5 Build to standing
**Key concepts**
- Standing build is a structured completion tool when grounded progress stalls.
- Heel/foot safety must remain intact during the rise; opponent will angle knees/structure to create exposure.

**Position–structure**
- Come-up milestone: from Irimi context -> momentum-based rise -> upper-body connection on top.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you need height to complete conversion -> THEN build to standing with momentum (not a static lift).
- IF heel exposure appears during the rise -> THEN stop and re-solve foot/angle before continuing.
- IF you reach standing with control -> THEN immediately connect into an upper-body lane (pin/attachment), not separation.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- Stand-up creates space and they re-square -> fix by closing distance immediately with your chosen upper-body connection.
- Heel becomes available mid-rise -> fix by reorienting foot line and re-entering safer conversion before continuing.

**Completion checks**
- You can stand without exposing the heel line.
- You arrive in a top control lane immediately after standing.

**System tags**
- Come-up milestone; Heel exposure management; Momentum rise; Conversion completion

### 2.6 Forward step -> north-south
**Key concepts**
- Forward step converts from the leg exchange into north-south pin.
- North-south is a stable platform that anticipates turtle attempts and feeds the next branch.

**Position–structure**
- Converter: leg exchange -> forward step -> north-south pin -> turtle/turn reactions -> back or pass continuation.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF you forward step past the legs -> THEN stabilize north-south (cranial control) before advancing.
- IF the opponent attempts to turtle from north-south -> THEN capture early (anchor) rather than letting turtle stabilize.
- IF pin is stable -> THEN use it to force turn reactions (toward = pass; away = back).

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- Loose north-south lets them build/turtle freely -> fix by tightening head/upper-body control and re-centering weight.
- You chase transitions before pin exists -> fix by stabilizing first, then branching.

**Completion checks**
- North-south pin is stable enough to prevent free turning/building.
- You can convert turtle attempts into your anchor/passing or back lanes.

**System tags**
- Forward-step converter; North-south pin; Cranial shift; Turtle anticipation

### 2.7 Backtake when opponent turtles
**Key concepts**
- Turtle is a predictable defense to passing pressure; the conversion continues through it.
- Timing emphasis: capture turtle as it forms (attempted turtle window) rather than late.

**Position–structure**
- Passing/north-south pressure -> opponent turtles -> you capture with anchor/control -> branch to back or flatten/pass.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF turtle begins forming -> THEN anchor immediately (tight waist control family).
- IF hooks/back line is clean -> THEN finish back; ELSE flatten and pass from anchor.
- IF turtle stabilizes (late) -> THEN prioritize flattening/passing rather than hovering behind.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You arrive late and turtle is stable -> fix by anchoring earlier (capture the attempt, not the finished turtle).
- You hover behind turtle with no control -> fix by anchoring and forcing a branch (back or pass).

**Completion checks**
- Anchor prevents them from freely building/standing.
- You can either (a) open back attachment, or (b) flatten and pass.

**System tags**
- Turtle capture timing; Tight waist anchor; Branching under turtle

---

### 2.8 Back take / passing from an attempted turtle
**Key concepts**
- Attempted turtle is a premium transition window; branch selection is immediate.
- Explicit fork: turn-away -> tight waist lane; turn-toward -> chest-to-chest pin and pass.

**Position–structure**
- Attempted turtle -> anchor/control at waist/hip line -> branch by turn direction.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF they turn away in the turtle attempt -> THEN insert tight waist control and climb to back exposure.
- IF they turn toward to face you -> THEN stay chest-to-chest and progress the pass.
- IF they start to stand from turtle -> THEN anchor and break posture before choosing lane.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- Hesitation during the attempt lets structure complete -> fix by committing to the correct lane within 1–2 beats.
- You lose connection during the attempt -> fix by anchoring first, then moving.

**Completion checks**
- Transition is captured before turtle stabilizes.
- You end in either a stabilized pass/pin lane or back attachment.

**System tags**
- Attempted turtle window; Reaction-led branching; Anchor-first

### 2.9 Forward step -> outside camping positions
**Key concepts**
- Camping is the stabilization layer after step-through when direct advancement would scramble.
- “Connected camping”: post-hand connection + weight over hip line to prevent scooting escape.

**Position–structure**
- Forward step -> camping stabilization -> restrict rotation/mobility -> force turn reactions for pass/back.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF forward step lands but progress is unstable -> THEN start camping (stabilize first).
- IF they try to scoot down/away -> THEN maintain connection (post hand) and keep weight over hips to deny escape.
- IF they turn away under camping pressure -> THEN attach to back; IF they turn toward -> pin/pass.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- Camping becomes “floating” and they slide away -> fix by re-establishing post-hand connection and hip-weight placement.
- You chase movement instead of stabilizing -> fix by camping first, then branching.

**Completion checks**
- You can hold them in place long enough to force a reaction.
- Your stabilization reliably feeds pass/back lanes.

**System tags**
- Camping stabilization; Connection maintenance; Hip-weight control; Reaction forcing

### 2.10 Taking the back when opponent turns away
**Key concepts**
- Turn-away is treated as the back-opening reaction produced by passing/camping pressure.
- The critical requirement is continuous connection to prevent gaps during the turn.

**Position–structure**
- Pin/camping pressure -> turn-away -> chest-to-upper-back attachment -> finish back.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF they turn away -> THEN attach chest-to-upper-back immediately (connection before hooks).
- IF distance/gap starts opening during the turn -> THEN close space and rebuild connection before chasing hooks.
- IF hips begin escaping your knee corridor -> THEN re-contain hips or branch to pin/pass.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You chase hooks and lose upper-back connection -> fix by rebuilding chest connection first.
- Hips run out from your knees during chase -> fix by restoring containment or switching lanes.

**Completion checks**
- Upper-back connection + centre-line alignment achieved during/after the turn.
- Hips remain contained enough to finish without slipping.

**System tags**
- Turn-away trigger; Chest-to-upper-back; Connection-before-hooks; Hip containment

---

### 2.11 Taking the back when opponent turns toward you
**Key concepts**
- Turn-toward is the primary back defense (deny hooks/attachment); correct response is pin/pass.
- Dilemma enforcement: turning toward concedes pin pressure; fleeing (turning away) concedes back.

**Position–structure**
- Back threat -> opponent turns toward -> chest-to-chest pin -> pass; pressure later re-opens back if they turn away.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF they turn toward -> THEN abandon hook chase and commit to chest-to-chest pin and pass.
- IF pin pressure causes turn-away -> THEN re-attach to the back as the new branch.
- IF you cannot stabilize chest-to-chest -> THEN use a stabilizer (camping/north-south) before progressing.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You insist on back chase into turn-toward defense -> fix by accepting pin lane immediately.
- You lose pin and they re-guard -> fix by stabilizing first (camping/north-south) then re-run the dilemma.

**Completion checks**
- Chest-to-chest pin is stable (they can’t freely frame/turn).
- Passing progress is real, not “almost passed.”

**System tags**
- Turn-toward defense; Pin-first passing; Dilemma enforcement; Stabilize-then-advance

### 2.12 Passing off failed backtakes
**Key concepts**
- Fail-safe loop stated: backtake fails -> pass; pass fails -> return to pass/back combinations (no reset).
- North-south stabilizing detail: keep the opponent’s head between your knees to reduce their build-up angle.
- Anti “wrestling moment”: if they reach/latch your leg while building, step it back so it isn’t available.
- Belly-down response: chase hips and circle to stay connected (don’t chase head).

**Position–structure**
- Failed backtake -> north-south / pin lane -> defender builds/turtles/belly-down -> stabilize + chase hips -> re-attach or pass.

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF backtake attempt fails -> THEN go directly to a passing/pinning lane (north-south emphasized).
- IF you want to hold north-south -> THEN keep their head between your knees.
- IF they reach for your leg to build -> THEN step the leg back to remove the latch.
- IF they go belly-down -> THEN chase hips and circle to maintain control.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- Build-up succeeds because north-south is loose -> fix by head-between-knees and tighter cranial control.
- They create a leg latch during your pressure -> fix by removing the leg target (step-back).
- You chase upper body while they belly-down -> fix by tracking hips as the control priority.

**Completion checks**
- Pin lane remains stable through build attempts.
- You convert build/turtle/belly-down into either pass completion or back re-attachment.

**System tags**
- Fail-safe looping; North-south control; Anti-leg-latch; Hip-chase; Pin retention

### 2.13 Backstepping to camping positions
**Key concepts**
- Backstep is the alternate converter when forward step stalls or is structurally blocked.
- Backstep reorients into camping/top control while preserving advantage and reopening pass/back branches.

**Position–structure**
- Forward step stalls -> backstep -> camping stabilization -> branch by reaction (toward = pass; away = back).

**Decision rules (IF/THEN)**
- IF forward step stalls -> THEN backstep (don’t grind).
- IF backstep lands -> THEN stabilize in camping first, then branch.
- IF stabilization produces turn-away -> THEN attach; IF turn-toward -> pin/pass.

**Failure signatures + fixes**
- You insist on forward step and create scramble -> fix by using backstep as the stall-breaker.
- You backstep but stay loose -> fix by stabilizing (camping connection) before advancing.

**Completion checks**
- Backstep reliably lands you in a stable top/stabilization layer.
- You can immediately run pass/back dilemma from the camping base.

**System tags**
- Backstep stall-breaker; Camping re-entry; Angle-change converter; Stabilize-first
