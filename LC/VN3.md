## VN3 — Top 10 Systems (Ordered by Significance)

### 1) Demarcation Theory OS
**Trigger:** Any time guard feels like it is “slipping” or the passer is gaining alignment.  
**Objective:** Diagnose *where* you are losing the guard (which line/zone), then route to the correct recovery tool.  
**Inputs:** Alignment signals (hip rotation, knee line exposure, shoulder line collapse, distance shrink).  
**Outputs:** A single declared “lost line” + a chosen recovery action family.  
**Rules:** Identify the earliest demarcation loss and fix it before it chains forward.  
**Failure signature:** You react late and end up defending penetration/pin.  
**Metric:** Time from first line-loss cue to corrective action.

---

### 2) Hip-Line Alarm Gate OS
**Trigger:** Passer’s body position threatens to cross your hip line (penetration depth increasing).  
**Objective:** Switch instantly from mobility solutions to emergency structure (frames/wedges) to prevent late-chain completion.  
**Inputs:** Penetration cues: hips close, chest entering, knee line collapsing, pin attempt forming.  
**Outputs:** Immediate “alarm response” (structure-first recovery).  
**Rules:** If hip-line alarm is on, stop trying to “win with movement” first—stabilize structure, then rebuild.  
**Failure signature:** You keep pummeling while being pinned.  
**Metric:** % hip-line alarms resolved without pin.

---

### 3) Mobility-First Retention OS
**Trigger:** Passer is still in front of the hip line; space exists to move.  
**Objective:** Preserve guard by re-centering and re-inserting legs using mobility before the exchange becomes structural.  
**Inputs:** Early chain pressure (angle/level threats) with available space.  
**Outputs:** Re-squared alignment + reinserted legs + restored distance line.  
**Rules:** Mobility solutions first *until* hip-line alarm triggers.  
**Failure signature:** You frame too early and get run around; lose angle.  
**Metric:** % early threats resolved without switching to emergency frames.

---

### 4) Defensive Framework Activation OS
**Trigger:** Hip-line alarm is on OR frames are collapsing under pressure.  
**Objective:** Establish load-bearing frames/wedges to stop penetration and buy time to rebuild legs.  
**Inputs:** Late-chain pressure signals.  
**Outputs:** Structure restored + distance re-created + legs reinserted.  
**Rules:** Frames are structural, not pushing; rebuild legs behind structure.  
**Failure signature:** Frames collapse because you push instead of wedge.  
**Metric:** Time to re-establish a stable frame and reinsert legs.

---

### 5) Essential Movement Selector OS
**Trigger:** Under pressure, when choices multiply and scrambling begins.  
**Objective:** Prevent improvisation by selecting from a canonical movement set based on the demarcation problem.  
**Inputs:** Declared lost line + current pressure direction.  
**Outputs:** One chosen movement tool (not a random sequence).  
**Rules:** Choose the smallest tool that solves the demarcation loss; reassess immediately after.  
**Failure signature:** Random movement chains that worsen angle and expose hip line.  
**Metric:** Reduction in “two-link losses” (angle+level) during scrambles.

---

### 6) Crossover Re-Square OS
**Trigger:** Angle begins to form; passer is moving laterally/run-around.  
**Objective:** Re-square hips/shoulders early to deny the angle link before it becomes level/penetration.  
**Inputs:** Lateral step cues, knee line exposure, hip rotation.  
**Outputs:** Front-facing alignment restored; passer returned to center.  
**Rules:** Fix angle early; do not wait for penetration.  
**Failure signature:** You chase; passer wins hip angle and enters.  
**Metric:** % angle threats neutralized before level changes.

---

### 7) Foot Pummeling Reclaim OS
**Trigger:** Passer clears/staples/scoops legs or begins winning inside lane access.  
**Objective:** Reclaim inside lanes by cycling feet back to inside position and restoring leg structure.  
**Inputs:** Loss of inside foot position; legs displaced.  
**Outputs:** Inside lanes regained; usable guard structure restored.  
**Rules:** Continuous cycling until inside lane control returns; pair with re-centering.  
**Failure signature:** Legs stay outside and late; distance collapses.  
**Metric:** Time to regain inside foot position after a clear attempt.

---

### 8) Framing Activation OS
**Trigger:** Distance collapses or penetration threat rises (especially during reinsert attempts).  
**Objective:** Use frames as wedges to preserve space and alignment while legs are rebuilt.  
**Inputs:** Compression pressure, shoulder line collapse, near-hip contact.  
**Outputs:** Restored space + protected hip line + time to reinsert legs.  
**Rules:** Frames are skeletal; they create space for leg recovery, not “push them away.”  
**Failure signature:** Arms fatigue quickly; frames fail; pin follows.  
**Metric:** Frame durability under pressure (seconds held) without collapse.

---

### 9) Shrimping Space-Creation OS
**Trigger:** Space is collapsing and you must create hip distance to reinsert legs.  
**Objective:** Create distance with hip escape mechanics to recover alignment and rebuild guard layers.  
**Inputs:** Pressure compressing hips; legs partially cleared.  
**Outputs:** Space created + hips repositioned + legs reinserted.  
**Rules:** Shrimp to restore the line you lost, then immediately re-attach legs/frames.  
**Failure signature:** Shrimp without reinsertion; opponent follows and re-collapses.  
**Metric:** % shrimps that result in successful leg reinsertion.

---

### 10) High-Leg Emergency Barrier OS
**Trigger:** Immediate close-range danger; passer is entering and inside lanes are compromised.  
**Objective:** Create an emergency barrier that blocks clean upper-body control and buys time to re-center.  
**Inputs:** Near-hip pressure, collapsing knee line, imminent pin threat.  
**Outputs:** Barrier restored + alignment stabilized long enough to rebuild structure.  
**Rules:** Use as an emergency barrier, then transition back to mobility or framework depending on hip-line status.  
**Failure signature:** You reach for complex solutions during emergency compression.  
**Metric:** % emergencies stabilized without pin in the next 3–5 seconds.

## VN3 — Next 10 Systems (11–20) (Operational Density)

### 11) Inverted Spin Recovery OS
**Trigger:** Angle loss is severe and re-squaring requires rotational reorientation.  
**Objective:** Reorient hips/shoulders to restore playable alignment when linear recovery is too slow.  
**Inputs:** Deep angle, passer outside your hip line trajectory, legs displaced.  
**Outputs:** Re-facing alignment + reinserted legs/frames.  
**Rules:** Use only when the demarcation loss demands rotation; re-attach immediately after reorientation.  
**Failure signature:** Spinning without connection; opponent steps away and resets.  
**Metric:** % spins that end with regained facing + leg reinsertion.

---

### 12) Scissor-and-Sit Reconfiguration OS
**Trigger:** You need to reconfigure hips/legs under pressure without conceding penetration.  
**Objective:** Rebuild leg structure and re-center while maintaining defensive integrity.  
**Inputs:** Compression pressure with enough space to move hips.  
**Outputs:** Re-centered hips + functional leg configuration restored.  
**Rules:** Reconfigure to restore inside lanes and square alignment; do not drift into hip-line danger.  
**Failure signature:** Reconfiguration creates knee separation and invites penetration.  
**Metric:** Time from scissor-sit to stable reinserted legs.

---

### 13) Pendulum Recovery OS
**Trigger:** Passer is driving angle while compressing space; you need hip repositioning with continuity.  
**Objective:** Swing hips to re-square and reinsert legs while preventing follow-through penetration.  
**Inputs:** Angle pressure + forward drive.  
**Outputs:** Angle reduced + distance restored + legs reinserted.  
**Rules:** Pendulum is for repositioning, not escape; pair with frames if hip-line alarm is near.  
**Failure signature:** Pendulum without frame support; passer collapses space.  
**Metric:** % pendulum reps that end with regained square alignment.

---

### 14) Scissor-and-Roll Recovery OS
**Trigger:** You must change orientation to escape worsening angle and recover guard line.  
**Objective:** Roll to replace a losing alignment with a recoverable one.  
**Inputs:** Severe angle and positional drift; limited linear recovery.  
**Outputs:** New facing + re-established guard structure.  
**Rules:** Roll must end with immediate re-attachment; do not roll into separation.  
**Failure signature:** Roll creates a clean disengagement for the passer.  
**Metric:** % roll recoveries that keep contact and prevent reset.

---

### 15) Reverse-Shrimping Re-Angle OS
**Trigger:** Standard shrimping does not restore the correct lane; you need reverse hip movement.  
**Objective:** Recover alignment by moving hips along the correct vector to re-square.  
**Inputs:** Misalignment where forward shrimp increases exposure.  
**Outputs:** Corrected hip line + restored leg insertion opportunities.  
**Rules:** Use when the demarcation indicates wrong-direction movement; reverse first, then rebuild.  
**Failure signature:** Repeated standard shrimp that worsens angle.  
**Metric:** Reduction in repeated failed shrimps in the same exchange.

---

### 16) Scooting Re-Center OS
**Trigger:** Micro-distance/angle drift where large movements are unnecessary or unsafe.  
**Objective:** Re-center hips and preserve alignment with minimal exposure.  
**Inputs:** Small alignment drift; passer probing for angle.  
**Outputs:** Re-centered position + maintained connection.  
**Rules:** Small corrections early prevent emergency corrections late.  
**Failure signature:** You ignore drift until hip-line alarm triggers.  
**Metric:** % times micro-drift is corrected before angle becomes obvious.

---

### 17) High-Framing / Low-Framing Selection OS
**Trigger:** Pressure is entering; you must choose frame height to control distance and posture.  
**Objective:** Select the appropriate frame level to stop penetration and preserve reinsert space.  
**Inputs:** Opponent posture height and entry line (high vs low pressure).  
**Outputs:** Correct frame placement that supports leg recovery.  
**Rules:** Frame level must match threat line; wrong height collapses structure.  
**Failure signature:** Frames exist but don’t stop the actual pressure line; penetration continues.  
**Metric:** % frame selections that prevent the next penetration step.

---

### 18) Frames + Feet/Hips Synchronization OS
**Trigger:** Any time you frame while rebuilding legs.  
**Objective:** Synchronize frames with hip movement and foot reinsertion so structure and mobility support each other.  
**Inputs:** Active framing + leg reinsertion attempt.  
**Outputs:** Protected space + functional leg structure restored.  
**Rules:** Frames buy time; feet/hips use the time—do both in one coordinated sequence.  
**Failure signature:** You frame but legs stay late; space is wasted.  
**Metric:** time from first frame to successful leg reinsertion.

---

### 19) Minimal-Correction Retention OS
**Trigger:** You detect early demarcation loss (before hip-line alarm).  
**Objective:** Solve problems with the smallest correction to preserve tempo and avoid over-rotation.  
**Inputs:** Early cues: angle beginning, distance shrinking, grips forming.  
**Outputs:** Corrected alignment with minimal exposure.  
**Rules:** Minimal correction first; escalate only if the problem persists.  
**Failure signature:** Big movements for small problems; you open new lanes.  
**Metric:** reduction in over-rotation events; fewer “two-link” losses.

---

### 20) Reinsert-Then-Reattach OS
**Trigger:** After any recovery movement that restores space (shrimp/pendulum/scoot/etc.).  
**Objective:** Convert recovered space into guard function by reinserting legs and rebuilding handles immediately.  
**Inputs:** Newly created space + partial alignment recovery.  
**Outputs:** Legs reinserted + connection re-established (back to connection layer).  
**Rules:** Space without reinsertion is temporary—use it immediately to rebuild structure.  
**Failure signature:** You create space but do not reinsert; opponent collapses again.  
**Metric:** % recoveries that end with legs reinserted + at least one handle rebuilt.

## VN3 — Next 10 Systems (21–30) (Operational Density)

### 21) Retention State Machine OS (Normal vs Alarm)
**Trigger:** Any retention exchange where pressure is changing rapidly.  
**Objective:** Prevent mixed priorities by running a two-state machine: **NORMAL** (mobility-first) vs **ALARM** (structure-first).  
**Inputs:** Hip-line status + distance compression.  
**Outputs:** Correct state selection + correct tool priority.  
**Rules:**  
- NORMAL: mobility/re-square/reinsert first.  
- ALARM: frames/wedges first, then rebuild.  
**Failure signature:** Using NORMAL tools (pummels/crossovers) while being pinned.  
**Metric:** % correct state selections under pressure.

---

### 22) Demarcation-to-Tool Router OS
**Trigger:** You identify a demarcation loss (drift) and must choose the recovery tool.  
**Objective:** Route to the correct movement family immediately (no improvisation).  
**Inputs:** Declared lost line (distance / angle / hip-line danger).  
**Outputs:** One selected recovery tool + one reinforcement action (reinsert/re-attach).  
**Rules:** One problem → one tool → re-assess; do not chain random tools.  
**Failure signature:** “Movement stacks” that worsen alignment.  
**Metric:** Reduction in multi-move scrambles that end in late-chain defense.

---

### 23) Early Angle Containment OS (Angle Before Level)
**Trigger:** The passer begins to win lateral alignment (angle formation).  
**Objective:** Stop angle from linking into level and penetration.  
**Inputs:** Hip rotation cues, knee-line exposure, run-around pressure.  
**Outputs:** Angle neutralized + square alignment restored.  
**Rules:** Treat angle as an “early link”; do not tolerate slow angle drift.  
**Failure signature:** You allow angle to settle, then fight level/penetration.  
**Metric:** % angle threats solved before level changes.

---

### 24) Knee-Line Preservation OS
**Trigger:** Legs are being cleared/stapled and knee line begins to open.  
**Objective:** Preserve knee line integrity so penetration lanes cannot form.  
**Inputs:** Leg displacement cues: outside legs late, knees separated, inside lanes lost.  
**Outputs:** Knee line restored + inside lane access regained.  
**Rules:** Knee line is a retention gate; if it collapses, prioritize reinsertion immediately.  
**Failure signature:** Knees stay split; opponent enters space and pins.  
**Metric:** Time-to-knee-line restoration after a clear attempt.

---

### 25) Compression Management OS (Space Budgeting)
**Trigger:** Opponent compresses space (forward drive, tight passing pressure).  
**Objective:** Maintain a usable “space budget” to enable reinsertion and re-centering.  
**Inputs:** Compression intensity + frame integrity.  
**Outputs:** Space preserved (via frames) + hips repositioned (via recovery tool).  
**Rules:** Frames preserve space; hips/feet spend it to recover—do both or you lose the budget.  
**Failure signature:** Space collapses because frames exist but hips/feet don’t move in time.  
**Metric:** Seconds you can hold usable space while reinserting legs.

---

### 26) Recovery Sequencing OS (Space → Align → Reinsert → Attach)
**Trigger:** Any time you successfully create space (shrimp/pendulum/scoot, etc.).  
**Objective:** Convert space into guard function using a fixed sequence.  
**Inputs:** Newly created space + partial alignment recovery.  
**Outputs:** Functional guard restored (legs + handles).  
**Rules (sequence):** **Space → Alignment → Leg reinsertion → Handle re-attachment**.  
**Failure signature:** Creating space but failing to reinsert legs; opponent collapses again.  
**Metric:** % recoveries that end with legs reinserted + a handle re-established.

---

### 27) Minimal Exposure Movement OS
**Trigger:** Early retention corrections when the passer is probing rather than committed.  
**Objective:** Stay safe by using minimal exposure movement rather than high-commitment rotations.  
**Inputs:** Early demarcation cues with available space.  
**Outputs:** Corrected alignment without opening new lanes.  
**Rules:** Escalate only if minimal correction fails; avoid high-commitment tools in low-threat states.  
**Failure signature:** Over-rotating and conceding separation/angle.  
**Metric:** Reduction in “self-created” exposure events.

---

### 28) Continuous Reinsertion OS (Never Let Legs Stay Late)
**Trigger:** Any time legs are cleared or drift outside functional lanes.  
**Objective:** Maintain continuous reinsertion so the passer cannot complete distance/angle/level links.  
**Inputs:** Leg-clear attempts and lane loss cues.  
**Outputs:** Legs returned to inside lanes + restored guard structure.  
**Rules:** Reinsertion is continuous, not occasional; late legs = late defense.  
**Failure signature:** You rely on hands/frames while legs remain out of position.  
**Metric:** Average “leg-late time” per round (should decrease).

---

### 29) Frame Integrity OS (Structure, Not Push)
**Trigger:** You frame under pressure (especially near hip-line danger).  
**Objective:** Keep frames load-bearing so they survive long enough for leg recovery.  
**Inputs:** Contact points + opponent pressure direction.  
**Outputs:** Frames that hold shape + preserved space.  
**Rules:** Frames wedge and align; pushing collapses structure and fatigues.  
**Failure signature:** Arms burn out; frames fold; pin follows.  
**Metric:** Frame hold time under pressure without collapse.

---

### 30) “Stop the Follow” OS (Prevent Re-Collapse)
**Trigger:** After you recover once, the opponent immediately follows to re-compress and rebuild.  
**Objective:** Prevent immediate re-collapse by adding a follow-stopper (structure + reinsertion).  
**Inputs:** Opponent chase/follow behavior after your recovery.  
**Outputs:** A second-layer barrier that slows follow long enough to re-attach.  
**Rules:** First recovery is rarely enough; expect follow and build the second layer immediately.  
**Failure signature:** You recover alignment, then get instantly re-collapsed to the same failure state.  
**Metric:** % recoveries that remain stable for 3–5 seconds (enough to re-attach).
