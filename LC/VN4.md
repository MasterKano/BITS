## VN4 — Top 25 Systems (Ranked by Significance) (Operational Density)

### 1) VN4 Master Loop OS (Archetype → Module → Hip-Line Gate → Golden Rule Counter)
**Trigger:** Opponent initiates a pass attempt.  
**Objective:** Run a single playbook: identify passer type → apply correct defense module → obey hip-line gate → counter immediately.  
**Rules:** Archetype first; hip-line is the master gate; cycle break must produce counter-offense via Golden Rule.  
**Failure signature:** You defend “moves” without module selection; you reset neutral after retention.  
**Metric:** % pass attempts routed correctly (module + gate + counter).

---

### 2) Passer Archetype Identification OS (Mobility vs Pressure)
**Trigger:** First 1–2 beats of a pass attempt.  
**Objective:** Classify passer as **MOBILITY** (circle/step-around) or **PRESSURE** (close/compress/stabilize).  
**Rules:** Classify early; do not hybridize responses until archetype shifts.  
**Failure signature:** Using pressure defenses on mobility passers (get run around) or mobility defenses under compression (get pinned).  
**Metric:** Correct archetype calls per round.

---

### 3) Hip-Line Master Gate OS
**Trigger:** Any time passer depth increases or alignment worsens.  
**Objective:** Decide whether you are in **DEFENSE** (past/at hip-line danger) or **OFFENSE-CAPABLE** (in front of hip line).  
**Rules:** Past hip line → frames + realign + rebuild barrier; in front → mobility loop + counter options exist.  
**Failure signature:** Countering while still structurally compromised; getting pinned immediately.  
**Metric:** Pin events per round; correct gate calls under speed.

---

### 4) Mobility Defense Loop OS (Feet + Re-Square)
**Trigger:** Mobility passer circles/step-arounds without deep compression.  
**Objective:** Keep them in front; deny angle accumulation.  
**Rules:** Feet-on-them first → create asymmetry → re-square immediately on circling; hands last.  
**Failure signature:** Dead feet; chasing with hands; early framing that stalls while they circle.  
**Metric:** % time passer kept front-facing; angle-loss events.

---

### 5) “First Contact Wins” OS (Feet On Them)
**Trigger:** Mobility exchange begins or legs are being guided aside.  
**Objective:** Establish active foot connection (hooks/meaningful contact) to prevent free steering.  
**Rules:** If feet are not “on them,” you are not playing guard.  
**Failure signature:** They push legs aside for free and win angle without resistance.  
**Metric:** Time-to-foot-connection; frequency of “free leg push” events.

---

### 6) Asymmetry Creation OS (Anti-Staple / Anti-Scoop)
**Trigger:** Mobility passer starts stapling/scooping/steering legs.  
**Objective:** Make legs harder to staple by maintaining staggered, non-symmetrical structure.  
**Rules:** Symmetry is passable; stagger one active hook while the other is ready to pummel/re-square.  
**Failure signature:** Symmetrical legs get stapled; you lose both lanes at once.  
**Metric:** Staple success rate against you.

---

### 7) Immediate Re-Square OS (Don’t Chase With Hands)
**Trigger:** Passer begins circling to win angle.  
**Objective:** Re-square hips/feet to return passer to center.  
**Rules:** Re-square with hips/feet; do not “reach chase” shoulders with hands.  
**Failure signature:** Hands chase; hips lag; angle snowballs into hip-line danger.  
**Metric:** Time-to-re-square after circle initiation.

---

### 8) Mobility Escalation Gate OS (Frames Only on Hip-Line Threat)
**Trigger:** Mobility passer shifts from circling to level change/closing distance.  
**Objective:** Escalate from mobility tools to short structural frames only when needed.  
**Rules:** In front → feet/re-square; hip-line threatened → frames briefly → reinsert feet → re-square.  
**Failure signature:** Framing too early (you stall) or too late (penetration).  
**Metric:** Correct escalation timing (frames used only when gate says).

---

### 9) Pressure Defense Ladder OS (Deny Lock Early → Wedge+Move → Reinsert Legs)
**Trigger:** Pressure passer closes distance (stack/body lock/knee cut).  
**Objective:** Stop lock formation early; if late, survive compression; finish by rebuilding legs.  
**Rules:** Early denial is mandatory; if late, wedge+move (not bench press); every defense ends with leg reinsertion.  
**Failure signature:** Accept lock then react late; handfight without rebuilding; shoulders pinned.  
**Metric:** Lock-denial success rate; leg-reinsertion completion rate.

---

### 10) Lock Formation Early-Denial OS (“Stop the Lock Early”)
**Trigger:** First contact of pressure sequence (hands/arms seeking clamps/locks).  
**Objective:** Prevent stable lock/control from forming.  
**Rules:** Deny distance + grips/lock mechanics immediately; re-establish frames/inside barriers.  
**Failure signature:** You “let them connect” then try to fight once compressed.  
**Metric:** % pressure sequences stopped before stable lock.

---

### 11) Wedge + Move OS (No Bench Press Under Compression)
**Trigger:** You are late and under stack/body-lock style compression.  
**Objective:** Survive compression by wedges and mobility, not pushing strength.  
**Rules:** Wedges buy movement; movement rebuilds guard. “Don’t bench press.”  
**Failure signature:** Arms fatigue; frames collapse; shoulders pin.  
**Metric:** Survival time without pin; shoulder-pin events.

---

### 12) Spine Shape Preservation OS (Late-Phase Survival)
**Trigger:** Hips immobilized; compression is stable (late phase).  
**Objective:** Preserve posture/spine shape while creating a frame window.  
**Rules:** Protect spine/shoulders → create frame window → rebuild legs ASAP.  
**Failure signature:** Flattened posture; shoulder pinned; no window to reinsert legs.  
**Metric:** % late phases stabilized without pin in next 3–5 seconds.

---

### 13) Shoulder Safety OS (Anti-Shoulder Pin Under Stack)
**Trigger:** Stack pressure drives knees toward face and compresses shoulders.  
**Objective:** Prevent shoulder pin while you recompose and rebuild barriers.  
**Rules:** Avoid gifting shoulders; prioritize mobility under compression to keep shoulders from being fixed.  
**Failure signature:** Shoulder pinned → guard function collapses.  
**Metric:** Shoulder-pin frequency under stack rounds.

---

### 14) Two-Hand Control Window OS (Stack/Lock Exposure Moment)
**Trigger:** Opponent’s hands/arms become exposed while locking/stacking.  
**Objective:** Convert exposure into two-hand control to disrupt lock integrity.  
**Rules:** When lock hands show, take two-hand control—do not single-hand swat.  
**Failure signature:** You handfight “in place” while lock remains intact.  
**Metric:** % exposures converted into meaningful lock disruption.

---

### 15) Shoulder-Walk Under Compression OS
**Trigger:** You must move while compressed to create an exit/rebuild window.  
**Objective:** Reposition under pressure without bench-pressing.  
**Rules:** Move your frame/body under pressure to recover alignment and space for legs.  
**Failure signature:** Static resistance; no reposition; compression increases.  
**Metric:** Time-to-create a rebuild window from compression.

---

### 16) Leg Reinsertion Terminal Step OS (Every Defense Ends With Legs Back In)
**Trigger:** After any successful wedge/mobility or lock disruption.  
**Objective:** Finish recovery by restoring feet/knees barriers between you and them.  
**Rules:** If legs aren’t back in front, the defense is incomplete.  
**Failure signature:** You “survive” then remain leg-late; they re-lock and re-compress.  
**Metric:** % defenses ending with leg reinsertion to playable open guard.

---

### 17) Pressure “Early vs Late” Gate OS
**Trigger:** Pressure passer begins to compress—decide state quickly.  
**Objective:** Choose early solution (deny lock) vs late solution (structure survival).  
**Rules:** Early = break grips + rebuild barriers; Late = spine/frames + mobility + rebuild legs.  
**Failure signature:** Using early-only handfighting while already late.  
**Metric:** Correct state selection rate; reduced pin outcomes.

---

### 18) Maximizing Retention (Cycle Logic) OS
**Trigger:** After completing either mobility or pressure defense module.  
**Objective:** Treat retention as cycles: defensive cycle → cycle break → offense (mandatory).  
**Rules:** Retention is incomplete if it ends in pause/reset.  
**Failure signature:** “Retention pride” (defend then pause; opponent restarts with momentum).  
**Metric:** % cycle breaks followed by immediate counter attempt.

---

### 19) Cycle Break Recognition OS (Guard Restored Signal)
**Trigger:** You re-center and regain barrier/front-facing alignment.  
**Objective:** Identify the exact moment you are offense-capable again.  
**Rules:** Cycle break = re-centered + barrier restored + not past hip line.  
**Failure signature:** Missing the window; letting them fully reset stance/grips.  
**Metric:** Time from cycle break to counter initiation.

---

### 20) Golden Rule of Counter-Offense OS (Head Height Selector)
**Trigger:** At cycle break (guard restored).  
**Objective:** Use a single read to select counter target family.  
**Rules:** Head high → attack legs; head low → attack arms/head + go-behind counters.  
**Failure signature:** Counter by habit; wrong target family for head height.  
**Metric:** Correct head-height calls; conversion success rate post-cycle-break.

---

### 21) Golden Rule Gate OS (Do Not Counter Until Safety Restored)
**Trigger:** Temptation to counter while still being compressed/past hip line.  
**Objective:** Prevent premature counters that donate pins.  
**Rules:** If hip-line gate says DEFENSE, do not counter—finish recovery first.  
**Failure signature:** Countering while late → immediate pin.  
**Metric:** Premature counter attempts per round (should trend to zero).

---

### 22) Head-High → Leg Counter Routing OS
**Trigger:** Cycle break with opponent head high.  
**Objective:** Route immediately into leg-targeting counters (fastest available lane).  
**Rules:** Treat head-high as posture exposure + balance opportunity.  
**Failure signature:** Choosing slower upper-body attacks while leg window is open.  
**Metric:** Time head-high read → leg threat initiation.

---

### 23) Head-Low → Upper-Body/Go-Behind Routing OS
**Trigger:** Cycle break with opponent head low (often hips withdrawing).  
**Objective:** Route into arm/head control + go-behind style counters rather than slow leg reaches.  
**Rules:** Head-low reduces under/leg access reliability; come-up style targets become higher value.  
**Failure signature:** Chasing legs while they withdraw; losing connection/reset.  
**Metric:** Time head-low read → come-up/go-behind initiation.

---

### 24) “Each Pass Attempt Carries Counter Risk” OS (Deterrence Loop)
**Trigger:** Over multiple exchanges in a round.  
**Objective:** Make passing cautious by ensuring every attempt meets immediate counter after defense.  
**Rules:** Defense must exit into counter; otherwise passer repeats with confidence.  
**Failure signature:** Opponent passes with increasing commitment because no counters punish attempts.  
**Metric:** Opponent pass attempts per minute (should decrease as deterrence improves).

---

### 25) Module Switching OS (When Archetype Changes Mid-Exchange)
**Trigger:** Opponent shifts from mobility circling to pressure compression (or vice versa).  
**Objective:** Switch modules cleanly without hybrid confusion.  
**Rules:** Re-run archetype ID; obey hip-line gate; apply the appropriate module immediately.  
**Failure signature:** Continuing mobility loop while being locked; continuing pressure frames while they disengage and run around.  
**Metric:** Correct switch timing; reduction in “caught mid-switch” pins.
