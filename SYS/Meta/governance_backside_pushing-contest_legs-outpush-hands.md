# Governance — Backside Pushing Contest (Legs Push Out vs Hands Peel)

## 1. Purpose
This module governs a recurring exchange pattern in leg entanglements and guard transitions:
- **your legs push outward** to create space/retention/control,
- **their hands peel your legs** to clear and disengage.

If unmanaged, this contest ends in one of two failures:
- your legs get peeled and they disengage/pass,
- or you overextend pushing and lose facing/base.

Operational intent: **stabilize structure, deny peel leverage, prevent disengage**, then route.

## 2. Inputs / Preconditions
- You are in a backside or semi-backside exchange where:
  - your legs are the primary barrier/connector,
  - opponent’s hands are actively peeling/shucking legs off,
  - hip rotation/disengage is available if your structure breaks.

## 3. Outputs / Success Criteria
- Opponent cannot peel your legs free fast enough to disengage.
- You maintain facing and do not get flattened/turned away.
- You can stabilize the exchange for **3 seconds** and route to:
  - a containment hub,
  - a backside stability platform,
  - or a reset/re-capture if contact breaks.

## 4. Core Model / Engine
**READ PUSH/PEEL CONTEST → STABILIZE LEG RELATIONSHIP → REMOVE PEEL LEVERAGE → DENY TWO STEPS → ROUTE**

Key idea: you don’t “push harder.” You **make peeling inefficient** and keep contact meaningful.

## 5. Gates (override rules)

### 5.1 Facing Gate (primary)
If you lose facing while legs are being peeled, you lose.
- **Default action:** re-square hips/shoulders; follow rotation while staying facing.

### 5.2 Peel-Leverage Gate
If opponent has a strong peel handle, pushing is wasted.
- **Default action:** remove the peel handle (re-seat legs/angles) before pushing outward.

### 5.3 Two-Step Gate (escape line)
If they peel and achieve disengage to two steps, contest is lost.
- **Default action:** clamp one anchor immediately; if contact breaks, reset and re-capture.

### 5.4 Don’t-Overextend Gate
Overextending legs to push creates a lane to peel and run.
- **Default action:** stabilize first; push only within stable structure.

## 6. Top-6 Situations Playbook
1) **Opponent starts peeling your top leg immediately**  
   → **Default:** remove peel leverage (re-seat relationship) before pushing.

2) **Opponent peels then turns/backs away**  
   → **Default:** deny two steps (keep anchor) per anti-flee.

3) **Opponent peels while rotating to create backside lane**  
   → **Default:** keep facing; follow rotation; re-seat before progressing.

4) **You can stabilize a backside relationship**  
   → **Default:** route to a backside stability platform (criss-cross style hub selection).

5) **You cannot stabilize relationship (keeps slipping)**  
   → **Default:** route back to containment (50/50 / double 50) or reset.

6) **You stabilized for 3 seconds**  
   → **Default:** progress (containment/finish) without overreaching.

## 7. Opponent Reactions → Responses
- **Strong peel grip/handle:** remove peel leverage first; do not keep extending.
- **Peel + pull-away:** clamp anchor; deny two steps; if broken, reset/re-capture.
- **Peel + rotation:** follow while facing; re-seat; then route to containment/stability.
- **Peel stalls (no progress):** you’re winning; stabilize 3 seconds then route.

## 8. Failure Signatures → Fix
- **“He peeled my legs and ran.”** → two-step gate violated; clamp anchor earlier.
- **“I kept pushing and got turned/flattened.”** → facing or overextend gate violated; stabilize first.
- **“I was fighting his hands instead of structure.”** → peel-leverage gate violated; fix leg relationship.
- **“I couldn’t stop the peel.”** → wrong platform; route to containment or criss-cross.

## 9. Key Cues
- opponent’s hands are consistently on your shins/ankles trying to strip,
- your legs feel “extended” and vulnerable to being pushed aside,
- peel attempts are followed by rotation/backstep or immediate disengage attempt.

## 10. Quality / Capture Checklist
- [ ] facing maintained throughout
- [ ] peel handle removed before pushing
- [ ] anchor retained to deny two steps
- [ ] no overextension of legs
- [ ] route chosen after 3-second stabilization

## 13. Drills and Games

### 13.1 Peel → Disengage Denial
- **Start:** backside exchange; passer’s goal is peel then disengage to two steps.
- **Defender wins:** prevent disengage for 10s and achieve one 3s stabilization.
- **Passer wins:** peel free and disengage to two steps within 5s.
- **Rules:** defender must prioritize structure over hand-fighting.
- **Reset:** 3s stabilization or two-step escape.
- **Rounds:** 8 reps each, switch.

### 13.2 No-Overextend Constraint Game
- **Start:** backside contest; defender is tempted to extend legs to push.
- **Defender wins:** keep structure compact, remove peel leverage, stabilize 3s within 10s.
- **Passer wins:** peel and turn defender away or disengage within 10s.
- **Rules:** if defender fully extends legs (obvious overextension), rep loss.
- **Reset:** 3s stabilization or escape/turn.
- **Rounds:** 8 reps each, switch.
