# Governance — Anti-Flee the Mat (Deny Disengage to Two Steps)

## 1. Purpose
This module governs the most common universal escape: **fleeing** (pull-away + disengage to distance).  
It defines:
- how to recognize the flee attempt early,
- what you must preserve (anchors + facing),
- and what you must deny (two-step separation).

This is not “chase faster.” It is **escape denial by contact governance**.

## 2. Inputs / Preconditions
- You are engaged (guard, open guard, leg entanglement, scramble, passing exchange).
- Opponent has access to a disengage lane:
  - hip-line separation,
  - hand-fighting to strip contact,
  - backing/turning to create steps.

## 3. Outputs / Success Criteria
- Opponent does **not** achieve “disengage to two steps.”
- You preserve at least one anchor and re-establish control posture within **5 seconds**.
- You force the opponent into one of:
  - re-engagement (they must address your anchor),
  - a slower escape (multi-step),
  - or a predictable re-entry lane you can punish.

## 4. Core Model / Engine
**READ FLEE CUE → PRIORITIZE ANCHORS → DENY TWO STEPS → RE-SQUARE → ROUTE (re-capture / re-attack / reset)**

Key principle:  
If you lose **anchor + facing** simultaneously, you are chasing.

## 5. Gates (override rules)

### 5.1 Two-Step Gate (hard failure line)
If opponent achieves two steps of clean distance with no meaningful contact, the flee succeeded.
- **Default action:** treat as reset; rebuild contact via safest re-entry (do not sprint-chase into bad alignment).

### 5.2 Anchor Gate (primary)
At least one anchor must remain during the flee attempt:
- leg line contact, wrist/2-on-1, collar tie/underhook (domain-dependent), or hips/frames (ground).
- **Default action:** clamp one anchor first; only then adjust position.

### 5.3 Facing Gate
Maintain facing (hips/shoulders) while following.
- **Default action:** re-square first; never follow with your back turned.

### 5.4 Anti-Overreach Gate
Overreaching to “keep contact” often causes loss of base.
- **Default action:** small steps + re-square + re-anchor (not lunges).

## 6. Top-6 Situations Playbook
1) **Opponent strips grips and backs away**  
   → **Default:** lock one anchor immediately (leg line or wrist line); re-square.

2) **Opponent turns away to create space**  
   → **Default:** keep facing; follow with re-square steps; deny hip-line separation.

3) **Opponent stands and tries to disengage**  
   → **Default:** keep anchor (ankle/2-on-1/shin-to-shin style attachment depending on domain) and scoot-follow.

4) **Opponent shoves legs/frames away and retreats**  
   → **Default:** retain one barrier (frame/leg) and convert to an anchor before they get two steps.

5) **You feel contact thinning (almost nothing left)**  
   → **Default:** choose the best single anchor and clamp; stop switching targets.

6) **Opponent gets two steps anyway**  
   → **Default:** accept reset; rebuild engagement with structure (don’t chase blindly).

## 7. Opponent Reactions → Responses
- **Pull-away/backstep:** clamp anchor + re-square; follow with base.
- **Hand-fight strip:** prioritize anchor replacement over winning a “grip exchange.”
- **Turn-away + run:** deny turning lane by staying facing; attach to a leg/wrist line that forces re-engagement.
- **Shuck-and-go (push then retreat):** keep one barrier; re-anchor before they clear.

## 8. Failure Signatures → Fix
- **“He just backed out and reset.”** → you lost anchors; clamp one first.
- **“I chased and got countered.”** → anti-overreach gate violated; small steps + re-square.
- **“I had contact but it didn’t matter.”** → anchor wasn’t meaningful; replace with a line that prevents two steps.
- **“I followed but got turned.”** → facing gate violated; re-square before following.

## 9. Key Cues (recognition)
- sudden posture rise / retreat steps,
- grip strip followed by immediate backing,
- turning the hips away to create exit lane,
- hands actively peeling frames/legs then retreating.

## 10. Quality / Capture Checklist
- [ ] I identified the flee attempt early (before two steps)
- [ ] I kept one meaningful anchor the whole time
- [ ] I stayed facing while following
- [ ] I avoided lunging/overreaching
- [ ] I forced re-engagement or a slower exit

## 13. Drills and Games

### 13.1 Two-Step Denial Game
- **Start:** neutral engagement with light contact (partner chooses flee timing).
- **Defender wins:** deny “disengage to two steps” for 10s.
- **Passer wins:** achieve two clean steps with no meaningful contact.
- **Rules:** defender must keep one anchor at all times; no lunging.
- **Reset:** two-step success or 10s denial.
- **Rounds:** 6 x 10s each, switch.

### 13.2 Anchor Replacement Reps
- **Start:** defender has one anchor; passer’s job is strip then retreat.
- **Defender wins:** replace anchor within 3s and prevent two steps.
- **Passer wins:** strip and disengage to two steps within 5s.
- **Rules:** defender may not switch anchors more than once per rep (forces commitment).
- **Reset:** replace + stabilize or disengage.
- **Rounds:** 10 reps each, switch.
