# Tight waist

## Purpose

A top connector hub used to:
- stabilize failed/denied backtakes without scrambling,
- control turtle/quadpod transitions,
- start passing progressions when the opponent is defending the back,
- re-enter the pass/back conversion loop with predictable reactions.

## Core idea

- Tight waist is the default “catch” when:
  - a backtake attempt is denied,
  - the opponent turns to turtle/quadpod,
  - or you need a tighter hip/torso connection before upgrading to pins.
- Tight waist is not an endpoint; it is a **stabilizer that forces a fork**:
  - they stay facing away/turtle → breakdown/back lane
  - they turn toward to re-guard/face → chest-to-chest pass lane
  - they build height/stand → collapse and re-stabilize

## Key positions

- Tight waist (primary)
  - Hip/torso connection strong enough to prevent stand-up and deny hip separation.
- Tight waist → breakdown
  - Used when they stay in turtle/quadpod; breakdown is the priority before chasing hooks.
- Tight waist → chest-to-chest
  - When they turn toward to face you; transition to pin/pass lanes.
- Tight waist → camping / north-south
  - After breakdown or when head/shoulder control is established and you can upgrade.

## Entries

- Denied backtake (hooks/attachment slipping)
  - When you lose hooks, do not chase; tighten waist connection and stabilize.
- Turtle/quadpod formation
  - When they fold to avoid the back, tight waist is the connector that prevents escape.
- Ride outcomes
  - After cross-body ride, if turtle forms or height builds, tight waist is the upgrade.

## Key cues (operational)

- “Connection before hooks”: if you chase hooks while they are still mobile, you create scrambles.
- “Height is the enemy”: your first job is denying the clean rise.
- “Knees are the alarm”: if knees insert under you, you are losing the pass lane.
- “Fork mindset”: tight waist exists to force a reaction, not to hold forever.

## Control gates (must be true before you branch)

### 1) Hips connected
- Their hips cannot freely slide away from your connection.
- You can follow their hip motion without losing the waist.

### 2) Height denied
- They cannot build to a stable base (stand/quadpod) without you collapsing it.
- If height is building, you pause advancement and collapse first.

### 3) Shoulder line controlled (enough to prevent easy re-square)
- They cannot freely rotate shoulders to fully face you without friction.
- If shoulders are too free, upgrade control before attempting to “finish” the back lane.

### 4) Knee insertion denied
- Knees cannot freely enter to recover guard.
- If knees are entering, re-angle and control hips before advancing.

## Decision rules (IF/THEN) — lane-by-lane

### A) Establishment (getting tight waist stable)
- IF backtake is denied -> THEN tight waist immediately (no reset).
- IF turtle/quadpod forms -> THEN tight waist is mandatory before hooks (stabilize first).
- IF height begins building -> THEN collapse the rise before any other goal.

### B) Branch: stays turtle (faces away)
- IF they stay facing away -> THEN breakdown first; do not chase hooks in a mobile turtle.
- IF breakdown succeeds and they flatten -> THEN choose:
  - camping/north-south if you can establish cranial shift,
  - back lane if upper-back connection is available,
  - pass lane if they turn toward.

### C) Branch: turns toward (faces you)
- IF they turn toward -> THEN accept chest-to-chest and start passing.
- IF frames stop your progress -> THEN upgrade cranial control before stepping deeper.

### D) Branch: builds height (stand/quadpod)
- IF they build height -> THEN your only job is collapse and re-stabilize.
- IF collapse forces them back down -> THEN re-run the fork (turtle/turn/height).

### E) Stabilizer hierarchy (when tight waist isn’t enough)
- IF they keep rotating and you lose connection -> THEN cross-body ride (follow rotation).
- IF you can step into a deeper cranial pin -> THEN north-south.
- IF you need hip freeze to stop knee insertion -> THEN camping.

## Opponent counters + immediate answers

- Counter: quadpod rise to escape.
  - Answer: height denial first; collapse, then re-run fork.
- Counter: sit-through/turn-in to recover guard.
  - Answer: accept pass lane; upgrade cranial shift and deny knees.
- Counter: continuous rotation from turtle to create space.
  - Answer: maintain connection; if rotation is heavy, switch to cross-body ride.
- Counter: explosive turn-away run after breakdown attempt.
  - Answer: re-capture connection to upper back before hooks; if distance opens, stabilize first.

## Failure signatures + fixes

- “I chase hooks and it becomes a scramble.”
  - Fix: breakdown/connection first; hooks are downstream.
- “They keep standing up.”
  - Fix: you are not denying height; collapse rise before chasing position.
- “They turn in and I insist on the back.”
  - Fix: take pass lane; back lane is from turn-away and stable connection.
- “Knees sneak back in.”
  - Fix: hip freeze/angle problem; deny knees before advancing.

## Completion checks

- You can keep them from:
  - building a clean stand/quadpod,
  - turning freely without friction,
  - inserting knees to recover guard.
- You can force one of:
  - breakdown/flattening (back or pin upgrade),
  - turn-in (pass lane),
  - rise attempt (collapse → re-run).
