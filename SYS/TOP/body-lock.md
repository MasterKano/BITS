# Body lock

## Purpose

A top control hub for converting leg entanglements and scrambles into:
- stable pinning control,
- high-percentage passing progressions,
- and pass/back branching when reactions appear.

## Core idea

- Body lock is an **angle + clamp** system:
  - create angle first (don’t drive square),
  - clamp hips with the lock (deny knee insertion),
  - use cranial shift to prevent re-square,
  - then branch: pass if they face you, back if they run.
- If the lock is degrading, you do not scramble—drop into a stabilizer hub (camping / north-south / ride / tight waist).

## Key positions

- Side body lock (primary hub)
  - Lock around hips/waist with angle; denies knee insertion and re-guard.
- Front tight waist (connector)
  - Bridge position to come up and connect when the full lock is not yet secured.
- Body lock → north-south
  - When forward step is available and cranial shift is present.
- Body lock → camping
  - When they are rotating/re-guarding and you need a hip-line freeze to re-run the fork.
- Body lock → tight waist
  - When turtle/quadpod forms or they are building height and you need a stronger connector.

## Entries

- From leg entanglement exit (knee line cleared)
  - Come up into tight waist/front tight waist, then upgrade to side body lock as angle is created.
- From stand-up/heist attempt
  - As they rise, connect to hips with lock and deny their ability to square and insert knees.
- From denied backtake (top-side outcome)
  - If hooks/attachment slip but you remain on top, lock hips instead of chasing hooks from distance.
- From scramble (hip proximity)
  - When hips are available, lock and freeze before attempting to pass.

## Key cues (operational)

- “Angle first”: square pressure invites knee insertion and frames.
- “Clamp low”: lock hips/waist; a high-floating lock loses hips and becomes chasey.
- “Knees are the alarm”: if knees are inserting, your lock effect is failing.
- “Head line matters”: without cranial shift, they rotate/re-square even if you’re past legs.
- “Turn is the trigger”: toward = pass; away = back; turtle = tight waist.

## Control gates (must be true before branching)

### 1) Angle created
- You are not driving square into frames.
- You can move around hips without getting stuck on knee insertion.

### 2) Hips clamped (lock effect)
- Their hips cannot freely slide away.
- Your lock is not floating; it is attached to their hip line.

### 3) Knees denied
- Knees cannot enter between you and hips.
- If knees are entering, you must re-angle and re-clamp before advancing.

### 4) Cranial shift present
- Head/shoulders cannot freely rotate to re-square.
- If cranial control is missing, they will face you and recover.

### 5) No clean rise
- They cannot build to a stable stand/quadpod without you collapsing it.
- If they build height, switch to tight waist / breakdown behavior.

## Decision rules (IF/THEN) — lane-by-lane

### A) Establishment
- IF you enter body lock from a leg exit -> THEN create angle before driving; clamp first, then move.
- IF you feel knees inserting -> THEN stop advancing; re-angle and re-clamp hips (deny knees).
- IF you are past legs but stuck -> THEN cranial shift is missing; fix head/shoulders before forcing pass steps.
- IF they rotate under you while you lock -> THEN you lack hip clamp or cranial shift; re-satisfy gates.

### B) Branch: turn-toward (faces/frames)
- IF they turn toward -> THEN commit to chest-to-chest and start pass progression.
- IF their frames stop your advancement -> THEN re-win cranial control and re-angle; do not drive straight into frames.
- IF they keep turning toward to stop the back -> THEN treat it as success: deepen pin, pass.

### C) Branch: turn-away (runs/turns out)
- IF they turn away -> THEN close distance to upper back immediately (connection before hooks).
- IF you are too far to connect -> THEN keep hip clamp and re-pin until distance collapses; do not reach.
- IF turn-away becomes turtle/quadpod -> THEN tight waist trigger: connect and break them down.

### D) Branch: turtle / quadpod
- IF turtle forms -> THEN tight waist is the stabilizer; breakdown first, then branch pass/back.
- IF they are building height from turtle -> THEN collapse the rise before chasing hooks.

### E) Stabilizer hierarchy (when body lock isn’t progressing)
- IF lock is stable but you are stalled -> force reaction with cranial pressure; re-run fork.
- IF knees keep inserting and you can’t clamp -> drop to camping to freeze hip line, then re-enter body lock.
- IF you need roll/angle to stay connected -> cross-body ride.
- IF turtle forms or height builds -> tight waist.

## Opponent counters + immediate answers

- Counter: insert knees to re-guard.
  - Answer: re-angle + clamp hips low; deny knees before advancing.
- Counter: rotate shoulders to face you (re-square).
  - Answer: cranial shift upgrade; head/shoulder control before pass steps.
- Counter: build height / stand / quadpod.
  - Answer: no clean rise—tight waist/breakdown immediately.
- Counter: turn-away sprint (distance creation).
  - Answer: keep hip clamp; close distance to upper back first; hooks are downstream.

## Failure signatures + fixes

- “I lock but they re-guard anyway.”
  - Fix: lock effect failed (angle/hip clamp); deny knees first.
- “I’m past legs but nothing progresses.”
  - Fix: cranial shift missing; head/shoulders first.
- “I chase hooks and lose them.”
  - Fix: upper-back connection first; hooks second.
- “Body lock becomes a scramble.”
  - Fix: drop to stabilizer (camping/north-south/ride) rather than forcing.

## Completion checks

- They cannot:
  - insert knees to re-guard,
  - rotate freely to re-square,
  - build a clean stand/quadpod without being collapsed.
- You can force one of:
  - turn-toward (pass start),
  - turn-away (back attachment start),
  - turtle (tight waist trigger),
  without resetting to neutral.
