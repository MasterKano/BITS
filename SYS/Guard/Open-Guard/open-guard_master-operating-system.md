# Open Guard — Master Operating System

## 1. Purpose
A repeatable open-guard operating loop that:
- prevents guard passing by denying required pass-building steps, and
- converts every defensive cycle into immediate counter-offense.

This file is the top-level controller for Open Guard Part 1. It defines the loop, gates, validation tests, and failure signatures.

## 2. Canonical Loop
**Read → Connect → Retain → Break Base → Enter → Stabilize → Finish → Recycle**

- **Read:** identify opponent commitment (push/pull; forward/back) and posture context.
- **Connect:** establish handles + redundancy (hands + feet) so the opponent cannot step free.
- **Retain:** deny the specific pass requirement currently being built.
- **Break Base:** force posting / base collapse to open a valid entry window.
- **Enter:** choose an entry family appropriate to range and posture.
- **Stabilize:** select the control hub that suppresses the opponent’s best exit.
- **Finish:** complete the submission or sweep/top conversion without allowing a reset.
- **Recycle:** if the opponent clears to neutral, immediately re-enter the loop (no passive reset).

## 3. Master Gates

### 3.1 Hip Line Gate (retention escalation)
**Question:** Is the opponent at or past your hip line?

- **In front of hip line (offense-capable):**
  - movement solutions dominate (feet/hips re-square; keep them in front)
  - frames are secondary and used sparingly
- **At/past hip line (defensive cycle):**
  - frames become structural (not reaching)
  - mandatory re-alignment + barrier rebuilding until guard is restored

**Cycle break condition (must be true to exit defense):**
- you are re-centered,
- legs/frames are between bodies again,
- feet or knees point at the opponent again (orientation restored).

### 3.2 Hands-Down Gate (entry validity)
**Question:** Is their base truly compromised?

- **YES (posting / hands down / equivalent collapse):**
  - entry window is open; commit to entry
- **NO (upright stable base):**
  - keep off-balancing; do not rotate/spin under a stable posture

This gate is the system’s validity test for spin-unders and leg-entry commitment.

### 3.3 Orientation Rule (retention integrity)
At all times:
- **feet or knees must face the opponent**
- if one is displaced, the other must replace it immediately

Violation of this rule is the fastest path to losing the hip line.

## 4. Retention Control Model (what you defend)
Passing is treated as a requirements chain:
**Distance → Grip → Angle → Level → Penetration → Pin**

Operational rule:
- identify which link is being built *now*,
- break that link decisively,
- re-center and rebuild barriers before the next link forms.

## 5. Counter-Offense Mandate (what “success” looks like)
Retention is incomplete until it produces initiative.

**Rule:** every cycle break must convert into immediate counter-offense.
- Use head posture as the fast selector:
  - head high → legs
  - head low → arms/head + go-behind family

## 6. Entry Families (selection by range/posture)
Use the simplest entry that matches range and posture, and that respects the hands-down gate:
- **Distance entries:** 2-on-1 ankle, partial inversion distance breakers, shin-to-shin
- **Kneeling entries:** single/double/reverse double; cross-catch → hip pin
- **Standing bridges:** X-guard, RDLR, HQ (all require base-break validity)

## 7. Hub Selection (stabilize before finishing)
Choose the hub that suppresses the opponent’s best exit route:
- **Inside senkaku:** forward barrier value vs drive-in pressure
- **50–50:** containment vs turn-away exits / disengagement
- **Double 50:** higher-control containment (reduces mobility exits)
- **Criss-cross (inside-outside):** backside stability + strong breaking alignment

## 8. Exit Governance (prevent reset)
Assume the opponent will attempt to reset rather than lose slowly.

Primary exits/defenses:
- backstep
- flee the mat
- heavy leg
- high leg
- leg closure (closing/triangling legs to deny wedge)
- backside flattening (hands pushing your shoulders flat)

Default counter-principles:
- maintain redundant attachment so disengagement is slow
- occupy space (remove ankle gaps) to prevent leg closure from clearing wedges
- backside is a pushing contest: legs must out-push hands

## 9. Failure Signatures (fast diagnosis)
- **Late defense:** framing after penetration/pin begins → hip line gate ignored
- **Dead feet:** legs touch but do not control → opponent clears legs for free
- **Hand chasing:** arms lead while hips lag → angle snowballs
- **Spin under stable base:** hands-down gate violated → easy step-away/smash
- **Thin entry:** one step clears attachment → no true handle (“knot” not secured)
- **Orientation loss:** neither feet nor knees facing → hip line reached quickly
- **Retention pride:** you stop the pass and pause → opponent restarts with momentum
