# Retention Movement — Foot Pummeling

## 1. Purpose
A primary reinsertion method for rebuilding barriers and restoring orientation in open guard. Foot pummeling is the default mobility solution before escalating to frames.

## 2. When to Use
Use foot pummeling when:
- opponent is in front but attempting to clear legs and circle (angle build)
- your feet have been pushed aside and you must reinsert barriers
- you must re-establish first contact after a clear attempt

Foot pummeling is the “in front of hip line” solution set.

## 3. Core Objectives
- reinsert feet between bodies (barrier restoration)
- regain orientation (feet or knees pointing at opponent)
- deny the angle before penetration begins
- rebuild connection (hooks/wedges rather than passive contact)

## 4. Constraints (what must stay true)
- do not hand-chase while feet are out of position
- keep hips able to re-square (do not get pinned flat while pummeling)
- if hip line is threatened during pummeling → escalate to defensive cycle (frames + re-align)

## 5. Common Failure Signatures
- feet “touch” but do not control → opponent clears again instantly
- pummeling occurs after opponent is already outside knee line → late angle denial
- you pummel while square and static → opponent steps around your legs
- you pummel while hip line is threatened → frames required, not just legs

## 6. Integration
- Works with: demarcation (toe line/center line reads), orientation rule, six-elements (angle/penetration denial).
- Feeds into: base-break attempts (once attachment is restored) and entries (after hands-down validity).
