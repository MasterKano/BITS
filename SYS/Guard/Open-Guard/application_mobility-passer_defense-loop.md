# Application — Mobility Passer (Defense Loop)

## 1. Purpose
A repeatable defense loop for mobility passers (step-around / torreando-style behaviors) whose primary weapon is **angle accumulation** with low grip/lock commitment.

## 2. Identification (mobility passer signals)
- rapid lateral stepping to win outside knee line
- clears legs with hands and circles rather than compressing
- low commitment to chest-to-chest connection
- frequent disengage/reset attempts (especially if threat is low)

## 3. Primary Threat Model
- the passer wins by building **angle first**, then penetration.
- if you hand-chase, angle snowballs.
- if you lose orientation (feet and knees not facing), hip line follows quickly.

## 4. Defense Loop (in-order)
1) **Feet-first contact** (barriers/hooks, not passive touch)  
2) **Asymmetry** (force them to address one side)  
3) **Re-square hips** (crossovers/pendulum/scoot)  
4) **Re-center** (opponent in front)  
5) **Reinsert barriers** (foot pummel)  
6) **Counter on cycle break** (immediate; do not reset)

## 5. Escalation Gate (when to frame)
- Frames are not the default vs mobility passers.
- Escalate to framing only when:
  - hip line is threatened, or
  - level collapses unexpectedly (they converted to pressure).

## 6. Common Failure Signatures
- you frame early and they simply circle around the frame → wrong module
- you hand-chase shoulders/upper body while feet are cleared → angle snowballs
- you accept disengagement → threat is not credible; anti-exit governance missing
- you keep them “off you” but never re-center → you are losing slowly

## 7. Integration
- Demarcation: toe line is the early warning; center line must be preserved.
- Orientation rule is the key invariant.
- Counter-offense mandate: every successful re-center/cycle break must become threat.
