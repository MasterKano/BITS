# Entries — Distance: Shin-to-Shin Connector

## 1. Purpose
A connector position that bridges distance into leg engagement. Shin-to-shin is used to attach, off-balance, and steer the opponent into entries while maintaining orientation and avoiding upper-body reaching.

## 2. When to Use
Use shin-to-shin when:
- opponent is standing within leg range but not yet committed
- you need a stable connector to begin off-balancing and base breaking
- 2-on-1 ankle control exists but you need a better steering platform

## 3. Objectives
- establish a stable leg-to-leg connection
- create off-balance (force step/post/widen)
- maintain orientation (feet or knees facing)
- transition into entries (knee line access / ashi hubs) after validity

## 4. Constraints
- shin-to-shin is a connector, not the finish:
  - it must create base break or a reliable transition
- do not let shin-to-shin become “stalling” while opponent circles
- rotations/spin-unders still require hands-down validity

## 5. Failure Signatures
- “He just circles around it” → re-centering/orientation not maintained
- “I connect but nothing happens” → no off-balance; no base break attempt
- “I rotate and get smashed” → validity error (stable base)

## 6. Integration
- Feeds from: 2-on-1 ankle or partial inversion attachments.
- Feeds into: kneeling entries, X-guard/RDLR bridges, or direct ashi hubs depending on reaction.
