# Defenses — Heavy Leg (Weighting the Leg to Kill Entries)

## 1. Purpose
“Heavy leg” is a defensive pattern where the opponent **weights and deadens** your leg connections to:
- prevent elevation/off-balancing,
- strip hooks/inside position,
- and convert your open guard into a flattening/pressure progression.

This page defines how to:
- recognize heavy-leg early,
- keep your hip line safe,
- and route to the correct counter-system (retain / re-angle / re-enter / switch target).

## 2. Inputs / Preconditions
- You are playing Open Guard with some form of leg contact (shin, hook, ankle line, or knee line touch).
- Opponent applies **downward weight** onto your controlling leg(s), often combined with:
  - knee pinning,
  - hip pressure forward,
  - grip/hand control that prevents pummeling.

## 3. Outputs / Success Criteria
- Your leg structure is not “pinned dead” (you can pummel/re-angle).
- Your hip line remains safe (not flattened).
- You either:
  - re-establish active guard (frames + facing + mobility), or
  - switch to a distance-entry that bypasses heavy-leg pressure.

## 4. Core Model / Engine
**READ HEAVY-LEG → PROTECT HIP LINE → UNWEIGHT (ANGLE/FRAME) → RE-PUMMEL → RE-ENTER / SWITCH**

Principle: you do not “push heavy leg off.” You **remove their weight line** by angle and structure.

## 5. Gates (override rules)

### 5.1 Hip Line Gate (primary safety)
If hip line is being captured/flattened, offense is suspended.
- **Default action:** frame high/low → turn-to-side → shrimp/re-square → rebuild barriers.

### 5.2 Weight-Line Gate (solve the pressure, not the symptom)
If their weight is directly on your leg connection, hooks are fragile.
- **Default action:** change angle (hip escape / scoot / pivot) to make their weight miss.

### 5.3 Facing Gate
If you lose facing while trying to “save the hook,” you concede pressure lanes.
- **Default action:** re-square first; recover feet/knees facing before re-pummeling.

### 5.4 Don’t-Force-Entry Gate
Heavy-leg is specifically designed to punish forced entries.
- **Default action:** reset to retention engine, then re-enter from a safer distance or new angle.

## 6. Top-6 Situations Playbook
1) **Opponent drops weight onto your shin/hook and stalls your legs**  
   → **Default:** angle out (scoot/shrimp/pivot) + re-pummel.

2) **Opponent uses heavy leg + forward drive**  
   → **Default:** treat as pressure profile; frames high/low + turn-to-side; cycle break first.

3) **Opponent pins your knee/foot and hand-controls your frames**  
   → **Default:** restore frames first; then remove weight line by angle.

4) **Opponent heavy-legs and tries to step around (outside pass)**  
   → **Default:** recover center line; re-square; feet/knees facing rule.

5) **Opponent heavy-legs but you keep facing and have space**  
   → **Default:** switch to distance entry (2-on-1 ankle / shin-to-shin) rather than forcing hooks.

6) **Opponent heavy-legs then disengages**  
   → **Default:** anti-flee governance; keep an anchor (ankle/wrist line) to prevent two steps.

## 7. Opponent Reactions → Responses
- **Weight drop on hook/shin:** angle change first → re-pummel second.
- **Forward drive / pressure:** frame stack appears → turn-to-side → shrimp/re-square.
- **Step-around attempt:** recover center line + facing; reinsert barriers.
- **Hand-fight strips frames:** replace frame high/low before addressing legs.
- **Disengage:** keep anchor; deny two steps; reset to distance entry.

## 8. Failure Signatures → Fix
- **“My hook died and I got smashed.”** → weight-line gate violated; you tried to hold hook under weight.
- **“I turned away saving my leg.”** → facing gate violated; re-square first.
- **“I forced an entry and got pinned.”** → don’t-force-entry gate violated; reset and re-enter.
- **“He stepped around easily.”** → center line lost; recover center line before re-attacking.

## 9. Key Cues
### 9.1 Recognition
- your leg feels “dead” (cannot pummel),
- opponent’s weight is directly onto your shin/ankle/knee line,
- forward drive increases as your legs stop moving,
- opponent’s knee/hip line pins your guard side.

### 9.2 Success landmarks
- you can re-pummel your feet/legs within 1–2 movements,
- you maintain facing while unweighting,
- you prevent flattening and keep hip line safe.

## 10. Quality / Capture Checklist
- [ ] hip line protected first
- [ ] angle change removed weight line
- [ ] re-pummel regained inside position
- [ ] no forced entries into heavy-leg pressure
- [ ] re-entry chosen based on space (distance vs close)

## 13. Drills and Games

### 13.1 Unweight → Repummel
- **Start:** passer kneeling, drops heavy leg onto your shin/hook.
- **Defender wins:** remove weight line + re-pummel to active guard within 5s.
- **Passer wins:** pin your legs dead for 5s or flatten you for 3s.
- **Rules:** defender may not force an entry; must unweight first.
- **Reset:** repummel success or pin/flatten.
- **Rounds:** 10 reps each, switch.

### 13.2 Heavy-Leg + Forward Drive Detector
- **Start:** passer heavy-legs then drives forward on cue.
- **Defender wins:** avoid flattening and reach a clear re-square (feet/knees facing) within 5s.
- **Passer wins:** flatten and hold 3s.
- **Rules:** defender must use frames high/low; no grabbing legs while being driven.
- **Reset:** 3s hold or re-square.
- **Rounds:** 8 reps each, switch.
