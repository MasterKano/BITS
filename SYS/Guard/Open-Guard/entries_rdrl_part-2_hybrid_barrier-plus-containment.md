# Entries (RDLR) — Part 2: Hybrid Barrier + Containment (When to “Hold Space” vs “Lock”)

## 1. Purpose
RDLR is not only an entry to leg attacks; it is a **hybrid system** that can behave as:
- a **barrier/retention layer** (deny chest-to-chest, deny immediate pass), and
- a **containment/locking layer** (deny pull-away/backstep, force heel exposure, enter finishes).

This page defines the decision rules that govern when to keep RDLR as a **space-holding barrier** and when to convert it into a **locked containment** configuration.

## 2. Inputs / Preconditions
- Opponent is standing and pressuring into your open guard.
- You have RDLR hook potential (they are close enough for hook + calf access).
- You can obtain calf control and can post/angle as needed.
- You can recognize whether opponent is:
  - attempting to **collapse space** (pressure consolidation), or
  - attempting to **withdraw space** (pull-away/backstep/flee).

## 3. Outputs / Success Criteria
- You can keep RDLR as a barrier when pressure is the immediate threat.
- You can convert to a lock when withdrawal/backstep becomes the threat.
- You can deny the two key opponent solutions:
  - **knees closing** (kills pummel and heel access),
  - **distance escape** (backstep/pull-away).
- You consistently route into stable leg-entanglement hubs after locking.

## 4. Core Model / Engine
**RDLR CONTACT → IDENTIFY THREAT TYPE → CHOOSE MODE → EXECUTE MODE-SPECIFIC ACTIONS**

Two modes:

### Mode A — Barrier Mode (retention-first)
Use when opponent is trying to **collapse you** (pressure pass development).
- Goal: keep space; keep hip line safe; force hands down only as needed to survive.

### Mode B — Containment Mode (lock-first)
Use when opponent is trying to **withdraw** (pull-away/backstep).
- Goal: lock structure; deny distance; force heel exposure and stabilize.

## 5. Gates (override rules)

### 5.1 Threat-Type Gate (mode selector)
Pick the mode based on what the opponent is doing *right now*:
- **Collapse threat:** pressure progression / chest-to-chest attempts.
- **Withdraw threat:** pull-away / backstep / flee.

- **Default action:** if unsure, start in Barrier Mode; convert to Containment as soon as withdraw threat is detected.

### 5.2 Knees-Closing Gate (pummel killer)
If opponent can bring knees together, RDLR transitions stall.
- **Default action:** post with second hand to prevent knees closing before pummel/locking actions.

### 5.3 Hands-to-Mat Gate (base-break requirement for “advance”)
To advance from barrier into decisive entanglement, a base-break event must occur.
- **Default action:** pull/angle until hands post; then proceed to locking actions.

### 5.4 Distance Governance Gate (anti-backstep)
If opponent backsteps, distance must be governed immediately.
- **Default action:** foot to the **center line** to govern distance; then bring legs together and lock.

### 5.5 Grip Quality Gate (calf control)
Weak calf grip becomes a “reach” and invites disengage.
- **Default action:** wrist-deep calf grip; clamp; do not rely on fingers.

## 6. Top-6 Situations Playbook
1) **Opponent drives forward with pressure intent**  
   → **Default:** Barrier Mode: RDLR hook + frames/angle to deny chest-to-chest.

2) **Opponent’s weight commits forward and hands begin to post**  
   → **Default:** transition toward Containment Mode (hands-down is the go signal).

3) **Opponent tries to pull away to escape your hook**  
   → **Default:** Containment Mode: lock structure; deny distance; knee-to-floor if needed.

4) **Opponent backsteps (hip turns; step behind)**  
   → **Default:** foot to center line immediately; follow foot; lock and finish alignment.

5) **Opponent tries to bring knees together to kill your pummel**  
   → **Default:** post second hand to keep knees apart; then pummel/lock.

6) **You feel you can’t progress—stuck in barrier indefinitely**  
   → **Default:** you’re missing base-break; re-prioritize hands-to-mat before trying to pummel.

## 7. Opponent Reactions → Responses
- **Collapses space:** barrier actions—frames/angle, keep hip line safe, maintain hook.
- **Withdraws space:** lock actions—deny pull-away, deny backstep, govern distance.
- **Knees close:** second-hand post to keep them apart; then execute.
- **Hands post:** convert immediately to the locking/pummel sequence; harvest the base break.
- **Backstep:** center-line foot distance governance; inside-shoulder follow alignment.
- **Grip strip:** re-establish wrist-deep calf clamp; do not chase with hands while losing facing.

## 8. Failure Signatures → Fix
- **“I stay in barrier and never get entries.”** → you didn’t create base-break; force hands to mat before advancing.
- **“He backsteps and I lose him.”** → you delayed distance governance; foot to center line earlier.
- **“My pummel dies when knees close.”** → you didn’t post second hand; prevent knees closing first.
- **“I get flattened trying to lock.”** → you chose Containment Mode under collapse threat; start Barrier Mode first.
- **“My grip slips and he runs.”** → grip quality failed; wrist-depth calf clamp.

## 9. Trigger Recognition Checklist
Switch to Containment Mode when 2+ cues are true:
- [ ] opponent’s hands post (base-break)
- [ ] opponent’s hips rise (tripod-like posture)
- [ ] opponent begins pull-away or backstep
- [ ] your calf grip is stable and can carry load
- [ ] you can prevent knees closing (second hand post available)

## 10. Quality / Capture Checklist
Barrier Mode quality:
- [ ] hip line protected; no flattening
- [ ] RDLR hook intact; frames/angle working
- [ ] opponent is prevented from clean chest-to-chest consolidation

Containment Mode quality:
- [ ] hands-down base-break achieved
- [ ] knees closing is denied
- [ ] distance governance is active (center-line foot on backstep)
- [ ] pull-away is denied (knee-to-floor + lock available)
- [ ] heel exposure pathway is present

## 13. Drills and Games

### 13.1 Mode-Switch Recognition (barrier → containment)
- **Start:** opponent standing; you establish RDLR hook + calf grip.
- **Defender wins:** correctly switch modes: survive collapse for 5s (under pressure), then on cue opponent withdraws/backsteps and you lock containment for 3s.
- **Passer wins:** flatten/pin 3s during collapse OR disengage to two steps during withdraw.
- **Rules:** coach/partner alternates collapse vs withdraw every rep; you must name “barrier” or “containment” before acting.
- **Reset:** 3s lock / 3s pin / disengage.
- **Rounds:** 8 reps each, switch.

### 13.2 Knees-Closing Prevention (pummel prerequisite)
- **Start:** you have hands-down base-break; opponent’s only job is to close knees.
- **Defender wins:** keep knees apart (second-hand post) and reach the pummel-through landmark (pause 2s).
- **Passer wins:** close knees and stall you for 10s.
- **Rules:** any pummel attempt without first preventing knee close = rep loss.
- **Reset:** 2s pause or 10s timer.
- **Rounds:** 8 reps each, switch.

### 13.3 Backstep Distance Governance (center-line foot)
- **Start:** containment attempt; opponent immediately backsteps.
- **Defender wins:** place foot to center line and deny escape for 5s.
- **Passer wins:** clear line and disengage within 5s.
- **Rules:** if your knee points to ceiling during backstep, rep loss.
- **Reset:** 5s hold or escape.
- **Rounds:** 8 reps each, switch.
