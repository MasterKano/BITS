# Retention Movement — Scooting

## 1. Purpose
A seated hip-travel method for re-centering and maintaining engagement without losing orientation. Scooting allows you to follow circling opponents and reinsert barriers without standing up.

## 2. When to Use
Use scooting when:
- opponent tries to circle out of your center line while staying just out of reach
- you need to track them laterally while keeping feet/knees pointed at them
- you are re-establishing contact after a brief disengagement (without accepting reset)

## 3. Objectives
- maintain center line control (keep them in front)
- preserve orientation rule while moving
- enable immediate barrier reinsertion (feet-first contact)
- deny angle accumulation created by their circling

## 4. Constraints
- scooting must be coupled with reinserted feet/frames; scooting alone is not control
- do not hand-chase while scooting; hips move first, hands reinforce
- if hip line is threatened, transition into defensive cycle (frames + re-align)

## 5. Failure Signatures
- scooting becomes “chasing” and you lose orientation → angle snowballs
- you scoot but remain unconnected → opponent steps away and resets
- you scoot while flat and compressed → shoulder line risk increases

## 6. Integration
- Demarcation: scooting is driven by toe-line/center-line drift.
- Six elements: denies **angle** by staying square; delays **distance** resets by following.
- Pairs with foot pummeling to reinsert barriers after tracking.
