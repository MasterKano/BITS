# Retention — Framing Layer (Frame High / Frame Low)

## 1. Purpose
Define the escalation layer for retention when mobility tools (feet/hips re-square) are insufficient. Frames prevent hip line loss and buy time to rebuild barriers.

## 2. When Frames Are Mandatory
Frames are mandatory when:
- hip line is threatened (phase switch to defensive cycle)
- level is collapsing (compression increasing; shoulder line risk rising)
- the opponent’s posture/pressure prevents clean foot reinsertion

If you can solve the problem with mobility while still in front of hip line, do that; frames are escalation, not default.

## 3. Frame High vs Frame Low (selection logic)
### 3.1 Frame High
**Purpose:** manage upper-body pressure and shoulder line threats.  
**Use when:** opponent’s chest/head/shoulder line is collapsing into you; pin formation risk.  
**Primary output:** protect shoulders, preserve ability to turn to side, prevent head/arm clamps.

### 3.2 Frame Low
**Purpose:** protect hip line and prevent penetration.  
**Use when:** opponent is stepping deep, controlling knees/hips, or trying to win knee line/hip line.  
**Primary output:** stop forward hip access long enough to reinsert legs.

## 4. Non-Negotiable Constraints
- frames are structural; do not “bench press”
- frames must end in barrier rebuilding:
  - frames buy time; legs reinsert to restore guard
- combine frames with shrimp/reverse shrimp as needed:
  - frame → create space → reinsert barriers → re-center

## 5. Failure Signatures
- framing while flat → shoulder line control risk; turn-to-side first
- framing without reinsertion → opponent resets pressure and continues
- framing too early vs mobility passer → you lose the ability to re-square and get circled
- “I framed and still got passed” → frame chosen wrong (high vs low) or applied late (hip line already lost)

## 6. Integration
- Demarcation: frames are the hip line/shoulder line response family.
- Six elements: frames primarily deny **level/penetration/pin**.
- Orientation rule: frames must support restoring “feet or knees facing,” not replace it.
