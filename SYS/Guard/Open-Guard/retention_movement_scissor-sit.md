# Retention Movement — Scissor Sit

## 1. Purpose
A seated reorientation tool for restoring alignment and barriers when the opponent is circling. Scissor sit re-establishes functional facing and can reset the exchange into a controllable open-guard posture.

## 2. When to Use
Use scissor sit when:
- opponent is circling and you must change hip orientation quickly
- you need to transition from a compromised alignment back to a seated, square guard
- you require a fast re-pointing of knees/feet to satisfy the orientation rule

Primarily “in front of hip line” or early “hip line warning” phases.

## 3. Objectives
- restore facing (knees/feet toward opponent)
- reinsert barriers (feet between bodies)
- re-center the opponent in front (deny angle continuation)

## 4. Constraints
- do not sit without barrier control (otherwise you sit into penetration)
- if hip line is already threatened, combine with structural frames (defensive cycle)
- scissor sit must end with reinserted feet/frames, not just posture change

## 5. Failure Signatures
- you sit but opponent remains at your flank → re-centering failure
- you sit and get immediately compressed → barrier not rebuilt; level advanced
- you sit too late (hip line already crossed) → frames required; sit is insufficient

## 6. Integration
- Demarcation: responds to toe-line and center-line drift.
- Six elements: denies **angle** and prevents **penetration** by reorientation.
- Pairs with foot pummeling for barrier reinsertion after reorientation.
