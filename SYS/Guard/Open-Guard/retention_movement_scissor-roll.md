# Retention Movement — Scissor Roll

## 1. Purpose
A rolling recovery used to restore facing and barrier position when angle has progressed beyond simple re-squaring. Scissor roll is a higher-commitment recovery that must end with legs reinserted and the opponent re-centered.

## 2. When to Use
Use scissor roll when:
- opponent has won meaningful angle and is threatening penetration
- you need a rotational recovery to place your legs back between bodies
- other mobility tools (crossovers/pendulum) are insufficient due to angle depth

## 3. Objectives
- rotate to reinsert legs as barriers
- prevent penetration to hip line
- recover orientation and re-center opponent in front

## 4. Constraints
- do not roll without a plan to reinsert barriers (rolling alone is not recovery)
- if hip line is already threatened, treat as defensive cycle: frames + structure are mandatory
- avoid ending flat with shoulder line controlled (pin danger)

## 5. Failure Signatures
- roll ends with opponent still at your flank → re-centering failure
- roll performed too late → hip line already crossed; late defense
- roll ends flat and compressed → shoulder line danger; pin forming

## 6. Integration
- Demarcation: a response to advanced toe/hip line warnings.
- Six elements: denies **penetration** and avoids **pin** by reinsert and re-center.
- Complements inverted spin as a rotational recovery family.
