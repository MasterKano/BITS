# Retention Movement — Reverse Shrimping

## 1. Purpose
A hip-travel recovery that moves your hips *back under you* to restore alignment, rebuild barriers, and re-square when standard shrimping (space creation) is not the required output.

## 2. When to Use
Use reverse shrimping when:
- you have created space but need to **re-center** and re-align under the opponent
- you are too far/angled away after a recovery and must re-square
- you need to bring hips back into position to reinsert legs effectively

## 3. Objectives (must produce)
- re-center hips under your base
- restore facing (orientation rule)
- enable barrier reinsertion (feet/knees between bodies)

## 4. Constraints
- reverse shrimping without barrier reinsertion is incomplete
- if hip line is threatened, treat as defensive cycle: frames + structure first
- do not reverse shrimp flat under shoulder line pressure

## 5. Failure Signatures
- you move hips but opponent stays at your flank → re-centering not achieved
- you reverse shrimp but remain unconnected → opponent circles again immediately
- you do it late at hip line → frames required; movement alone insufficient

## 6. Integration
- Complements shrimping:
  - shrimp = create space
  - reverse shrimp = re-center after space is created
- Six elements: primarily denies **angle/penetration** by restoring alignment.
