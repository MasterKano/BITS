# Defenses — High Leg (Leg Elevation / Leg Clear to Beat Retention)

## 1. Purpose
“High leg” is a passing-defense pattern where the opponent elevates and/or clears your legs **up and away** to:
- remove your frames and hooks,
- expose your hip line,
- and step into passing lanes (often outside pass, knee-cut style lanes, or chest-to-chest consolidation).

This page defines how to:
- recognize the high-leg attempt early,
- keep the center line closed,
- and route to the correct retention movement (re-square, pummel, or invert) without being flattened.

## 2. Inputs / Preconditions
- You are in Open Guard with legs between you and opponent.
- Opponent gains ability to lift/clear your legs:
  - underhooks/scoops under calves,
  - ankle/foot lifts,
  - hip-driven leg shelfing,
  - or stepping into a leg-clearing angle.

## 3. Outputs / Success Criteria
- Your center line is not opened long enough for opponent to connect chest-to-chest.
- Your legs return to a functional barrier (pummel/recovery) within **1–2 movements**.
- If elevation persists, you transition to an angle or inversion that removes the passing lane.

## 4. Core Model / Engine
**READ HIGH-LEG CUE → PROTECT HIP LINE → RE-SQUARE / PUMMEL → RECOVER CENTER LINE → ROUTE (retain / invert / re-enter)**

Principle: you cannot win by “holding your legs down.” You win by **re-orienting + re-inserting barriers**.

## 5. Gates (override rules)

### 5.1 Hip Line Gate (primary safety)
If opponent is close to chest-to-chest, your priority is to prevent consolidation.
- **Default action:** frame high/low → turn-to-side → recover legs to center line.

### 5.2 Center-Line Gate
If your legs are off-center, opponent will pass around you.
- **Default action:** recover center line immediately (pummel/recovery step), even if it costs a grip.

### 5.3 Facing Gate
If you turn away as legs go high, you get flattened.
- **Default action:** stay facing; use turn-to-side without giving your back.

### 5.4 Don’t-Reach Gate
Reaching with hands while legs are cleared accelerates the pass.
- **Default action:** rebuild barriers first; then hand-fight.

## 6. Top-6 Situations Playbook
1) **Opponent starts lifting your ankles/calves**  
   → **Default:** re-square + pummel; recover center line before they step.

2) **Opponent clears legs high and steps outside**  
   → **Default:** recover center line fast; angle out (scoot/shrimp) to remove the lane.

3) **Opponent high-legs to force chest-to-chest**  
   → **Default:** frames high/low + turn-to-side; deny consolidation.

4) **Opponent high-legs then pins with heavy pressure**  
   → **Default:** treat as pressure ladder; protect hip line; reset to framing layer.

5) **Opponent high-legs but leaves distance**  
   → **Default:** recover legs and re-attach to distance entries (2-on-1 ankle / shin-to-shin).

6) **Your legs are repeatedly cleared despite pummeling**  
   → **Default:** change geometry (inverted spin / high-leg recovery / angle reset) rather than repeating the same repummel.

## 7. Opponent Reactions → Responses
- **Ankle/calf lift:** immediate pummel/re-square; regain inside position.
- **Step around (outside lane):** recover center line; angle out to keep them in front.
- **Drive to chest-to-chest:** frames high/low; turn-to-side; rebuild barriers.
- **Pin after clear:** pressure defense ladder; don’t reach.
- **Disengage after clear:** anti-flee logic; keep an anchor if possible.

## 8. Failure Signatures → Fix
- **“My legs got cleared and I reached.”** → don’t-reach gate violated; rebuild barriers first.
- **“He stepped around my hips.”** → center-line gate violated; recover center line earlier.
- **“I turned away and got flattened.”** → facing gate violated; turn-to-side while staying oriented.
- **“I kept pummeling but nothing changed.”** → geometry unchanged; use angle/inversion/high-leg recovery.

## 9. Key Cues
### 9.1 Recognition
- opponent’s arms/shoulders under your calves,
- your knees/feet get pushed toward your head,
- you feel your legs “floating” (no wedge), and opponent’s step begins.

### 9.2 Success landmarks
- center line recovered within 1–2 movements,
- opponent cannot connect chest-to-chest,
- you remain facing and able to re-attach.

## 10. Quality / Capture Checklist
- [ ] hip line protected during leg-clear attempt
- [ ] center line recovered fast
- [ ] re-square achieved (feet/knees facing)
- [ ] no hand reaching before barriers restored
- [ ] geometry changed when repetition failed

## 13. Drills and Games

### 13.1 High-Leg Clear Denial
- **Start:** passer kneeling; attempts to lift/clear legs high.
- **Defender wins:** recover center line and re-square within 5s.
- **Passer wins:** step around hips or achieve chest-to-chest and hold 3s.
- **Rules:** defender may not reach for grips until barriers restored.
- **Reset:** re-square success or 3s hold/pass.
- **Rounds:** 10 reps each, switch.

### 13.2 Repetition Break (change geometry)
- **Start:** passer repeats high-leg clears; defender initially uses standard pummel.
- **Defender wins:** on 2nd clear attempt, switch to geometry change (high-leg recovery/inversion) and prevent pass for 5s.
- **Passer wins:** pass or pin for 3s.
- **Rules:** defender must demonstrate a distinct second response (not same pummel).
- **Reset:** 5s survival or 3s hold.
- **Rounds:** 8 reps each, switch.
