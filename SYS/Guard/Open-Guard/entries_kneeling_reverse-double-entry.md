# Entries (Kneeling) — Reverse Double Leg (Close-Contact Two-Leg Capture)

## 1. Purpose
A two-leg capture from **close contact** (typically underhooks/waist grip) when opponent is kneeling. You collect both legs from the “wrong side” (reverse direction), then immediately transition into the best stabilization platform (criss-cross / shoelace / double 50 / 50-50) based on whether the opponent can push your shoulder to the mat.

## 2. Inputs / Preconditions
- Opponent is on **two knees**.
- You have **close contact**: underhook/waist grip or similar torso connection.
- You can drop your level to collect legs while maintaining control of opponent’s torso orientation.

## 3. Outputs / Success Criteria
- You secure **both legs** (reverse double) so opponent cannot pull away.
- You can lock a strong entanglement (initially inside senkaku may appear, but you must correct if backside pushback occurs).
- You can win the **pushing contest** (your legs push hamstrings better than their hands can push your upper body).
- You can route to:
  - **criss-cross ashi** (preferred pushing platform),
  - **shoelace** configuration if threading is delayed,
  - **double 50** via inside-foot “chop” over knee,
  - or fall back to **classic 50-50** if required.

## 4. Core Model / Engine
**CLOSE CONTACT → HAND OUTSIDE LEG → SECOND HAND COLLECTS BOTH → LOCK CONTROL → THREAD/FOOT POSITION CORRECTION → ROUTE TO BEST HUB**

Operational sequence:
1) Establish close contact around waist/underhook.
2) Drop and place **one hand outside the leg**.
3) Bring **second hand around to grab both legs** (reverse double).
4) Control prevents pull-away; lock your base.
5) As opponent tries to push your shoulder down, **thread and place your foot/leg position** to create hamstring push advantage.
6) Route to best finishing platform.

## 5. Gates (override rules)

### 5.1 Close-Contact Requirement Gate
This entry is typically from closer grips (waist/underhook). If you don’t have close contact, don’t force it.
- **Default action:** switch to the standard double-leg off Irimi/Ashi entry (if available) or to single-leg entries.

### 5.2 Backside Pushback Gate (shoulder-to-mat threat)
Inside senkaku can be poor from backside if opponent can push your shoulder down.
- **Default action:** immediately transition to a pushing-dominant platform: criss-cross or shoelace-to-obliques.

### 5.3 Threading Delay Gate
If threading the preferred foot position is delayed and opponent is winning the pushback race:
- **Default action:** choose shoelace or inside-foot “chop” option (double 50) rather than insisting on the first-choice thread.

## 6. Top-6 Situations Playbook
1) **You have waist/underhook close contact**  
   → **Default:** reverse double collection (outside-hand + second hand gathers both legs).

2) **Opponent tries to pull away/flee**  
   → **Default:** reverse double prevents pull-away; consolidate and lock.

3) **Opponent pushes your shoulder toward the mat (backside)**  
   → **Default:** transition immediately to criss-cross / shoelace placement to win push contest.

4) **You cannot thread foot in front of hip cleanly**  
   → **Default:** go shoelace or inside-foot “chop” to double 50.

5) **Opponent backsteps / tries to create space**  
   → **Default:** double 50 containment makes flee/backstep difficult.

6) **Opponent clears the chosen option**  
   → **Default:** fall back to classic 50-50 and continue containment.

## 7. Opponent Reactions → Responses
- **Pull away:** reverse double capture denies escape; keep legs collected.
- **Pushback (shoulder-to-mat):** switch to criss-cross (foot in front of hip) or shoelace-to-obliques.
- **Flee/backstep:** double 50 option is described as making both difficult.
- **Clears the foot:** convert to classic 50-50.

## 8. Failure Signatures → Fix
- **“I got the reverse double but lost the pushing contest.”** → you stayed in a weak backside configuration; transition to criss-cross/shoelace.
- **“I couldn’t thread and got smashed.”** → use shoelace or double 50 option as the immediate alternative.
- **“I tried this without close contact.”** → wrong entry choice; acquire waist/underhook first or use other entry family.

## 9. Trigger Recognition Checklist
You’re cleared to run reverse double when:
- [ ] you have close contact (waist/underhook)
- [ ] you can place a hand outside the leg without losing torso control
- [ ] the second hand can collect both legs before opponent disengages

## 10. Quality / Capture Checklist
- [ ] both legs are controlled (pull-away feels “almost impossible”)
- [ ] you can stabilize enough to begin hub selection (you’re not immediately flattened)
- [ ] you have a plan for pushback (criss-cross/shoelace/double 50)

## 13. Drills and Games

### 13.1 Reverse Double Capture (pull-away denial)
- **Start:** opponent on two knees; you start with waist grip/underhook.
- **Defender wins:** collect both legs (reverse double) and prevent pull-away for 3s.
- **Passer wins:** disengage to two steps or break both leg controls before 3s.
- **Rules:** close contact is mandatory; no attempts from distance.
- **Reset:** 3s hold or disengage.
- **Rounds:** 10 reps each, switch.

### 13.2 Pushback Routing (hub selection under pressure)
- **Start:** reverse double achieved; opponent immediately tries to push your shoulder to mat.
- **Defender wins:** route to criss-cross or shoelace and stop shoulder-to-mat for 3s.
- **Passer wins:** shoulder-to-mat held 3s.
- **Rules:** you must pick one route quickly; no stalling in weak configuration.
- **Reset:** 3s control either side.
- **Rounds:** 8 reps each, switch.

### 13.3 Threading Failure Plan (double 50 fallback)
- **Start:** reverse double; you are “late” on threading by design.
- **Defender wins:** hit the double 50 option and deny flee/backstep for 3s.
- **Passer wins:** flee/backstep to clear within 5s.
- **Rules:** you may not attempt the preferred thread; you must use the fallback.
- **Reset:** 3s hold or clear.
- **Rounds:** 8 reps each, switch.
