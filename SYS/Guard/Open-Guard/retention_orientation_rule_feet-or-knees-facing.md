# Retention — Orientation Rule (Feet or Knees Facing)

## 1. Purpose
A non-negotiable retention integrity rule: you cannot reliably defend angle and hip-line threats if you lose orientation. This rule defines the minimum condition for functional open guard.

## 2. The Rule
At all times:
- **either your feet or your knees must face the opponent**
- if one is displaced, the other must replace it immediately

This is the fast test for whether you still have a guard or you are in a losing scramble.

## 3. Why it matters (what it prevents)
Orientation loss enables:
- uncontested angle accumulation (opponent gets outside your knee line),
- rapid penetration to hip line,
- forced defensive cycling and pin exposure.

## 4. Operational Implementation (replacement logic)
- If the opponent clears your **feet** (pushes legs aside), your **knees** must immediately re-point (re-square).
- If the opponent collapses or steps inside your **knees**, your **feet** must immediately reinsert as barriers/hooks.

Do not accept the “double loss” condition:
- feet cleared AND knees turned away.

## 5. Integration with Other Models
- **Demarcation:** orientation supports center/toe line control.
- **Six elements:** orientation primarily protects **angle → penetration** transition.
- **Hip line gate:** if orientation is lost and hip line is threatened, defensive cycle is mandatory.

## 6. Failure Signatures
- “He’s always on my side” → orientation replacement is late
- “My frames are always desperate” → orientation lost before hip line; frames are compensating
- “I get passed without grips” → orientation/angle failure, not grip failure
