# Entries (Distance) — 2-on-1 Ankle System (True Handle Hub)

## 1. Purpose
A distance-entry system for establishing a **true handle** on the opponent’s leg line using a **2-on-1 ankle** configuration.

This entry is a primary enabler for:
- denying reset/backstep (anti-exit),
- enforcing constant leg threat,
- creating base events (post/widen/forced step),
- setting up downstream transitions (shin-to-shin, inversion distance breaker, kneeling entries).

## 2. Inputs / Preconditions
- You are at distance (the passer is not already in heavy pressure on your hips).
- You can maintain orientation (feet or knees facing).
- At least one of:
  - the opponent’s lead leg is reachable,
  - the opponent is stepping in/out (movement gives access).

## 3. Outputs / Success Criteria
- You establish **2-on-1 control** on the ankle line (true handle quality).
- You prevent **disengage to two steps** (exit denied).
- You force at least one **base event** (post/widen/forced step) or produce a stable connection that survives a step.
- You keep opponent in front (no free outside knee line).

## 4. Core Model / Engine

### 4.1 What “2-on-1 ankle” is (operational definition)
A 2-on-1 ankle system is any configuration where:
- you have **two points of control** on one ankle/heel line (hand+hand, hand+foot, or equivalent redundancy),
- the opponent cannot freely withdraw the leg without first stripping a control point.

This is a **handle system**, not a “grab and pray.”

### 4.2 Why this is a hub (what it buys you)
- It makes retreat/backstep expensive (anti-reset).
- It slows circling because the leg cannot travel freely.
- It creates off-balancing opportunities that produce hands-down/base compromise (entry gating).

### 4.3 Entry loop (repeatable)
**REACH (safe) → SECURE 2nd control → SQUARE → RESTRICT EXIT → FORCE BASE EVENT / CONNECTOR**

The loop is designed to remain valid against both:
- mobility passers (exit/circle), and
- pressure passers (step in then compress).

## 5. Gates (override rules)

### 5.1 Hip Line Gate
If hip line is threatened, entries are suspended; defend first.

**Default action:** frames high/low → turn-to-side → shrimp/recover barriers → re-square → re-enter at distance.

### 5.2 Hands-Down Gate (commitment validity)
Do not commit to high-risk rotations/underhooks/inversions under stable base.

**Default action:** use 2-on-1 ankle to force post/widen/forced step, then commit to deeper actions.

### 5.3 Orientation Rule
Feet or knees must face opponent while building the handle.

**Default action:** re-square hips before upgrading grips; do not reach while turned away.

## 6. Opponent Reactions → Responses (Handle Maintenance)

1) **Backstep / retreat**
   - Response: follow with hips; keep 2-on-1 redundancy; deny disengage to two steps.

2) **Circle to outside knee line**
   - Response: keep connection while re-squaring (crossover/pendulum); don’t let the handle pull you sideways.

3) **Shove-to-clear (push your legs away)**
   - Response: maintain ankle control while reinserting barriers (foot pummel); then re-center.

4) **Pressure drop to crush (step in and compress)**
   - Response: keep handle if possible, but prioritize structure; frame stack and defensive cycle if hip line is threatened.
   - Rule: never sacrifice hip line for a “good grip.”

5) **Grip strip (attack your hands/feet)**
   - Response: reattach immediately; restore redundancy; do not settle for single-point control.

## 7. Failure Signatures → Fix
- **He just steps away** → you never achieved true handle; add the second control point earlier.
- **My grip pulls me out of square** → orientation violation; re-square first, then pull.
- **I lose the ankle when he circles** → you followed with hands not hips; keep hips facing and move (crossover/pendulum).
- **I chase the ankle into pressure and get smashed** → hip line gate ignored; defend first, re-enter at distance.
- **I can hold the ankle but nothing happens** → you are not restricting exit; force base events and connect to a downstream system.

## 10. Drills and Games (Game Cards)

### 10.1 True Handle or Lose (quality enforcement)
- **Start:** passer standing at range; defender seated open guard.
- **Defender wins:** establish 2-on-1 ankle control and prevent **disengage to two steps** for **15s**.
- **Passer wins:** strips contact then **disengages to two steps**, or achieves **outside knee line** then **hip line touch**.
- **Rules:** single-point ankle touch does not count; defender must create redundancy.
- **Reset:** timer / disengage / hip line touch.
- **Rounds:** 8×20s, switch.

### 10.2 Handle Under Retreat (anti-exit)
- **Start:** defender begins with 2-on-1 ankle already secured.
- **Defender wins:** maintain the handle through one full retreat/backstep attempt and deny disengage for **15s**.
- **Passer wins:** strips and disengages to two steps.
- **Rules:** passer’s only goal is retreat + strip; defender must follow with hips (no hand chasing).
- **Reset:** timer / disengage.
- **Rounds:** 8×20s, switch.

### 10.3 Handle While Re-Squaring (circle pressure)
- **Start:** defender starts with one control on the ankle; passer must circle to outside knee line.
- **Defender wins:** complete redundancy (2-on-1) and re-square to keep passer in front for **3s**.
- **Passer wins:** **outside knee line** for **3s** or **hip line touch**.
- **Rules:** defender must move hips (crossover/pendulum) while building the handle.
- **Reset:** 3s hold / hip line touch.
- **Rounds:** 8×20s, switch.

### 10.4 Base Event Creation (hands-down gate)
- **Start:** defender has 2-on-1 ankle; passer tries to keep stable base.
- **Defender wins:** force a **post/widen/forced step** within **10s**.
- **Passer wins:** maintain stable base for **10s** without posting/widening.
- **Rules:** defender may off-balance but may not commit to inversion/rotation until a base event occurs.
- **Reset:** base event / timer.
- **Rounds:** 10 reps each, switch.
