# Entries — Distance: 2-on-1 Ankle System

## 1. Purpose
A distance-breaking entry family that creates attachment from outside range by controlling the ankle/foot line as a true handle. This is the “first contact” system used to prevent the opponent from staying unengaged.

## 2. Core Principle (handle selection)
- Legs are posture-proof targets.
- The ankle/foot line functions as a “knot on the rope”:
  - if you control it, the leg cannot freely slide away while you climb.

## 3. Trigger Conditions
Use 2-on-1 ankle control when:
- opponent is standing and hovering just outside your feet
- opponent is disengaging / backstepping / fleeing the mat
- you need first attachment without reaching to the upper body

## 4. Objectives (must produce)
- establish a durable handle (ankle/foot line)
- prevent clean retreat (make disengagement slow)
- create a base-break opportunity (force step, post, or widened stance)
- transition into a deeper entry or a containment hub when they react

## 5. Constraints
- do not chase the upper body from distance; attach to the leg first
- attachment must be redundant where possible (hand + foot connection)
- if you lose the handle, reattach immediately; do not accept reset
- rotations/spin-under still require validity (hands-down gate)

## 6. Common Opponent Reactions (governance)
- **Backstep / retreat:** follow and keep the ankle handle; switch to containment if entangled
- **Heavy leg:** treat as a wedge/angle problem; improve close contact before climbing
- **High leg:** occupy space and maintain the “knot” control so leg cannot lift free cleanly

## 7. Failure Signatures
- “He just steps away” → handle not secured; attachment thin
- “I keep reaching and missing” → wrong target; should have attached to ankle first
- “I got connected but it didn’t lead anywhere” → no base-break attempt; no follow-up entry selection
