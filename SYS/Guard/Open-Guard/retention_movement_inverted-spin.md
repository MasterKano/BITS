# Retention Movement — Inverted Spin

## 1. Purpose
A rotational recovery used to restore guard when the opponent has advanced angle and is approaching penetration. Inverted spin is a higher-cost movement that must obey validity and demarcation: it is not a default response.

## 2. When to Use
Use inverted spin when:
- opponent has meaningfully won angle and is threatening hip line
- conventional re-square (crossovers/foot pummeling) is failing due to angle depth
- you need to rotate to reinsert legs between bodies

## 3. Pre-Conditions (validity)
- If hip line is threatened: treat as part of the defensive cycle (frames + structure) while spinning.
- Do not spin “blind” without maintaining barriers/frames: spinning must preserve orientation recovery.
- If opponent base is stable and upright and you are spinning under their structure: you are likely violating the hands-down validity concept (stable posture punish).

## 4. Objectives
- rotate to reinsert legs between bodies
- deny penetration and prevent pin formation
- exit the spin with opponent re-centered in front

## 5. Constraints
- spinning is a tool to restore barriers, not a substitute for connection and re-centering
- do not accept shoulder line control during inversion (pin risk)
- if spin ends with you flat and without barriers, you have lost the exchange

## 6. Failure Signatures
- spin ends with opponent past hip line and you flattened → late spin / shoulder line failure
- you spin under stable posture and get smashed/stepped away from → validity error
- you spin and opponent stays at your flank → re-centering failure (angle not resolved)

## 7. Integration
- Demarcation: used when toe/hip line warnings have progressed too far for simple re-square.
- Six elements: denies **penetration** and avoids **pin** by reinsert.
- Must end by restoring orientation rule and barriers.
