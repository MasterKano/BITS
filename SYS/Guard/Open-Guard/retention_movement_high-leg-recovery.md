# Retention Movement — High Leg Recovery

## 1. Purpose
A recovery method for regaining inside position and barrier integrity when angle is being won. High leg recovery rebuilds a functional knee/foot line before penetration becomes irreversible.

## 2. When to Use
Use high leg recovery when:
- opponent begins to clear past your knee line
- your near-side leg is being stapled/redirected and you need to reinsert it
- you must recover inside barriers quickly to prevent hip line access

High leg recovery is an “angle-to-penetration” prevention tool.

## 3. Objectives
- reinsert the leg as a barrier before penetration
- regain orientation (feet or knees facing)
- prevent the opponent from stepping deep past your knees
- create the space needed to return to foot pummeling / re-square

## 4. Constraints
- high leg recovery must occur before hip line is conceded
- do not reach with hands while legs are out of position
- if hip line is already threatened, treat this as part of the defensive cycle (frames + re-align)

## 5. Failure Signatures
- you lift the leg but do not re-center → opponent keeps angle and enters anyway
- you attempt it after the opponent is already deep at hip line → late defense
- you recover the leg but remain flat → shoulder line risk increases

## 6. Integration
- Demarcation: high leg is a response to toe/hip line warnings.
- Six elements: denies **penetration** by restoring the barrier line.
- Pairs with: crossovers (re-square) and foot pummeling (reinsert feet).
