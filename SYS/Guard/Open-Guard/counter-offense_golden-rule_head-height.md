# Counter-Offense — Golden Rule (Head-Height Selector)

## 1. Purpose
A deterministic selector for what to attack immediately after a successful guard retention cycle (cycle break). It prevents the common failure pattern: defend, pause, then get passed on the next attempt.

## 2. Trigger (when to apply)
Apply the Golden Rule at **cycle break**:

Cycle break conditions:
- opponent is re-centered (no dominant angle),
- your barriers are rebuilt (legs/frames between bodies),
- orientation is restored (feet or knees facing),
- you are no longer in immediate hip-line danger.

## 3. The Rule
Use head height to select the counter family:

- **Head high → attack legs**
- **Head low → attack arms/head + go-behind family**

This is a speed/clarity rule: it prioritizes immediate initiative over “perfect” selection.

## 4. Operational Notes (how to use it)
- The selector is not about preference; it is about **availability under posture**.
- The goal is to make every pass attempt carry a cost so the opponent becomes cautious (behavior shaping).

## 5. Integration with Master Gates
- If hip line is still threatened, remain in defensive cycle (do not counter early).
- If base is stable and upright, do not rotate under it (hands-down gate still applies for spin-under entries).

## 6. Failure Signatures
- **Stop-pass-stop:** you defended and reset to neutral without initiating → Golden Rule not applied
- **Forced scramble:** you attacked legs under a low head without validity → wrong side of hands-down gate
- **Angle loss after “success”:** cycle break was declared early (orientation/barriers not truly restored)
