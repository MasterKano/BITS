# Retention Movement — Crossovers

## 1. Purpose
A re-squaring movement used to deny angle when the opponent circles. Crossovers are a primary tool for keeping the passer in front while preserving orientation.

## 2. When to Use
Use crossovers when:
- the opponent is building angle by circling outside your knee line
- your feet/legs are being redirected and you must re-square quickly
- you need to move your hips to match their lateral movement (not chase with hands)

Crossovers are primarily “in front of hip line” solutions.

## 3. Objectives
- keep opponent in front (center line control)
- prevent angle progression (toe line early read)
- maintain a condition where feet or knees face opponent (orientation rule)
- preserve ability to reinsert feet as barriers

## 4. Constraints
- do not allow “double loss” orientation (feet cleared + knees turned away)
- do not get pinned flat while crossing; keep hips mobile
- if opponent reaches hip line during crossover → defensive cycle immediately

## 5. Typical Failure Signatures
- crossing too late: opponent already outside knee line and entering penetration
- hands lead the movement: reaching for upper body while hips lag
- crossing without feet reinsertion: you square but remain unconnected and get cleared again

## 6. Integration
- Demarcation: crossovers are driven by toe line and center line reads.
- Six elements: crossovers primarily deny **angle** and prevent **penetration**.
- Works with foot pummeling as the reinsertion layer once you re-square.
