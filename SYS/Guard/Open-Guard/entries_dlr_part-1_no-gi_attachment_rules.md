# Entries (DLR) — Part 1: No-Gi Attachment Rules (How DLR Exists Without Gi Handles)

## 1. Purpose
Define how **De La Riva (DLR)** functions in **no-gi**: you cannot rely on pant grips, so the system is governed by **attachment rules**—where and how you connect so the opponent cannot simply shake, backstep, or strip your hook.

This module establishes:
- the minimum viable attachment stack in no-gi,
- the “true handle” hierarchy (what holds under sweat + motion),
- and the immediate routing rules when attachments degrade.

## 2. Inputs / Preconditions
- Opponent is standing (or transitioning to standing) within open-guard range.
- You can get a DLR hook entry opportunity (outside hook around near leg).
- You can maintain orientation (feet/knees facing) and avoid being flattened while attaching.
- You can identify stance: staggered vs square, near-leg vs far-leg availability.

## 3. Outputs / Success Criteria
- You establish DLR with **redundant attachments** (hook + a true handle) rather than hook-only.
- You deny immediate backstep and pull-away exits long enough to force a base event (post/step/widen).
- You can route cleanly into Part 2 (outside-to-inside migration) or to alternative entry hubs if attachment quality drops.

## 4. Core Model / Engine
**HOOK → TRUE HANDLE → BASE EVENT → OFF-BALANCE → ROUTE**

No-gi DLR works when:
- the hook creates directional constraint, and
- a true handle prevents the opponent’s simplest solution (strip/backstep/pull-away).

## 5. Gates (override rules)

### 5.1 Hook-Only Gate (insufficient in no-gi)
A DLR hook without a true handle is unstable under sweat and motion.
- **Default action:** treat hook-only as temporary; immediately add a true handle.

### 5.2 True Handle Gate (priority)
If your handle is not “true,” attachment will fail under athletic resistance.
- **Default action:** upgrade to a true handle (see Section 10 checklist) before attempting major off-balancing.

### 5.3 Anti-Backstep Gate (first escape to deny)
Backstep is the cleanest way to nullify DLR when your attachments are thin.
- **Default action:** keep a center-line distance governor (foot placement) and track the stepping foot; do not freeze.

### 5.4 Anti-Pull-Away Gate (second escape to deny)
If the opponent can withdraw to two steps, DLR collapses.
- **Default action:** clamp with a true handle; follow and re-square; if you lose both, route to distance connectors.

### 5.5 Hip Line Safety Gate (no attaching while being flattened)
If pressure is collapsing you, attachment attempts are suspended.
- **Default action:** frames high/low + turn-to-side + shrimp → rebuild → re-attach.

## 6. Top-6 Situations Playbook
1) **You get DLR hook but no secondary control yet**  
   → **Default:** acquire a true handle immediately (do not off-balance hook-only).

2) **Opponent starts to backstep as soon as hook appears**  
   → **Default:** track stepping foot; maintain distance governor; upgrade handle; then off-balance.

3) **Opponent pulls away to disengage**  
   → **Default:** clamp with true handle; follow; if contact breaks, route to shin-to-shin / 2-on-1 ankle.

4) **Opponent squares stance to kill your angle**  
   → **Default:** use attachment to force a step or widen base; then migrate (Part 2).

5) **Opponent collapses forward pressure**  
   → **Default:** frames first; do not “hang on”; re-attach from safety.

6) **Your handle becomes slippery/weak**  
   → **Default:** downgrade expectations: switch to a different true handle or route to another entry family (distance or kneeling entries).

## 7. Opponent Reactions → Responses
- **Immediate backstep:** follow foot; maintain distance governance; re-attach with true handle before re-attacking.
- **Pull-away/disengage:** clamp with true handle; if lost, convert to distance systems.
- **Grip strip / handfight:** you must maintain redundancy (hook + handle); if handle is compromised, replace rather than chase.
- **Pressure collapse:** frames/angle first; re-attach only after hip line is safe.
- **Stance change (square/stagger flip):** use attachments to force a base event, then migrate inside (Part 2).

## 8. Failure Signatures → Fix
- **“My DLR hook keeps slipping off.”** → hook-only; add true handle immediately.
- **“He backsteps every time.”** → anti-backstep gate violated; track foot + distance governor + handle upgrade.
- **“He just walks away.”** → anti-pull-away failure; clamp earlier or route to distance connectors when contact breaks.
- **“I get flattened while attaching.”** → hip line safety ignored; frames first.

## 9. Trigger Recognition Checklist
Proceed to off-balance/migration when 2+ cues are true:
- [ ] hook is secure and not being peeled
- [ ] a true handle is established (see Section 10)
- [ ] opponent’s posture/base shows a base event is available (post/step/widen)
- [ ] you have distance governance (can track backstep)

## 10. True Handle Checklist (no-gi)
Use this as your “attachment quality” audit. Prefer higher items.

**A-tier (highest reliability)**
- [ ] **Two-on-one on a leg segment** (ankle/shin/calf) with wrist depth, not fingertips
- [ ] **Underhook/overhook connection** that prevents free hip rotation (when available)
- [ ] **Clamp + foot placement** that pins the leg line to your center (leg can’t step-clear in one motion)

**B-tier (situational)**
- [ ] **Wrist/arm control** that meaningfully blocks posting or stripping (must be paired with hook)
- [ ] **Head/shoulder line control** (only if it directly forces base events and doesn’t expose hip line)

**C-tier (temporary)**
- [ ] **Hook-only** (allowed only as a momentary bridge while upgrading)

Rule: if you cannot maintain at least **hook + B-tier**, route away (do not force DLR).

## 13. Drills and Games

### 13.1 True-Handle Upgrade Drill (no hook-only offense)
- **Start:** you have a DLR hook only; opponent standing and mobile.
- **Defender wins:** upgrade to a true handle within 5s and hold attachment 3s.
- **Passer wins:** strip hook or disengage to two steps within 5s.
- **Rules:** no off-balancing attempts until true handle is achieved.
- **Reset:** 3s hold or strip/disengage.
- **Rounds:** 10 reps each, switch.

### 13.2 Backstep Denial Reps (track + govern distance)
- **Start:** DLR hook + any true handle; opponent’s only defense is immediate backstep.
- **Defender wins:** maintain contact through backstep and keep attachment 5s.
- **Passer wins:** complete backstep escape/disengage within 5s.
- **Rules:** if defender freezes (fails to track stepping foot), rep loss.
- **Reset:** 5s hold or escape.
- **Rounds:** 8 reps each, switch.

### 13.3 Pull-Away to Distance Route (planned downgrade)
- **Start:** DLR attachment; opponent’s only defense is pull-away/disengage.
- **Defender wins:** either (a) clamp and prevent disengage for 3s, or (b) if contact breaks, convert to shin-to-shin or 2-on-1 ankle within 5s.
- **Passer wins:** disengage to two steps with no contact and no immediate recapture.
- **Rules:** defender must choose clamp vs route immediately (no chasing hands).
- **Reset:** 3s hold, successful conversion, or escape.
- **Rounds:** 8 reps each, switch.
