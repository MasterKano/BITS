# Entries — Distance: Partial Inversion Distance Breaker

## 1. Purpose
A distance-entry method that uses partial inversion to attach from outside range and prevent the opponent from freely hovering and circling. The inversion is a connector, not a commitment to spin under a stable base.

## 2. When to Use
Use partial inversion distance breakers when:
- opponent is standing at range and clearing your feet before you can attach
- you need a longer “reach” without hand-chasing
- you must connect to the legs from a position where standard seated attachment is failing

## 3. Objectives
- create attachment to leg lines from distance
- reinsert barriers and restore orientation
- force a predictable reaction (step, post, widen, retreat) that you can follow

## 4. Constraints (validity)
- inversion does not override the hands-down gate:
  - do not rotate/spin under a stable posture as an entry commitment
- inversion must end in attachment + re-centering, not in a scramble
- protect shoulder line; do not invert into pin exposure

## 5. Failure Signatures
- “I invert and he just steps away” → inversion produced no handle; attachment thin
- “I invert and get smashed” → you rotated under stable base / shoulder line compromised
- “I invert but can’t re-square” → inversion did not produce re-centering; you stayed at flank

## 6. Integration
- Pairs with: 2-on-1 ankle and shin-to-shin as follow-up connectors after contact is made.
- Supports: constant threat strategy by enabling first attachment at range.
