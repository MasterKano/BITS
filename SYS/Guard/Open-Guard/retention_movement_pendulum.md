# Retention Movement — Pendulum

## 1. Purpose
A swing-based hip and leg movement used to recover alignment, rebuild barriers, and re-center the opponent. Pendulum is used to regain facing when the opponent is circling or when your legs have been redirected.

## 2. When to Use
Use pendulum when:
- your hips need to travel to match opponent lateral movement
- you must swing legs to reinsert barriers and recover orientation
- you are transitioning out of a compromised angle toward a square guard

Primarily used before deep penetration; escalates with frames if hip line is threatened.

## 3. Objectives
- swing hips/legs to recover facing (orientation rule)
- restore barriers (feet between bodies)
- re-center opponent in front (center line control)

## 4. Constraints
- pendulum must produce re-centering; swinging without re-centering is wasted motion
- avoid hand chasing; hips/legs do the alignment work
- if hip line is threatened, frames become structural and pendulum becomes part of the defensive cycle

## 5. Failure Signatures
- legs swing but opponent keeps angle → re-centering failure
- pendulum performed too late → opponent already at hip line; frames required
- swinging creates exposure to compression → level advanced while barriers absent

## 6. Integration
- Demarcation: toe-line warning often precedes pendulum needs.
- Six elements: denies **angle** and delays **penetration** by restoring barriers.
- Pairs well with crossovers and foot pummeling (restore contact after swing).
