# Defenses — Leg Closure (Fill Space / Shut the Door)

## 1. Purpose
“Leg closure” is a defensive/passing pattern where the opponent **fills the leg space** so you cannot insert hooks, wedges, or inside position. They:
- close gaps at ankle/knee/hip lines,
- deny your leg pummels and shin insertion,
- and force you into a low-mobility guard where they can progress pressure.

This page defines how to:
- identify leg-closure early,
- reopen the space (by angle + barrier re-insertion),
- and route to the correct entry family.

## 2. Inputs / Preconditions
- You are in Open Guard and attempting to insert connection (shin-to-shin, hooks, 2-on-1 ankle, etc.).
- Opponent is “shutting the door” by:
  - clamping knees tight,
  - filling ankle space,
  - pinning your legs together,
  - stapling your leg to the mat,
  - or keeping elbows/knees tight to deny insertion.

## 3. Outputs / Success Criteria
- You re-open a usable lane for insertion within **1–2 movements**.
- You re-establish an active barrier (frame + leg position) without being flattened.
- You route to an entry that matches the available space (do not force the wrong connector).

## 4. Core Model / Engine
**READ CLOSURE → PROTECT HIP LINE → CREATE SPACE (ANGLE/FRAME) → INSERT BARRIER → RE-ATTACH (ENTRY)**

Principle: if they fill space, you must **create space** before inserting.

## 5. Gates (override rules)

### 5.1 Hip Line Gate (primary safety)
If closure is paired with forward drive, you are in a pressure lane.
- **Default action:** frame high/low → turn-to-side → shrimp/re-square → rebuild barriers.

### 5.2 Space-Creation Gate
No insertion without space.
- **Default action:** angle change + frames to open a lane, then insert.

### 5.3 Don’t-Force-Connector Gate
Choosing the wrong connector under closure wastes time and gets you pinned.
- **Default action:** pick the connector that matches current gap:
  - ankle line open → 2-on-1 ankle,
  - mid-range gap → shin-to-shin,
  - no gap → retention movement first (scoot/shrimp/pendulum).

### 5.4 Center-Line Gate
If your center line is lost while you try to “make space,” they step around.
- **Default action:** recover center line concurrently (barrier always points between you and them).

## 6. Top-6 Situations Playbook
1) **Opponent closes knees and denies shin insertion**  
   → **Default:** angle out (scoot/shrimp) + frame; then insert barrier.

2) **Opponent fills ankle space (no ankle access)**  
   → **Default:** create space first; do not chase ankle line.

3) **Opponent staples one leg and clamps the other**  
   → **Default:** recover mobility via movement vocabulary; re-square; then re-attach.

4) **Opponent leg-closure + forward drive**  
   → **Default:** treat as pressure; framing layer then reopen.

5) **Small ankle gap appears during closure**  
   → **Default:** route to 2-on-1 ankle capture immediately (take what’s available).

6) **Repeated closures with no progress**  
   → **Default:** change geometry (pendulum / scissor-sit / inversion as needed) rather than continuing to “fish.”

## 7. Opponent Reactions → Responses
- **Clamp tight:** angle + frame to open; then insert.
- **Staple leg:** remove staple via hip escape; recover center line; reinsert.
- **Step-around threat:** maintain barrier orientation; re-square before re-attaching.
- **Drive forward:** framing layer + turn-to-side; cycle break first.
- **Disengage:** anti-flee logic; keep anchor if possible.

## 8. Failure Signatures → Fix
- **“I kept fishing and got pinned.”** → space-creation gate violated; you inserted with no lane.
- **“I forced shin-to-shin and got stapled.”** → wrong connector; choose based on actual gap.
- **“He stepped around while I framed.”** → center line lost; barrier must stay between.
- **“I opened space but didn’t insert.”** → sequence failure; create space → insert immediately.

## 9. Key Cues
### 9.1 Recognition
- knees tight + elbows tight; no lane for shin/hook,
- ankle space filled; your hands/feet can’t access cleanly,
- your legs are being gathered together (closed and controlled).

### 9.2 Success landmarks
- you open a visible lane (ankle or mid-range) and insert immediately,
- your hip line never gets flattened,
- you return to an active guard state.

## 10. Quality / Capture Checklist
- [ ] hip line protected first
- [ ] space created before insertion
- [ ] connector chosen based on actual gap (ankle/mid/none)
- [ ] center line maintained throughout
- [ ] insertion happens immediately after lane opens

## 13. Drills and Games

### 13.1 Create-Space → Insert (no fishing)
- **Start:** passer clamps/closure posture; defender in open guard.
- **Defender wins:** create a lane and insert a functional barrier within 5s.
- **Passer wins:** keep closure intact for 5s or flatten for 3s.
- **Rules:** insertion only after visible lane; “fishing” with no lane = rep loss.
- **Reset:** successful insertion or timer/flatten.
- **Rounds:** 10 reps each, switch.

### 13.2 Connector Selection Under Closure
- **Start:** passer varies closure (sometimes ankle gap, sometimes mid-range, sometimes none).
- **Defender wins:** choose correct connector within 5s (2-on-1 ankle vs shin-to-shin vs retention movement) and stabilize 3s.
- **Passer wins:** pin/flatten for 3s or deny all attachments for 5s.
- **Rules:** wrong connector choice = rep loss.
- **Reset:** 3s stabilize, 3s pin, or timer.
- **Rounds:** 12 reps each, switch.
