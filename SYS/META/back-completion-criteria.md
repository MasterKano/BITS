# Back completion criteria

## Purpose

Define the minimum structural criteria to treat a backtake as **completed** (stable enough to finish) rather than “almost on the back.”

## Core idea

- The back is not “taken” when you are behind them.
- The back is taken when you have:
  - upper-back connection,
  - centre-line alignment,
  - and hip/knee positioning that prevents them from re-facing you.

## ASCII completion checklist

~~~text
[Behind them] -> {UPPER-BACK CONNECTION?} -> {CENTRE-LINE ALIGNED?} -> {HIPS INSIDE KNEES?} -> (hooks/body triangle)
        |                 |                        |                         |
       no                no                       no                        no
        |                 |                        |                         |
 rebuild connection   re-align to spine        re-square hips            clamp + reinsert
~~~

## Completion criteria (must-have)

### 1) Upper-back connection (connection before hooks)
- Chest-to-upper-back contact is established (not arm-reaching from distance).
- Head line is controlled enough that they cannot freely rotate to face you.

### 2) Centre-line alignment
- Your chest is aligned to their spine line (not hanging off to one side).
- Your “behind” position does not allow them to turn into you without first breaking your connection.

### 3) Hips-inside-knees principle (positional lock)
- Your hips are positioned so their knees cannot point back into you to re-guard/re-square.
- You are not “outside their hip line” in a way that makes them able to rotate and sit through.

### 4) Hook insertion is downstream
- Hooks/body triangle are applied **after** criteria 1–3 are satisfied.
- If you insert hooks without criteria 1–3, you are trading control for a scramble.

## Decision rules (IF/THEN)

- IF you do not have upper-back connection -> THEN do not chase hooks; rebuild connection first.
- IF you have connection but they can rotate -> THEN you are misaligned; re-centre to the spine line.
- IF you are behind them but their knees can point back into you -> THEN correct hips-inside-knees before advancing.
- IF head-and-arm / head-line control is slipping -> THEN tighten clamp (shrink circle) before hook pursuit.
- IF you have one hook but connection degrades -> THEN pause hook hunting and rebuild the connection/alignment.
- IF they turn toward to face you -> THEN accept it as the pin/pass branch (do not insist on back if the criteria cannot be met).

## Failure signatures + fixes

- Failure: “I’m behind them” but they re-face immediately.
  - Fix: missing upper-back connection and/or alignment; rebuild chest-to-back and re-centre.
- Failure: you chase hooks and lose them in transition.
  - Fix: connection-first; hooks come after criteria are met.
- Failure: you hang off one side and they sit-through.
  - Fix: re-centre to the spine line; correct hip position before advancing.
- Failure: head walks out of your control.
  - Fix: tighten head-line control (shrink circle / chin positioning) before continuing.

## Completion checks

- You can hold them in place for a beat without scrambling while you choose:
  - second hook, body triangle, or finishing control.
- They cannot:
  - turn their head and shoulders to face you,
  - sit-through to re-guard,
  - or rotate out without first breaking your connection.
