# System: Backside 50/50 (High-Offence Asymmetry Upgrade)

## 1. Purpose
- Convert 50/50-type exchanges into a more asymmetrical, attacker-favoured configuration with improved heel exposure and constrained defender rotations.
- Act as a primary upgrade hub after following rolls from Cross/Inside Ashi or standard 50/50.
- Provide a stable platform for decisive finishing and structured follow-ups when standard entanglements become symmetric or unstable.
- Encode the core 50/50 resolution logic: separation + inside-knee structure + timing (“attack until you’re defending”) determines advantage vs danger.

## 2. Key Positions
- Backside 50/50 base
  - Attacker orientation rotates “behind” the defender’s knee/hip line, improving knee-line stacking and reducing defender’s ability to unwind freely.
- Backside entry off roll-following
  - Common arrival after defender rolls out of Cross/Inside or standard 50/50; the goal is to arrive with wedge + knee-line, not just “stay attached.”
- Backside with inside-knee advantage (safety gate)
  - Inside-knee structure limits defender’s access to your upper body and reduces their ability to separate your legs while you finish.
- Backside with cross-waist control (asymmetry amplifier)
  - Cross-waist variants deny upper-body access, narrow defender’s defensive options, and stabilise breaking alignment once achieved.
- Backside ↔ Far-Hip transitions (safety shell)
  - Used when defender’s counters increase exposure to your own feet or when separation battles are being lost; Far-Hip becomes the stabilising “safe lock” that still attacks.
- Backside → finish / sweep-to-top
  - Platform for submissions and for sweep/top outcomes when defender’s reactions create clean top conversion.

## 3. Key Cues
- Land in a hub, not in a scramble
  - On roll-follow arrival: if you can’t name wedge + knee-line + inside-knee status, you didn’t stick the landing.
- Knee-line and hip-line stacking (structure before break)
  - Clamp, wedge, hips close; do not rush breaking mechanics if knee-line is drifting.
- Attack until you’re defending (timing rule)
  - If they begin separating your legs or gaining dominant hand access: defence now (reconnect / rebuild inside-knee / re-wedge), offence later.
- Break symmetry immediately (preferred resolution route)
  - In any 50/50-like exchange, bias to Backside to remove mirrored danger and reduce their rotational freedom.
- Heel exposure through constrained rotation (alignment > grip)
  - Create heel visibility by constraint; do not “pull harder” while structure degrades.
- Toe-hold ↔ separation as an entry-enabler (when access is blocked)
  - Use toe-hold threat to force opening, then re-enter Backside separation/finishing sequences.
- Follow-the-roll mechanics (weight + hips forward)
  - Bring hips forward so they carry your weight through rotation; re-establish wedge on the far side, then unwind into alignment.
- Far-hip capture to prevent “running away”
  - If they create distance during the roll-follow or Backside settling: control far hip to force re-entanglement into your structure.
- Protect your own feet while attacking
  - Backside is safer than generic 50/50, not invulnerable; if your heel line becomes accessible, prioritise re-wedging / inside-knee or resolve to Far-Hip.

## 5. Decision Rules
- Entry and stabilisation
  - If you arrive off a roll without a clear wedge: stabilise first (hips close + knee-line clamp + inside-knee), then restart offence.
  - If they are climbing to upper body: rebuild inside-knee and deny head/elbow access before trying to finish.
  - If they lock legs to deny separation: force opening (toe-hold threat) or shift to two-leg control logic (if available), then return to Backside finishing.
- Finish vs transition selection
  - Finish when: knee-line stable, your heel protected, their rotation constrained, and alignment is “locked” (not improvised).
  - Transition to Far-Hip when: they are separating your legs, they are winning hand access faster than you are building break pressure, or your heel is exposed.
  - Sweep/top when: their defence creates predictable top entry and you can stabilise without giving leg re-entries.

## 6. Drills & Games
- Follow-the-roll → Backside landing drill
  - Start in Cross/Inside or standard 50/50; defender performs a controlled roll; attacker must follow and stick the landing in Backside with a named wedge.
- Backside stabilisation reps (no finish)
  - Hold Backside with clean knee-line + inside-knee for time before any breaking mechanics.
- Backside separation game (50/50 constraint)
  - Defender scores by separating attacker’s legs; attacker scores by preventing separation and maintaining Backside asymmetry.
- Toe-hold ↔ separation dilemma drill (positional tool)
  - Start in 50/50 where second-leg access is blocked; use toe-hold threat to force opening, then re-enter Backside stabilisation.
- Backside decision rounds
  - Start in Backside; attacker must choose one: finish / transition to Far-Hip / sweep-top and stabilise.
- Backside vs re-pummel game
  - Defender goal: pummel back to symmetry; attacker goal: keep Backside alignment + inside-knee, deny separation.

## 7. Common Mistakes
- Following the roll but failing to settle, allowing defender to re-pummel into standard 50/50 or escape knee-line.
- Over-focusing on hands/grips while losing body alignment and wedges.
- Treating Backside as “automatic finish” and rushing breaking mechanics before structure is stable.
- Allowing upper-body access (head/elbow/2-on-1) and continuing offence while they win separation.
- Ignoring toe-hold as a separation tool when access is blocked; repeatedly reaching for the second leg without changing configuration.
- Ignoring sweep/top outcomes and tunnelling on submissions when defender is structurally safe.
