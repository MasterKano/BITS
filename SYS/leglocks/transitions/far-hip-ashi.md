# System: Far-Hip Ashi (Safety Shell + Asymmetry Rebuild)

## 1. Purpose
- Provide a safer asymmetrical leg-entanglement structure that prioritises hiding your own feet while maintaining credible offence.
- Act as a primary exit/upgrade from symmetric danger (especially 50/50 and mutual ashi) back into asymmetry.
- Stabilise control during scrambles/roll-following when standard hubs (Outside/Cross/50/50) become unsafe or too mirrored.
- Encode the resolver principle: when mutual exposure exists, resolve to a structure that denies hand access and reduces heel exposure before escalating finishing mechanics.

## 2. Key Positions
- Far-Hip Ashi base (safety shell)
  - Far-hip connection is the anchor that limits defender hip rotation, prevents them “running away,” and reduces access to your heel line during exchanges.
- Far-Hip off 50/50 separation
  - Conversion route after legs open or after you stabilise inside-knee; used to break mirrored exchange and re-establish foot safety.
- Far-Hip off mutual ashi (shootout resolver)
  - Preferred “safe lock” when you end up in mutual outside-heel-hook exposure; used to remove the duel and create a controlled path to offence.
- Far-Hip as roll-follow landing
  - Arrival position when defender rolls/spins hard and you choose safety + control over chasing a specific finish; used to re-load wedges and prevent fast counters.
- Far-Hip ↔ Backside 50/50
  - Two-way linkage:
    - Far-Hip for safety and re-asymmetrisation,
    - Backside for high-offence once exposure is clean and your heel safety is maintained.
- Far-Hip → Outside Ashi transfer (common endgame)
  - When defender rolls through, bias to follow and transfer to Outside Ashi rather than holding mirrored shapes.

## 3. Key Cues
- Heel safety first
  - Build Far-Hip when you sense mirrored danger; do not initiate big attacks if your own heel is accessible.
- Control the hip to control the leg
  - The far-hip connection is the stabiliser; if you lose hip control, defender regains turning/spinning options and can re-enter symmetry.
- “They can’t get to my hands” success condition
  - Far-Hip works when your legs block their upper-body access; if they are climbing to head/elbow/2-on-1 grips, the shell is failing.
- Use Far-Hip to reset the exchange (without stalling)
  - Structural reset that buys time to re-pummel, re-align, and choose a higher-offence hub; not a place to pause indefinitely.
- Attack while insulated
  - Offence should occur without sacrificing the safety shell; if you must open to attack, do it knowingly with a planned transition (Backside / Outside / re-entry).
- Far-hip capture stops “running away”
  - If defender creates distance during rolls/transitions, far-hip control is the anti-exit handle that forces them to carry your connection.
- Go-last compatibility (mutual ashi)
  - Default “go-last” resolver: let opponent expose first, then resolve into Far-Hip before committing to major exposure/finishing.
- Transition rule (global)
  - If you can’t clearly define: (1) wedge, (2) knee-line status, (3) heel safety, default to Far-Hip rather than forcing offence from symmetry.

## 5. Decision Rules
- When to resolve to Far-Hip (default triggers)
  - IF symmetry/mutual exposure is forming, OR your heel line feels available, THEN resolve to Far-Hip before any major finishing attempts.
  - IF you cannot state wedge + knee-line + heel safety in real time, THEN resolve to Far-Hip (do not “guess” offence).
- When to leave Far-Hip (upgrade selection)
  - IF the shell is intact and their rotation is constrained, THEN upgrade to:
    - Backside 50/50 when you can amplify asymmetry and keep your heel protected.
    - Outside Ashi when hip-control lanes are available and you can re-enter a primary finishing hub safely.
  - IF separation to top is clean and stable, THEN come up and stabilise (do not fall back into mirrored legs).
- When to reset instead of upgrading
  - IF they gain upper-body access (head/elbow/2-on-1), OR you lose far-hip control, THEN tighten shell or exit/re-enter rather than forcing an upgrade.

## 6. Drills & Games
- “Resolve on cue” drill (symmetry → Far-Hip)
  - Start in 50/50 or mutual ashi; coach calls “heel threat”; attacker must resolve to Far-Hip and freeze with shell intact.
- Roll-follow safety landing
  - Start in Outside/Cross; defender rolls; attacker chooses Far-Hip landing (not finish) and must keep heel protected for 3–5 seconds.
- Far-Hip → upgrade reps
  - From intact Far-Hip, perform clean upgrade to Backside OR Outside (no finish allowed); score for arriving with named gate intact.
- Go-last rounds (mutual exposure)
  - Both parties in mutual exposure; attacker wins by resolving to Far-Hip first, then upgrading to a named hub without giving heel access.

## 7. Common Mistakes
- Treating Far-Hip as “stalling” and refusing to upgrade (missing the purpose: reset → rebuild → leave).
- Losing far-hip control and allowing “run away” distance while you chase the foot.
- Allowing upper-body climb (head/elbow/2-on-1) and continuing offence instead of re-sealing the shell.
- Upgrading to Backside/Outside while your heel is still exposed (skipping the shell gate).
