# System: Far-Hip Ashi (Safety Shell + Asymmetry Rebuild)

## 1. Purpose
- Provide a safer asymmetrical leg-entanglement structure that prioritises hiding your own feet while maintaining credible offence.
- Act as a primary exit/upgrade from symmetric danger (especially 50/50 and mutual ashi) back into asymmetry.
- Stabilise control during scrambles/roll-following when standard hubs (Outside/Cross/50/50) become unsafe or too mirrored.
- Encode the resolver principle: when mutual exposure exists, resolve to a structure that denies hand access and reduces heel exposure before escalating finishing mechanics.

## 2. Key Positions (Operating Variants)
- Far-Hip base (safety shell)
  - Far-hip connection is the anchor that limits defender hip rotation, prevents “running away,” and reduces access to your heel line.
- Far-Hip off 50/50 separation
  - Conversion route after legs open or after inside-knee stabilisation; breaks mirrored exchange and re-establishes foot safety.
- Far-Hip off mutual ashi (shootout resolver)
  - Preferred “safe lock” when mutual outside-HH exposure exists; removes the duel and creates a controlled path to offence.
- Far-Hip as roll-follow landing
  - Arrival shape when defender rolls/spins hard and you choose safety + control over chasing a specific finish; used to re-load wedges and prevent fast counters.
- Far-Hip ↔ Backside 50/50 linkage
  - Far-Hip for safety and re-asymmetrisation; Backside for high-offence once exposure is clean and heel safety is maintained.
- Far-Hip → Outside transfer (common endgame)
  - When defender rolls through, bias to follow and transfer to Outside rather than holding mirrored shapes.

## 3. Key Cues (What “Good” Feels Like)
- Heel safety first
  - Build Far-Hip when mirrored danger appears; do not initiate major attacks if your heel is accessible.
- Control the hip to control the leg
  - Far-hip connection is the stabiliser; if hip control is lost, defender regains turning/spinning options and can re-enter symmetry.
- “They can’t get to my hands” success condition
  - Far-Hip works when your legs block upper-body access; if they climb to head/elbow/2-on-1 grips, the shell is failing.
- Reset without stalling
  - Far-Hip is a structural reset to buy time to re-align and choose a higher-offence hub; not a place to pause indefinitely.
- Attack while insulated
  - Offence should occur without sacrificing the shell; if you must open to attack, do it knowingly with a planned transition (Backside / Outside / re-entry).
- Far-hip capture stops “running away”
  - If defender tries to create distance during rolls/transitions, far-hip control forces them to carry your connection.
- “Go-last” compatibility (mutual ashi)
  - Default resolver: let opponent expose first, then resolve into Far-Hip before committing to major exposure/finishing.
- Global fallback rule
  - If you cannot clearly define: (1) wedge, (2) knee-line status, (3) heel safety, default to Far-Hip rather than forcing offence from symmetry.

## 5. Decision Rules
### 5.1 Resolve-to-Far-Hip triggers (default)
- IF symmetry/mutual exposure is forming OR your heel line feels available, THEN resolve to Far-Hip before major finishing attempts.
- IF you cannot state wedge + knee-line + heel safety in real time, THEN resolve to Far-Hip (do not “guess” offence).

### 5.2 Leaving Far-Hip (upgrade selection)
- IF shell is intact and their rotation is constrained, THEN upgrade to:
  - Backside 50/50 when you can amplify asymmetry and keep heel protected.
  - Outside when hip-control lanes are available and you can re-enter a primary finishing hub safely.
- IF separation-to-top is clean and stable, THEN come up and stabilise (do not fall back into mirrored legs).

### 5.3 Reset instead of upgrading
- IF they gain upper-body access (head/elbow/2-on-1) OR you lose far-hip control, THEN tighten shell or exit/re-enter rather than forcing an upgrade.

## 6. Drills & Games
- Resolve-on-cue drill (symmetry → Far-Hip)
  - Start in 50/50 or mutual; coach calls “heel threat”; attacker resolves to Far-Hip and freezes with shell intact.
- Roll-follow safety landing
  - Start in Outside/Cross; defender rolls; attacker lands Far-Hip (not finish-first) and holds heel protection 3–5 seconds.
- Far-Hip → upgrade reps
  - From intact Far-Hip, upgrade to Backside OR Outside (no finish allowed); score arrival only if gate remains intact.
- Go-last rounds (mutual exposure)
  - Both in mutual exposure; attacker wins by resolving to Far-Hip first, then upgrading to a named hub without giving heel access.

## 7. Common Mistakes
- Treating Far-Hip as stalling and refusing to upgrade (purpose is reset → rebuild → leave).
- Losing far-hip control and allowing “run away” distance while you chase the foot.
- Allowing upper-body climb (head/elbow/2-on-1) and continuing offence instead of re-sealing the shell.
- Upgrading to Backside/Outside while heel is still exposed (skipping the shell gate).
