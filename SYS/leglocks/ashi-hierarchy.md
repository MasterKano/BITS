# System: Ashi Hierarchy and Hub Network

## 1. Purpose
- Provide a single operating map for leg-lock navigation: entry → control hub → escalation hub → finish / sweep / top-out / exit.
- Maintain a consistent strategic bias toward asymmetry (clean attacking advantage) and away from prolonged symmetry (mutual danger / shootouts).
- Standardise decision-making around:
  - Knee-line control as the primary success condition.
  - Hip control + back-exposure denial as the primary safety condition.
  - Follow-the-leg + leg switching as the primary continuity skill under rolls/scrambles.
  - Dilemma-building (Achilles ↔ HH, toe hold ↔ separation, toe hold ↔ HH/KB) as the primary “force reaction” engine.
  - Top-out + cranial shift as the primary “win the exchange” conversion after separation.

## 2. Key Positions
- Neutral domains
  - Standing / wrestling exchanges
  - Guard / seated entries
  - Top vs guard and scrambles
- Entry hubs
  - Irimi Ashi (default structured entry; reliable knee-line capture + early dilemmas)
  - Situational direct falls into Outside / 50/50 / Cross off scrambles and failed passes
- Asymmetrical control hubs (preferred “homes”)
  - Outside Ashi (hip clamp + back-safety; heel exposure families; strong finishing platform)
  - Cross Ashi (high-offence inverted hub; two-leg bias; higher back-exposure tax if wedges drift)
  - Inside Ashi (cross-family variant oriented to outside HH alignment; follow-the-turn hub)
- Escalation / endpoint hubs (high-offence or high-safety)
  - Backside 50/50 (offensive resolution; constrained rotations; common endpoint after roll-follow)
  - Far-Hip Ashi (safety shell; protects your feet while keeping attack credibility; preferred resolver)
  - Top-out outcomes (pass/back-take ecosystems once you come up after separation)
- Symmetrical battlegrounds (transient)
  - 50/50 (initiative + inside-knee + separation; cross-waist variants can restore asymmetry)
  - Mutual Ashi (outside-HH shootout zone; heel-safety-first discipline; resolve to Far-Hip/Outside/Backside)
- Outcome nodes
  - Submissions: heel hooks (inside/outside), toe holds, straight ankle/Achilles locks, knee bars
  - Positional conversions: sweep to top, pass/camping, north-south, tight-waist/body-lock, back takes
  - Safety exits: exit to guard, exit to stand, reset and re-enter

## 3. Key Cues
- Global strategic rules
  - Prefer asymmetry: spend most time in Outside / Cross / Inside / Backside / Far-Hip / top-out outcomes.
  - Treat symmetry (50/50, mutual) as a problem to solve, not a place to live.
  - Win inside position early: inside position battles decide exposure quality and safety in most leg exchanges.
  - Keep two-leg awareness on: the far/secondary leg is a control problem, not background noise.

## 4. Default Hierarchy
- Entry → consolidation → endpoint
  - Irimi → Outside / Cross / Inside → Backside 50/50 / Far-Hip → finish OR top-out
- Symmetry resolution rule
  - If you enter directly into 50/50 or mutual, your first job is to solve it (inside-knee, heel safety, separation) and exit to an asymmetrical hub.

## 5. Role Clarity by Hub Family
- Irimi
  - Acquire knee-line + wedge; start early dilemmas (Achilles ↔ heel) to force posture/rotation decisions; upgrade when they commit to escapes.
- Outside
  - Hip clamp + deny back exposure; run heel exposure families; transition when they high-leg/turn/roll rather than “holding shape.”
- Cross/Inside
  - Power hubs; manage back exposure/inversion; integrate two-leg control (Double Trouble / Lace / Dracula) when they lock legs or rescue with the free leg.
- Backside / Far-Hip
  - Backside = offence amplifier; Far-Hip = safety-first re-asymmetrisation.
- Top-out outcomes
  - When separation + come-up happens: convert to pass/back takes via cranial shift (head/shoulder control replaces leg-only control).

## 6. Primary Control Conditions (Non-Negotiables)
- Knee-line first
  - If knee-line is not owned, prioritise re-wedging / hip-chasing / re-pummelling over finishing pressure.
- Hips close
  - Extended hips create slippage, weaken wedges, and delay roll-following.
- Far leg is never “free”
  - Track/manage the non-target leg to prevent stands, heists, re-pummels, and locked-legs defence.
- Inside vs outside heel-hook awareness
  - Identify which HH line is available and whether your current hub supports it safely; do not force a HH family from the wrong hub.
- Symmetry safety gate
  - In 50/50/mutual: heel safety + inside-knee + separation control precede offence.

## 7. Transition Triggers (IF/THEN)
- Opponent backs out / straightens
  - Chase hips + re-wedge; re-establish Irimi capture or upgrade to Outside if hip angle is available.
- Opponent drives forward pressure
  - Switch to Post/Butterfly-type connectors; preserve wedges; then return to a stable hub (Outside / Irimi).
- Opponent high-legs / steps over / back-steps
  - Re-capture with hip angle + cross-shin/scissor; deny back exposure; transition if the original hub loses safety.
- Opponent locks legs to deny separation
  - Convert to two-leg controls (Double Trouble / Lace / Dracula) to force leg-opening and restore lanes.
- Opponent rolls / spins
  - Follow the leg; land in Cross/Inside or Backside/Far-Hip rather than accepting random shapes.
  - Single-roll continuity rule: arrive in the next hub with wedge + knee-line, not just “stay attached.”
- Opponent heel slips / peels hands
  - Rebuild grips via tighter-circle progression (connect → climb), or change finishing posture (belly-up ↔ belly-down / roll-through).
- Opponent re-pummels inside position
  - Win inside position back; accept brief symmetry only as a transition; exit immediately.

## 8. Symmetry Management Rules
- 50/50
  - Initiative matters: if they begin separating your legs, defence now.
  - Inside-knee structure is a safety gate: build it before long hand-fights.
  - Separation is the decision point: toe hold ↔ separation is a primary tool when access is blocked.
  - Cross-waist style control (when achieved) restores asymmetry by denying upper-body access and narrowing defence.
- Mutual Ashi
  - Heel-safety-first discipline: don’t “race” exposure with unsafe feet.
  - Resolve via roll-through to Outside or exit to Far-Hip as soon as you can.

## 9. Top-Out Overlay (POD): “Rule of the Achilles” + Cranial Shift
- Rule of the Achilles (top-side decision hinge)
  - IF they have a **real Achilles bite/tether**, treat it as a live constraint: clear that first (don’t step into their control).
  - IF they **do not** have Achilles bite and are still **chasing exposure**, you can often step-through/come-up faster into pass/back-take lanes.
- Cranial shift (after you come up)
  - Winning legs/hips is not enough once you’re top: shift control to **head/shoulders** to prevent them re-guarding/re-leg-entering.
  - Practical meaning: once you “top-out,” immediately build top-control handles (north-south/camping/tight-waist/body-lock) before hunting finishes.

## 10. Grip and Finishing Integration (Hierarchy)
- Positional control first, then breaking mechanics, then grip sophistication.
- Grip “circle” rule
  - Climb to smaller circles when structure allows: ten-finger → palm-to-palm → wrist-to-wrist/tighter locks.
  - Do not sacrifice wedge/knee-line to chase a grip upgrade.

## 11. Reset Rules (Simplicity Under Chaos)
- If you cannot clearly name:
  - where the knee-line is,
  - what wedge is holding it,
  - and how your back/heel is protected,
  - then reset (re-centre, simplify to a reliable hub, or exit to guard/stand).

## 13. Drills & Games
- Hub map flow (low intensity)
  - Neutral → Irimi → Outside/Cross/Inside → Backside/Far-Hip → finish OR top-out → cranial shift → stabilise.
- Transition ladders (scripted)
  - Irimi → Outside (hip-control cue)
  - Outside → Cross/Inside (turn/high-leg/back-step cue)
  - Cross/Inside → Backside (roll cue)
  - 50/50 → Backside or Far-Hip (separation cue)
  - Mutual → Far-Hip (heel-risk cue)
- Top-out conversion reps (POD overlay)
  - Start in leg entanglement → achieve separation/top-out → immediate cranial shift → camping/north-south/tight-waist stabilization.

## 14. Common Mistakes
- Living in symmetry (50/50/mutual) without a fast plan to separate/extract to asymmetry.
- Chasing the foot instead of following the leg (arm-pulling while hips drift; no arrival in a new hub).
- Finishing while knee-line is lost (cranking a structurally freed leg).
- Ignoring the far leg (stands/heists/locked-legs defence/re-pummels).
- Top-out without cranial shift (you “win the legs” then immediately lose top control back to guard/leg re-entries).
