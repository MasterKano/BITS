# System: Ashi Hierarchy and Hub Network

## 1. Purpose
- Provide a single operating map for leg-lock positional navigation: entry → control hub → escalation hub → finish / sweep / exit.
- Maintain a consistent strategic bias toward asymmetry (clean attacking advantage) and away from prolonged symmetry (mutual danger / shootouts).
- Standardise decision-making around:
  - Knee-line control as the primary success condition.
  - Hip control + back-exposure denial as the primary safety condition.
  - Follow-the-leg + leg switching as the primary continuity skill under rolls/scrambles.
  - Dilemma-building (Achilles ↔ heel hook, toe hold ↔ separation, toe hold ↔ heel hook/knee bar) as the primary “force reaction” engine.

## 2. Key Positions
- Neutral domains
  - Standing / wrestling exchanges
  - Guard / seated entries
  - Top vs guard and scrambles
- Entry hubs
  - Irimi Ashi (default structured entry; reliable for knee-line capture + early dilemmas)
  - Situational direct falls into Outside / 50/50 / Cross off scrambles and failed passes
- Asymmetrical control hubs (preferred “homes”)
  - Outside Ashi (hip control + back-exposure denial; heel exposure families; strong finishing platform)
  - Cross Ashi (high-offence inverted hub; integrates two-leg control; higher back-exposure risk if wedge discipline is loose)
  - Inside Ashi (cross-like structure oriented to outside-heel exposure; used to deny certain escapes and funnel to stronger endpoints)
- Escalation / endpoint hubs (high-offence or high-safety)
  - Backside 50/50 (offensive resolution of entanglement; strong heel exposure; limits defensive rotations; frequent endpoint after roll-following)
  - Far-Hip Ashi (safety shell that still attacks; protects your feet while keeping wedge/knee-line; preferred exit from mutual danger)
  - Top outcomes (top-control conversions from leg exchanges when separation permits)
- Symmetrical battlegrounds (transient)
  - 50/50 (initiative + inside-knee; rapid separation logic; cross-waist variants can restore asymmetry)
  - Mutual Ashi (outside-HH shootout zone; go-last discipline; resolve to Far-Hip / Outside / Backside pathways)
- Outcome nodes
  - Submissions: heel hooks (inside/outside), toe holds, straight ankle/Achilles locks, knee bars
  - Positional conversions: sweep to top, back exposure/back take, top-control/passing hubs
  - Safety exits: exit to guard, exit to stand, reset and re-enter

## 3. Key Cues
- Global strategic rules
  - Prefer asymmetry: spend most time in Outside / Cross / Inside / Backside / Far-Hip / top outcomes.
  - Treat symmetry (50/50, mutual) as a problem to solve, not a place to live.
  - Win inside position early: inside position battles decide exposure quality and safety in most leg exchanges.
  - Keep two-leg awareness on: the far/secondary leg is a control problem, not background noise.

## 4. Default Hierarchy
- Entry → consolidation → endpoint
  - Irimi → Outside / Cross / Inside → Backside 50/50 / Far-Hip / top outcomes
- Symmetry resolution rule
  - If you enter directly into 50/50 or mutual, your first job is to solve it (inside-knee, heel safety, separation) and exit to an asymmetrical hub.

## 5. Role Clarity by Hub Family
- Irimi
  - Acquire knee-line + wedge; start early dilemmas (Achilles ↔ heel) to force posture/rotation decisions; upgrade when they commit to escape routes.
- Outside
  - Hip control + deny back exposure; build heel exposure families; transition when they high-leg/turn/roll rather than “holding shape.”
- Cross/Inside
  - Power hubs; manage back exposure and inversion; integrate two-leg control (Double Trouble / Leg Lace / Dracula) when their defence locks legs together.
- Backside / Far-Hip / top outcomes
  - Finish or convert to top; highest-value outcomes; prioritise maintaining your own heel safety while tightening finishing mechanics.

## 6. Primary Control Conditions (Non-Negotiables)
- Knee-line first
  - If knee-line is not owned, prioritise re-wedging / hip-chasing / re-pummelling over finishing pressure.
- Hips close
  - Shorten distance; extended hips create slippage, weaken wedges, and delay roll-following.
- Far leg is never “free”
  - Track and manage the non-target leg to prevent stands, heists, re-pummels, and locked-legs defence.
- Inside vs outside heel-hook awareness
  - Identify which heel-hook line is available (inside vs outside) and whether your current hub supports that line safely; do not force a HH family from the wrong hub.

## 7. Transition Triggers (IF/THEN)
- Opponent backs out / tries to straighten and pull
  - Chase hips + re-wedge; re-establish Irimi capture or upgrade to Outside if hip angle is available.
- Opponent drives forward pressure into you
  - Switch to structures that deny crush and preserve wedges (Post / Butterfly type responses), then upgrade back to a stable hub (Outside / Irimi).
- Opponent high-legs / steps over / back-steps
  - Re-capture with hip angle + cross-shin/scissoring logic; do not accept back exposure; transition if the original hub loses safety.
- Opponent locks legs to deny separation
  - Convert to two-leg control hubs (Double Trouble / Leg Lace / Dracula) to force leg-opening and restore finishing lanes.
- Opponent rolls / spins
  - Follow the leg; land in Cross/Inside or Backside/Far-Hip rather than accepting random shapes.
  - Single-roll continuity rule: you must arrive in the next hub with wedge + knee-line, not just “stay attached.”
- Opponent heel slips / peels your hands
  - Rebuild grips via tighter-circle progression (connect → climb), or change finishing posture (belly-up ↔ belly-down / roll-through) rather than chasing with weak hands.
- Opponent re-pummels inside position
  - Win inside position back; either re-enter asymmetry or accept brief symmetry then exit immediately.

## 8. Symmetry Management Rules
- 50/50
  - Attack until you’re defending: initiative matters; if they begin separating your legs, defence now.
  - Inside-knee structure is a safety gate: build it before long hand-fights; if you lose it, rebuild before committing to a break.
  - Separation is the decision point: toe hold ↔ separation dilemma is a primary tool to force legs open when access is blocked.
  - Cross-waist variants (when achieved) restore asymmetry by denying upper-body access and narrowing their defence options.
- Mutual Ashi
  - Go last: first-mover heel exposure is often the losing move; prioritise heel safety and counter-timing.
  - Resolve via roll-through to Outside or exit to Far-Hip as soon as you can; do not duel in mutual exposure longer than necessary.

## 9. Grip and Finishing Integration (Hierarchy)
- Positional control first, then breaking mechanics, then grip sophistication.
- Grip “circle” rule
  - Climb to smaller circles when structure allows: ten-finger (when blocked) → palm-to-palm → wrist-to-wrist / tighter locks.
  - Do not sacrifice wedge/knee-line to chase a grip upgrade; upgrade only when stability is secured.

## 10. Reset Rules (Simplicity Under Chaos)
- If you cannot clearly name:
  - where the knee-line is,
  - what wedge is holding it,
  - and how your back/heel is protected,
  - then reset (re-centre, simplify to a reliable hub, or exit to guard/stand).
- General escape direction awareness
  - Anticipate the common escape direction of the hub you’re in and pre-load your follow (hip chase + leg follow) so you arrive in the next hub with structure.

## 12. Drills & Games
- Hub map flow (low intensity)
  - Neutral → Irimi → Outside → Cross/Inside → Backside / Far-Hip → finish or sweep → top outcome → reset.
- Transition ladders (scripted)
  - Irimi → Outside (on “angle/hip-control” cue)
  - Outside → Cross/Inside (on “turn/high-leg/back-step” cue)
  - Cross/Inside → Backside 50/50 (on “roll” cue)
  - 50/50 → Backside or Far-Hip (on “separate” cue)
  - Mutual → Far-Hip exit (on “mutual exposure” cue)
- Dilemma games (constraint-based)
  - Achilles ↔ heel hook: score for forcing reaction (turn/roll/hand commitment), then converting to the correct hub.
  - Toe hold ↔ separation: score for opening legs (clean separation) rather than hunting finish without structural gain.
- Stress-test circuit (positional validation)
  - Start in each main hub; defender cycles: back-out → forward pressure → roll/spin → re-pummel; attacker maintains wedge/knee-line or transitions cleanly.
- Follow-the-roll rounds
  - Defender performs 1–3 controlled rolls; attacker lands in a pre-chosen hub (Backside/Far-Hip/Outside/Cross) with a named wedge.
- Symmetry resolution games
  - 50/50: score for (a) inside-knee win, (b) clean separation, (c) exit to asymmetry; penalise passive stalling.
  - Mutual ashi: first 2 seconds are heel safety only before offence; score for safe exit to Far-Hip/Outside and/or clean roll-through conversion.
- Finish-mechanics micro-drills (only when hub is stable)
  - Achilles lock finishing repetitions from stable wedge.
  - Finishing mechanics once wedge + posture are stable (do not treat this as a substitute for knee-line control).

## 13. Common Mistakes
- Living in symmetry (50/50 or mutual) without a fast plan to separate/extract to asymmetry.
- Losing inside position early, then trying to win late with hand strength.
- Chasing the foot instead of following the leg (arm-pulling while hips drift; no arrival in a new hub).
- Finishing while knee-line is lost (cranking a structurally freed leg).
- Hips too far / posture too extended, making wedges weak and transitions late.
- Ignoring the far leg, allowing stands, heists, locked-legs defence, or re-pummels that strip control.
- Back exposure in Cross/Inside transitions due to poor chest/hip orientation and loose wedges.
- Attacking first in mutual ashi without heel safety (go-last violation).
- Treating grip upgrades as primary instead of climbing grip circles after control is secured.
- Failure to reset when position is unidentifiable; forcing low-percentage finishes instead of rebuilding structure.
