# System: Outside Ashi Garami (Hip Control + Outside Heel Hook Hub)

## Purpose
- Establish a stable asymmetrical entanglement that prioritises hip control, back-exposure denial, and methodical heel exposure.
- Serve as a primary finishing platform for outside heel hooks while maintaining an Achilles–heel hook dilemma layer to force reactions and open exposure.
- Provide reliable transition pathways into Cross/Inside Ashi, Backside 50/50, and Far-Hip Ashi as the defender high-legs, turns, kneels, posts, or rolls.
- Encode the operating rule: hip control is the gate; if hips rotate freely, heel exposure and breaking mechanics are imaginary.

## Key Positions
- Outside Ashi baseline (supine)
  - Leg scissoring + knee pinch controlling the trapped leg while keeping attacker oriented to the defender’s hips (back-safety orientation).
- Hip-scissor clamp (rotation denial core)
  - Scissor angle that clamps thigh/hip rotation and prevents turn-behind; treated as the primary “do not lose this” control.
- Cross-shin control variant (hip-line brake)
  - Shin frame across waist/hip line to reinforce hip pinning, deny high-leg attempts, and stabilise heel exposure work.
- Heel exposure lanes (outside-ashi families)
  - Cross-shin heel exposure (open heel by braking the hip line without drifting away).
  - Post Ashi heel exposure (use a posting wedge concept to keep knee-line while opening heel).
  - Post-feet heel exposure (use their foot/feet posting posture to stabilise angle and limit rotation).
  - Belly-down exposure (follow-the-roll posture change when it improves alignment and leverage).
- Kneeling-opponent Outside Ashi
  - Defender drops to knees to turn out; attacker maintains hip pin + heel exposure while denying turn-behind and re-pummel.
- Counter-high-leg configurations
  - Re-capture structure using scissor + cross-shin; when heel access is blocked, use toe-hold threat (wrist-to-wrist style) as a separator tool to open the leg.
- Transitions
  - Outside → Cross/Inside Ashi when defender turns/high-legs/inverts and outside-heel access degrades.
  - Outside → Backside 50/50 when a roll/separation creates clean asymmetry amplification.
  - Outside → Far-Hip when your heel line is threatened or symmetry/mutual exposure is forming.

## Key Cues
- Hip control is the engine
  - If hips rotate freely, heel exposure fails; clamp hips first (scissor + cross-shin), then hunt heel.
- Back-exposure denial is non-negotiable
  - Maintain chest/hip orientation to their hips; if you feel them getting behind you, re-centre the clamp or transition immediately.
- Knee line before grip
  - If the knee is escaping past your hip line, stop finishing and re-wedge / re-angle / transition.
- Scissor + cross-shin work as a pair
  - Scissor clamps rotation; cross-shin brakes hip line and blocks high-leg; losing either increases back exposure and heel slips.
- High-leg response (scripted)
  - Treat high-leg as a trigger: re-capture knee-line with scissor/cross-shin; do not let them step over into safety.
  - If heel access is blocked by the high-leg configuration, use toe-hold threat to force opening, then return to outside-heel exposure.
- Follow the roll, don’t chase the foot
  - Rotate hips/torso with their hip line; preserve wedges; land in a named hub (Outside again, belly-down, Backside, or Far-Hip) rather than random leg shapes.
- Heel slip is a structure failure first
  - If the heel slips: hips close + rebuild scissor/cross-shin + correct hand position, then re-expose; do not reach while extended.
- Dilemma framing (Achilles ↔ heel)
  - Achilles pressure is tether/control to force reactions; when they react to relieve pressure or hide heel, convert to heel exposure and finish or transition.

Operational use:
- Run the CONTROL GATE before exposure: if you cannot name your clamp/brake and knee-line status, rebuild first.
- Treat each defence as a scripted branch: re-capture hip control first, then choose an exposure family.
- Toe-hold is positioned as a configuration tool: it forces opening/separation when heel access is blocked, then you return to heel exposure.
- Roll/spin is resolved by where you LAND: Outside (continue), Backside (amplify), Far-Hip (insulate).

## Decision Rules
- Control gate (before exposure)
  - If scissor + cross-shin are not controlling the hip line, rebuild them; do not hand-fight for heel exposure while hips are free.
  - If knee-line drifts, re-wedge immediately; do not continue exposure attempts while the knee escapes.
- High-leg branch
  - Re-capture first; if heel access is blocked, use toe-hold threat to open the configuration, then return to outside-heel exposure.
  - If re-capture fails and you’re losing back safety, transition to Cross/Inside rather than forcing.
- Turn/kneel branch
  - If they are kneeling but you own hip control, expose heel via kneeling-appropriate lane; if they are turning behind you, deny turn-behind first.
- Roll branch
  - If control stays intact, re-land in Outside and continue.
  - If separation cleanly creates Backside 50/50, upgrade immediately.
  - If mutual exposure forms or your heel is threatened, go to Far-Hip first.
- Finish vs transition
  - Finish only when: knee-line stable + hips constrained + your heel protected.
  - Transition when: heel threatened, knee-line drifting, or back-safety compromised during exposure.

## Drills & Games
- Outside-Ashi stress-test circuit
  - Defender cycles: back-out (knee-line escape), forward pressure, high-leg, controlled roll; attacker focuses on hip pin + knee-line retention before exposure.
- High-leg capture reps
  - Defender repeatedly high-legs; attacker’s only goal: re-trap knee-line with scissor/cross-shin and re-expose heel.
- Toe-hold as separator drill
  - Start with heel access blocked; attacker uses toe-hold threat to force opening, then returns to heel exposure (no rushing finishes).
- Belly-up ↔ belly-down conversion reps
  - From heel exposure, defender initiates a roll; attacker follows and must land belly-down with intact knee-line and hip control.
- Kneeling opponent scenario rounds
  - Start with defender on knees in Outside Ashi; attacker chooses: heel exposure finish OR sweep/transition without losing hip pin.
- Achilles-to-heel dilemma drill
  - Start with Achilles tether; defender gives one scripted reaction (turn/roll/pull knee); attacker responds with pre-defined heel exposure or transition.

## Common Mistakes
- Allowing hips to rotate (weak scissor/cross-shin), creating back exposure or losing heel line.
- Over-focusing on the heel while the knee line is sliding out.
- Letting the defender’s high-leg turn into a step-over escape because the cross-shin/scissor wasn’t rebuilt.
- Following rolls with arm strength instead of moving hips/torso with the defender’s rotation.
- Treating toe-hold as the goal rather than a separation tool that returns you to heel exposure.
- Staying in a deteriorated Outside Ashi instead of transitioning to Cross/Inside or Backside/Far-Hip when reactions demand it.
