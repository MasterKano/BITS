# System: Irimi Ashi Garami (Entry Hub)

## Purpose
- Establish the default first entanglement for a structured leg-entry: capture the knee line, stabilise hips, and create immediate threat.
- Function as a control hub (not a finish-only position): hold the leg through common escape directions, then upgrade to stronger hubs (Outside / Butterfly / Post / Cross-family / Backside) dictated by reactions.
- Build the first dilemma engine (most commonly Achilles pressure to force reactions that expose heel, open transitions, or force a roll you can follow).
- Provide a repeatable stress test and correction loop so Irimi remains reliable under extraction, forward pressure, posting, standing/heisting, and roll/turn counters.
- Encode the “lower→upper” conversion principle: leg attack/defence should create timing windows to access backtakes and passes (trilemma behaviour).

## Key Positions
- Irimi Ashi baseline structure
  - Primary leg captured with knees pinched + hips close enough that defender cannot simply walk the knee out.
  - Secondary leg has a job (deny far-leg stand/heist, manage hip line, prevent re-pummel lanes) rather than floating.
- Irimi with Achilles control (tether + dilemma starter)
  - Achilles pressure used to slow the defender, force posture/rotation decisions, and create the first Achilles ↔ heel hook dilemma when heel exposure becomes available.
- Irimi with elbow-position reinforcement (anti-forward pressure / stand-up frame)
  - Elbow positioning used to resist forward drive and maintain wedge integrity when defender tries to crush/stand into you.
  - “Inside elbow position” is also used as part of the stand-up build (prevents easy over-rotation / heel exposure during your rise).
- Irimi heist / stand-up build (defensive-to-offensive converter)
  - A structured rise: tuck foot → roll up over shin → head-rock momentum → come to knee without offering heel rotation → stand bent (shoulders forward) → only then separate legs / pass.
- Post Ashi link (forward pressure / posting responder)
  - Posting wedge structure used when defender drives forward or posts hard and you need an additional structural stop to preserve knee-line.
- Butterfly Ashi link (mobility and re-angle)
  - Used when defender drives forward, stands, or you need a cleaner re-angle without losing attachment; also used as a connector to other entanglements.
- Irimi → Outside upgrade (common high-percentage upgrade)
  - Standard progression once stability is secured and/or heel exposure appears; also a common outcome after roll-through sequences.
- Belly-down Achilles posture (situational finish posture)
  - Used when the defender’s reactions and alignment create a clean belly-down finishing opportunity; treated as a posture change after control, not a substitute for control.
- Opportunistic extraction window (Irimi not set correctly)
  - When the attacker’s Ashi structure is not properly set, forward pressure can create a moment to extract the leg and force inversion/transition; worst case you exit to neutral with no leg attack on you.

## Key Cues
- Knee line first
  - If the defender’s knee is not deeply controlled relative to your hip line, treat everything else as secondary; fix structure before finishing.
- Hips close, not extended
  - Pelvis chases their hip line; long-legged stretched shapes produce false confidence and increase heel slip and back-out escapes.
- Achilles as tether, not as hope
  - Use Achilles pressure to force reactions (turn, roll, posture change, hand commitment). If it is not forcing reaction, treat it as a control cue to re-angle or upgrade.
- Rule of the Achilles (upper-body grip gate)
  - If the opponent has a strong Achilles grip, backtake chances from Irimi are near-zero; choose repalm/extract/pass lines.
  - If there is no Achilles grip (or it is loose/absent at the moment you rise), backtake and upper-body conversion options enter the equation.
- Irimi wedge constraint (why backtakes differ vs Outside)
  - Irimi has a physical wedge between bodies; pressure-forward “scoot to the corner” is harder than in Outside.
  - Expect fewer direct “pressure-forward to back” options; instead use stand-up / turtle forcing / tight-waist passing connectors.
- Counter the basic extraction (back-out)
  - When they retract the knee: chase hips + reinforce knee pinch + re-wedge; do not pull the foot while hips drift away.
- Counter forward pressure with structure (not strength)
  - Forward drive is solved by elbow/leg structure and/or Post/Butterfly connectors; do not accept being flattened and then try to finish harder.
- Far leg awareness (escape engine)
  - The far leg is the engine for standing, posting, heisting, and re-pummelling; assign a control solution early.
- Heel slip is a structure failure first
  - If the heel slips during exposure/finish attempts: fix hips close + wedge + hand position, then re-lock; do not chase a slipping heel with extended posture.
- Follow the leg, don’t chase the foot
  - When they rotate/roll, move hips and torso with their hip line; preserve wedges and aim to land in a known hub (Outside / Cross-family / Backside) with knee-line still owned.
- Stand-up posture rule (anti-off-balance)
  - When standing, keep shoulders in front of hips and head down/forward; do not come upright until feet are separated and balance is established.
  - Do not present the second leg during the stand-up (wide base; deny easy knock-back/off-balance).
- Upgrade mindset
  - Irimi is often the entry, not the final home: if defender starts solving it (high-leg, posture, forward pressure, post knee), transition decisively to the correct connector or stronger hub.

## Decision Rules
- Entry validation (stress test gate)
  - Immediately test Irimi structure against: back-out extraction, forward pressure, initial turn/roll tendency, and re-pummel attempts.
  - If it fails a test: adjust wedges/hip angle/secondary leg assignment first; do not muscle a submission to compensate.
- Rule of the Achilles (attack selection gate)
  - If opponent has Achilles grip: default to repalm/extract/guard-pass lines; do not bias toward backtakes from Irimi.
  - If opponent does not have Achilles grip (or it breaks during your rise): backtake/passing conversions become viable; pursue upper-body connection timing.
- Achilles ↔ heel hook dilemma (reaction engine)
  - Use Achilles pressure to force the defender to choose between protecting heel exposure and escaping knee-line; when their defence commits, switch to the line that the defence opens.
  - If Achilles pressure is not creating commitment, use it as a tether to re-angle and upgrade rather than staying static.
- Stand-up build (defensive-to-offensive converter)
  - If Irimi is locked tight on you: hide foot (tuck toward butt) -> roll up over shin -> head-rock for momentum -> come to knee while controlling opponent knee line.
  - As you come to knee: do not allow opponent to over-rotate your heel; control the knee line direction so you are not “pointing their knees” into heel exposure.
  - Head placement during rise: bring head over/near opponent head line to reduce their ability to expose heel during your build.
  - Standing posture: shoulders stay in front of hips; hand can post to prevent knock-back; keep wide stance; do not present second leg.
  - Only when feet are separated and balance is stable: come to vertical posture and begin passing sequences.
- Forward pressure branch
  - If they drive forward and collapse posture: reinforce elbow structure; if still collapsing, switch to Post Ashi or Butterfly Ashi to preserve knee-line and re-angle.
  - If forward drive turns into standing/posting: prioritise controlling the far leg and transition to a connector that denies the stand.
- Heel slip branch (attacking)
  - If heel slips during exposure: first rebuild structure (hips close + knee-line + wedge), then re-lock with high-hands positioning; if they roll/turn, use roll-follow rather than chasing.
- Heel exposed vs you (defensive)
  - If heel becomes exposed: prefer heel slip when available; otherwise use half-roll/separation mechanics or stand-up build to exit and begin passing.
  - After knee touches down in the defensive rise: immediately sit back to opponent hips to prevent them switching to alternate attacks (do not hover in space).
- Roll / single-roll logic
  - Treat the defender’s roll as a predictable transition: follow the leg and arrive in the next hub with wedge and knee-line; do not fight the roll with arms.
- Upgrade selection (when to leave Irimi)
  - Upgrade to Outside when heel exposure is clean, forward pressure is managed, or roll-through presents Outside as the safest finishing platform.
  - Upgrade to Butterfly when mobility/re-angle is required and you need to keep attachment without losing knee-line.
  - Upgrade to Post Ashi when forward pressure/posting is threatening knee-line and you need a stronger wedge stop.

## Drills & Games
- Irimi build + four-direction stress test
  - Start in Irimi; defender cycles (light-to-moderate): back-out → forward pressure → turn/roll → re-pummel.
  - Attacker goal: keep knee-line and structure using minimal hand strength; upgrade on cue when structure is stable.
- Irimi freeze checks
  - Enter Irimi, freeze at checkpoints:
    - hips close / knee-line secure,
    - far leg assigned and controlled,
    - Achilles tether available (if used),
    - ready-to-upgrade angle (Outside/Butterfly/Post).
- Achilles gate rounds (rule of the Achilles)
  - Defender alternates: strong Achilles grip vs no Achilles grip.
  - Attacker must choose correctly:
    - Achilles present -> repalm/extract/pass bias
    - Achilles absent -> pursue upper-body conversion timing (pass/back)
- Stand-up safety ladder (from tight Irimi)
  - Script: tuck foot → roll up over shin → head-rock → knee rise (knee line control) → bent-over stand (shoulders forward) → separate feet → pass start.
- Achilles tether → reaction flow
  - Apply light Achilles control to force reaction; attacker must choose correct branch:
    - heel exposure appears → upgrade to Outside
    - defender turns/rolls → follow and land in Outside/Backside
    - defender posts/stands/forward drives → Post/Butterfly connector then upgrade.
- Heel-slip recovery drill (attacking)
  - Start with partial heel exposure; defender performs heel slip attempt; attacker must rebuild hips close + high-hands reset or roll-follow to re-secure exposure.
- Irimi → Outside on cue
  - Immediate clean upgrade without losing knee-line; repeat both sides.
- No-finish retention rounds
  - Rounds starting in Irimi where submissions are disallowed; score only for maintaining knee-line and achieving a clean upgrade to Outside/Backside.

## Common Mistakes
- Entering Irimi and immediately chasing the foot while the knee line is not owned.
- Letting the far leg float, then being surprised when defender posts/stands/re-pummels.
- Staying flat and extended (hips far), then losing control to back-out, heel slip, or forward pressure.
- Trying to win with grip strength instead of correcting leg wedges and hip angle.
- Treating Achilles pressure as the finish without using it to force reactions and upgrades.
- Ignoring the “Achilles grip gate” and attempting backtakes when opponent has strong Achilles control.
- Standing up with shoulders over hips (getting knocked backwards) or presenting the second leg (easy off-balance).
- Coming to the knee and allowing opponent to over-rotate heel exposure during your rise.
- Failing to commit to upgrades (hesitating between Irimi and Outside/Butterfly/Post until defender clears the knee).
- Fighting rolls with arms instead of following the leg into the next hub.
