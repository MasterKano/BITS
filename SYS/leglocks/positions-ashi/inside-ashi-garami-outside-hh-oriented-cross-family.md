# System: Inside Ashi Garami (Cross-Family Variant, Outside Heel Hook Orientation)

## 1. Purpose
- Provide a Cross-Ashi-adjacent control hub that preserves asymmetry while orienting finishing and control toward outside heel hook lines.
- Serve as a reliable transition destination when the defender corrects hip alignment during Cross-Ashi defence, allowing you to follow the turn without losing knee line.
- Maintain leg safety and back-safety while continuing to build heel exposure, dilemma threats, and structured exits into higher-offence hubs (Backside 50/50) and safety hubs (Far-Hip Ashi).

## 2. Key Positions
- Inside Ashi baseline
  - Structural cousin of Cross Ashi; leg configuration reoriented so the primary finishing line is outside-heel-hook aligned.
- Cross → Inside transition frame
  - The moment the defender rotates/turns to correct hips; you rethread/reorient to keep wedges and heel access.
- Inside Ashi + secondary-leg awareness
  - May be single-leg dominant at first; opportunities to convert into two-leg constraint (partial Double Trouble / lace-style binding) depending on defender posture.
- Inside Ashi → Backside 50/50 pathway
  - Roll-following transition when defender inverts/rolls; used to upgrade to stronger breaking alignment.
- Inside Ashi → Far-Hip Ashi safety shell
  - Used when exchange drifts toward symmetry or when your own foot exposure increases.

## 3. Key Cues
- Arrive because they turned
  - When the defender rotates to escape Cross, treat that rotation as the trigger to re-orient into Inside Ashi rather than fighting to hold Cross at all costs.
- Preserve knee line first, then expose heel
  - Inside Ashi still obeys the knee-line gate; if the knee is slipping, re-wedge/re-thread before hunting heel.
- Outside-heel-hook alignment
  - Your rotational line must match outside HH mechanics; deny the defender’s ability to freely turn knee/hip to unwind.
- Back-safety via chest-to-hip orientation
  - Keep chest and hips aimed toward the defender’s hip line; if you feel them behind you, re-centre or transition.
- Use dilemmas to force predictable rotation
  - Toe-hold and Achilles threats can create the rotation you need to expose the heel and lock the outside HH.

## 4. Control Gates (Quick Audit)
- Knee line: can they slide the knee out with one clean back-step/hip turn?
- Hip distance: are your hips close enough that wedges, not arm pull, are holding them?
- Back-safety: is your torso drifting away from their hip line to “save grips”?
- Your heel safety: is your heel line accessible as you re-thread/transition?
If any answer is yes: rebuild wedges and orientation before escalation.

## 5. Decision Rules (IF/THEN)
- IF the defender turns to correct Cross alignment, THEN transition to Inside Ashi immediately (do not “hold Cross” while wedges degrade).
- IF knee line starts drifting during the transition, THEN stop and re-wedge/re-thread before chasing heel exposure.
- IF you feel back exposure developing (torso drifting away from their hips), THEN re-centre chest-to-hip or transition to a safer hub (Far-Hip / Backside) instead of forcing grips.
- IF the exchange becomes roll-heavy and you can follow the leg cleanly, THEN upgrade to Backside 50/50 as the preferred offence amplifier.
- IF symmetry/mutual exposure begins forming or your heel becomes targetable, THEN resolve to Far-Hip first, then rebuild offence.

## 7. Drills & Games
- Cross → Inside on the turn (reaction drill)
  - Start in Cross Ashi; defender’s job is to turn to correct alignment; attacker must transition to Inside Ashi immediately without losing knee line.
- Inside Ashi heel-exposure reps (control-first)
  - Controlled rounds focused only on heel visibility and re-wedging; no finish allowed until exposure is stable for a set count (e.g., 3 seconds).
- Inside Ashi roll-follow to Backside
  - Defender performs 1–2 controlled rolls; attacker follows and lands in Backside 50/50 or returns to Inside with improved control.
- Inside Ashi safety-shell exits
  - From Inside Ashi, attacker practices exiting to Far-Hip Ashi when own foot becomes exposed or symmetry emerges.

## 8. Common Mistakes
- Staying in Cross Ashi too long while defender turns, causing loss of wedges and back exposure instead of transitioning cleanly.
- Chasing heel without knee-line stability, leading to almost-heel exposure that collapses under rotation.
- Letting exchange degrade into 50/50/mutual without an explicit plan (failure to choose a landing hub).
- Over-rotating torso away from hips to maintain grip, inviting back takes.
- Continuing offence while your heel becomes targetable (not resolving to Far-Hip/Backside early enough).
