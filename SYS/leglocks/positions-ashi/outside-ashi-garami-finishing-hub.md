# System: Outside Ashi Garami (Primary Asymmetrical Finishing Hub)

## Purpose
- Establish a stable asymmetrical leg entanglement that denies back exposure while creating reliable heel/toe/ankle attack pathways.
- Serve as the main home base after entry (commonly upgraded from Irimi) where you can stress-test control, expose the heel methodically, and finish without sacrificing structure.
- Provide a robust framework to handle common defensive reactions (high-leg, kneeling/turning, posts, rolls) while maintaining knee-line dominance.
- Encode the core outside-ashi strategy: hip control first (scissor + cross-shin), then heel exposure families, then breaking mechanics; if the hip is free, the finish is imaginary.

## Key Positions
- Outside Ashi baseline (belly-up control)
  - Asymmetrical orientation with leg configuration built to clamp hip rotation and trap the knee line while denying back exposure.
- Leg-scissor hip clamp (primary stabiliser)
  - Scissoring legs around the trapped thigh/hip line to restrict turning, prevent high-leg escalation, and keep your alignment safe.
- Cross-shin control (hip-line brake)
  - Shin placed across waist/hip line to redirect pelvic rotation, recapture high-leg attempts, and stabilise angles for heel exposure.
- Outside Ashi: heel exposure families (common lanes)
  - Belly-down heel exposure (roll-follow / posture change exposure)
  - Post Ashi heel exposure (posting wedge + knee-line retention while opening heel)
  - Post-feet heel exposure (their feet posture creates a stable heel line)
  - Cross-shin heel exposure (hip-line brake opens heel without losing back safety)
- Outside Ashi: kneeling opponent exposure
  - Exposure against a kneeling defender while maintaining hip clamp and preventing turn-behind.
- Outside Ashi → belly-down posture (situational finishing posture)
  - Rotational follow-through into belly-down variants when defender rolls or when angle improves breaking leverage; posture change after control, not a substitute for control.
- Outside Ashi → connectors and upgrades
  - Transition to Cross/Inside Ashi when defender turns/high-legs/inverts and the outside hub loses heel access.
  - Upgrade to Backside 50/50 when rolls/separation create a clean asymmetry amplifier.
  - Upgrade to Far-Hip when your heel line becomes threatened or symmetry is forming.

## Key Cues
- Back-exposure denial is non-negotiable
  - Maintain chest/hip orientation and scissor integrity; if you cannot keep safe alignment, re-centre or transition rather than forcing a finish.
- Knee line stays captured during every adjustment
  - Every heel hunt and grip change must preserve wedges; if knee-line starts to clear, prioritise re-wedging immediately.
- Hip control is the gate (scissor + cross-shin)
  - Scissor limits rotation; cross-shin controls the hip line and blocks high-leg. If either is missing, treat heel exposure as unsafe or premature.
- High-leg is a trigger, not a problem
  - High-leg cues: re-capture knee-line with scissor/cross-shin, then choose:
    - heel exposure track (if hip is constrained and heel is appearing),
    - toe-hold threat track (if heel access is blocked and you need leg opening/separation).
- Turning / kneeling reactions are exposure opportunities
  - When they turn to kneeling: keep hip clamp, deny turn-behind, then expose heel via the appropriate family (cross-shin/post variants) rather than forcing with arms.
- Achilles as tether / toe hold as separator / heel hook as escalation
  - Achilles pressure: control + reaction forcing (move them, slow them, trigger turns/rolls).
  - Toe hold: separation tool when leg configuration blocks your access lanes.
  - Heel hook: escalation once heel exposure is structurally constrained.
- Roll-following with structure (single-roll discipline)
  - When defender rolls: follow the leg and arrive in a known hub with wedge + knee-line; do not accept random shapes.
- Heel slip response is structural
  - If heel slips: fix hips close + scissor/cross-shin + hand position before re-locking; do not chase a slipping heel with extended posture.

Operational use:
- Run the CONTROL GATE before exposure attempts; if the gate fails, your “exposure” is a trap (you’re trading safety for hope).
- Treat high-leg and turn/kneel as scripted branches: re-wedge first, then pick an exposure family; do not “save” exposure with arms.
- Use toe-hold as a configuration tool when heel access is blocked; it exists to force opening/separation, not to replace control.
- Roll/spin is resolved by where you LAND: Outside (continue), Backside (amplify), Far-Hip (insulate). Choose safety if your heel line is in play.

## Decision Rules
- Control gate (before exposure)
  - If scissor + cross-shin are not stabilising the hip line, build them first; do not attempt long hand fights for heel exposure.
  - If knee-line is drifting, re-wedge immediately; do not continue exposure attempts while the knee escapes.
- Selecting the exposure family
  - If posture/rotation is constrained and heel is visible: choose the family that preserves hip clamp (cross-shin/post/belly-down), not a complete reconfiguration.
  - If heel access is blocked: use toe-hold threat to force opening, then return to heel exposure.
- High-leg branch
  - If re-capture is late: prioritise back safety and re-wedging; if alignment is compromised, transition to a safer connector rather than saving the finish.
- Roll branch
  - If roll maintains your hip clamp: re-land in Outside and continue.
  - If roll creates a clean Backside opportunity: upgrade immediately (asymmetry amplifier).
  - If roll threatens your heel line or forms mutual exposure: transition to Far-Hip first, then rebuild offence.
- Kneeling branch
  - If kneeling but you own hip control: use kneeling exposure family; if they are turning behind you, deny back exposure first.
- Finish vs transition
  - Finish when: knee-line stable + hip rotation constrained + your heel line protected.
  - Transition when: heel line becomes accessible, knee-line is drifting, or back safety is compromised during exposure attempts.

## Drills & Games
- Outside Ashi stress-test circuit
  - Start in Outside; defender cycles: high-leg → kneel/turn → post → controlled roll(s).
  - Attacker goal: maintain knee-line and back safety while re-establishing heel exposure each time.
- High-leg response ladder
  - Defender repeatedly high-legs; attacker must:
    - re-capture with scissor/cross-shin,
    - then choose: heel exposure track OR toe-hold separation track (light, technical).
- Exposure-family rounds (constraint)
  - 1–2 minute rounds where attacker is limited to one exposure family (cross-shin / post / belly-down) and must maintain hip clamp throughout.
- Belly-up ↔ belly-down transition reps
  - From stable Outside, rotate to belly-down posture and return, keeping knee-line and hip clamp intact throughout.
- Follow-the-roll from Outside
  - Defender performs 1–3 controlled rolls; attacker must stay attached and finish in a stable entanglement (Outside/Backside/Far-Hip as dictated by safety).
- Kneeling opponent scenario
  - Defender starts on knees; attacker in Outside must maintain hip control and either expose heel or convert to sweep/top while denying turn-behind.
- Toe-hold ↔ separation tool drill
  - Start in Outside where heel access is blocked; attacker must use toe-hold threat to open the leg configuration, then return to heel exposure.

## Common Mistakes
- Losing hip clamp while chasing heel, creating back exposure.
- Allowing defender to clear the knee line because scissor/cross-shin wedges were not maintained during transitions.
- Treating high-leg as panic instead of a scripted trigger for re-capture and toe-hold/heel-exposure options.
- Rolling with the foot (arm pull) instead of moving hips/torso with the defender’s rotation (disconnecting from knee line).
- Fighting a heel slip with reach instead of rebuilding hips close + scissor/cross-shin.
- Staying in Outside when defender’s reaction clearly calls for a cleaner transition to Cross/Inside or Backside/Far-Hip.
