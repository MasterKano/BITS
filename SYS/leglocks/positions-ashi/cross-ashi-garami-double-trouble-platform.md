# System: Cross Ashi Garami (Inverted Heel Hook + Double Trouble Platform)

## 1. Purpose
- Provide a high-offence asymmetrical entanglement optimised for inside (inverted) heel hook mechanics, strong secondary-leg control, and roll-follow continuity.
- Act as a primary platform for two-leg control (Double Trouble / Leg Lace / Dracula family), limiting the defender’s ability to stand, spin, heist, or freely roll.
- Create structured pathways to decisive endpoints (heel hook / Achilles / toe hold / knee bar where applicable) and positional outcomes (Backside 50/50, Top outcomes, sweep-to-top) while managing Cross Ashi’s primary risk: back exposure.
- Encode the core operating rule: Cross Ashi is a power hub—if you do not control the second leg (or compensate with wedges + chest alignment), you are gambling.

## 2. Key Positions
- Cross Ashi baseline (core hub)
  - Cross-body entanglement that aligns your body for rotational breaking mechanics and exposes inside heel lines when knee-line + hip control are owned.
- Cross-Ashi family variants (anti-slip / anti-pummel / angle management)
  - Shoelace Ashi (secondary-leg framing and heel access control when defender is actively pummelling/clearing).
  - Stretch Ashi (used to counter primary leg escape attempts and maintain knee-line capture when the defender tries to elongate and extract).
- Achilles tether configurations (control + dilemma layer)
  - Figure-4 Achilles (control and reaction forcing while you build secondary-leg capture).
  - Reverse figure-4 Achilles (alternate tension/angle to prevent certain escapes and to manage back-safety while maintaining threat).
  - Achilles → heel hook conversion (tether to open heel line, then switch when exposure appears).
- Two-leg control platforms (Double Trouble family)
  - Double Trouble (two-leg capture that collapses base and blocks heist/spin).
  - Leg Lace (systematic secondary-leg capture line used to lock both legs together and restrict rotation).
  - Dracula family (Half Dracula / Double Dracula) for passing the leg and progressively collecting the secondary leg.
  - 2-on-1 ankle passing (a collect-and-steer method to move from one-leg to two-leg control without losing knee-line).
- Cross Ashi → Backside 50/50 landing
  - Common arrival state after roll-follow sequences; used as an asymmetry amplifier when the defender’s roll resolves into a clean backside alignment.
- Cross Ashi → Inside Ashi transition
  - When defender turns to correct hip alignment or you need a safer finishing angle while keeping heel access.
- Cross Ashi → Far-Hip Ashi safety shell
  - Used when your heel line becomes threatened or mutual exposure forms during double inversion/roll exchanges.
- Cross Ashi → Top outcomes (when available in the exchange)
  - Conversions that lead to top control hubs after sweeps or after forcing the defender to concede position while defending legs.

## 3. Key Cues
- Knee-line dominance first
  - If the defender’s knee starts to clear your hip line, you are no longer in a reliable finishing structure—re-wedge, re-centre, or transition.
- Center-chest principle (anti-back exposure)
  - When Cross Ashi becomes unstable or inversion exchanges begin, re-centre your chest toward their hips/legs to keep alignment and deny turn-behind.
- Back-safety is the tax of Cross Ashi
  - Do not over-rotate away from their hips to chase heel exposure.
- Two-leg control changes the game
  - Whenever feasible, collect the secondary leg (Double Trouble / Leg Lace / Dracula). One-leg Cross Ashi is higher variance; two-leg Cross Ashi is a system.
- Achilles as stabiliser (buy time to build structure)
  - Use Achilles grips/pressure to tether + force reaction while you re-centre chest, improve wedges, and complete secondary-leg capture.
- Follow the leg, not the foot
  - When they roll/invert, stay connected to the leg structure and land in a named hub (Backside, Inside, Far-Hip, or re-Cross).
- Double inversion rule (solve, don’t fight)
  - Maintain knee + heel connection, re-centre chest, prioritise landing in Backside / Inside / Far-Hip rather than finishing mid-scramble.
- Secondary-leg awareness is continuous
  - If the far leg is free, expect base-building (stand/heist/spin) and pummel-based escapes.

## 4. Control Gates (Quick Audit)
Run this before you “hunt heel”:
- Knee-line: can they start clearing knee past your hip line with a back-out / turn?
- Chest orientation: is your chest centred to their hips (not drifting away to save grips)?
- Back-safety: are you giving them a turn-behind lane as you rotate?
- Secondary leg: is the far leg trapped/neutralised (or at least assigned a job)?
If any answer is yes: rebuild wedges + chest-centre, or upgrade to two-leg control, or transition hubs.

## 5. Decision Rules
- When to commit to finishing mechanics
  - Commit only when: knee-line stable, chest centred, back-safety intact, and ideally the secondary leg controlled or functionally neutralised.
- When to prioritise two-leg control
  - If the secondary leg is giving them base (stand/heist/spin potential), collect it before escalating heel exposure.
  - If they are actively pummelling/slipping: switch to Shoelace/Stretch variants to preserve control while you re-collect the second leg.
- Achilles tether selection
  - If heel line is not clean: apply Achilles tether to force reaction and buy time to complete secondary-leg capture.
  - If their defence to Achilles creates heel exposure: convert immediately while keeping chest centred.
- Countering primary leg escape (knee-line drift)
  - If knee-line drifts: re-wedge first; if it continues drifting, switch to Stretch Ashi and re-centre chest; do not chase heel.
- Handling forced inversion / double inversion
  - If inversion exchange begins: maintain connection, re-centre chest, and select a landing hub.
  - If your heel becomes threatened during inversion: choose Far-Hip first, then rebuild offence.
- Backside 50/50 upgrade trigger
  - If a roll resolves into clean Backside alignment (rotation constrained + chest stays centred), upgrade immediately.
- Inside Ashi transition trigger
  - If defender turns to correct hips and it aligns a safer finishing angle, transition to Inside while keeping knee-line.

## 7. Drills & Games
- Cross-Ashi pummelling ladder
  - Cycle: Cross Ashi → Shoelace → Stretch → back to Cross; priority is knee-line retention + chest centring through each switch.
- Secondary-leg capture reps (Double Trouble build)
  - Start with primary leg trapped; goal is to collect the second leg (Double Trouble / Lace / Dracula) before any finishing mechanics are allowed.
- Center-chest constraint rounds
  - Defender’s goal: force back exposure (turn/hip escape). Attacker goal: keep chest centred to hips while maintaining knee-line and progressing to two-leg control.
- Achilles tether → collect → convert
  - Begin with figure-4/reverse figure-4 Achilles tether; defender gives a scripted reaction (turn/roll/pummel); attacker collects the secondary leg, then converts to heel exposure.
- Force-the-roll → planned landing
  - Defender performs a scripted roll/double inversion; attacker must follow and land in Backside / Inside / Far-Hip (coach calls the landing).
- Two-leg control maintenance game
  - Start in Double Trouble/Leg Lace; defender’s only goal is to free one leg and stand/heist. Attacker scores for keeping both legs controlled and producing a clean outcome (finish/sweep/top).

## 8. Common Mistakes
- Chasing heel exposure while allowing back exposure (turning away from their hips).
- Attacking with one-leg control when two-leg control is available, making spins/escapes easier.
- Letting knee-line drift during pummelling transitions (Cross ↔ Shoelace ↔ Stretch).
- Fighting double inversions with arm strength instead of staying connected, centring chest, and landing in a planned hub.
- Refusing to reset when structure is unclear (failing to re-centre chest), leading to counters or mutual exposure.
