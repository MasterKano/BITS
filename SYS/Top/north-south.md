# North-south

## Purpose

A top control hub to:
- stabilize after leg exits / denied backtakes / passing entries,
- lock cranial control to prevent re-square,
- force predictable reactions that feed pass/back branching.

## Core idea

- North-south is a **cranial-pin platform**.
- Your job is to remove the opponent’s ability to:
  - turn head/shoulders to face you,
  - recover knees inside,
  - build height to stand/sit-up.
- Once pinned, you force the **reaction fork**:
  - turn-toward (frames/face you) → pass progression
  - turn-away (run/turn out) → back attachment
  - turtle/quadpod attempt → tight waist / breakdown

## Key positions

- North-south pin (primary)
  - Head/shoulder line controlled so they cannot rotate to re-square.
  - Hip line managed so knees cannot re-enter.
- North-south → pass angle
  - You circle/angle to clear frames and establish chest-to-chest.
- North-south → back angle
  - You close distance to upper back as they turn away; connection first, hooks second.
- North-south → tight waist
  - When they turtle/quadpod or try to rise; tight waist becomes the connector.

## Entries

- From camping
  - When forward step is available and cranial control is present, step into north-south to deepen the pin.
- From body lock / side body lock
  - When you clear legs but need a stronger cranial pin to prevent re-square, drop to north-south.
- From denied backtake
  - If hooks/attachment slip but you remain on top, north-south is a safe landing to re-stabilize.
- From leg entanglement exit
  - As you separate legs and come up, if they are rotating to re-guard, step to north-south to lock head/shoulders first.

## Key cues (operational)

- “Head line first”: if head/shoulders are free, north-south becomes a chase.
- “Knees denied”: if knees can point back into you, expect re-guarding.
- “No float”: north-south requires continuous connection; loose contact = re-square.
- “Turn is the trigger”: don’t force an outcome until you see their turn.

## Control gates (must be true before branching)

### 1) Cranial shift present
- They cannot freely rotate head/shoulders to face you.
- Their frames are not driving your head/torso away.

### 2) Hip line managed
- Their hips cannot freely slide away to insert knees.
- If hips are escaping, you are not pinning—reposition first.

### 3) Knees denied
- They cannot bring knees between you and their torso to re-guard.
- If knees are inside, fix that before advancing.

### 4) No clean rise
- They cannot sit-up/stand without you collapsing the attempt.
- If they build height, you switch to tight waist / breakdown behavior.

## Decision rules (IF/THEN) — lane-by-lane

### A) Establishment
- IF you enter north-south -> THEN satisfy cranial shift first, then manage hips/knees.
- IF they are re-squaring (head turning toward you) -> THEN increase cranial control before trying to pass.
- IF you feel “float” (connection loosens) -> THEN pause advancement and re-connect before moving.

### B) Branch: turn-toward (frames / faces you)
- IF they turn toward -> THEN you are in the pass lane: circle/angle to chest-to-chest without driving into frames.
- IF frames stop your circling -> THEN re-win cranial control, then re-angle; do not push straight through frames.
- IF they keep turning toward to prevent back -> THEN take the gift: deepen pin and pass.

### C) Branch: turn-away (runs / turns out)
- IF they turn away -> THEN close distance to upper back immediately (connection before hooks).
- IF you are too far to connect -> THEN return to pin pressure until distance collapses; do not reach for hooks.
- IF the turn-away becomes a turtle/quadpod -> THEN switch to tight waist and break them down.

### D) Branch: turtle / quadpod attempt
- IF turtle forms -> THEN tight waist is the connector; breakdown first, then branch pass/back.
- IF they are building height in turtle -> THEN collapse the rise (no clean stand), then stabilize.

### E) Stabilizer hierarchy (when north-south isn’t holding)
- IF north-south is stable -> stay and force the fork (default).
- IF you need more hip control to stop re-guarding -> switch to camping.
- IF you need roll/angle to stay connected -> switch to cross-body ride.
- IF turtle forms -> tight waist.

## Opponent counters + immediate answers

- Counter: re-square by turning head/shoulders toward you.
  - Answer: increase cranial shift; do not chase pass angles without head-line control.
- Counter: re-guard by inserting knees.
  - Answer: deny knees first (hip management), then return to cranial pressure.
- Counter: sit-up / build height.
  - Answer: no clean rise—switch to tight waist/breakdown behavior immediately.
- Counter: turn-away sprint (distance creation).
  - Answer: stay connected; if distance opens, re-pin first, then attach.

## Failure signatures + fixes

- “North-south feels loose and they re-square.”
  - Fix: cranial shift missing; re-connect head/shoulders before moving.
- “They keep getting knees in.”
  - Fix: hip/knee denial is failing; re-angle and deny knees before circling.
- “I chase hooks and lose them.”
  - Fix: connection first; close upper-back distance, then hooks.
- “I stall with no progress.”
  - Fix: force the turn with cranial pressure; if no reaction, switch stabilizer (camping/ride).

## Completion checks

- You can hold them pinned such that:
  - head/shoulders can’t freely rotate,
  - knees can’t re-enter,
  - height can’t build cleanly.
- You can reliably force one of:
  - turn-toward (pass start),
  - turn-away (back attachment start),
  - turtle (tight waist trigger),
  without resetting to neutral.
