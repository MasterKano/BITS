# Kata gatame (bridge to back)

## Purpose

A connector system that uses kata gatame control to:
- prevent re-square during turn battles,
- force predictable reactions (turn-toward / turn-away),
- convert into stable back attachment or stable pin/passing outcomes.

## Core idea

- Kata gatame is a **shoulder-and-head clamp** that wins the shoulder line.
- It is used when the opponent is:
  - turning hard to face you (turn-toward defense),
  - framing and rotating to deny backtakes,
  - or threatening to re-square during your top transitions.
- Kata gatame is not just a submission threat; it is a **rotation-control bridge** into back or pass.

## Key positions

- Kata gatame clamp (primary)
  - Head + arm/shoulder captured; shoulder line is controlled so they cannot freely rotate to face you.
- Kata gatame → back connector
  - As they turn away under pressure, you keep chest contact and switch to upper-back attachment.
- Kata gatame → pin upgrade
  - If they stay facing/turning in, you stabilize chest-to-chest and progress passing.
- Kata gatame → tight waist
  - If they turtle/quadpod to avoid the turn battle, tight waist becomes the connector.

## Entries

- From denied backtake (they turn-in hard)
  - When your back lane is being denied by aggressive turn-in, kata gatame becomes the clamp that prevents re-square.
- From top control hubs (camping / north-south / ride)
  - If cranial shift is present but they frame/turn-in to stop advancement, kata gatame captures the shoulder line.
- From leg-to-top conversions
  - When leg entanglement exits create a shoulder-line battle, kata gatame is used to “win the turn” and branch pass/back.

## Key cues (operational)

- “Win the shoulder line”: kata gatame is about stopping shoulder rotation first.
- “Clamp before you move”: if you transition without the clamp, they re-square.
- “Turn decides the lane”: toward = pass; away = back; turtle = tight waist.
- “Connection stays”: if chest contact breaks, expect escape/re-square.

## Control gates (must be true before branching)

### 1) Shoulder line controlled
- They cannot freely rotate shoulders to face you.
- If shoulder rotates freely, kata gatame is not doing its job.

### 2) Head line controlled
- Head cannot turn freely to face you.
- If head is free, re-square is coming.

### 3) Chest contact maintained
- You are attached enough that their rotation must break your control to escape.
- If you are reaching, you are losing.

## Decision rules (IF/THEN) — lane-by-lane

### A) Establishment
- IF they turn in hard to deny the back -> THEN establish kata gatame clamp (win shoulder line).
- IF you can’t clamp because frames are too strong -> THEN re-establish cranial shift from a top hub (camping/north-south) and re-enter.
- IF clamp is loose -> THEN do not transition; tighten control first.

### B) Branch: turn-toward (faces hard / keeps framing)
- IF they keep turning toward -> THEN accept pass/pin lane; stabilize chest-to-chest and progress passing.
- IF they frame and create space -> THEN increase cranial control and re-close distance before advancing.
- IF they turn-toward to stop back -> THEN treat it as success: pass is the correct outcome.

### C) Branch: turn-away (runs/rolls)
- IF they turn away under kata pressure -> THEN switch to back lane:
  - close distance to upper back first,
  - then satisfy back-completion criteria before hooks.
- IF distance opens during the switch -> THEN return to top hub (camping/ride) and re-force the turn.

### D) Branch: turtle / flattening
- IF turtle/quadpod forms -> THEN tight waist trigger; breakdown first, then branch.
- IF they flatten and stop turning -> THEN upgrade to pin (north-south/camping) and re-run conversion loop.

### E) No scramble rule
- IF the clamp fails and you are losing position -> THEN drop to a stabilizer hub rather than chasing (camping / north-south / ride / tight waist).

## Opponent counters + immediate answers

- Counter: strong turn-in + frames to square.
  - Answer: kata gatame clamp to win shoulder line; if clamp fails, re-pin cranial shift first.
- Counter: explosive turn-away to create distance.
  - Answer: stay connected; if distance opens, re-stabilize in camping/ride then re-attach.
- Counter: turtle/quadpod.
  - Answer: tight waist/breakdown; do not chase hooks on a mobile turtle.
- Counter: re-square by freeing head line.
  - Answer: head line is a gate; re-close head/shoulder control before transitioning.

## Failure signatures + fixes

- “I clamp but they still re-square.”
  - Fix: shoulder line or head line gate is failing; tighten clamp before moving.
- “I switch to back and lose them.”
  - Fix: you broke chest contact; connection first, hooks second.
- “I insist on back when they’re turning in.”
  - Fix: turn-toward = pass lane; take it.
- “Clamp attempt becomes scramble.”
  - Fix: no scramble rule—drop to top stabilizer and rebuild.

## Completion checks

- They cannot rotate shoulders/head freely to face you without breaking your control first.
- You can force:
  - continued turn-in (pass lane),
  - turn-away (back lane),
  - turtle (tight waist),
  without losing top advantage.
- If back lane occurs, you can satisfy back completion criteria before hook pursuit.
