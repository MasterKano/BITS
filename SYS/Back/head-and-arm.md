# Head-and-arm (back control)

## Purpose

A connector and retention system for:
- converting turn-away reactions into stable back attachment,
- preventing the opponent from “walking the head out” to re-square,
- finishing back takes when hooks are not yet available or are being denied.

## Core idea

- Head-and-arm is the **bridge** between “I’m behind them” and “back is completed.”
- The opponent’s primary escape is to free the head line (walk head out / rotate shoulders), then re-face.
- Your job is to:
  - close the head line (shrink the circle),
  - keep chest-to-upper-back connection,
  - and only then insert hooks/body triangle.

## Key positions

- Head-and-arm bridge (primary)
  - A “closed circle” around head/arm/shoulder line that prevents re-facing.
- Head-and-arm → upper-back clamp
  - Reinforces chest-to-upper-back connection so hooks are inserted from stability.
- Head-and-arm → tight waist fallback
  - When they turtle/quadpod or you lose back alignment; regain stabilization without scrambling.

## Entries

- From leg-to-back conversions (turn-away)
  - When the opponent turns away during leg-to-top sequences: head-and-arm becomes your first connector before hooks.
- From denied backtake attempts
  - If hooks are slipping, keep head-and-arm rather than chasing legs/hooks from distance.
- From turtle transitions
  - When they fold to turtle to deny hooks, head-and-arm can be used to keep them from turning back in as you break them down.

## Key cues (operational)

- “Shrink the circle”: if the circle is big, the head walks out.
- “Connection before hooks”: hooks do not replace head-line control.
- “Centre-line or lose it”: hanging off to one side invites sit-through/re-face.
- “If head line is free, the back is not real.”

## Control gates (must be true before hook pursuit)

### 1) Head line closed
- Their head cannot freely rotate to face you.
- They cannot walk head out without first breaking your control.

### 2) Chest-to-upper-back connection
- You are attached, not reaching.
- If space exists, expect rotation and head escape.

### 3) Centre-line alignment
- Your chest is aligned to spine line enough that they can’t sit-through to re-guard.
- You are not hanging off “one shoulder only.”

### 4) Hips-inside-knees path available
- Your hip position prevents their knees from pointing back into you to re-face/re-guard.
- If their knees are pointing into you, expect the re-square.

## Decision rules (IF/THEN) — lane-by-lane

### A) Establishment (getting head-and-arm stable)
- IF opponent turns away and you can attach upper back -> THEN head-and-arm bridge immediately (don’t hunt hooks first).
- IF you are behind but not connected -> THEN close distance first; do not reach for hooks.
- IF they begin walking head out -> THEN shrink the circle and re-close head line before anything else.

### B) Retention (stopping re-face)
- IF they rotate shoulders toward you -> THEN head line is opening; re-close and re-centre.
- IF you feel your position “sliding off” -> THEN you are off centre-line; re-align to spine line.
- IF their knees begin pointing back into you -> THEN correct hip position (hips-inside-knees path) before hook chasing.

### C) Hook insertion (downstream)
- IF control gates 1–3 are satisfied -> THEN insert hooks/body triangle.
- IF one hook goes in but control gates degrade -> THEN pause hook hunting and rebuild head line + connection.
- IF hook attempts create space -> THEN abort hook pursuit and re-close head line.

### D) Denied attempts (no scramble rule)
- IF they defeat hook insertion and start to re-square -> THEN keep head-and-arm as long as possible.
- IF head-and-arm is lost -> THEN immediately drop to a top stabilizer (tight waist / camping / ride) rather than scrambling.
- IF turtle forms during the denial -> THEN tight waist trigger; breakdown first, then re-enter back lane.

## Opponent counters + immediate answers

- Counter: walk head out (head turns toward daylight) → re-face.
  - Answer: shrink circle + re-close head line; do not chase hooks while head is escaping.
- Counter: shoulder rotation to face you (frames appear).
  - Answer: re-centre to spine line and re-attach upper back before continuing.
- Counter: sit-through / knee-pointing into you to recover guard.
  - Answer: fix hips-inside-knees path; if lost, accept pass lane via top stabilizer.
- Counter: turtle/quadpod to deny hooks.
  - Answer: tight waist/breakdown, then re-enter back lane; do not hook-chase a mobile turtle.

## Failure signatures + fixes

- “I’m behind them but they re-face instantly.”
  - Fix: missing head line closure and/or upper-back connection; rebuild bridge first.
- “I get one hook and lose the rest.”
  - Fix: you’re inserting hooks from instability; re-close head line and attach upper back first.
- “I’m hanging off one side and they sit through.”
  - Fix: centre-line alignment failure; re-centre to spine line.
- “Hook chasing creates scrambles.”
  - Fix: hooks are downstream; treat head line as the primary gate.

## Completion checks

- They cannot:
  - walk head out to re-face,
  - rotate shoulders freely to square,
  - sit-through to recover guard without breaking your control first.
- You can insert hooks/body triangle without losing connection.
- Back completion criteria are satisfied (connection + alignment + hips path).
